# Ancient & Medieval History — Chronology

Events, people and periods in chronological order.

Ranges are placed at their start date. Approximate dates remain labelled. Year-only entries do not establish an exact order within that year.

## Before 10,000 BCE · Early stone-tool traditions

### c.500,000–10,000 BCE · teaching range · Palaeolithic traditions

Broad chapter convention; newer site chronologies extend beyond this band. Hunting–gathering, flaked tools, caves and open sites; regional transitions overlap.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.40,000–10,000 BCE · Upper Palaeolithic reference horizon

Blades, burins, bone tools, ornaments and painting; not the first appearance of Homo sapiens.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

## 10,000–3001 BCE · Foragers and early farming

### c.10,000–6000 BCE · teaching range · Mesolithic traditions

Microlithic tools, hunting/fishing/gathering, rock art and changing settlement patterns; later survival and herding vary by region.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.9700 BCE · about 11,700 years ago · Beginning of the Holocene

Approximate BCE placement of the geological boundary. Later forager and early farming cultures overlap this epoch; cultural boundaries are regional.

*Environment · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### Conventionally c.7000 BCE; revised dating disputed · Early farming at Mehrgarh

Conventional textbook placement before urban Harappa. A 2025 tooth-enamel study instead places Period I about 5250–4650 cal BCE; retain the distinction rather than two supposedly separate settlements.

*Settlement · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.6000–1000 BCE · teaching range · Regional Neolithic cultures

Farming/herding, ground tools, durable villages and pottery in many phases. Conventional earlier Mehrgarh dates lie outside this simplified band.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.3300/3200–2600 BCE · Early Harappan / Regionalisation

Regional villages and fortified towns, crafts, trade and wheeled transport develop; dates vary by site.

*Harappan period · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

## 3000–1 BCE · Regional cultures and oral traditions

### c.3000–1000 BCE · broad sequence · Burzahom habitation sequence

Kashmir site spans Neolithic, Megalithic and early historical phases; pit dwellings and human/animal burials are important. This is not one homogeneous 2000-year Neolithic phase.

*Settlement · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.3000–500 BCE · teaching range · Chalcolithic regional cultures

Copper and stone, rural production/crafts/exchange. Some phases precede or overlap Harappa; others follow it.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.2600 BCE–1900 BCE · Ahar–Banas reference band

Banas basin; Ahar, Gilund and Balathal; chapter chronology, with broader regional ranges in other studies.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.2600–1900 BCE · Mature Harappan / Integration

Large cities, shared script/seals, weights and brick proportions; regional diversity persists.

*Harappan period · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.2500 BCE–1500 BCE · Northeastern Neolithic reference horizon

Broad chapter range, or later; Daojali Hading and Garo hills illustrate regional diversity.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.2100 BCE–2000 BCE · Kayatha reference band

Chhoti Kali Sindh/Chambal drainage, Madhya Pradesh; dates depend on phase and excavation.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1900–1300 BCE · Late Harappan / Localisation

Urban contraction, regional settlement/craft changes and agricultural adaptation; not complete disappearance of population or culture.

*Harappan period · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1800 BCE–1400 BCE · Prabhas reference band

Prabhas Patan/Somnath, Gujarat; chapter reference chronology.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1700 BCE–1400 BCE · Malwa reference band

Navdatoli on the Narmada; regional Chalcolithic culture.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1500 BCE–500 BCE · Vedic textual composition traditions

Broad conventional textbook range; early Vedic oral traditions are often placed c.1500–600 BCE under a broader protohistoric label.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1400 BCE–700 BCE · Jorwe reference band

Maharashtra; Jorwe, Inamgaon, Daimabad and Chandoli. Sites occupy different tributaries and phases.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.300 BCE–300 CE · Sangam literary reference period

Broad conventional span; Silappadikaram and Manimekalai are later/post-Sangam epics, not evidence for a single composition date.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

## 1–1499 CE · Historical sources referenced

### 1st century CE · Periplus of the Erythraean Sea

Anonymous Greek-language account of navigation, ports and commodities; useful for Indian Ocean trade.

*Historical source · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 2nd century CE · Ptolemy’s Geography

Greek geographical account supplying evidence on places, ports and trade.

*Historical source · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### Early 5th century CE · Faxian’s Indian journey

Chinese Buddhist pilgrim; account illuminates Gupta-era society. The chapter’s 500 CE should not be treated as an exact visit date.

*Historical source · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.629–645 CE · journey · Xuanzang’s Indian journey

Chinese Buddhist pilgrim associated with Harsha and Nalanda; 7th century, not an exact 700 CE visit.

*Historical source · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 12th century CE · Kalhana’s Rajatarangini

Kashmir historical narrative; evaluate literary form and retrospective traditions.

*Historical source · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

## 1500 onwards · Archaeological discoveries and research

### 1863 · Foote discovers Pallavaram handaxe

Robert Bruce Foote’s discovery is a landmark in the study of Indian prehistory.

*Archaeological discovery · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1867–68 · Carlleyle documents Indian rock paintings

Early modern discovery/documentation, not the date the paintings were made.

*Archaeological discovery · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1921 · Sahni excavates Harappa

Daya Ram Sahni’s excavations initiate recognition of the major urban site.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1922 · Banerji investigates Mohenjo-daro

R.D. Banerji’s work helps reveal the second great urban site.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1924 · Indus civilisation publicly announced

John Marshall announces the recognition of a previously unknown ancient civilisation; distinguish discovery, excavation and publication.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1929 · Majumdar excavates Amri

Early regional occupation and later cultural sequences documented.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1931 · Majumdar investigates Chanhudaro

Subsequent Mackay excavations reveal specialised craft activity.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1953 · Y.D. Sharma begins Ropar excavations

Multi-period settlement and cemetery evidence; the site is on the Sutlej.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1955 · F.A. Khan excavates Kot Diji

Early Harappan fortified settlement and destruction layers documented.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1955–1962 · Lothal excavations

S.R. Rao’s excavations identify the basin/dockyard interpretation, workshops and other urban features.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1957–58 · Wakankar identifies Bhimbetka shelters

V.S. Wakankar’s survey brought the rock shelters to scholarly attention.

*Archaeological discovery · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1958 · Alamgirpur excavated

Y.D. Sharma investigates Late Harappan remains in the Hindon region.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1960–1969 · Kalibangan major excavation programme

B.B. Lal, B.K. Thapar and colleagues investigate early/mature phases, ploughed field and features interpreted as fire altars.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1964 · Surkotada identified

J.P. Joshi identifies the Kutch site; equid remains later prompt a disputed horse identification.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1973–1974 · Banawali excavations begin

R.S. Bisht’s work documents early/mature settlement and the terracotta plough model.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1989–2005 · Dholavira major excavations

R.S. Bisht’s programme exposes fortified divisions, water management and the ten-sign inscription; field seasons are often described as 1989/90–2005.

*Archaeological discovery · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 2016 · Bhirrana chronology and climate study

Research proposes earlier local occupation and examines monsoon/subsistence change; this does not redate the mature urban phase to 7000 BCE.

*Research and heritage · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 2018 · Attirampakkam Middle Palaeolithic dating published

Luminescence study dates the local transition around 385 ±64 ka and continuation to 172 ±41 ka; not a uniform subcontinental chronology.

*Research · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 2019 · Rakhigarhi genome study published

One successfully analysed Harappan genome shows no detected Steppe-pastoralist ancestry in that individual; broad genetic/linguistic claims require more evidence.

*Research and heritage · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 2020 · Five iconic archaeological destinations proposed

Budget 2020–21 includes Rakhigarhi, Dholavira, Hastinapur, Sivasagar and Adichanallur; proposal is distinct from completion.

*Research and heritage · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 2021 · Dholavira inscribed as World Heritage

UNESCO recognises the Harappan city; its urban layout and water management are key attributes.

*Research and heritage · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 2025 · Mehrgarh Period I re-dating study

Human tooth-enamel radiocarbon results propose c.5250–4650 cal BCE, challenging conventional much earlier dates; broader regional conclusions require further evidence.

*Research · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)
