# Ancient & Medieval History — Chronology

Events, people and periods in chronological order.

Ranges are placed at their start date. Approximate dates remain labelled. Year-only entries do not establish an exact order within that year.

## Before 10,000 BCE · Early stone-tool traditions

### c.500,000–10,000 BCE · teaching range · Palaeolithic traditions

Broad chapter convention; newer site chronologies extend beyond this band. Hunting–gathering, flaked tools, caves and open sites; regional transitions overlap.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.40,000–10,000 BCE · Upper Palaeolithic reference horizon

Blades, burins, bone tools, ornaments and painting; not the first appearance of Homo sapiens.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

## 10,000–3001 BCE · Foragers and early farming

### c.10,000–6000 BCE · teaching range · Mesolithic traditions

Microlithic tools, hunting/fishing/gathering, rock art and changing settlement patterns; later survival and herding vary by region.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.9700 BCE · about 11,700 years ago · Beginning of the Holocene

Approximate BCE placement of the geological boundary. Later forager and early farming cultures overlap this epoch; cultural boundaries are regional.

*Environment · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### Conventionally c.7000 BCE; revised dating disputed · Early farming at Mehrgarh

Conventional textbook placement before urban Harappa. A 2025 tooth-enamel study instead places Period I about 5250–4650 cal BCE; retain the distinction rather than two supposedly separate settlements.

*Settlement · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.6000–1000 BCE · teaching range · Regional Neolithic cultures

Farming/herding, ground tools, durable villages and pottery in many phases. Conventional earlier Mehrgarh dates lie outside this simplified band.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.3300/3200–2600 BCE · Early Harappan / Regionalisation

Regional villages and fortified towns, crafts, trade and wheeled transport develop; dates vary by site.

*Harappan period · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

## 3000–1 BCE · Regional cultures and oral traditions

### c.3000–1000 BCE · broad sequence · Burzahom habitation sequence

Kashmir site spans Neolithic, Megalithic and early historical phases; pit dwellings and human/animal burials are important. This is not one homogeneous 2000-year Neolithic phase.

*Settlement · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.3000–500 BCE · teaching range · Chalcolithic regional cultures

Copper and stone, rural production/crafts/exchange. Some phases precede or overlap Harappa; others follow it.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.2600 BCE–1900 BCE · Ahar–Banas reference band

Banas basin; Ahar, Gilund and Balathal; chapter chronology, with broader regional ranges in other studies.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.2600–1900 BCE · Mature Harappan / Integration

Large cities, shared script/seals, weights and brick proportions; regional diversity persists.

*Harappan period · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.2500 BCE–1500 BCE · Northeastern Neolithic reference horizon

Broad chapter range, or later; Daojali Hading and Garo hills illustrate regional diversity.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.2100 BCE–2000 BCE · Kayatha reference band

Chhoti Kali Sindh/Chambal drainage, Madhya Pradesh; dates depend on phase and excavation.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### Second millennium BCE · broad context · Indo-Aryan movements and interactions

Broad second-millennium BCE context for movements through Central Asia and interaction in South Asia. Linguistic, archaeological and genetic evidence have different scopes; neither pottery nor DNA directly identifies an individual’s language.

*Cultural period · [C3](03.%20The%20Vedic%20Age%28Rig%20Vedic%20%26%20Later%20Vedic%29%20%28c.%201500-500%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1900–1300 BCE · Late Harappan / Localisation

Urban contraction, regional settlement/craft changes and agricultural adaptation; not complete disappearance of population or culture.

*Harappan period · [C2](02.%20Harappan%20Civilisation%3ABronze%20Age%28c.%202600-1900%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1800 BCE–1400 BCE · Prabhas reference band

Prabhas Patan/Somnath, Gujarat; chapter reference chronology.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1700 BCE–1400 BCE · Malwa reference band

Navdatoli on the Narmada; regional Chalcolithic culture.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1500–1000 BCE · Early / Rig Vedic phase

Northwestern geographical emphasis; pastoralism alongside cultivation; jana, chiefs and assemblies; older Rigvedic family books. Boundaries are conventional and approximate.

*Cultural period · [C3](03.%20The%20Vedic%20Age%28Rig%20Vedic%20%26%20Later%20Vedic%29%20%28c.%201500-500%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1500 BCE–500 BCE · Vedic textual composition traditions

Broad conventional range for Vedic oral/textual traditions; early Rigvedic and later Vedic layers overlap regional cultural changes. Oral composition, textual layering and surviving manuscript dates are distinct.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html) / [C3](03.%20The%20Vedic%20Age%28Rig%20Vedic%20%26%20Later%20Vedic%29%20%28c.%201500-500%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1400 BCE–700 BCE · Jorwe reference band

Maharashtra; Jorwe, Inamgaon, Daimabad and Chandoli. Sites occupy different tributaries and phases.

*Cultural period · [C1](01.%20Pre-Historic%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1000–500 BCE · conventional chapter range · Later Vedic phase

Kuru–Panchala and eastward expansion; agriculture, stronger territorial kingship, elaborate sacrifice and philosophical inquiry. Some periodisations end this phase around 600 BCE.

*Cultural period · [C3](03.%20The%20Vedic%20Age%28Rig%20Vedic%20%26%20Later%20Vedic%29%20%28c.%201500-500%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.600–300 BCE · Dharmasutra traditions

Broad chapter composition band for normative texts on conduct and social duties; dates differ by text. Prescriptions must not be equated with universal practice.

*Textual tradition · [C3](03.%20The%20Vedic%20Age%28Rig%20Vedic%20%26%20Later%20Vedic%29%20%28c.%201500-500%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.600–300 BCE · broad regional horizon · Second urbanisation and territorial states

Ganga-basin towns, surplus agriculture, craft specialisation, trade, early coinage and larger territorial states; regional chronologies vary. Agrarian surplus, crafts, coins, trade and new social groups formed the setting for Buddhist, Jaina and other shramana movements. Economic change is one factor, not a complete explanation of every doctrine.

*Cultural period · [C4](04.%20The%20Mahajanpadas%20of%20the%20Indo-Gangetic%20Plain%28c.%20600-300%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html) / [C5](05.%20Buddhism%20and%20Jainism/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.600 BCE · broad teaching horizon · Upanishadic inquiry in the later Vedic setting

Around this broad horizon, traditions associated with Panchala and Videha develop discussions of self, knowledge, action and liberation. This does not date every Upanishad to one year; early texts and later layers span a wider period.

*Textual tradition · [C3](03.%20The%20Vedic%20Age%28Rig%20Vedic%20%26%20Later%20Vedic%29%20%28c.%201500-500%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 599–527 BCE · traditional, disputed chronology · Mahavira: traditional life chronology

Traditional dates used in the chapter; historical chronologies vary. Twenty-fourth Tirthankara; Kundagrama near Vaishali, Jnatrika clan; final liberation associated with Pavapuri. Ages and family details belong to traditions with sect differences.

*Religious biography · [C5](05.%20Buddhism%20and%20Jainism/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 563–483 BCE · conventional, disputed chronology · Gautama Buddha: conventional life chronology

One traditional chronology, not an uncontested historical dating. Lumbini birth; renunciation, enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, first sermon at Sarnath, final passing at Kushinara. Some historical chronologies place his life later.

*Religious biography · [C5](05.%20Buddhism%20and%20Jainism/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.559–530 BCE · conventional reign · Cyrus and the eastern Achaemenid frontier

Expansion towards northwestern South Asia belongs to the sixth-century BCE background; exact local Indian conquest claims remain uncertain.

*Reign · [C4](04.%20The%20Mahajanpadas%20of%20the%20Indo-Gangetic%20Plain%28c.%20600-300%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.544–492 BCE · conventional chronology · Bimbisara consolidates Magadha

Haryanka ruler; Rajagriha capital, conquest of Anga, matrimonial alliances and diplomacy. Founder status and precise regnal dates are not certain.

*Reign · [C4](04.%20The%20Mahajanpadas%20of%20the%20Indo-Gangetic%20Plain%28c.%20600-300%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.518 BCE · conventional approximation · Darius I extends authority in the Indus region

Persian incorporation/consolidation in parts of the northwest; Gandara and Hindush are separate imperial geographical names, not all India.

*Political change · [C4](04.%20The%20Mahajanpadas%20of%20the%20Indo-Gangetic%20Plain%28c.%20600-300%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### Fifth century BCE · broad period · Indian and Gandarian troops in Persian service

Contingents appear in accounts of Xerxes' campaigns against Greece; does not imply Persian conquest of the whole subcontinent.

*Military history · [C4](04.%20The%20Mahajanpadas%20of%20the%20Indo-Gangetic%20Plain%28c.%20600-300%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.492–460 BCE · conventional chronology · Ajatashatru expands Magadha

Conflict over Kashi and defeat of Vajji; war-machine traditions and Vassakara diplomacy. Buddhist tradition places Buddha's death and First Council in his reign, but absolute chronologies differ.

*Reign · [C4](04.%20The%20Mahajanpadas%20of%20the%20Indo-Gangetic%20Plain%28c.%20600-300%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.483 BCE · traditional date; chronology disputed · First Buddhist council: Rajagriha tradition

Traditionally shortly after Buddha’s passing, under Ajatashatru, led by Mahakassapa; Upali recites discipline and Ananda discourses. Absolute date follows one Buddha chronology; do not imply the entire later Abhidhamma was already fixed.

*Council · [C5](05.%20Buddhism%20and%20Jainism/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.460–444 BCE · conventional chronology · Udayin and the capital at Pataliputra

Associated with transferring the Magadhan capital to Pataliputra at strategic ancient river junctions; not Udayana of Vatsa.

*Reign · [C4](04.%20The%20Mahajanpadas%20of%20the%20Indo-Gangetic%20Plain%28c.%20600-300%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.412–345 BCE · conventional chronology · Shishunaga dynasty

Shishunaga annexes Avanti and is associated with Vaishali; Kalashoka with Pataliputra and the Second Buddhist Council at Vaishali. Exact chronology varies.

*Dynasty · [C4](04.%20The%20Mahajanpadas%20of%20the%20Indo-Gangetic%20Plain%28c.%20600-300%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.fourth–third centuries BCE · later traditional association · Bhadrabahu and southward migration: traditional setting

Later traditions connect a twelve-year Magadhan famine, Bhadrabahu’s movement south and Chandragupta Maurya’s Jaina renunciation at Shravanabelagola. Not an independently established exact itinerary or a single instantaneous sectarian split.

*Religious history · [C5](05.%20Buddhism%20and%20Jainism/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html) / [C6](06.%20The%20Maurya%20Empire%20%28c%20324-187BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.fourth–third centuries BCE · traditional setting · Jaina Pataliputra council and transmission traditions

Associated with Sthulabhadra and recovering/arranging Angas. Source’s 310 BCE is a teaching convention, not an established exact date. Related famine/migration accounts are later traditions; Digambara tradition does not accept this recension as authoritative.

*Council · [C5](05.%20Buddhism%20and%20Jainism/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.400 BCE–400 CE · Mahabharata: layered composition

Broad conventional composition/redaction band, with oral formation and later layers. The epic’s story setting is not the date of its surviving textual compilation.

*Textual tradition · [C3](03.%20The%20Vedic%20Age%28Rig%20Vedic%20%26%20Later%20Vedic%29%20%28c.%201500-500%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.400 BCE–300 CE · Ramayana: layered composition

Broad chapter composition band; the received text contains different layers. Traditional story chronology is distinct from historical composition dates.

*Textual tradition · [C3](03.%20The%20Vedic%20Age%28Rig%20Vedic%20%26%20Later%20Vedic%29%20%28c.%201500-500%20BCE%29/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.383 BCE · conventional date; chronology disputed · Second Buddhist council: Vaishali tradition

Traditionally about a century after Buddha, associated with Kalashoka and Sabbakami; discipline disputes. Accounts of the early Sthavira–Mahasanghika division vary.

*Council · [C5](05.%20Buddhism%20and%20Jainism/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 356–323 BCE · Life of Alexander III of Macedon

Mahapadma Nanda associated with wider conquest, including Kalinga, taxation and irrigation; Dhana Nanda is the last major ruler. Origin and army-size traditions require caution.

Campaign through the northwest and Punjab, return down the Indus and land/sea withdrawal. Resistance extended beyond Porus; no Magadha conquest.
