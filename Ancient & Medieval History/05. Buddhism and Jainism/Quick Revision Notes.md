# Buddhism and Jainism

## 1. Why new religious movements emerged

**Mid-first millennium BCE:** Buddhism, Jainism and other **shramana** traditions grew amid the mahajanapadas and the **second urbanisation, c.600–300 BCE**. They challenged Vedic ritual authority and offered paths of ethical conduct and liberation. They were independent traditions, not simply branches of a single reform organisation.

| Change | Connection with religious change |
|---|---|
| **Varna hierarchy and priestly privileges** | Kshatriya rulers, merchants and others questioned hereditary ritual superiority; Buddha and Mahavira belonged to Kshatriya clans |
| **Agriculture expanded into eastern UP–Bihar** | Iron tools, fertile land and cattle-assisted cultivation increased surplus; criticism of animal sacrifice appealed to cultivators. This is an explanatory connection, not proof that sacrifice alone obstructed farming |
| **Towns, crafts, exchange and punch-marked coins** | Merchants/artisans sought recognition and peaceful conditions for trade; donations supported wandering teachers and monasteries |
| **Social conflict and inequality** | Non-violence, truth, generosity, restraint and accessible teaching offered ethical answers to insecurity and suffering |
| **Intellectual debate** | Questions about self, rebirth, karma and liberation circulated among Brahmanical thinkers and renouncers; no single economic cause explains every doctrine |

Urban centres to recognise: **Rajagriha, Shravasti, Varanasi, Vaishali, Champa, Kaushambi, Ujjaini and, later, Pataliputra**. Do not assume all were equally developed throughout 600–300 BCE. Confucius and Greek thinkers provide a wider background of inquiry; **Zoroaster's dates are disputed**, so do not put all these teachers in one securely dated sixth-century cohort.

## 2. Buddha: life, places and symbols

**Buddha = awakened one; Siddhartha Gautama = Shakyamuni / Tathagata.** Traditional accounts combine history with sacred biography. **563–483 BCE** is the conventional chronology used in the chapter; other chronologies place his life/death later. Retain the sequence without treating these exact years as settled archaeological facts.

| Stage | Essential recall |
|---|---|
| **Birth** | **Lumbini**, present-day Nepal, near the Shakya territory of Kapilavastu; father **Suddhodana**, Shakya chief; mother **Mahamaya/Maya**; raised by **Mahaprajapati Gautami** after Maya's death, traditionally seven days after birth; **Vaishakha Purnima** birth festival tradition |
| Family | Tradition names **Yashodhara** as wife (marriage traditionally at **16**) and **Rahula** as son; **32 mahapurusha marks** underpin the sacred prediction of universal ruler or renouncer. Maya is usually associated with the **Koliya** clan; do not confuse **Koliya** with **Kosala**, the larger kingdom connected with Shakya territory |
| **Four sights** | Old age, sickness, death and an ascetic → inquiry into suffering |
| **Great Renunciation / Mahabhinishkramana** | Traditionally at **29**; attendant **Channa**, horse **Kanthaka** |
| Search | Teachers **Alara Kalama** and **Uddaka Ramaputta**; severe austerities with five ascetics; rejected self-mortification; **Sujata's milk-rice offering** belongs to the sacred life narrative—her caste should not be asserted from an unverified label |
| **Enlightenment / Bodhi** | Traditionally at **35**, after six years' search; **Uruvela/Bodh Gaya**, beside **Niranjana**, under the **peepal/Bodhi tree** |
| **First sermon** | **Deer Park, Isipatana/Rishipattana, Sarnath**, near Varanasi; **Dhammachakkappavattana / Dharmachakrapravartana** = setting the Dhamma wheel in motion |
| First five companions | **Kondanna, Vappa, Bhaddiya, Mahanama, Assaji**; the first core of the monastic community |
| Teaching and final passing | Taught for over four decades; **Mahaparinibbana/Mahaparinirvana at Kushinara/Kushinagar**, in **Malla** territory, traditionally aged **80** |

**Kosala and Magadha** are the kingdoms most directly associated with his life in the recurring PYQ; **Avanti and Gandhara** are distractors in that question. Kushinara's Malla affiliation and Sarnath's location should still be retained separately.

### Symbols and gestures

| Symbol | Recall |
|---|---|
| Lotus; bull in conventional exam symbolism | Birth; bull relates to the traditional birth association, not every depiction |
| Riderless horse | Great Renunciation |
| Bodhi tree / empty seat | Enlightenment / Buddha's presence in early narrative art |

Narratives of attempts on the Bodhi tree name **Tissarakkha under Ashoka**, **Pushyamitra Shunga in the second century BCE**, and **Shashanka in the early seventh century CE**. Their details come from later religious accounts and have differing historical reliability; do not present every destruction episode or a precise **600 CE** date as independently proven.

## 3. Buddhist teaching: the shortest complete map

### Three jewels: distinguish from the Jaina three jewels

**Buddha = teacher/awakened one → Dhamma = teaching/path → Sangha = community of practitioners, especially the monastic order.** Lay followers are **upasakas** and **upasikas**; monks and nuns are **bhikkhus** and **bhikkhunis**.

### Four Noble Truths

| Truth | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Dukkha** | Conditioned existence is marked by suffering, instability and unsatisfactoriness; not merely physical pain |
| **Samudaya** | Craving/**tanha/trishna**, tied to ignorance and attachment, produces suffering |
| **Nirodha** | Cessation of craving/suffering is possible: **Nibbana/Nirvana** |
| **Magga/Marga** | The **Noble Eightfold Path** leads to cessation |

<!-- flow:The Four Noble Truths|Dukkha: recognise suffering|Samudaya: understand craving and its causes|Nirodha: cessation is possible|Magga: cultivate the Eightfold Path -->

### Eightfold Path: remember 2 + 3 + 3

| Training | Right factors | Practical meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Wisdom / panna-prajna** | **View** (*samma-ditthi*); **intention/thought** (*samma-sankappa*) | Understand reality; cultivate renunciation, goodwill and harmlessness |
| **Ethical conduct / sila** | **Speech** (*vaca*); **action** (*kammanta*); **livelihood** (*ajiva*) | Avoid false/harmful speech, harmful acts and exploitative occupations |
| **Mental discipline / samadhi** | **Effort** (*vayama*); **mindfulness** (*sati*); **concentration** (*samadhi*) | Prevent/unlearn unwholesome states, sustain awareness and train attention |

**Middle Path:** avoids both sensual indulgence and extreme self-mortification. It is not indifference between ethical and unethical conduct. The first sermon connects the Middle Way, Four Truths and Eightfold Path. [Primary discourse, SN 56.11](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn56/sn56.011.than.html).

### Five precepts and renunciation

**Abstain from killing → taking what is not given → sexual misconduct → false speech → intoxicants causing heedlessness.** Lay sexual ethics concern misconduct; monastic discipline requires celibacy. Renunciants also follow rules concerning eating after noon, entertainment/adornment, luxurious beds and money; **the complete Vinaya is much larger than “five plus three” rules**.

### Karma, self and liberation

- **Anicca/anitya:** impermanence. **Anatta/anatman:** no permanent, independent self. Buddhism accepts **karma and rebirth without a transmigrating eternal atman**.
- **Karma is intentional action**, not merely priestly ritual; ethical intention, speech and bodily action have consequences.
- **Nirvana:** extinguishing greed/craving, hatred and delusion; achievable during life. **Not physical death, annihilation of an eternal soul, or a temporary heavenly holiday.** Final passing of the liberated is parinirvana.
- Buddhism does not ground liberation in a **creator God**, but traditional cosmologies contain **devas, heavens, hells, hungry ghosts and other rebirth realms**. The source table's “no heaven or hell” claim is wrong. [Canonical examples, Itivuttaka](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/iti/iti.3.050-099.than.html).
- The **ten realms** sequence in the chapter—Buddha, bodhisattva, pratyekabuddha, shravaka, devas, humans, asuras, animals, pretas and hell beings—is a **later classificatory scheme**, not the one universal early Buddhist cosmology. The last are **hell beings**, not a social class of “depraved men”.
- **Ahimsa** is central; early monastic acceptance of certain donated meat means **all Buddhists were not uniformly vegetarian**. Rules differ by tradition; this does not license killing specifically to feed monks.
- Teachings as a **raft**: use the path to cross suffering; do not turn attachment to doctrine into another obstacle. **Upaya** = skilful means, especially developed in Mahayana thought.

## 4. Sangha, women and social limits

The Sangha spread teaching through itinerant preaching, rainy-season residence, collective discipline and lay patronage. It offered spiritual recognition beyond inherited varna; **Upali**, traditionally a barber, stands beside Brahmana disciples **Sariputta, Mahamoggallana and Mahakassapa** and Kshatriya figures **Ananda and Aniruddha**. **Chunda**, associated with the final meal, is traditionally a smith.

- **Mahaprajapati Gautami:** first nun in the traditional account; **Ananda** intercedes for women's admission. Nuns such as **Khema, Uppalavanna/Utpalavarna, Dhammadinna, Sumedha and Sumangalamata** illustrate women's religious agency; **Therigatha** preserves elder nuns' verses.
- **Ambapali/Amrapali of Vaishali**, **Addhakasi of Kasi** and **Padmavati of Ujjaini** illustrate inclusion of women from socially stigmatised backgrounds in Buddhist narratives.
- Traditional Vinaya accounts impose **eight garudhammas**: seniority of monks over nuns; residence near monks during rains; fortnightly instruction; joint post-rains review; joint procedure for serious offences; two-year training before full ordination; no abuse of monks; asymmetric admonition. These show **institutional inequality despite equal spiritual capacity**. Their textual formation/historicity is debated; predictions blaming women for shortening the religion's life are not verified historical causation.
- **Not a modern abolition programme:** debts, slavery and military service could restrict admission without release/permission. Monastic communities depended on donations and generally recognised property, family obligations and political authority.
- Buddhist criticism targets **birth-based spiritual superiority**; some texts still privilege Kshatriya rank. Avoid the claim that every social distinction vanished.

### Buddhism and Brahmanical/Upanishadic traditions

Shared questions: karma, rebirth, discipline, knowledge and liberation; there were **debates and borrowing as well as disagreements**. Upanishads developed within Brahmanical traditions, not simply outside them as a united anti-Brahmana movement.

| Issue | Buddhism | Upanishadic traditions |
|---|---|---|
| Self | **Anatman**: rejects permanent self | Inquiry into **atman**, often related to **Brahman** |
| Ultimate reality | No necessary creator-God foundation | Diverse accounts of Brahman/ultimate reality; **Brahman is not simply the god Brahma** |
| Liberation | Nirvana; cessation of craving/ignorance | Moksha; explanations vary—do not reduce every school to literal merging with a personal deity |
| Authority | Vedas not binding authority | Located within the Vedic textual tradition |

## 5. Buddhist literature and scholars

**Canonical status varies by school.** Texts were orally transmitted, edited and written down over time; the whole Tripitaka was not physically composed on leaves at the Buddha's death. **Tipitaka** is Pali; **Tripitaka** is Sanskrit: “three baskets”. The **Pali canon belongs to Theravada**; other Buddhist canons differ.

| Basket | Contents and subdivisions |
|---|---|
| **Sutta Pitaka** | Discourses; five **Nikayas**: **Digha**—long; **Majjhima**—middle-length; **Samyutta**—connected; **Anguttara**—numerical; **Khuddaka**—smaller collection |
| **Vinaya Pitaka** | Monastic discipline, procedures and narratives; **Suttavibhanga** includes rules for monks/nuns; **Khandhaka → Mahavagga + Cullavagga**; **Parivara** supplementary analysis; **Patimokkha** rule collection |
| **Abhidhamma Pitaka** | Systematic analysis of mind, phenomena and doctrine; seven books: **Dhammasangani, Vibhanga, Dhatukatha, Puggalapannatti, Kathavatthu, Yamaka, Patthana** |

**Khuddaka recall:** **Jataka**—former births; **Dhammapada**—ethical verses; **Theragatha/Therigatha**—elder monks/nuns; **Buddhavamsa**—Buddha lineage/history; **Niddesa**—exposition; **Patisambhidamagga**—analytical knowledge. Its number of books varies by Theravada recension; **15** is a common count, not a universal total for every Buddhist canon. Dhammapada is **canonical**, not simply a non-canonical treatise.

| Work / writer | Identification |
|---|---|
| **Milindapanha** | Pali dialogue: **Menander/Milinda ↔ Nagasena**; not Nagarjuna. Its canonical standing varies regionally |
| **Nettipakarana** | Interpretative guide to Buddhist teaching; **Buddhist**, not Jaina |
| **Visuddhimagga** | **Buddhaghosa**, **fifth century CE**; Path of Purification, Theravada synthesis |
| **Nidanakatha** | Connected sacred life narrative associated with the Jataka commentary |
| **Dipavamsa / Mahavamsa** | Pali Sri Lankan chronicles: traditions about councils, Ashoka, missions and royal history; historical and legendary layers |
| **Mahavastu** | Sacred biography associated with **Lokottaravadins**, in Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit; not a Jaina work |
| **Buddhacharita—Ashvaghosha** | Sanskrit life of Buddha; **c.first–second centuries CE**, traditionally linked with Kanishka; poet/dramatist |
| **Nagarjuna** | **c.second–third centuries CE**, **Madhyamaka** and emptiness; Satavahana patronage traditions exist, but identification of one exact contemporary king is debated |
| **Aryadeva** | Buddhist Madhyamaka scholar; **not Jaina** |
| **Asanga and Vasubandhu** | **c.fourth–fifth centuries CE**, brothers linked with **Yogachara/Vijnanavada**; Maitreyanatha associated with its development. Vasubandhu's **Abhidharmakosha** is a major Abhidharma work |
| **Dignaga / Dinnaga** | **c.fifth–sixth centuries CE**, Buddhist logic and epistemology |
| **Dharmakirti** | **Seventh century CE**, Buddhist logic/epistemology; do not confuse with Buddhist translators of similar names |

## 6. Councils and schools: do not collapse the timeline

Dates below are **traditional/conventional**; the earliest councils are known through later school histories. Their dating depends partly on the chronology adopted for the Buddha.

| Council tradition | Place / patron / leading figure | Recall and qualification |
|---|---|---|
| **First**, shortly after Buddha's passing; conventionally **c.483 BCE** | **Rajagriha; Ajatashatru; Mahakassapa** | **Upali—Vinaya; Ananda—discourses**. Do not assert that the entire later Abhidhamma was already fixed in its final form |
| **Second**, about a century later; conventionally **c.383 BCE** | **Vaishali; Kalashoka; Sabbakami** | Discipline disputes, traditionally “ten points”. Early division into **Sthaviras and Mahasanghikas** is associated with this general period, but accounts of the schism differ |
| **Third**, **c.250 BCE** | **Pataliputra; Ashoka; Moggaliputta Tissa** | Theravada account of doctrinal purification, **Kathavatthu** tradition and missions; not the single moment at which every Buddhist school accepted one canon |
| **Fourth—Sri Lankan tradition**, **first century BCE** | **Alu Vihara/Aloka Lena; Vattagamani Abhaya** | Writing down the Pali canon is associated with this council tradition |
| **Fourth—Sarvastivada tradition**, **Kanishka's reign, broadly second century CE** | **Kashmir/Kundalavana; Vasumitra**; Ashvaghosha/Parshva also occur in accounts | Scholastic commentaries/**Mahavibhasha** tradition. **Not a one-day division into Mahayana and “Hinayana”**; the chapter's first-century date reflects older Kushan chronologies |

The modern **c.127 CE** accession framework places Kanishka in the second century. [British Museum coin chronology](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_IOC-298).

### Early schools and later vehicles

- **Sthavira** = elder; **Thera** is the Pali cognate. **Theravada** is a particular surviving lineage, **not identical to every Sthavira school or all “Hinayana”**. “Hinayana” is a polemical Mahayana label, not an appropriate neutral name for all early Buddhism.
- **Mahasanghika:** early school group; **Lokottaravada** an offshoot. Its exalted conception of Buddha helped the background of later Mahayana, but **Mahasanghika ≠ Mahayana**.
- **Sarvastivada:** dharmas exist across past, present and future in its distinctive analysis; an early Buddhist school, not simply a subdivision of present-day Theravada.
- **Sautrantika:** stresses sutra authority over certain Abhidharma positions; **Sammitiya:** early Buddhist school. **Both are Buddhist, not Jaina**.

| Tradition | Distinctive focus | Texts / practice / geography |
|---|---|---|
| **Theravada: “Teaching of the Elders”** | Arhat ideal; ethical discipline, meditation, wisdom; the bodhisatta career is also recognised | Pali canon; Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand and much of mainland Southeast Asia. **Images and devotional worship are practised**—“no idols” is false |
| **Mahayana: “Great Vehicle”** | **Bodhisattva ideal**, compassion and Buddhahood for all; many Buddhas/bodhisattvas; **Madhyamaka and Yogachara** major philosophical currents | Sanskrit and other textual traditions, later Chinese/Tibetan translations; East Asia and Himalayan regions. **Amitabha/Pure Land** devotion offers one important path, not the whole of Mahayana |
| **Vajrayana: “Diamond/Thunderbolt Vehicle”** | Esoteric methods within a Mahayana framework; guru/**lama**, initiation, mantra, **mandala** and visualisation | Developed across the **later first millennium CE**, prominent in eastern India, especially Bihar–Bengal; Tibetan transmissions in **eighth century** and renewed links in **eleventh century**, including Vikramashila. Tibet, Bhutan, Nepal, Mongolia and Himalayan areas |

**Two truths**—conventional and ultimate—are important to Mahayana philosophy and Vajrayana, not exclusive to the latter. **Sandhya-bhasha** = symbolic “twilight language”. Vajrayana is not reducible to acquiring magical power; **Tara** has peaceful and wrathful forms, not only fierce ones. **Ashoka predates these later vehicle labels**; Kanishka's patronage does not establish one uniform sect for his whole empire. Harsha later supported Buddhist institutions alongside other traditions.

### Bodhisattvas and paramitas

**Bodhisattva:** one committed to awakening for the benefit of beings; Mahayana stresses compassionate assistance to all. **Paramitas = perfections on this path**: six commonly listed are **generosity, ethical conduct, patience, energy, meditation and wisdom**; an expanded ten adds skilful means, aspiration/resolution, power and knowledge. These lists vary between traditions.

| Bodhisattva | Recognition clue |
|---|---|
| **Manjushri** | **Wisdom**; flaming sword cuts ignorance; Prajnaparamita book; sometimes lion |
| **Avalokiteshvara / Padmapani / Lokeshvara** | **Compassion**; lotus, often Amitabha in headdress; Padmapani painting at **Ajanta** |
| **Vajrapani** | **Power/energy**; vajra, often forceful/wrathful form |
| **Maitreya** | **Future Buddha**, presently a bodhisattva; waiting in Tushita; representations vary—do not define him by an “orange bush” |
| **Kshitigarbha** | Vow to aid beings, especially in hell realms; monk's robe, staff and wish-fulfilling jewel |
| **Akashagarbha** | “Space treasury”; wisdom/merit, associated with purification; iconography varies |
| **Samantabhadra** | Excellent conduct and great vows; often **six-tusked elephant**; with Shakyamuni and Manjushri in a familiar triad |
| **Sarvanivaranavishkambhin** | Removes hindrances/obscurations; lotus and varied attributes in different traditions |

**Three family protectors = Manjushri—wisdom; Avalokiteshvara—compassion; Vajrapani—power.** Colours/attributes can differ by regional iconographic system; do not memorise one painting as a universal rule. [Metropolitan Museum exhibition](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/exhibitions/bodhisattvas-wisdom-compassion-power).

## 7. Stupas, caves and monastic vocabulary

**Stupa = mound/heap**, developed from funerary/relic traditions; **not exclusively Buddhist in origin**. In Buddhism it can hold relics, commemorate sacred events or serve as a votive object. Ashokan patronage helped make it a major sacred monument.

## 8. Spread, contribution and decline of Buddhism

### Why it spread

**Accessible ethics + teaching in vernacular linguistic settings + organised Sangha + merchants/lay donors + rulers + trade/mission networks.** The language of the preserved Pali canon should not be equated mechanically with the Buddha's exact spoken dialect. Sanskrit later became an important Buddhist language, alongside many others.

- Royal/elite supporters included rulers of **Magadha and Kosala**, republican groups, **Ashoka**, later **Kushans**, **Harsha** and regional patrons. Monks/nuns and small donors also mattered; spread was not solely a royal programme.
- Sri Lankan tradition credits **Mahinda/Mahendra** and **Sanghamitta/Sanghamitra** with major missions under Ashoka; **Sona and Uttara—Suvarnabhumi**, **Mahadeva—Mahishamandala**, **Maharakkhita—Yona country**. These are **chronicle traditions**, and exact modern boundaries of mission destinations are debated. The chapter confuses names in the Himalayan mission: **Majjhima** is associated with the Himalayan region; **Dhammarakkhita** with Aparantaka.
- Monks, merchants and translators linked India, Sri Lanka, Central Asia, Southeast Asia and China. **Kumarajiva reached Chang'an in 401 CE**; **Faxian/Fa-Hien** and **Xuanzang/Hiuen Tsang** later travelled to India. Traditions of an initial first-century Chinese invitation name **Kashyapa Matanga and Dharmaratna**, not a secure itinerary for every similarly named monk.
- The source's **“Dharmadeva went to China in 1001 CE”** is not used as a firm date: identification/chronology needs verification.
- Contributions: ethical reflection, compassion, monastic education, manuscript/translation networks, stupas/caves/sculpture and donor inscriptions. Indian Buddhist World Heritage examples include **Sanchi, Ajanta, Mahabodhi at Bodh Gaya and Nalanda**; their histories are distinct, not all Ashokan constructions.

### Why it declined in many Indian regions

**A long, uneven process—not disappearance everywhere from one year.** Patronage and trade patterns changed; some large endowed monasteries became vulnerable; devotional Brahmanical traditions competed, absorbed Buddhist figures and offered alternative networks; institutional concentration and regional politics mattered. Warfare and attacks on monasteries, especially in the **late twelfth–early thirteenth centuries**, intensified losses in parts of northern/eastern India.

Claims that Sanskrit, image worship, “corrupt monks” or any single religion alone caused decline are insufficient. Buddhism remained vigorous under some later rulers; **Gupta rulers were not uniformly anti-Buddhist**. Accounts of persecution by **Pushyamitra**, **Mihirakula** and **Shashanka** have different evidential strengths and should not be fused into one continuous empire-wide policy. Buddhist traditions **survived in Himalayan and other communities and later revived**; “completely extinct in India” is false.

## 9. Mahavira and the Jaina tradition

**Jina = conqueror of passions; Tirthankara = ford-maker who teaches a path across rebirth.** Jainism recognises **24 Tirthankaras in each half-cycle**. Mahavira is the **24th**, not the first founder of an entirely new tradition. Historicity is firmer for the last teachers than for the whole sacred lineage.

| Tirthankara | Recall |
|---|---|
| **1. Rishabha / Adinatha** | **Bull** emblem; traditional first Tirthankara. Similar names in Vedic/Puranic literature do not by themselves prove an exact historical identification |
| **19. Malli / Mallinatha** | Female Tirthankara in Shvetambara tradition; male in Digambara tradition |
| **22. Neminatha / Arishtanemi** | Saurashtra/Girnar association |
| **23. Parshvanatha** | Varanasi/Benaras; serpent emblem; fourfold restraint tradition |
| **24. Vardhamana Mahavira** | **Lion** emblem; Jitendriya/Arihant; systematised the five great vows |

### Mahavira: traditional life sequence

- **599–527 BCE** is the traditional chronology used here; other historical chronologies differ. Broadly a contemporary of Buddha; do not infer exact synchronisms from every traditional date.
- Born at **Kundagrama near Vaishali**, in the **Jnatrika/Naya** Kshatriya clan; father **Siddhartha**, mother **Trishala**. Linked by tradition to the Lichchhavis and **Chetaka**, with varying kinship accounts. Vaishali was a centre of the **Vajji confederacy**; do not call it simply the capital of monarchical Videha.
- **Shvetambara tradition:** wife **Yashoda**, daughter **Priyadarshana**; renunciation at **30** after a householder's life. **Digambara tradition:** unmarried; other details also differ.
- After **12 years** of austerity, attained **kevalajnana**, traditionally aged **42**, beside **Rijupalika/Rijubalika**, near **Jrimbhikagrama**, associated with a householder **Samaga**.
- Final liberation, traditionally aged **72**, at **Pavapuri/Pava near present-day Patna**. Keep this separate from the Buddhist sacred geography; similarly named places create traps.
- **Ganadharas:** chief disciples/transmitters of the teaching. Do not assume every disciple was already omniscient throughout his entire discipleship.

## 10. Jaina doctrines: soul, karma and many-sided truth

**Jiva = soul/conscious living substance.** Jainism accepts enduring individual souls and no creator of the universe. The world operates through its own eternal constituents/order; **universal soul is not its creator**. Gods are recognised as beings within samsara, below the spiritually liberated Jinas in the relevant hierarchy.

| Principle | Meaning and exam distinction |
|---|---|
| **Anekantavada** | Reality has multiple aspects; one finite standpoint does not exhaust it. Blind-men-and-elephant illustration. **Not “every contradictory claim is equally correct”** |
| **Syadavada** | Conditional predication: **syat = in a certain respect / under stated conditions**; not mere indecision or random “maybe” |
| **Nayavada** | Analysis from partial standpoints/**nayas**, without mistaking one for the whole truth |
| **Kevalajnana** | Perfect/complete knowledge; finite observers know only aspects |
| **Karmic bondage** | Karmic matter binds the soul through activity/passions; **meritorious as well as demeritorious karma can bind**, not only “bad deeds” |
| **Asrava → bandha** | Influx → bondage of karma |
| **Samvara → nirjara → moksha** | Stop new influx → shed accumulated karma → liberation |

<!-- flow:Jaina liberation|Jiva becomes bound by karmic matter|Right faith, knowledge and conduct restrain passions|Samvara stops new karmic influx|Nirjara sheds accumulated karma|Moksha: liberated soul / siddha -->

### Three jewels / Triratna

**Samyag-darshana = right faith/perception → samyag-jnana = right knowledge → samyag-charitra = right conduct.** These are **not** the Buddhist Buddha–Dhamma–Sangha triad.

### Five great vows

| Vow | Recall |
|---|---|
| **Ahimsa** | Non-injury in **thought, speech and action**; neither commit, commission nor approve harm. Care extends to minute life; combines intention, vigilance and restraint rather than dismissing all unintended physical harm |
| **Satya** | Truthful, responsible speech; avoid speech causing needless injury |
| **Asteya / achaurya** | Do not take what is not given, including by unfair means; restraint in alms/consumption |
| **Brahmacharya** | Complete celibacy for ascetics; chastity/restraint for householders. Traditionally made a separate fifth vow by Mahavira alongside Parshva's four |
| **Aparigraha** | Non-possession/non-attachment; limit desires and accumulation |

**Mahavratas:** full ascetic vows. **Anuvratas:** limited householders' vows, not a Buddhist sect. **Sallekhana:** religious discipline of gradual fasting and detachment approaching death under prescribed conditions; not a recommendation that every follower must fast to death.

### Life and cosmology

- Time cycles between ascending **utsarpini** and descending **avasarpini**, each with **six aras/stages**; the present traditional half-cycle is descending. No supported historical year is assigned to mythic cycles.
- Living beings include gods, humans, hell beings and animals/plants. **Ekendriya** = one-sensed life; **nigoda** = extremely minute forms of life in Jaina doctrine. Earth-, water-, fire-, air- and plant-bodied living beings explain the exceptionally wide scope of ahimsa. **Not a modern scientific assertion that every physical atom is an individual soul**.
- **Dravya** = substance; **guna** = quality; **paryaya** = mode. Six substances commonly counted: **jiva, pudgala/matter, dharma/motion-medium, adharma/rest-medium, akasha/space and kala/time**. Here **dharma/adharma do not mean merit/sin**.
- Additional terms: **pudgala**—matter/atomic aggregates; **chaitanya**—consciousness; **virya**—energy; **mohaniya**—deluding karma; **gunasthanas**—stages of purification; **avadhijnana**—limited supersensory knowledge, not omniscience; **arhat/arihant**—embodied conqueror with perfect knowledge; **siddha**—fully liberated; **basadi**—Jaina temple/establishment, especially Karnataka; **eva** simply means “indeed”, not a separate substance.
- Strict ahimsa encouraged vegetarianism and avoidance of livelihoods causing injury. **Trade became important, but all Jains were not prohibited from every form of cultivation or confined to commerce in actual history**; Jaina patrons included rulers and diverse communities.

## 11. Jaina literature, councils and spread

### Texts and transmission

**Agama / Siddhanta** = sacred teaching/textual tradition; the early material was orally transmitted, especially in **Ardhamagadhi Prakrit**. Later authors used **other Prakrits, Sanskrit, Apabhramsha, Kannada, Tamil** and more languages. The two principal traditions do not recognise an identical surviving canon.

- **Angas**: traditionally **12**, but the **twelfth, Drishtivada, is lost**; **11 survive in the Shvetambara canon**. **Acharanga/Acaranga** and **Sutrakritanga** are major early texts. Do not claim 12 complete surviving Angas. [Jainpedia—Angas](https://jainpedia.org/article/themes/principles/sacred-writings/svetambara-canon/angas/).
- **Purvas** = ancient/previous teachings; **shruta-kevalin** = master of the scriptural tradition, distinct from the kevalin's direct omniscience. **Angabahya** = texts outside the Angas.
- Conventional Shvetambara categories: **12 Upangas**, **10 Prakirnakas** (miscellanies), **6 Chedasutras** (discipline), **4 Mulasutras** (basic instruction), plus **Nandi** and **Anuyogadvara**; accepted collections differ among subtraditions.
- **Kalpasutra** is an important Shvetambara text with Tirthankara lives and monastic material. **Tattvarthasutra**, associated with **Umasvati/Umasvami**, is a systematic doctrinal work respected across the main traditions, with variant versions.
- **Digambaras** hold the original Angas lost and preserve a different scriptural corpus; this is not absence of literature.
- **Divya-dhvani / samavasarana:** teaching of an omniscient Jina in a universal preaching assembly; Digambara and Shvetambara accounts differ over the nature of the sound/language.

| Later work | Author / period / content |
|---|---|
| **Harivamsha Purana** | **Eighth century CE** Jaina work; Jaina versions of Krishna/Balarama and epic lineages; distinguish similarly named Brahmanical works |
| **Adi Purana** | **Ninth century CE**; Rishabha/Adinatha narrative; Jinasena associated with the Sanskrit work |
| **Trishashtilakshana Mahapurana** | **Jinasena and Gunabhadra, ninth century CE**; lives of illustrious beings and kings, with social/ritual/political material; **Jaina** |
| **Parishishtaparvan** | **Hemachandra, twelfth century CE**; Jaina teachers and political traditions; **Jaina**, unlike Nettipakarana/Avadanashataka |

### Councils and sect formation

| Council tradition | Essential recall |
|---|---|
| **Pataliputra, c.fourth–third centuries BCE** | Associated with **Sthulabhadra** and recovery/arrangement of the Angas; chapter's **310 BCE** is a conventional teaching date, not established exact chronology; rejected as authoritative by Digambara tradition |
| **Vallabhi, fifth–sixth centuries CE** | **Devarddhigani Kshamashramana**; writing/redaction of the Shvetambara canon. Dates vary: **453/466 CE** occur in scholarship; **512 CE** occurs in conventional Indian teaching accounts. Remember place, presiding figure and purpose; **do not force all accounts into one exact year** ([IGNOU teaching chronology](https://egyankosh.ac.in/bitstream/123456789/62860/1/Block-4.pdf)) |

**Bhadrabahu–southern migration** and **Sthulabhadra–Magadha** are associated in later traditions with a twelve-year famine and emerging differences. **Chandragupta Maurya's Jaina renunciation at Shravanabelagola** is a later traditional association. Do not treat a single famine as a fully documented instantaneous split; Digambara/Shvetambara identities crystallised gradually over the early centuries CE.

Spread: Gangetic beginnings → **Karnataka and other southern regions**, **Tamil-Brahmi Jaina associations by the last centuries BCE**, **Kalinga**, and western/central centres in **Gujarat, Rajasthan and Malwa**. **Kharavela of Kalinga**, conventionally **first century BCE**, patronised the Jaina tradition; **he was not a fourth-century BCE king**. Mercantile networks, householders, temples, libraries, literary activity and regional patrons helped continuity. Jainism received less extensive early transregional royal patronage than Buddhism, but “no royal patronage” is false.

## 12. Jaina sects and subsects

| Aspect | **Digambara—sky-clad** | **Shvetambara—white-clad** |
|---|---|---|
| Ascetic clothing | Full male mendicant ideal includes nudity; female renouncers/**aryikas** wear clothes | Mendicants wear white; nudity not necessary for liberation |
| Women's liberation | Traditional doctrine requires male rebirth for final liberation | Women can attain liberation; **Malli** accepted as female Tirthankara |
| Omniscient's food | Kevalin no longer needs ordinary food | Kevalin may still take food |
| Mahavira's life | Traditionally unmarried; rejects embryo-transfer narrative | Yashoda/Priyadarshana tradition; narrative of embryo transfer from **Devananda to Trishala** by divine agency |
| Scripture | Original Angas lost; own authoritative texts | Surviving Agamas accepted, with subsect variations |
| Images | Generally nude, unadorned, contemplative | Commonly clothed/ornamented, sometimes inset eyes; image worship is not followed by every subsect |
| Mendicant practice | Traditionally food in cupped palms, standing, once daily; feather broom/**picchi** and water-pot are permitted implements, not worldly ownership | Alms collection and permitted equipment/clothing under its own discipline; not unlimited possessions |

**Nudity applies to ascetic ideals, not every Digambara layperson.** Shvetambara female mendicants are **sadhvis**, Digambara female renouncers **aryikas**; terminology can overlap more broadly.

### Subsects: two different Terapanthas

| Group | Recognition and practices |
|---|---|
| **Digambara Bisapantha** | Image worship, bhattaraka religious heads; offerings may include flowers/fruits, lamps/arati; important in southern/western communities |
| **Digambara Terapantha** | Reform against bhattaraka dominance; retains **image worship**; avoids living/sachitta offerings, uses dry offerings; traditionally no arati/prasada as in Bisapantha; prominent in north/central India |
| **Digambara Taranapantha / Samaiyapantha** | Sacred books/spiritual practice emphasised; **non-image worship**; Bundelkhand–Malwa/Khandesh associations |
| **Digambara Gumanapantha** | Austere conduct; image viewing without offerings; avoids temple lamps as injury; Rajasthan associations |
| **Digambara Totapantha** | Small tradition between Bisa/Terapantha practices; pockets of central India |
| **Shvetambara Murtipujaka / Deravasi / Mandiramargi** | Images, decorated temples, offerings; temple/upashraya associations; strong in Gujarat |
| **Shvetambara Sthanakvasi** | **Non-image worship**; **sthanakas/prayer halls**; also called Dhundhiya/Sadhumargi; Gujarat, Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana associations |
| **Shvetambara Terapantha** | **Non-image worship**; disciplined organisation under **one Acharya**, simplicity and meditation; Rajasthan associations |

**Digambara Terapantha worships images; Shvetambara Terapantha does not. They are different traditions sharing a name.** Regional associations are broad, not exclusive modern distribution maps.

## 13. Buddhism, Jainism and Ajivikas compared

| Issue | Buddhism | Jainism |
|---|---|---|
| Vedas / birth privilege | Vedas not binding; spiritual worth tied to conduct | Vedas not binding; conduct/knowledge, not Brahmana birth alone |
| Self | **No permanent atman** | **Individual enduring jivas** |
| Karma | Intention-driven moral causation | Karmic matter binds soul; stop influx and shed it |
| Path | **Middle Way**, Eightfold Path | Stricter ascetic discipline, Triratna and vows |
| Ahimsa | Important; varied food rules | Particularly exacting; strict vegetarianism and minute-life care |
| Creator | Not required for liberation | No creator; universe eternal/self-regulated |
| Community | Monks, nuns and lay supporters | Monks, nuns and lay supporters; sect differences about final liberation |
| Common ground | **Renunciation, self-effort, karma/rebirth, criticism of sacrifice and priestly monopoly** | These similarities do **not** mean identical metaphysics or equal avoidance of severe penance |

### Ajivikas

- **Makkhali Gosala**, traditionally once a companion of Mahavira; a distinct shramana movement. Traditions speak of many competing teachings—**“62”** is a literary enumeration, not a verified census.
- **Niyati = destiny/determinism**: liberation unfolds through an ordained sequence of rebirths; human effort cannot change that sequence in the account attributed to them. Yet they practised **severe austerities**.
- Their own writings are largely lost; much doctrine survives through **rival Buddhist/Jaina accounts**, requiring caution about hostile characterisations.
- Mauryan patronage, especially **third century BCE** cave dedications at **Barabar/Nagarjuni**, provides important evidence; inscriptions of **Ashoka and Dasharatha** matter more than polemical anecdotes.
- Later southern references continue into medieval times, sometimes cited as late as the **fourteenth century CE**; identification/continuity is debated. Do not mistake Ajivikas for **Ajita Kesakambalin's materialism**.
