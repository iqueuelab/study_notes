Chronology

Events, people and periods in chronological order

Ranges appear at their start date. Approximate dates remain labelled. Year-only entries do not establish an exact order within that year.

Before 10,000 BCE · Early stone-tool traditions

c.500,000–10,000 BCE · teaching range
Cultural periodTurning point

Palaeolithic traditions

Broad chapter convention; newer site chronologies extend beyond this band. Hunting–gathering, flaked tools, caves and open sites; regional transitions overlap.

C1
c.40,000–10,000 BCE
Cultural period

Upper Palaeolithic reference horizon

Blades, burins, bone tools, ornaments and painting; not the first appearance of Homo sapiens.

c.10,000–6000 BCE · teaching range
Cultural periodTurning point

Mesolithic traditions

Microlithic tools, hunting/fishing/gathering, rock art and changing settlement patterns; later survival and herding vary by region.

C1
c.9700 BCE · about 11,700 years ago
EnvironmentTurning point

Beginning of the Holocene

Approximate BCE placement of the geological boundary. Later forager and early farming cultures overlap this epoch; cultural boundaries are regional.

C1

SettlementTurning point

Early farming at Mehrgarh

Conventional textbook placement before urban Harappa. A 2025 tooth-enamel study instead places Period I about 5250–4650 cal BCE; retain the distinction rather than two supposedly separate settlements.

C1
c.6000–1000 BCE · teaching range
Cultural periodTurning point

Regional Neolithic cultures

Farming/herding, ground tools, durable villages and pottery in many phases. Conventional earlier Mehrgarh dates lie outside this simplified band.

C1

Early Harappan / Regionalisation

Regional villages and fortified towns, crafts, trade and wheeled transport develop; dates vary by site.

Settlement

Burzahom habitation sequence

Kashmir site spans Neolithic, Megalithic and early historical phases; pit dwellings and human/animal burials are important. This is not one homogeneous 2000-year Neolithic phase.

C1

Chalcolithic regional cultures

Copper and stone, rural production/crafts/exchange. Some phases precede or overlap Harappa; others follow it.

C1

Banas basin; Ahar, Gilund and Balathal; chapter chronology, with broader regional ranges in other studies.

C1

Mature Harappan / Integration

Large cities, shared script/seals, weights and brick proportions; regional diversity persists.

Broad chapter range, or later; Daojali Hading and Garo hills illustrate regional diversity.

C1

Kayatha reference band

Chhoti Kali Sindh/Chambal drainage, Madhya Pradesh; dates depend on phase and excavation.

Indo-Aryan movements and interactions

Broad second-millennium BCE context for movements through Central Asia and interaction in South Asia. Linguistic, archaeological and genetic evidence have different scopes; neither pottery nor DNA directly identifies an individual’s language.

Late Harappan / Localisation

Urban contraction, regional settlement/craft changes and agricultural adaptation; not complete disappearance of population or culture.

Prabhas Patan/Somnath, Gujarat; chapter reference chronology.

C1
c.1700 BCE–1400 BCE
Cultural period

Malwa reference band

Navdatoli on the Narmada; regional Chalcolithic culture.

C1
c.1500–1000 BCE
Cultural period

Early / Rig Vedic phase

Northwestern geographical emphasis; pastoralism alongside cultivation; jana, chiefs and assemblies; older Rigvedic family books. Boundaries are conventional and approximate.

C3
c.1500 BCE–500 BCE
Cultural period

Vedic textual composition traditions

Broad conventional range for Vedic oral/textual traditions; early Rigvedic and later Vedic layers overlap regional cultural changes. Oral composition, textual layering and surviving manuscript dates are distinct.

C1C3
c.1400 BCE–700 BCE
Cultural period

Jorwe reference band

Maharashtra; Jorwe, Inamgaon, Daimabad and Chandoli. Sites occupy different tributaries and phases.

C1
c.1000–500 BCE · conventional chapter range
Cultural period

Later Vedic phase

Kuru–Panchala and eastward expansion; agriculture, stronger territorial kingship, elaborate sacrifice and philosophical inquiry. Some periodisations end this phase around 600 BCE.

C3
c.600–300 BCE
Textual tradition

Dharmasutra traditions

Broad chapter composition band for normative texts on conduct and social duties; dates differ by text. Prescriptions must not be equated with universal practice.

C3
c.600–300 BCE · broad regional horizon
Cultural period

Second urbanisation and territorial states

Ganga-basin towns, surplus agriculture, craft specialisation, trade, early coinage and larger territorial states; regional chronologies vary. Agrarian surplus, crafts, coins, trade and new social groups formed the setting for Buddhist, Jaina and other shramana movements. Economic change is one factor, not a complete explanation of every doctrine.

C4C5
c.600 BCE · broad teaching horizon
Textual tradition

Upanishadic inquiry in the later Vedic setting

Around this broad horizon, traditions associated with Panchala and Videha develop discussions of self, knowledge, action and liberation. This does not date every Upanishad to one year; early texts and later layers span a wider period.

C3
599–527 BCE · traditional, disputed chronology
Religious biography

Mahavira: traditional life chronology

Traditional dates used in the chapter; historical chronologies vary. Twenty-fourth Tirthankara; Kundagrama near Vaishali, Jnatrika clan; final liberation associated with Pavapuri. Ages and family details belong to traditions with sect differences.

C5
563–483 BCE · conventional, disputed chronology
Religious biography

Gautama Buddha: conventional life chronology

One traditional chronology, not an uncontested historical dating. Lumbini birth; renunciation, enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, first sermon at Sarnath, final passing at Kushinara. Some historical chronologies place his life later.

C5
c.559–530 BCE · conventional reign
Reign

Cyrus and the eastern Achaemenid frontier

Expansion towards northwestern South Asia belongs to the sixth-century BCE background; exact local Indian conquest claims remain uncertain.

C4
c.544–492 BCE · conventional chronology
Reign

Bimbisara consolidates Magadha

Haryanka ruler; Rajagriha capital, conquest of Anga, matrimonial alliances and diplomacy. Founder status and precise regnal dates are not certain.

C4
c.518 BCE · conventional approximation
Political change

Darius I extends authority in the Indus region

Persian incorporation/consolidation in parts of the northwest; Gandara and Hindush are separate imperial geographical names, not all India.

C4
Fifth century BCE · broad period
Military history

Indian and Gandarian troops in Persian service

Contingents appear in accounts of Xerxes' campaigns against Greece; does not imply Persian conquest of the whole subcontinent.

C4
c.492–460 BCE · conventional chronology
Reign

Ajatashatru expands Magadha

Conflict over Kashi and defeat of Vajji; war-machine traditions and Vassakara diplomacy. Buddhist tradition places Buddha's death and First Council in his reign, but absolute chronologies differ.

C4
c.483 BCE · traditional date; chronology disputed
Council

First Buddhist council: Rajagriha tradition

Traditionally shortly after Buddha’s passing, under Ajatashatru, led by Mahakassapa; Upali recites discipline and Ananda discourses. Absolute date follows one Buddha chronology; do not imply the entire later Abhidhamma was already fixed.

C5
c.460–444 BCE · conventional chronology
Reign

Udayin and the capital at Pataliputra

Associated with transferring the Magadhan capital to Pataliputra at strategic ancient river junctions; not Udayana of Vatsa.

C4
c.412–345 BCE · conventional chronology
Dynasty

Shishunaga dynasty

Shishunaga annexes Avanti and is associated with Vaishali; Kalashoka with Pataliputra and the Second Buddhist Council at Vaishali. Exact chronology varies.

C4
c.fourth–third centuries BCE · later traditional association
Religious history

Bhadrabahu and southward migration: traditional setting

Later traditions connect a twelve-year Magadhan famine, Bhadrabahu’s movement south and Chandragupta Maurya’s Jaina renunciation at Shravanabelagola. Not an independently established exact itinerary or a single instantaneous sectarian split.

C5
c.fourth–third centuries BCE · traditional setting
Council

Jaina Pataliputra council and transmission traditions

Associated with Sthulabhadra and recovering/arranging Angas. Source’s 310 BCE is a teaching convention, not an established exact date. Related famine/migration accounts are later traditions; Digambara tradition does not accept this recension as authoritative.

C5
c.400 BCE–400 CE
Textual tradition

Mahabharata: layered composition

Broad conventional composition/redaction band, with oral formation and later layers. The epic’s story setting is not the date of its surviving textual compilation.

C3
c.400 BCE–300 CE
Textual tradition

Ramayana: layered composition

Broad chapter composition band; the received text contains different layers. Traditional story chronology is distinct from historical composition dates.

C3
c.383 BCE · conventional date; chronology disputed
Council

Second Buddhist council: Vaishali tradition

Traditionally about a century after Buddha, associated with Kalashoka and Sabbakami; discipline disputes. Accounts of the early Sthavira–Mahasanghika division vary.

C5
356–323 BCE
Life

Life of Alexander III of Macedon

Son of Philip II; died at Babylon in 323 BCE, not in his Punjab campaign year 326 BCE.

C4
c.345–321 BCE · conventional chronology
Dynasty

Nanda expansion

Mahapadma Nanda associated with wider conquest, including Kalinga, taxation and irrigation; Dhana Nanda is the last major ruler. Origin and army-size traditions require caution.

C4
327–325 BCE
Military campaign

Alexander campaigns in northwestern South Asia

Campaign through the northwest and Punjab, return down the Indus and land/sea withdrawal. Resistance extended beyond Porus; no Magadha conquest.

C4
326 BCE · no exact within-year sequence implied
Military campaign

Army refuses advance beyond the Hyphasis / Beas

Beas becomes the eastern limit; no march to the Ganga or battle against the Nandas.

C4
326 BCE
Battle

Battle of the Hydaspes / Jhelum

Alexander defeats Porus and restores him as a subordinate ruler; Porus was not Ambhi of Taxila.

C4
323 BCE
Political change

Alexander dies at Babylon

His death is followed by successor struggles; Seleucus was not immediately appointed ruler of all northwestern India during the Indian retreat.

C4
c.321 BCE · approximate
Political change

Chandragupta establishes Mauryan rule

Overthrow of the Nandas, traditionally with Kautilya's assistance; slightly different accession dates occur in scholarship.

C4
c.third century BCE–third century CE · broad building range
Architecture

Amaravati stupa develops through multiple phases

Dhanyakataka/Amaravati in Andhra Pradesh was an important Buddhist centre with Mahasanghika associations. Stupa construction and alteration extended over centuries; not one uniformly dated building.

C5
Third century BCE · background period
Cultural exchange

Ashokan inscriptions and artistic connections

Northwestern Kharoshthi inscriptions and possible Persian connections in Mauryan royal display; bell-shaped capitals belong to pillars, not rock edicts.

C4
third century BCE · chronicle traditions
Religious history

Buddhist mission traditions under Ashoka

Sri Lankan chronicles associate Mahinda and Sanghamitta with Sri Lanka; Sona/Uttara with Suvarnabhumi, Mahadeva with Mahishamandala, Majjhima with Himalayan region, Dhammarakkhita with Aparantaka and Maharakkhita with Yona country. Exact destination boundaries are debated.

C5
third century BCE
Institutional history

Mauryan cave patronage for Ajivikas

Third-century BCE inscriptions at Barabar and Nagarjuni, associated with Ashoka and Dasharatha, provide important evidence for Ajivika patronage beyond rival textual accounts.

C5
third century BCE · Ashokan nucleus
Architecture

Sanchi: Ashokan brick nucleus

The Great Stupa began with an Ashokan-period brick nucleus; later enlargements, stone casing and gateways belong to further building phases.

C5
c.300 BCE–300 CE
Cultural period

Sangam literary reference period

Broad conventional span; Silappadikaram and Manimekalai are later/post-Sangam epics, not evidence for a single composition date.

C1
c.250 BCE · conventional chronology
Council

Third Buddhist council: Pataliputra tradition

Theravada accounts associate Ashoka and Moggaliputta Tissa with doctrinal purification, Kathavatthu and missions. Not all school histories narrate the same council.

C5
second century BCE · principal surviving sculpture
Architecture

Bharhut: railings, sculpture and donors

Famous surviving railings and narrative sculpture are largely second-century BCE. Inscriptions record lay, monastic and female donors; earlier Ashokan foundation is conventionally attributed, not a uniform date for every element.

C5
c.200 BCE–900 CE
Textual tradition

Dharmashastra / Smriti development

Broad chapter band for a diverse normative literary tradition; not the date of one single text or a universally enforced legal code.

C3
c.second–first centuries BCE · broad reference period
Religious history

Jaina associations in the Tamil south

The last centuries BCE form an early southern reference horizon for Jaina communities and Tamil-Brahmi associations; regional transmission and dating are not one single event.

C5
first century BCE · conventional dating
Patronage

Kharavela and Jaina patronage in Kalinga

Conventionally dated to the first century BCE; important Jaina royal patron. Do not place Kharavela in the fourth century BCE.

C5
first century BCE · traditional setting
Council

Pali canon writing: Sri Lankan fourth-council tradition

Alu Vihara/Aloka Lena under Vattagamani Abhaya is associated with writing down the Pali canon. Separate from the later Kashmir council in Sarvastivada tradition.

C5

1–1499 CE · Historical sources referenced

c.first–second centuries CE
Literature

Ashvaghosha and the Buddhacharita

Sanskrit sacred life of Buddha; poet and dramatist traditionally linked with Kanishka. Broad first–second-century CE association.

C5
early centuries CE · gradual formation
Religious history

Digambara and Shvetambara identities crystallise

Differences in clothing, scripture, religious biography and women’s final liberation developed over centuries. Do not date a single fully formed split to one famine or one exact year.

C5
first century CE · traditional mission account
Religious history

Early Chinese invitation tradition

Traditions associate Kashyapa Matanga and Dharmaratna with an early Chinese invitation and translation activity. This is a traditional account, not a verified itinerary for every similarly named monk.

C5
1st century CE
Historical source

Periplus of the Erythraean Sea

Anonymous Greek-language account of navigation, ports and commodities; useful for Indian Ocean trade.

C1
second century CE · broad Kanishka-period setting
Council

Kashmir council under Kanishka

Sarvastivada fourth-council tradition associated with Kashmir/Kundalavana, Vasumitra and scholastic commentaries. Not the instantaneous origin of Mahayana or a simple Mahayana/Hinayana division.

C5
c.second–third centuries CE
Philosophy

Nagarjuna and Madhyamaka

Broad second–third-century CE placement. Madhyamaka and emptiness; Satavahana patronage traditions exist but identification of one exact contemporary king is debated.

C5
2nd century CE
Historical source

Ptolemy’s Geography

Greek geographical account supplying evidence on places, ports and trade.

C1
c.127 CE · accession framework
Political chronology

Kanishka: modern accession framework

The c.127 CE accession framework places Kanishka in the second century CE, explaining why older first-century datings of his council require qualification.

C5
c.fourth–fifth centuries CE
Philosophy

Asanga and Vasubandhu

Brothers associated with Yogachara/Vijnanavada development; Vasubandhu’s Abhidharmakosha is a major Abhidharma work. Broad scholarly chronology, not exact life dates.

C5
c.4th–6th centuries CE · major formation, not final closure
Textual tradition

Major Puranic textual formation

The chapter places important Puranic textual formation in the fourth–sixth centuries CE. These are layered traditions with earlier material and continuing later redaction, not uniformly completed books.

C3
Early 5th century CE
Historical source

Faxian’s Indian journey

Chinese Buddhist pilgrim; account illuminates Gupta-era society. The chapter’s 500 CE should not be treated as an exact visit date. Chinese pilgrim collected Buddhist texts and observed religious institutions; one of the translation/travel networks linking India and East Asia.

C1C5
fifth century CE
Literature

Buddhaghosa and the Visuddhimagga

Fifth-century Pali scholar associated with the Path of Purification, a major Theravada synthesis.

C5
c.fifth–sixth centuries CE · broad phase
Architecture

Dhamekh: major surviving structural phase

The present massive Dhamekh stupa at Sarnath is substantially Gupta-period, broadly fifth–sixth centuries CE, in a sacred landscape with earlier Ashokan remains. Avoid an unsupported exact reconstruction year.

C5
c.fifth–sixth centuries CE
Philosophy

Dignaga develops Buddhist logic

Broad fifth–sixth-century CE setting; Buddhist logician and epistemologist, not Jaina.

C5
401 CE
Translation

Kumarajiva arrives at Chang’an

An important stage in his Chinese Buddhist translation activity; do not describe every later translator of a similar name as the same individual.

C5
fifth–sixth centuries CE · varying traditional dates
Council

Vallabhi council and Shvetambara canonical redaction

Associated with Devarddhigani Kshamashramana. Dates vary: 453/466 CE occur in scholarship; 512 CE in conventional teaching accounts. Surviving Shvetambara collection has eleven Angas, the twelfth lost; Digambaras preserve a different authoritative corpus.

C5
seventh century CE
Philosophy

Dharmakirti and Buddhist epistemology

Seventh-century Buddhist philosopher of logic and knowledge; distinguish him from similarly named translators.

C5
from seventh century CE · growing popularity
Religious art

Miniature votive stupas become especially common

Seventh-century CE reference horizon for popular miniature terracotta offerings in India; votive objects also occur in stone and metal, and the practice is not limited to one century.

C5
later first millennium CE · broad development horizon
Religious history

Vajrayana develops in later first-millennium India

Esoteric methods within a Mahayana framework: guru, initiation, mantra, mandala and visualisation. Especially important in eastern India; not reducible to magic or a single foundation year.

C5
c.629–645 CE · journey
Historical source

Xuanzang’s Indian journey

Chinese Buddhist pilgrim associated with Harsha and Nalanda; 7th century, not an exact 700 CE visit. Chinese pilgrim and scholar; his travel/textual transmission contributed to connections between Indian Buddhist institutions and East Asia.

C1C5

An important early transmission phase; renewed eleventh-century links followed later. Neither date is the first existence of all Vajrayana ideas.

C5

Eighth-century Jaina epic-lineage work associated with Krishna, Balarama and other figures; distinguish similarly titled non-Jaina works.

C5
ninth century CE
Literature

Ninth-century Adi Purana/Rishabha narrative and Trishashtilakshana Mahapurana tradition; sacred biography includes social and political material.

C5

Religious history

Renewed Buddhist transmissions to Tibet

Eleventh-century renewed links with Indian teachers and institutions, including Vikramashila, were important to Tibetan lineages.

C5
twelfth century CE
Literature

Twelfth-century Jaina work on teachers and political traditions; a Jaina text unlike Buddhist Nettipakarana or Avadanashataka.

C5

C1

C5

Religious history

Late medieval references associated with Ajivikas

Southern references are sometimes cited into the fourteenth century; identification and continuity with the ancient movement are debated. Not proof of a fully documented unbroken organisation.
