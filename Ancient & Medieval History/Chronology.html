Chronology

Events, people and periods in chronological order

Ranges appear at their start date. Approximate dates remain labelled. Year-only entries do not establish an exact order within that year.

Cultural periodTurning point

Palaeolithic traditions

Broad chapter convention; newer site chronologies extend beyond this band. Hunting–gathering, flaked tools, caves and open sites; regional transitions overlap.

Upper Palaeolithic reference horizon

Blades, burins, bone tools, ornaments and painting; not the first appearance of Homo sapiens.

Microlithic tools, hunting/fishing/gathering, rock art and changing settlement patterns; later survival and herding vary by region.

Approximate BCE placement of the geological boundary. Later forager and early farming cultures overlap this epoch; cultural boundaries are regional.

Conventional textbook placement before urban Harappa. A 2025 tooth-enamel study instead places Period I about 5250–4650 cal BCE; retain the distinction rather than two supposedly separate settlements.

C1

Regional Neolithic cultures

Farming/herding, ground tools, durable villages and pottery in many phases. Conventional earlier Mehrgarh dates lie outside this simplified band.

Early Harappan / Regionalisation

Regional villages and fortified towns, crafts, trade and wheeled transport develop; dates vary by site.

c.3000–1000 BCE · broad sequence
Settlement

Burzahom habitation sequence

Kashmir site spans Neolithic, Megalithic and early historical phases; pit dwellings and human/animal burials are important. This is not one homogeneous 2000-year Neolithic phase.

C1

Chalcolithic regional cultures

Copper and stone, rural production/crafts/exchange. Some phases precede or overlap Harappa; others follow it.

C1

Banas basin; Ahar, Gilund and Balathal; chapter chronology, with broader regional ranges in other studies.

C1

Mature Harappan / Integration

Large cities, shared script/seals, weights and brick proportions; regional diversity persists.

Broad chapter range, or later; Daojali Hading and Garo hills illustrate regional diversity.

Kayatha reference band

Chhoti Kali Sindh/Chambal drainage, Madhya Pradesh; dates depend on phase and excavation.

Cultural period

Indo-Aryan movements and interactions

Broad second-millennium BCE context for movements through Central Asia and interaction in South Asia. Linguistic, archaeological and genetic evidence have different scopes; neither pottery nor DNA directly identifies an individual’s language.

Late Harappan / Localisation

Urban contraction, regional settlement/craft changes and agricultural adaptation; not complete disappearance of population or culture.

Cultural period

Malwa reference band

Navdatoli on the Narmada; regional Chalcolithic culture.

C1
c.1500–1000 BCE
Cultural period

Early / Rig Vedic phase

Northwestern geographical emphasis; pastoralism alongside cultivation; jana, chiefs and assemblies; older Rigvedic family books. Boundaries are conventional and approximate.

C3
c.1500 BCE–500 BCE
Cultural period

Vedic textual composition traditions

Broad conventional range for Vedic oral/textual traditions; early Rigvedic and later Vedic layers overlap regional cultural changes. Oral composition, textual layering and surviving manuscript dates are distinct.

c.1400 BCE–700 BCE
Cultural period

Jorwe reference band

Maharashtra; Jorwe, Inamgaon, Daimabad and Chandoli. Sites occupy different tributaries and phases.

C1
c.1000–500 BCE · conventional chapter range
Cultural period

Later Vedic phase

Kuru–Panchala and eastward expansion; agriculture, stronger territorial kingship, elaborate sacrifice and philosophical inquiry. Some periodisations end this phase around 600 BCE.

C3
c.600–300 BCE
Textual tradition

Dharmasutra traditions

Broad chapter composition band for normative texts on conduct and social duties; dates differ by text. Prescriptions must not be equated with universal practice.

C3
c.600–300 BCE · broad regional horizon
Cultural period

Second urbanisation and territorial states

Ganga-basin towns, surplus agriculture, craft specialisation, trade, early coinage and larger territorial states; regional chronologies vary.

C4
c.600 BCE · broad teaching horizon
Textual tradition

Upanishadic inquiry in the later Vedic setting

Around this broad horizon, traditions associated with Panchala and Videha develop discussions of self, knowledge, action and liberation. This does not date every Upanishad to one year; early texts and later layers span a wider period.

C3
c.559–530 BCE · conventional reign
Reign

Cyrus and the eastern Achaemenid frontier

Expansion towards northwestern South Asia belongs to the sixth-century BCE background; exact local Indian conquest claims remain uncertain.

C4
c.544–492 BCE · conventional chronology
Reign

Bimbisara consolidates Magadha

Haryanka ruler; Rajagriha capital, conquest of Anga, matrimonial alliances and diplomacy. Founder status and precise regnal dates are not certain.

C4
c.518 BCE · conventional approximation
Political change

Darius I extends authority in the Indus region

Persian incorporation/consolidation in parts of the northwest; Gandara and Hindush are separate imperial geographical names, not all India.

C4
Fifth century BCE · broad period
Military history

Indian and Gandarian troops in Persian service

Contingents appear in accounts of Xerxes' campaigns against Greece; does not imply Persian conquest of the whole subcontinent.

C4
c.492–460 BCE · conventional chronology
Reign

Ajatashatru expands Magadha

Conflict over Kashi and defeat of Vajji; war-machine traditions and Vassakara diplomacy. Buddhist tradition places Buddha's death and First Council in his reign, but absolute chronologies differ.

C4
c.460–444 BCE · conventional chronology
Reign

Udayin and the capital at Pataliputra

Associated with transferring the Magadhan capital to Pataliputra at strategic ancient river junctions; not Udayana of Vatsa.

C4
c.412–345 BCE · conventional chronology
Dynasty

Shishunaga dynasty

Shishunaga annexes Avanti and is associated with Vaishali; Kalashoka with Pataliputra and the Second Buddhist Council at Vaishali. Exact chronology varies.

C4
c.400 BCE–400 CE
Textual tradition

Mahabharata: layered composition

Broad conventional composition/redaction band, with oral formation and later layers. The epic’s story setting is not the date of its surviving textual compilation.

C3
c.400 BCE–300 CE
Textual tradition

Ramayana: layered composition

Broad chapter composition band; the received text contains different layers. Traditional story chronology is distinct from historical composition dates.

C3
356–323 BCE
Life

Life of Alexander III of Macedon

Son of Philip II; died at Babylon in 323 BCE, not in his Punjab campaign year 326 BCE.

C4
c.345–321 BCE · conventional chronology
Dynasty

Nanda expansion

Mahapadma Nanda associated with wider conquest, including Kalinga, taxation and irrigation; Dhana Nanda is the last major ruler. Origin and army-size traditions require caution.

C4
327–325 BCE
Military campaign

Alexander campaigns in northwestern South Asia

Campaign through the northwest and Punjab, return down the Indus and land/sea withdrawal. Resistance extended beyond Porus; no Magadha conquest.

C4
326 BCE · no exact within-year sequence implied
Military campaign

Army refuses advance beyond the Hyphasis / Beas

Beas becomes the eastern limit; no march to the Ganga or battle against the Nandas.

C4
326 BCE
Battle

Battle of the Hydaspes / Jhelum

Alexander defeats Porus and restores him as a subordinate ruler; Porus was not Ambhi of Taxila.

C4
323 BCE
Political change

Alexander dies at Babylon

His death is followed by successor struggles; Seleucus was not immediately appointed ruler of all northwestern India during the Indian retreat.

C4
c.321 BCE · approximate
Political change

Chandragupta establishes Mauryan rule

Overthrow of the Nandas, traditionally with Kautilya's assistance; slightly different accession dates occur in scholarship.

C4
Third century BCE · background period
Cultural exchange

Ashokan inscriptions and artistic connections

Northwestern Kharoshthi inscriptions and possible Persian connections in Mauryan royal display; bell-shaped capitals belong to pillars, not rock edicts.

C4
c.300 BCE–300 CE
Cultural period

Sangam literary reference period

Broad conventional span; Silappadikaram and Manimekalai are later/post-Sangam epics, not evidence for a single composition date.

C1
c.200 BCE–900 CE
Textual tradition

Dharmashastra / Smriti development

Broad chapter band for a diverse normative literary tradition; not the date of one single text or a universally enforced legal code.
