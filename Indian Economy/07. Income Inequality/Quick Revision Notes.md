# Income Inequality

## 1. Meaning and causes

- **Inequality:** uneven distribution of income, wealth, resources, services and opportunities. **Income = flow** over a period; **wealth = stock** of assets net of liabilities at a point in time.
- Income inequality concerns **relative shares**, whereas absolute poverty concerns meeting a minimum standard. Poverty can fall while inequality rises; higher average income does not establish equal distribution.
- Causes: **asset concentration**; unequal access to education, skills, health and finance; urban bias in investment; unequal regional infrastructure; discrimination and gender barriers; informality; capital-intensive production that fails to create sufficient productive jobs.
- High prices can hurt households differently depending on their consumption baskets and income adjustment. Inflation does **not automatically** increase every measure of inequality.

## 2. Measure inequality: Lorenz, Gini and Hoover

| Measure | What to remember | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lorenz curve | X: cumulative population, poorest to richest; Y: cumulative income/wealth share | 45-degree line = equality; a curve farther below it generally indicates more inequality |
| Gini coefficient | Area between equality line and Lorenz curve / total area below equality line | **0 = equality; 1 = limiting complete inequality**; also reported on a 0-100 scale |
| Hoover / Robin Hood / Schutz index | Largest vertical gap between equality line and Lorenz curve | Share of total income that would need redistribution to achieve equal shares |

![Gini uses an area ratio; Hoover uses the largest vertical gap.](Revision%20Diagrams/lorenz.svg)

*Gini uses an area ratio; Hoover uses the largest vertical gap.*

- Gini named after **Corrado Gini (1912)**. With shares on a 0-1 scale: **G = 1 - 2 × area under Lorenz curve**.
- Example: Gini **0.29 → 0.36** means greater measured inequality on a comparable series. It does not tell us whether poor people's absolute incomes fell.
- **Hoover = ½ Σ |income share of group - population share of group|**. It is a **maximum gap**, not the shaded area used for Gini.
- If the Lorenz curve coincides with the equality line, **Gini = Hoover = 0**. If two Lorenz curves cross, a simple dominance ranking is unavailable; one summary statistic may hide different distributions.
- Always compare the **same variable, population, method and period**: consumption Gini, income Gini and wealth Gini are different. Household surveys can miss the very rich; tax and national-account estimates also need assumptions.

## 4. World Inequality Report 2026

- Produced by the **World Inequality Lab**, using the World Inequality Database. Distributional estimates combine multiple sources; they are not simply India's consumption-survey results.

| Group in India | Income share | Wealth share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bottom 50% | **15.0%** | **6.4%** |
| Middle 40% | **27.3%** | **28.6%** |
| Top 10% | **57.7%** | **65.0%** |
| Top 1% | **22.6%** | **40.1%** |

- **Top 1% is included within top 10%**: do not add the two shares. The report's country table uses **2024** for this distribution snapshot. [WIR 2026 country tables](https://wir2026.wid.world/www-site/uploads/2026/01/World_Inequality_Report_2026.pdf)
- Global picture highlighted in the book: top 10% receive **over half of income**, bottom half **under 10%**; top 10% own about **three-quarters of wealth**, bottom half about **2%**; top **0.001%** own more wealth than the bottom half combined.
- Climate inequality: top 10% associated with around **77% of private capital ownership emissions**, bottom half about **3%**. This is **not the same accounting as household consumption emissions**. [WIR emissions accounting](https://wir2026.wid.world/insight/executive-summary/)
- Women receive about **28% of global labour income**; roughly **16% in Middle East/North Africa**, around **40% in Europe/North America**. Rich regions' average incomes are around **13 times** those of sub-Saharan Africa in the cited comparison. International finance can favour low-cost borrowing by rich economies while developing economies face higher financing costs. [WIR 2026](https://wid.world/document/world-inequality-report-2026/)

## 5. Reduce inequality: three linked routes

![Combine taxation, services and productive work.](Revision%20Diagrams/policy.svg)

*Combine taxation, services and productive work.*

- **Progressive taxation:** improve effective taxation of high incomes, capital and wealth; close evasion and avoidance channels. A billionaire wealth levy is a **policy proposal**, not an enacted Indian tax. Consider valuation, enforcement, capital mobility and revenue use.
- **Public services and social protection:** education, healthcare, nutrition, infrastructure and income security improve capabilities and reduce unequal starting conditions. Spending quality and access matter as much as totals.
- **Productive jobs:** help workers move from low-productivity agriculture to manufacturing and services; expand labour-intensive industries, MSMEs, skills, credit, women's employment and regional opportunities. Sectoral movement alone is insufficient if new jobs remain precarious.
- **Mains sequence:** distinguish poverty/income/wealth → identify asset and opportunity gaps → give a dated indicator → combine better jobs, services and fair taxation → evaluate inclusion and implementation.
- **Recall:** Lorenz = distribution curve; Gini = area ratio; Hoover = maximum gap; income ≠ wealth ≠ consumption; top 1% is a subset of top 10%.
