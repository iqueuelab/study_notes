Poverty

Editable notesPDF

1. Meaning, causes and types

  • Poverty: deprivation of resources and capabilities needed for basic food, clothing, shelter, health, education and a dignified life. India's traditional monetary measure focuses on a minimum living standard, not a comfortable lifestyle.
  • Causes interact: colonial legacy; low productivity and underemployment; unequal income/assets; rural–urban and regional divides; weak education/health; exclusion and rigid social structures; infrastructure/resource constraints; demographic pressure without adequate jobs.
  • Relative poverty: deprivation compared with prevailing living standards, often measured against a fraction of median income. Related to inequality, but poverty and inequality are not identical.

2. Monetary poverty: line, incidence, depth and severity

  • Poverty line (z): monetary cost of a specified minimum basket, expressed per person for a defined period. Historical calorie norms helped price baskets; a calorie norm and a monetary poverty line are different units.
  • Lines differ across countries, States and rural/urban areas because baskets, needs and prices differ. ₹47/day in the book is a historical illustration, not a present nationwide threshold.
  • Consumption preferred in Indian surveys: informal incomes fluctuate and are hard to record; consumption better reflects sustained living standards, though recall, valuation and survey comparability still matter.
  • Planning Commission historically published monetary estimates using NSS household consumption data. NSO/MoSPI conducts consumption surveys; NITI Aayog publishes national MPI. Data collection, poverty estimation and beneficiary identification are different tasks.

  • Example: z = ₹400, average poor consumption = ₹330 → I = 17.5%. If H = 20%, P₁ = 3.5%, not 17.5%. Use fractions in multiplication.
  • Theoretical perfectly targeted transfer needed = N × z × P₁ for the same period; actual programmes also have delivery costs, errors and behavioural effects. Squaring gaps does not itself create an entitlement to higher assistance.
  • HCR example: 13.31 crore / 121 crore ×100 = 11%; incidence is not the absolute number of poor.

  • Thirty days: other food, fuel/light, miscellaneous services including non-hospital medical care and rent. Annual group includes clothing/bedding, footwear, education, hospital care and durables. MoSPI survey definitions

  • Shorter recall can raise reported frequent consumption and change poverty estimates. URP > MRP > MMRP poverty is not a mathematical law; also check basket, price adjustments, survey design and poverty line.

4. Global Multidimensional Poverty Index

  • UNDP + OPHI, first introduced 2010, replacing the Human Poverty Index in global reporting. Measures acute overlapping deprivations, complements monetary poverty; not a dollar income line.
  • Alkire–Foster method; 3 dimensions, 10 indicators; each dimension weight 1/3. Household deprivation status is assigned to members, then aggregated to people.
  • Poor: weighted deprivation score at least 1/3. MPI = H × A, where H = population share poor and A = average weighted deprivation among poor; use fractions, not raw percentage numbers.
  • Vulnerable: score 20% to below 1/3; severe multidimensional poverty: at least 1/2.

8. Poverty trap and escape routes

  • Supply side: low income → low saving → weak physical/human-capital investment → low productivity → low income.
  • Demand side: low purchasing power → small market → weak investment incentives → low productivity/income. Savings tend to rise with income (S = f(Y), f′ > 0), other things equal; poor households face immediate needs and shocks.
  • Education/health deficits hinder skills, adoption and opportunity. Coordinated “big push”: health/nutrition, education/skills, infrastructure, productive jobs, financial access, social protection and accountable institutions.
  • Population pressure is mediated by education, reproductive health and opportunities; growth need not automatically exclude people or inevitably trap poor countries.
  • BPL identification ≠ estimating poverty incidence. SECC 2011 deprivation data informs welfare targeting alongside scheme-specific lists; Census 2027, planned digital/caste enumeration, is not automatically a new monetary-poverty survey or guaranteed replacement of every beneficiary list. Census framework

9. Sen: capability approach and poverty index

  • Capability: real freedom/opportunity to achieve valued lives; functioning: actual achievement, e.g. being nourished, educated or mobile. Income is a means; disability, discrimination or poor services affect conversion into wellbeing.
  • Poverty = capability deprivation; empowerment and enabling conditions matter. The book's claim that Sen rejects welfare is too absolute: public health, education and protection can expand capabilities.
  • Critiques: selecting/weighting capabilities, data gaps, implementation and insufficient attention to collective institutions in some applications; people-focused does not mean society is irrelevant.
  • Sen poverty index, 1976: commonly expressed S = H[I + (1 − I)Gₚ]. H: fraction poor; I: mean proportional gap among poor; Gₚ: Gini of income/consumption among poor, not overall population Gini. Combines incidence, depth and inequality; avoid substituting P₁ for I. OPHI on Sen

10. Randomised controlled trials

  • Randomly allocate eligible units to treatment and control; compare outcomes to estimate a programme's causal impact. Random sampling and random treatment assignment are different.
  • Example: additional teachers randomly assigned across schools; compare learning outcomes under a pre-specified design, account for clustering and spillovers.
  • 2019 Economics laureates: Abhijit Banerjee, Esther Duflo, Michael Kremer, for experimental work on global poverty. Nobel account
  • Strength: tests what works and for whom. Limits: ethics/consent, attrition, spillovers, cost, short follow-up and whether local effects generalise or survive scale-up; not every policy is suitable for randomisation.

11. Bare Necessities Index and IBSA Fund

  • BNI, Economic Survey 2020–21: 26 indicators / five dimensions—water, sanitation, housing, micro-environment, other facilities; includes basic electricity/cooking access. Uses NSO 2012 and 2018 surveys; distinct from MPI's poverty cutoff/weights. Survey chapter
  • IBSA = India, Brazil, South Africa; poverty/hunger development projects through South–South cooperation. 2017 trust-fund agreement; each country's annual US$1 million contribution is the source's arrangement; fund administered through UN South–South machinery. Book India US$15.1 million cumulative is a dated total, not current contribution. IBSA reports

12. Universal Basic Income

  • Periodic cash payment to individuals, universal and unconditional, without means/work test. Universality + unconditionality + agency; agency means recipients choose spending. Targeted cash assistance/DBT is not automatically UBI.
  • Economic Survey 2016–17 proposal: illustration ₹7,620/person/year, 75% quasi-universal coverage, cost 4.9% GDP; not a fully universal programme or a current expenditure commitment. Book's ~950 schemes / ~5.2% GDP comparison is from that historical policy debate. Survey calculation

  • JAM: Jan Dhan–Aadhaar–Mobile; operational access and safeguards matter, not merely account opening. Agree Centre–State finance, ensure rural banking capacity and payment reliability.
  • Replacement or complement? Replacing health/education/nutrition with cash can remove essential services; retaining every scheme plus UBI raises cost. A single failed channel becomes dangerous if all other support is withdrawn.
  • Claims of alcohol/gambling misuse or universal work withdrawal require evidence; book's 2011 Madhya Pradesh pilot cannot establish effects of a national permanent scheme.
  • Canada, Brazil, Iran and other experiments/transfers differ in universality/conditions; do not label all as full UBI. Evaluate adequacy, sustainable financing, public-service protection, pilots and independent outcomes before scale-up.

13. PYQs and answer cues

  • Prelims 2019: State poverty lines differ because price levels vary—(b); incidence, GSDP and PDS quality are not the direct price-adjustment explanation.
  • Prelims 2012: MPI covers household education/health/assets/services deprivation—statement 1 only, (a); not national PPP or budget-deficit/GDP-growth measurement.
  • Mains framework: define the measure → identify jobs/capability/exclusion/shock channels → combine productive opportunity and public services → accessible protection → evaluate outcomes using comparable periods/methods.
  • Recall: line ≠ ratio; incidence ≠ depth; MPI ≠ monetary poverty; survey year ≠ report year; estimate ≠ beneficiary list; percentage points ≠ percent; UBI ≠ every cash transfer.
