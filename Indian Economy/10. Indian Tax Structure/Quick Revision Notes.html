Tax basics and who can levy what

Tax: compulsory payment imposed by law, without a direct individual quid pro quo; finances public services and redistribution. Article 265: no tax without authority of law. Tax powers are distributed constitutionally; local bodies exercise powers delegated by state law.

LevelMain examples
UnionNon-agricultural income tax, corporation tax, customs, central excise on retained products, CGST and IGST
StateSGST, excise on alcohol for human consumption, stamp/registration charges, vehicle/profession/electricity taxes, agricultural income tax where levied
LocalProperty tax and other levies authorised by state law

SGST belongs to states, not the Union. IGST is levied/collected by the Union and apportioned under law. A fee for a service and a non-tax receipt are not automatically taxes.

Impact: initial payer. Incidence: final economic burden. Actual incidence depends on supply/demand elasticities; even a direct tax can be partly shifted. Marginal rate applies to the next rupee; average rate = total tax / total income. Entering a higher slab does not tax all income at that higher rate.

Personal income tax and Income-tax Act, 2025

Personal income tax covers eligible income of individuals, HUFs, firms and other non-company persons. Heads: salary, house property, business/profession, capital gains and other sources. Agricultural income is treated separately; poultry/wool-rearing income is not automatically exempt agricultural income.

Income-tax Act, 2025: received assent in August 2025; effective 1 April 2026, replacing the 1961 Act for new tax years while preserving the old law for earlier periods/proceedings. Tax year = twelve months beginning 1 April; replaces the previous-year/assessment-year distinction for the new Act. Simplifies language, removes obsolete provisions and uses tables/formulae; does not itself imply every tax rate changed. Includes virtual digital assets in capital-asset terminology and consolidates procedures. 536 sections; avoid the source's misleading claim that the 1961 Act had exactly 800 sections.

Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended by Finance Act, 2026.

Eligible resident individuals with ordinary taxable income up to ₹12 lakh receive rebate, subject to conditions; this is not a ₹12-lakh nil-rate slab. With ₹75,000 standard deduction, eligible salaried ordinary income can reach ₹12.75 lakh without income tax. Special-rate income such as capital gains is excluded from this blanket claim. Updated return (ITR-U): window expanded to 48 months; the source incorrectly calls this a four-year revised-return deadline. Revised and updated returns are distinct. ITR-U generally cannot increase a refund, reduce liability or enhance a loss; additional tax applies. Income-tax return FAQs.

Do not apply the source's old ₹250-crore turnover cut-off to every current year. Tax residence, not just registration, determines worldwide-income taxation. Foreign companies have different rules/rates. Concessional corporate regimes are distinct from individual old/new regimes.

Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT)

Why: profitable companies using concessions should not become 'zero-tax companies'. Compare normal tax with tax on adjusted book profit; pay the higher where MAT applies. First introduced in 1987, withdrawn in 1990, reintroduced from 1 April 1997.

  • Historical ordinary MAT: 18.5%, reduced to 15% in 2019.
  • Tax year 2026-27 onward: ordinary MAT 14%, plus applicable surcharge/cess; special IFSC provisions differ.
  • No new MAT credit accumulation from 1 April 2026. Domestic companies shifting to the concessional regime can use eligible pre-April-2026 credit, generally capped at 25% of tax liability, within its original 15-year limit. Foreign-company set-off rules differ.
  • MAT does not apply to companies opting for the specified concessional corporate regimes. Book profit ≠ taxable income ≠ turnover.

Current MAT guidance; Budget 2026 corporate-regime FAQs.

STT, CTT and abolished taxes

Securities Transaction Tax (STT): introduced in 2004; on specified securities transactions, not every financial asset. Delivery-based equity transactions generally attract 0.1% on each eligible purchase/sale; equity-fund redemption has separate treatment.

Futures/options sale is charged to seller; exercised-option STT to purchaser. The changed rates began 1 April, not the Budget announcement date of 1 February. NSE STT computation.

Commodity Transaction Tax (CTT): on specified exchange-traded commodity derivatives, notably non-agricultural derivatives; proposed in 2008 but introduced operationally in 2013, not continuously levied since 2008.

Profession tax: state/local levy under Article 276; constitutional ceiling ₹2,500 per person per year. Its name does not mean it applies only to formally licensed professions.

Capital gains, indexation and buybacks

Capital gain: profit from transfer of a capital asset. Capital assets generally include property/securities; exclude stock-in-trade/raw materials, qualifying personal effects and rural agricultural land. Jewellery, art and specified collections are not exempt merely as personal effects. Rural-land status uses municipality population and distance rules (2/6/8 km as applicable), not a universal eight-kilometre rule.

Holding-period rule for transfers from 23 July 2024Classification
Specified listed securities/equity-oriented fund unitsUp to 12 months short-term; above 12 months long-term
Most other assets, including land/building, unlisted shares and physical goldUp to 24 months short-term; above 24 months long-term

Special deeming rules apply to specified debt funds, market-linked debentures and certain bonds; the two-period rule is not universal. The source's 36-month physical-gold rule is historical. Holding-period guidance.

23 July 2024 changes: specified equity STCG 15% → 20%; specified equity LTCG 10% → 12.5%, with annual exempt gains threshold ₹1 lakh → ₹1.25 lakh. General LTCG rate becomes 12.5% without indexation, subject to exceptions. Ordinary STCG may be taxed at normal applicable rates. Income-source tax treatment.

Indexation: inflation-adjust acquisition cost using the Cost Inflation Index; reduces nominal gain to recognise inflation. Indexed cost = original cost × CII in transfer year / CII in acquisition year (subject to base-year rules).

Grandfathered property relief: resident individuals/HUFs transferring land/building acquired before 23 July 2024 get protection where 12.5% without indexation exceeds 20% with indexation. This is a tax-liability cap, not a universal option to use 12.5% with indexation or carry forward old indexed losses. Capital-gain guidance.

GST: timeline, Constitution and core mechanism

GST = destination-based, multi-stage tax on supply of goods/services with input tax credit. Taxable event is supply; eventual burden is on consumption. 'One Nation, One Tax' is a policy slogan, not a claim of one rate or one taxing authority.

Timeline: Kelkar task force recommendation (2003) → Budget 2006-07 proposed GST by April 2010 → discussion paper (2009) → 115th Amendment Bill (2011) → 122nd Amendment Bill (2014) → 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016 → central/state GST laws (2017) → GST launched 1 July 2017. The source's '101st Amendment Act, 2017' is incorrect.

  • Intrastate: CGST + SGST; CGST + UTGST in applicable UTs without legislatures. Total 18% can split 9% + 9%; this is an illustration, not a rate for all goods.
  • Interstate: IGST; Union collects and settles destination-state share. Imports may bear basic customs duty plus IGST. Exports and supplies to SEZ developers/units for authorised operations are zero-rated.
  • Zero-rated ≠ nil-rated/exempt: zero-rated supplies may retain input-credit/refund benefits subject to law; exempt supplies generally restrict attributable ITC.
  • Not supply: employee services to employer in employment, funeral/burial/cremation services, sale of land and completed buildings subject to Schedule III conditions. Under-construction property can be taxable.

GST rates: separate old slabs from 2025 reform

Older mainstream structure: 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%, plus exemptions/special rates/cess. 56th Council, September 2025: mainstream 5% merit + 18% standard, special 40% demerit rate; major changes effective 22 September 2025, with specified tobacco products initially deferred. Exemptions and special rates remain: it is not literally only two rates for all supplies.

Fresh/unpackaged staples can be exempt; pre-packaged and labelled supplies may be taxable. Hospitality/apparel/footwear thresholds and product definitions matter; the supplied table combines different periods and should not be memorised wholesale.

Input tax credit, registration and compliance

ITC = eligible tax paid on business inputs used to offset output GST. Prevents tax-on-tax cascading; depends on invoices, receipt, reporting/payment and other statutory conditions. Personal use and blocked-credit categories do not qualify.

Illustration at 18%: inputs ₹100 + ₹18 GST; sale ₹300 + ₹54 GST → net remittance ₹54 - ₹18 = ₹36. Total tax through chain = ₹54 on final ₹300. This illustrates credit mechanics, not a prescribed product rate.

Credit availableFirst useRemaining eligible use
IGSTIGSTCGST and SGST/UTGST in permitted order/proportions; exhaust IGST credit first

SGST / UTGSTSame state/UT taxIGST; never directly CGST

Interstate supplies do not create a blanket CGST↔SGST cross-credit exception. Inverted duty structure: higher input rate than output rate can accumulate credit and lock working capital; permitted refunds are conditional, not automatic. Customs duty inversion is a related but separate application.

  • Registration: eligible exclusive goods suppliers may use ₹40-lakh threshold in adopting states; generally ₹20 lakh elsewhere. Services generally ₹20 lakh, ₹10 lakh in specified states. Do not describe every northeastern/hilly state identically; compulsory-registration cases and exemptions alter the result.
  • Composition scheme: qualifying small suppliers, generally preceding-year turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (lower in specified states); service/mixed-supply scheme up to ₹50 lakh, conditions apply. Not every taxpayer below the threshold is eligible; composition suppliers cannot generally collect GST separately or claim ITC.
  • E-invoice: invoice details registered electronically to obtain IRN/QR; eligible turnover threshold above ₹5 crore from 1 August 2023, with exempt classes. Different from an e-way bill. GST IRP mandate.
  • E-way bill: electronic movement document, generally for consignment value above ₹50,000, subject to exceptions/state provisions; ordinarily one day per 200 km or part thereof. The source's 'sale beyond 10 km' is not the general trigger; an invoice alone is not a replacement.
  • Reverse charge: recipient pays for notified categories/imported services etc.; not every unregistered purchase is automatically under RCM. E-commerce operator liability under separate provisions is not always recipient reverse charge. Self-invoice is required in applicable notified unregistered-supplier cases.
  • Circular trading: fake invoices without actual supplies to fraudulently claim ITC; evasion, not legitimate tax planning.

GST institutions and federal issues

InstitutionRole
GST CouncilConstitutional recommendation forum: rates, exemptions, model laws and related questions
GSTNNon-profit technology backbone: registration, returns, payment, refunds, e-way bill/e-invoice support; fully government-owned, Centre/states 50:50
GSTATStatutory appellate tribunal under CGST section 109; hears appeals from appellate/revisional authorities; Principal Bench in New Delhi and state benches

Council: Union Finance Minister chairs; Union Minister of State (Revenue/Finance) and each state's nominated finance/taxation minister are members; states choose vice-chairperson. Quorum = half of total members. Decisions require at least 3/4 of weighted votes of members present and voting: Union 1/3, states together 2/3. Not 75% of an unweighted headcount. Council recommendations are not binding legislation by themselves (Supreme Court, *Mohit Minerals*, 2022).

Anti-profiteering: pass rate-cut/ITC benefits through commensurate price reductions. Historical NAA (chair + four members) → CCI from December 2022 → GSTAT Principal Bench for continuing matters. New applications stopped from 1 April 2025; do not present NAA as the current standing body for new complaints. Restitution/Consumer Welfare Fund and judicial remedies arise under relevant law. 53rd Council decisions; GSTAT.

HSN: Harmonized System of goods classification, developed by WCO; international six-digit core, national extensions. SAC: Services Accounting Code for services. HS 2022 is the seventh edition, effective 1 January 2022; do not call SAC itself a globally adopted WCO commodity system.

Advance ruling: legal clarification on specified proposed or ongoing transactions; reduces uncertainty/litigation. Its binding scope is limited to the applicant and relevant authorities, not every taxpayer nationwide.

Compensation and assessment of GST

Compensation: five-year transition beginning July 2017, protected state revenue growth 14% annually over 2015-16 base. COVID-19 reduced cess receipts and intensified disputes over shortfalls/borrowing. Original compensation entitlement period ended June 2022; later cess continuation for loan repayment is a separate issue.

Benefits: less cascading, common market, formalisation, invoice trail, easier interstate logistics and wider base. Challenges: multiple rates/classification, compliance costs for MSMEs, refund delays/working-capital lock-up, evasion/fake invoices, unsettled exclusions and federal revenue concerns. Reforms: simplification, rate rationalisation, higher small-business thresholds, composition, e-invoicing, better refunds and operational appellate institutions. Revenue increases must be judged against nominal growth, rates, compliance and state distribution.

Revenue-neutral rate: rate/structure that would preserve aggregate revenue relative to the earlier system under given assumptions; not necessarily an unchanged rate for every product or no price impact.

Other indirect taxes and economic ideas

  • Basic customs duty: product-specific import levy; no universal 5-40% range. Social Welfare Surcharge is generally 10% of its prescribed duty base, with exclusions/exemptions. The old 3% education-cess claim is historical, not a universal current customs cess.
  • Anti-dumping duty: below-normal-value imports causing material injury, following investigation. Safeguard measures: import surge causing/threatening serious injury; unfair pricing is not necessary. Customs Tariff Act, 1975 provides the framework.
  • Stamp duty: specified documents/transactions; differs by state, alongside registration fees. Road/motor-vehicle tax: state levy, may depend on value, weight, seating/engine capacity; distinct from GST on purchase.
  • Sin tax: tax on socially harmful consumption such as tobacco/alcohol; can discourage use and internalise costs. Not every harmful activity is legal or taxable in an identical way.
  • Pigouvian tax: correct negative externalities, ideally aligned to marginal external cost. Tobin tax: proposed levy on short-term foreign-exchange transactions to curb speculation; not India's STT or a universal enacted tax.

Tax performance and Laffer curve

Tax-to-GDP ratio = specified tax revenue / nominal GDP × 100. State whether Union gross taxes, Union net taxes or general-government revenue is used. A larger ratio may indicate greater fiscal capacity; a fall alone cannot prove weaker growth or inequality because exemptions, policy and compliance also matter. OECD comparisons require comparable years and coverage.

Buoyancy = % change in tax revenue / % change in nominal GDP, including discretionary policy effects. Elasticity measures response to the tax base/income after removing discretionary tax changes. It is not simply response to a change in the tax rate. Above-one buoyancy means revenue grows faster than GDP.

Laffer curve: conceptual inverted-U relation between rate and revenue; high rates may shrink the base through disincentives/avoidance/evasion. The revenue-maximising point is empirically uncertain; 15% is not a universal optimum, and tax cuts do not always raise revenue. Revenue maximisation is not identical to social welfare maximisation.

Tax expenditure: estimated revenue impact of exemptions, concessional rates, deductions, rebates, deferrals and credits relative to a benchmark. Like an implicit subsidy; not direct cash spending. India first presented a revenue-forgone statement in Budget 2006-07, separately in 2007-08; now a statement of revenue impact of tax incentives. Assess targeting, additional investment, distribution and transparency. Examples: qualifying concessional corporation-tax routes, old-regime saving/housing deductions, charitable exemptions.

Tax administration, rights and anti-black-money measures

CBDT: direct-tax apex board. CBIC: indirect taxes/customs/GST/retained excise, formerly CBEC. Both are statutory under the Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963, Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance. The old combined board was divided in 1964.

TARC: constituted 2013 under Parthasarathi Shome, reports from 2014; taxpayer services, organisational reform, ICT/data exchange, wider base, compliance management, capacity building, revenue forecasting and impact assessment. Project Insight: data integration/analytics to identify compliance risk and mismatches in high-value transactions; PAN/Aadhaar linkage helps identity matching. Do not reduce it to universal social-media surveillance.

Taxpayers' Charter (2020): fair/courteous treatment, presume honesty unless evidence otherwise, accurate information, impartial appeals, time-bound decisions, correct tax, privacy/confidentiality and grievance redressal. Duties: honest/full disclosure, maintain records, respond to notices and pay/file on time.

Faceless reforms: e-assessment 2019; faceless assessment and appeals 2020; faceless penalty 2021. Digital allocation/review reduces physical interface but preserves lawful hearing/due process; these schemes already had statutory enabling provisions before the 2025 Act.

Vivad Se Vishwas: dispute settlement, first 2020 and second 2024; second covered eligible appeals pending on 22 July 2024. For new appellants, 100% of disputed tax OR 25% of disputed interest/penalty/fee, depending on the dispute category, at the lower-payment deadline; not 100% tax plus 25% penalty in every case. Higher amounts applied later; do not use expired deadlines as open windows. CBDT 2024 scheme clarification.

Benami law: 1988 Act substantially amended in 2016; property held by one person for another's benefit using the other's consideration, fictitious owners or untraceable payers, subject to statutory exceptions (specified family/fiduciary situations). Prohibited transactions can attract confiscation; qualifying offences 1-7 years rigorous imprisonment and fine up to 25% of fair market value.

Black Money Act, 2015: undisclosed foreign income/assets of covered residents; 30% tax, concealment penalty three times tax (=90% of asset/income, not 90% of the tax). Willful evasion can attract 3-10 years imprisonment; filing/non-disclosure penalties have specific exceptions and thresholds. Do not apply every punishment automatically to every error.

Panama Papers (2016): leaked Mossack Fonseca offshore documents; Paradise Papers (2017) and Mauritius leaks (2019) exposed other offshore arrangements. Offshore incorporation is not itself proof of illegality; concealment, evasion and beneficial ownership require investigation.

Financial Secrecy Index: Tax Justice Network, first 2009; combines secrecy with scale of offshore activity. Historical 2022: US first, Switzerland second, India 36th (47th in 2020). A lower rank is not automatically proof that secrecy improved because size and other jurisdictions also affect ranking.

International taxation: BEPS, DTAA and information exchange

BEPS (base erosion and profit shifting): arrangements exploiting gaps/mismatches to move taxable profits away from real activity. May involve transfer pricing, financing/intangibles, treaty shopping or artificial structures; not all BEPS is criminal evasion and not every shell company is unlawful.

  • DTAA: allocates taxing rights and offers exemption/credit relief; supports investment. Abuse through treaty shopping/round-tripping requires anti-abuse rules; signing a DTAA does not automatically remove all tax.
  • PoEM: place where key management/commercial decisions for the business as a whole are substantively made. Relevant to foreign-company residence; effective application from AY 2017-18, following Finance Act 2015 introduction. Incorporation address alone is insufficient.
  • AEOI: regular automatic exchange of specified taxpayer/financial-account information; distinct from exchange on request. CRS: OECD common reporting standard for financial-account information. India participates in the Global Forum on Transparency and Exchange of Information for Tax Purposes; exchange is reciprocal under applicable agreements.
  • OECD/G20 two pillars: Pillar One reallocates certain taxing rights toward market jurisdictions; Pillar Two aims at 15% effective minimum taxation for in-scope large MNE groups (generally €750-million revenue threshold). Agreement is not universal automatic implementation on one date; national legislation and exceptions matter. OECD cross-border framework.
  • Equalisation levy: separate Finance Act 2016 regime, not inserted as a tax under the Income-tax Act. Historical 6% online advertising from 2016; 2% e-commerce from April 2020 subject to threshold/conditions. 2% withdrawn from 1 August 2024; 6% withdrawn from 1 April 2025. Credit under a DTAA was not automatically available for this separate levy. Income-tax Department equalisation-levy guidance.

Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)

EU Regulation 2023/956: carbon cost on specified imports to prevent carbon leakage (production moving to jurisdictions with weaker carbon constraints). Six initial sectors: iron/steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, electricity, hydrogen.

  • Transitional reporting began October 2023; definitive regime 1 January 2026. EU importer/authorised declarant reports embedded emissions and surrenders corresponding certificates, with adjustments including carbon price effectively paid abroad.
  • Certificate prices track EU ETS auction prices: quarterly averages in 2026, weekly from 2027. Purchases for 2026 obligations begin in February 2027; first declaration/surrender deadline 30 September 2027.
  • 50-tonne annual threshold applies to the covered mass-based goods; electricity/hydrogen are treated separately, not universally exempt below 50 tonnes.
  • India: potential cost/competitiveness pressure especially on steel/aluminium; improve verified emissions data and decarbonisation. India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (2023), following the Energy Conservation amendment (2022), does not automatically cancel CBAM liability; qualifying carbon payments/recognition matter.
  • Concerns: trade discrimination, developing-country capacity and climate equity/CBDR; balance against leakage prevention. Do not claim all Indian exports bear a fixed full tariff or that CBAM covers all sectors.

EU definitive-regime rules; sector/threshold guidance; certificate pricing; first compliance deadline.
