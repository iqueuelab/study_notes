# Indian Art & Culture — Chronology

Events, people and periods in chronological order.

Ranges are placed at their start date. Approximate dates remain labelled. Year-only entries do not establish an exact order within that year.

## Before 10,000 BCE · Human origins and stone tools

### c.2.5 million years ago–10,000 BCE · broad chapter scheme · Palaeolithic tool traditions

Old Stone Age hunting and gathering; broad global teaching range, not a securely dated start for India. Numeric placement of the remote starting point is approximate.

*Period · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.385 ±64 thousand years ago · Early Middle Palaeolithic transition at Attirampakkam

Prepared-core/flake transition dated by luminescence; this Indian sequence extends to about 172 thousand years ago. Placement is approximate, not an exact BCE date.

*Technology · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### About 300,000 years ago · Homo sapiens evolves in Africa

Modern human origins predate the Upper Palaeolithic. Numeric placement is approximate; a tool industry is not a biological species.

*Human origins · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### Around 100,000 BCE · conventional boundary · Lower Palaeolithic in the simplified chapter scheme

Approximate ending boundary used by the chapter; not a synchronous change across India. Attirampakkam demonstrates much earlier Middle Palaeolithic technology.

*Period · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.100,000–40,000 BCE · simplified range · Middle Palaeolithic: conventional teaching range

Flake and prepared-core industries. This conventional range does not override earlier securely dated regional evidence.

*Period · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.40,000–10,000 BCE · Upper Palaeolithic: conventional range

Blade–burin toolkits, bone working, ornaments and symbolic expression; regional variation remains.

*Period · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

## 10,000 BCE onwards · Food production and regional cultures

### c.10,000–7000 BCE · simplified range · Mesolithic: conventional chapter range

Microlithic hunting/fishing/gathering communities; regional Mesolithic traditions persisted later than this range.

*Period · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### From c.7000 BCE · Early farming settlement at Mehrgarh

Balochistan farming village with wheat/barley, livestock and mud-brick houses; early levels preceded pottery.

*Settlement · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.7000–2000 BCE · regional variation · Neolithic: broad chapter range

Farming, animal keeping, polished tools and village life developed unevenly.

*Period · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.3000–700 BCE · broad range · Chalcolithic regional villages

Copper and stone used together in farming villages; overlaps Harappan Bronze Age urbanism rather than universally preceding it.

*Period · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### Third–second millennia BCE · Southern Neolithic agro-pastoral traditions

Cattle keeping, ashmounds and ground stone tools characterise several Deccan communities; beginning/end varied locally.

*Regional culture · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.2800–2200 BCE · Ganeshwar–Jodhpura: conventional regional range

Rajasthan copper-working sites linked to wider Harappan-period exchange; not a universal beginning date for copper use.

*Regional culture · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.2600–1900 BCE · Mature Harappan civilisation: contextual comparison

Bronze Age towns coexisted with regional copper-stone villages. Undeciphered writing places Harappan studies within protohistory.

*Regional culture · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### c.1500–200 BCE · simplified range · Iron Age: broad chapter range

Regional iron-using traditions overlapped later Chalcolithic societies; this is not a universal date for the earliest iron.

*Period · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

## Discoveries and research

### 30 May 1863 · Foote recognises a Palaeolithic handaxe at Pallavaram

Robert Bruce Foote’s recognised discovery near Chennai opened a major phase of Indian prehistoric research.

*Discovery · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1865 · Lubbock publishes Pre-historic Times

John Lubbock introduced the Palaeolithic and Neolithic terminology.

*Research · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)

### 1956 · Sankalia identifies the Nevasa flake industry

H. D. Sankalia’s Pravara-valley work is associated with identifying the Indian Middle Palaeolithic/Nevasian industry.

*Research · [C1](01.%20Prehistoric%20Cultures%20in%20India/Quick%20Revision%20Notes.html)
