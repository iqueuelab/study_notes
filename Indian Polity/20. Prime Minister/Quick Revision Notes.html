Prime Minister

1. Appointment and parliamentary confidence

  • President = Head of State/formal executive; PM = Head of Government/real executive leader, operating through the Council of Ministers.
  • 75: President appoints PM; Constitution prescribes no popular/direct PM election or detailed party-selection procedure. With clear LS majority, appoint its leader; with hung House, select person likely to command confidence, coalition/support evidence matters.
  • Largest party alone has no absolute constitutional entitlement. Floor test within reasonable time, not an invariable constitutional “one-month” limit. Appointment need not await a prior confidence vote; majority must subsequently be established when required.
  • 1979: N. Sanjeeva Reddy appointed Charan Singh after Morarji Desai government's fall. Examples of appointment before proving confidence: Charan Singh 1979; V.P. Singh 1989; Chandra Shekhar 1990; P.V. Narasimha Rao 1991; Vajpayee 1996/1998; Deve Gowda 1996; Gujral 1997.
  • Sudden death/no clear successor requires situational presidential judgment: 1964/1966 Gulzarilal Nanda interim PM after Nehru/Shastri; 1984 Rajiv Gandhi appointed after Indira's assassination, then confirmed as party leader.
  • 75(5): non-MP may be appointed but must enter either House within six consecutive months, otherwise ceases to be Minister/PM; no recurring appointments to evade rule. SC upheld non-MP PM appointment in 1996.
  • PM may belong to LS or RS: Indira Gandhi at initial 1966 appointment, Deve Gowda after 1996 parliamentary entry, Manmohan Singh 2004 were RS-associated examples. Deve Gowda initially not an MP, so do not imply he was already RS member at appointment.

2. Oath, tenure and emoluments

  • President administers office and secrecy oaths under 75(4)/Third Schedule: constitutional allegiance; sovereignty/integrity; faithful conscientious duty; justice without fear/favour, affection/ill-will; confidentiality except due official discharge.
  • No fixed PM term; presidential pleasure operates through LS confidence, not arbitrary dismissal of a majority-backed government. Loss of confidence → resignation or constitutional replacement after majority determination.
  • Pay/allowances governed by Parliament, including Ministers' Salaries/Allowances Act, 1952: MP-linked remuneration plus prescribed sumptuary/residence/travel/medical facilities. Chapter records PM sumptuary allowance rise ₹1,500 → ₹3,000/month in 2001; not total salary.

3. PM and Council of Ministers

  • Recommends persons for presidential ministerial appointment; allocates/reshuffles portfolios; asks Minister to resign or recommends removal; chairs Cabinet/Council discussions and coordinates policy.
  • Guides, directs and coordinates Ministers; government's cohesion and collective responsibility centre on PM leadership.
  • PM resignation/death ends existing ministry's political basis, requiring successor/reconstituted arrangements; does not imply that public administration stops or no caretaker continuity is possible. Other Minister's departure usually creates only individual vacancy.
  • Laski's recall: PM central to ministry's formation, life and end.

4. PM and President: 74, 75, 78

  • 74: Council aids/advises President; President may ask reconsideration once, must act on reconsidered advice. 75: PM appointed by President, other Ministers on PM advice; ministry collectively responsible to LS.
  • 78's three duties: communicate Council's administration/legislation decisions; supply information President requests; submit individual Minister's decision to Council when President requires.
  • PM principal channel of President-ministry communication; advice on appointments operates through office-specific constitutional/statutory selection processes, not unilateral selection of every constitutional officer.

5. Parliament and broader leadership

  • Advises parliamentary summoning/prorogation; may recommend LS dissolution, subject to constitutional consideration where majority lost/alternative exists; announces government policy and manages legislative programme.
  • PM may be RS member, so textbook “always leader of Lower House” is too broad; government's LS Leader can be another Minister. 88: Minister can speak/take part in either House and specified committees, but votes only in House of membership.
  • Chairs NITI Aayog, Inter-State Council, National Integration Council and specified national coordination bodies; chapter also lists National Water Resources Council. These roles arise under respective arrangements, not one omnibus constitutional Article.
  • Shapes foreign policy, spokesperson of Union government, political crisis coordination, party/coalition leadership; receives State/public representations; political head of administrative machinery while constitutional/statutory safeguards remain.

6. Role descriptions: match person to phrase

  • Lord Morley: first among equals (primus inter pares), keystone of Cabinet arch. Herbert Morrison: “first among equals” understates modern authority.
  • William Vernon Harcourt: moon among lesser stars. Ivor Jennings: sun around which planets revolve; all constitutional roads lead to PM.
  • H.J. Laski: pivot; central to ministry's formation/life/death. H.R.G. Greaves: leader masters governmental machinery.
  • W.B. Munro: captain of State's ship. Ramsay Muir: steersman. R.H. Crossman: shift from Cabinet to Prime-Ministerial government. Humphry Berkeley: criticism of excessive PM power weakening effective parliamentary sovereignty.
  • Treat metaphors as commentary: strength varies with majority, party organisation, coalition, Cabinet colleagues, institutions and political circumstances. Ambedkar: Indian executive leadership comparable to US President lies chiefly with PM rather than ceremonial President; systems remain different.

7. Former Chief Ministers who became PMs

  • Morarji Desai: Bombay CM 1952-56, PM 1977, first non-Congress PM. Charan Singh: UP CM 1967-68/1970, PM 1979.
  • V.P. Singh: UP CM, PM December 1989-November 1990. P.V. Narasimha Rao: AP CM 1971-73, PM 1991-96, first PM from South India.
  • H.D. Deve Gowda: Karnataka CM before PM June 1996. Narendra Modi: Gujarat CM 2001-14, PM first took office May 2014. Six people in chapter's list; these are historical office transitions.

8. Caretaker government: convention and continuity

  • Constitution has no separate caretaker-government category; responsible ministry continues as necessary after resignation/LS dissolution until new arrangements. Free/fair elections conducted by ECI, with administration required to support lawful, neutral process.
  • Routine administration and urgent national/security-interest decisions continue; convention discourages major new policy, avoidable spending, appointments or electoral advantage without proper justification. Caretaker does not mean all legal executive powers vanish.
  • Tarkunde/JP Committee: unofficial electoral-reform committee 1974, report 1975, chair Justice V.M. Tarkunde; proposed no new policies/projects, allowances/loans/pay rises or partisan minister-attended official functions.
  • August 1979 presidential communique: government promised neutral elections, no major new policy/spending; urgent national-interest work not held up. Historical proposed Nov-Dec timetable must not be confused with actual later election dates.
  • Calcutta HC, December 1979: extraordinary continuity arrangement for necessary day-to-day administration until alternative ministry. Restraint rationale: dissolved LS weakens normal parliamentary accountability; outgoing government must not exploit office in elections.
  • Distinguish caretaker convention, EC's Model Code of Conduct, and binding constitutional/statutory duties; they overlap but are not identical.

9. One-minute recall / Mains

  • 74 advice; 75 appointment/LS responsibility/6 months; 77 government business; 78 communication; 88 participation.
  • Either House eligible; no fixed term; majority support central; President appoints but does not have unrestricted policy choice.
  • Mains: PM links President-Cabinet-Parliament; leadership enables coordination, while collective responsibility, opposition, courts, federal institutions and internal Cabinet/coalition consultation curb concentration.

Reference

  • Official Constitution: Articles 74-78 and 88.
