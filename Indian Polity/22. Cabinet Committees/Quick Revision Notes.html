Cabinet Committees

1. Why Cabinet uses committees

  • Division of labour + delegation: reduce Cabinet’s workload; examine specialised issues closely; coordinate ministries; formulate proposals and take authorised decisions.
  • Extra-constitutional: not named in the Constitution. They operate under the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961, made under Article 77(3); extra-constitutional does not mean unlawful.
  • Standing committees: continuing bodies, but composition/functions can change. Ad hoc committees: temporary, task-specific; dissolved when their work ends. Historical example: Emergency Committee during the 1962 Chinese invasion.
  • PM determines standing committees’ number, functions and ministerial composition. Ad hoc committees/GoMs may be constituted by Cabinet, a standing committee or PM, under Rule 6.
  • Usually Cabinet ministers; non-Cabinet ministers may be members or special invitees. Membership includes relevant portfolio ministers and other senior ministers; committee size varies.
  • PM chairs committees of which PM is a member; others may be led by senior ministers. Not every Cabinet Committee is PM-headed.
  • Under Rule 6, committees can make authorised decisions as well as recommendations; not every case must subsequently go to full Cabinet. Matters reserved for Cabinet and limits on delegation still apply.
  • Cabinet may review committee decisions. Interdepartmental matters require consultation with concerned departments; committees facilitate coordination rather than replacing it.

2. Eight committees: functions to remember

  • Political Affairs: major domestic/foreign policy matters; intergovernmental political issues. Described in the chapter as “Super-Cabinet” because of its wide political influence; this is not a legal superiority over Cabinet.
  • Economic Affairs: directs/co-ordinates economic policy and governmental economic activity; major economic proposals within its assigned remit.
  • Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC): specified senior appointments/empanelments, including designated central administration, public enterprise, bank and financial-institution posts. It does not appoint every government employee or replace constitutional appointment procedures.
  • Parliamentary Affairs: government’s parliamentary business, priorities and progress.
  • Security: defence, internal/external security, law-and-order policy issues within Union remit and atomic-energy/security matters. It does not erase State policing powers.
  • Accommodation: policy/allotment of government accommodation for MPs, central employees and other eligible persons/organisations.
  • Investment and Growth: investment, capital inflows, growth, exports, import substitution and ease of doing business.
  • Employment and Skill Development (chapter name): skills, employability and workforce participation, including women. Current name: Skill, Employment and Livelihood.

3. Official composition update — 27 July 2026

  • Official Cabinet Secretariat list retains eight committees. It uses Cabinet Committee on Skill, Employment and Livelihood, replacing the chapter’s older name. Official composition.
  • PM leads six: Appointments, Economic Affairs, Political Affairs, Security, Investment and Growth, Skill/Employment/Livelihood.
  • Accommodation: Amit Shah heads it. Parliamentary Affairs: Raj Nath Singh heads it. Chairmanship and membership are changeable, not constitutionally fixed.
  • ACC: PM + Amit Shah. Security: PM + Defence (Raj Nath Singh), Home (Amit Shah), Finance (Nirmala Sitharaman), External Affairs (S. Jaishankar).
  • MoS participation is illustrated by special invitees, including Jayant Chaudhary in Skill/Employment/Livelihood and Jitendra Singh in Accommodation. Special invitee status differs from committee membership.

4. Groups of Ministers (GoMs)

  • GoM: ministers brought together for an identified cross-ministry issue; investigates/co-ordinates and recommends. An empowered GoM may decide within explicitly delegated authority. Concerned portfolio ministers participate; task-bound groups may be disbanded after completion.
  • These are different from the standing Cabinet Committees. Neither the number nor existence of particular GoMs is permanent.
  • Second ARC (2005–2009), Organisational Structure of Government report (2009): too many GoMs, irregular meetings and unfinished work caused delays. Recommended selective use, clear mandates, prescribed deadlines and effective coordination; decision-making authority where appropriate. Commission chaired by Veerappa Moily.
  • 31 May 2014: government abolished the then-existing 9 EGoMs + 21 GoMs, returning pending issues to ministries/departments with Cabinet Secretariat/PMO facilitation. This historical decision did not remove the Rules’ provision allowing later ad hoc GoMs. PIB announcement.

5. One-minute recall

  • Cabinet = constitutional definition in 352(3); Cabinet Committees = business-rule bodies; GoMs = issue-specific coordination.
  • Standing ≠ immutable; ad hoc ≠ standing; recommendation ≠ only function; delegation ≠ unlimited power.
  • Eight now; six PM-headed; Accommodation—Home; Parliamentary Affairs—Defence. ACC = two members; Security = PM + four key ministers.
  • Mains: committee system improves specialisation/speed; safeguards are inter-ministry consultation, clear authority, Cabinet review and time-bound coordination.

Reference

  • Transaction of Business Rules: Rule 6 and First Schedule.
