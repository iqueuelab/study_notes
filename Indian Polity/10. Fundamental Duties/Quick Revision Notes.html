Fundamental Duties

1. Origin and constitutional location

  • Original Constitution had Fundamental Rights and State-directed DPSPs, but no separate Fundamental Duties list.
  • USSR influence; Swaran Singh Committee 1976; 42nd Amendment 1976 → Part IVA, Article 51A, ten duties. 86th Amendment 2002 → eleventh educational duty, 51A(k).
  • Committee appointed by Congress during 1975–77 Emergency; recommended a separate chapter and eight duties, while final amendment included ten.
  • Unaccepted recommendations: tax-payment duty; general penalties for non-compliance; immunity of penalty laws from constitutional challenge. Tax duties exist under ordinary law, but paying tax is not an express 51A duty.
  • Comparative caution: Japan has constitutional duties, but the chapter's claim that it is the only democracy with duties is too absolute; distinguish a named consolidated list from duties elsewhere in constitutions.

2. All eleven duties: clause-wise recall

  • (a) Constitution and symbols: abide by Constitution, respect ideals/institutions, National Flag and Anthem.
  • (b) Freedom struggle: cherish/follow the noble ideals inspiring national struggle.
  • (c) Nation: uphold/protect sovereignty, unity, integrity.
  • (d) Defence: defend country and render national service when called upon.
  • (e) Harmony and women: common brotherhood across religious, linguistic, regional/sectional diversity; renounce practices derogatory to women's dignity.
  • (f) Culture: value/preserve rich heritage of composite culture.
  • (g) Environment: protect/improve forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife; compassion for living creatures.
  • (h) Reason and reform: scientific temper, humanism, inquiry and reform.
  • (i) Public property: safeguard it and abjure violence.
  • (j) Excellence: strive in individual/collective activities so nation rises in endeavour/achievement.
  • (k) Education: citizen parent/guardian provides educational opportunities to child/ward 6–14. Added 86th, alongside 21A and revised 45.
  • Recall groups: Constitution–struggle–nation–defence; harmony–culture; environment–reason; property–excellence–education.

3. Nature and legal effect

  • Applies to citizens, unlike some Rights that extend to persons/foreigners. Ordinary laws can still impose obligations on non-citizens.
  • Moral and civic: freedom-struggle ideals are a moral example; constitutional/symbol respect a civic one; categories overlap.
  • Reflect civic values, constitutional unity, dignity and inherited cultural/environmental responsibilities; not a licence to impose one religious interpretation on everyone.
  • Not independently justiciable: no automatic constitutional punishment for every breach. Existing or valid new legislation may enforce particular obligations with penalties.
  • Duties can guide interpretation/reasonableness; they do not automatically override Rights or exempt laws from judicial review. Legislative competence and constitutional limits still apply.
  • 21A: State's educational right obligation; 45: State's early-childhood policy goal; 51A(k): parent's/guardian's duty—different duty-bearers and age bands.

4. Criticisms and balanced assessment

  • Incomplete: voting, tax payment, family planning absent from express list. Their absence does not nullify separately enacted legal obligations.
  • Vague: noble ideals/composite culture/scientific temper can invite differing interpretations; D.D. Chawla called for clearer, accessible wording.
  • Non-justiciable: criticised as moral precepts without direct sanctions. Response: educational/interpretive value and specific implementing laws.
  • Superfluous/paternalistic: C.K. Daphtary/A.K. Sen questioned formal instruction to citizens and State distrust; these were political opinions, not constitutional rulings.
  • Placement: critics preferred adjacency to Part III, rather than after Part IV. Their actual location is a separate Part IVA, not merely provisions within Part IV.
  • Emergency-era inclusion should be assessed alongside later retention and broad civic purposes.

5. Significance and judicial use

  • Remind citizens that public life involves responsibility alongside rights; encourage active participation, discipline, solidarity, environmental care and excellence.
  • Oppose destruction of public property, violence and insults covered by lawful legislation; avoid treating every dissenting act as anti-national.
  • Mohan Kumar Singhania 1991: duties assist interpretation of ambiguous law. Ramlila Maidan 2012: read III, IV, IVA together when assessing rights, governance and duties.
  • H.R. Gokhale/Indira Gandhi: defended duties as promoting legal-order respect and democratic balance; retain these as historical justifications, not a rule subordinating liberty.
  • Janata Government retained duties while undoing several Emergency amendments through 43rd 1977/44th 1978. Addition in 2002 reinforces their continuing constitutional role.

6. Verma Committee 1999: existing legal support

  • Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act 1971: Constitution, Flag, Anthem protections.
  • Enmity/national-integration offences: historical IPC, now relevant BNS provisions since July 2024, subject to savings and offence-specific elements.
  • Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955: untouchability-related disabilities; renamed from Untouchability (Offences) Act in 1976. It is not a general punishment for every religious disagreement.
  • UAPA 1967: prescribed unlawful-association framework; Representation of the People Act 1951: prohibited electoral appeals/corrupt practices and disqualification machinery under law.
  • Wildlife Protection Act 1972: regulated/prohibited wildlife trade; Forest Conservation Act 1980, later renamed Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam: conservation/diversion control.
  • Committee identified legal support already available; 1999 did not insert the duties, and no single Act automatically criminalises all eleven.

7. One-minute recall and mains structure

  • 42nd → ten; 86th → eleven; Part IVA → 51A; Swaran Singh → introduction; Verma → legal support.
  • (e) women/harmony; (g) environment/compassion; (h) science/humanism; (k) education 6–14.
  • Mains: origin → citizenship responsibilities → examples/laws → non-justiciability and rights safeguards → education/public participation rather than indiscriminate penalties.
