Amendments Summary

Constitutional amendments mentioned in the completed Polity chapters and common Articles Summary. Each entry brings together the changes across chapters. Years are Amendment Act title years; later commencement, repeal and judicial limits are stated where relevant.

Amendment 1 — 1951

  • Article 15(4): enabled special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes and SCs/STs.
  • Article 19(2): recast reasonable speech restrictions; added friendly relations with foreign States, public order and incitement to an offence.
  • Article 19(6): protected State monopolies in trade/business.
  • Inserted Articles 31A and 31B and the Ninth Schedule, initially covering 13 laws, to protect specified reform legislation. Ninth-Schedule placement is subject to later basic-structure review.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights; 09. Directive Principles of State Policy; 04. Salient features of the Constituion

Amendment 4 — 1955

  • Article 31: restricted challenges based on inadequacy of acquisition compensation and distinguished acquisition from other property restrictions; this former provision was later omitted by Amendment 44.
  • Expanded Article 31A protection beyond estate acquisition to specified property/economic restructuring laws; added further laws to the Ninth Schedule.
  • Article 305: protected State-monopoly laws against the Article 301 freedom-of-trade objection.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights; 09. Directive Principles of State Policy

4th Amendment Act

Amendment 7 — 1956

  • Replaced Part A/B/C/D State classifications with States and Union territories; accompanying reorganisation produced 14 States and 6 UTs on 1 November 1956.
  • Omitted Part VII/Article 238 and original Part IX/Article 242; reorganised First and Fourth Schedules.
  • Inserted Article 258A: a State may entrust its executive functions to the Union with Union consent.
  • Omitted former Part B State provisions: Articles 259 (armed forces), 278 (financial agreements) and 306 (trade restrictions).
  • Reworked High Court arrangements, including provision for a common High Court for two or more States, or States and a UT.

Related chapters: 15. Center-State Relations; 06. Union and its Territory; 08. Fundamental Rights; 04. Salient features of the Constituion; Articles Summary

Amendment 9 — 1960

  • Amended the First Schedule to enable territorial transfers under India–Pakistan agreements, including the Berubari settlement; foreign cession required constitutional amendment.

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory

Amendment 10 — 1961

  • Added Dadra and Nagar Haveli as a Union territory in the First Schedule; adjusted Article 240 administration provisions.

Related chapters: 15. Center-State Relations; 06. Union and its Territory

Amendment 11 — 1961

  • Article 66: replaced election of the Vice-President at a joint meeting with an electoral college of members of both Houses of Parliament, including nominated members.
  • Article 71: vacancies in the relevant electoral college cannot alone invalidate a President/Vice-President election.

Related chapters: 19. Vice-President

Amendment 12 — 1962

  • Added Goa, Daman and Diu as a Union territory; adjusted the First Schedule and Article 240.

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory

Amendment 14 — 1962

  • Incorporated Puducherry, then Pondicherry, as a Union territory; amended the First Schedule and Article 240.
  • Inserted Article 239A: Parliament could provide legislatures and/or Councils of Ministers for specified UTs—Himachal Pradesh, Manipur, Tripura, Goa/Daman/Diu and Puducherry.
  • Article 81: increased the maximum Lok Sabha representation of UTs from 20 to 25; the Fourth Schedule allocated one Rajya Sabha seat to Puducherry.
  • Article 239A laws use the ordinary legislative process, not Article 368; Article 240 limited presidential regulation-making once the specified UT legislature began functioning.

Related chapters: 15. Center-State Relations; 06. Union and its Territory

14th Amendment Act

Amendment 16 — 1963

  • Added sovereignty and integrity of India as a restriction ground under Article 19 for speech, assembly and association.
  • Amended constitutional oaths to include upholding India's sovereignty and integrity.

Related chapters: 15. Center-State Relations; 06. Union and its Territory; 08. Fundamental Rights

Amendment 17 — 1964

  • Expanded Article 31A's definition of estate, including ryotwari and other agricultural land.
  • Protected personally cultivated land within the ceiling limit, and associated buildings/structures, against acquisition without at least market-value compensation.
  • Added further land-reform laws to the Ninth Schedule.

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory; 08. Fundamental Rights; 09. Directive Principles of State Policy

Amendment 18 — 1966

  • Clarified Article 3: State includes a UT for the substantive territorial-reorganisation powers, but not for the proviso requiring reference to a State legislature.
  • Clarified Parliament's power to form a new State or UT by combining parts of States/UTs.

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory; 23. Parliament

Amendment 21 — 1967

  • Added Sindhi to the Eighth Schedule: scheduled languages increased from 14 to 15.

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory; 04. Salient features of the Constituion

Amendment 22 — 1969

  • Inserted Article 244A: Parliament may form an autonomous State within Assam with a legislature and/or Council of Ministers; enabled the autonomous Meghalaya arrangement preceding full statehood.

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory

Amendment 24 — 1971

  • Article 368: expressly affirmed Parliament's constituent power to amend the Constitution by addition, variation or repeal.
  • Inserted Article 13(4) and Article 368(3), excluding constitutional amendments from Article 13's ordinary-law rule.
  • Made presidential assent mandatory for a duly passed constitutional-amendment Bill.
  • Later judicial limit: Kesavananda Bharati (1973) upheld the amendment but established that amendment cannot damage the basic structure.

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory; 09. Directive Principles of State Policy; 11. Amendment of the Constitution; 12. Basic Structure of the Constitution; 18. President

Amendment 25 — 1971

  • Former Article 31: replaced compensation with amount in compulsory acquisition; this provision was later omitted by Amendment 44.
  • Inserted Article 31C: protected laws genuinely implementing Article 39(b)/(c) against specified Fundamental Rights challenges—originally Articles 14, 19 and 31; the surviving shield concerns Articles 14 and 19.
  • The attempted exclusion of judicial inquiry into a law's connection with Article 39(b)/(c) was invalidated in Kesavananda Bharati (1973).

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory; 08. Fundamental Rights; 09. Directive Principles of State Policy

Amendment 26 — 1971

  • Abolished recognition of former rulers and privy purses: inserted Article 363A and omitted Articles 291 and 362.

Related chapters: Articles Summary

Amendment 35 — 1974

  • Gave Sikkim associate-State status through Article 2A and an original Tenth Schedule specifying its association with India.
  • This original Tenth Schedule was subsequently repealed; it was distinct from today's anti-defection Schedule.

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory; Articles Summary

Amendment 36 — 1975

  • Made Sikkim a full State, India's 22nd; amended the First/Fourth Schedules and inserted Article 371F special provisions.
  • Repealed Article 2A and the original Tenth Schedule introduced by Amendment 35.

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory; Articles Summary

Amendment 38 — 1975

  • Allowed multiple National Emergency proclamations on different grounds to coexist.
  • Made presidential satisfaction for National Emergency, President's Rule and Financial Emergency final/conclusive and purportedly beyond judicial challenge.
  • Similarly insulated satisfaction underlying ordinances by the President, Governors and UT administrators.
  • Later status: Amendment 44 removed these satisfaction-immunity provisions; multiple-proclamation power remains.

Related chapters: 17. Emergency Provisions; 18. President

Amendment 39 — 1975

  • Inserted Article 329A concerning elections of the Prime Minister and Lok Sabha Speaker; changed Article 71 arrangements for President/Vice-President election disputes.
  • Added further laws to the Ninth Schedule.
  • Later status: Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975) invalidated Article 329A(4), which insulated/validated the Prime Minister's election; Amendment 44 omitted Article 329A. The entire 39th Amendment was not struck down.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights; 12. Basic Structure of the Constitution

Amendment 40 — 1976

  • Substituted Article 297: specified offshore lands, minerals and resources in territorial waters, the continental shelf and exclusive economic zone vest in the Union; Parliament determines maritime limits.
  • Added further laws to the Ninth Schedule, including land-reform and economic-control legislation.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights

40th Amendment Act

Amendment 42 — 1976

  • Preamble: inserted Socialist and Secular; changed unity of the Nation to unity and integrity of the Nation.
  • Inserted Part IVA/Article 51A: ten Fundamental Duties, following the Swaran Singh Committee.
  • DPSPs: revised Article 39(f) on child development; inserted 39A (equal justice/free legal aid), 43A (workers' management participation) and 48A (environment/forests/wildlife).
  • Article 74: expressly made the Council of Ministers' advice binding on the President.
  • Transferred five subjects from State to Concurrent List: education; forests; weights and measures; protection of wild animals/birds; administration of justice and organisation of courts other than SC/HCs.
  • Inserted Part XIVA: Article 323A administrative tribunals and 323B tribunals for other specified matters.
  • Expanded Article 31C protection from Article 39(b)/(c) to all DPSPs. This expansion was struck down in Minerva Mills (1980).
  • Inserted Article 368(4)/(5), attempting to exclude amendment review and confer unlimited constituent power; both were struck down in Minerva Mills (1980).
  • Curtailed judicial review through provisions including 32A, 131A, 144A, 226A and 228A; these curtailments were reversed by Amendment 43.
  • Inserted Article 31D protection for anti-national-activity legislation; repealed by Amendment 43.
  • Extended Lok Sabha and State Assembly terms from five to six years; Amendment 44 restored five years.
  • Froze census-based seat allocation using the 1971 population base until the first census after 2000; Amendment 84 later extended the freeze.
  • Allowed National Emergency to cover a specified part of India; changed President's Rule approval/continuation arrangements, later revised by Amendment 44.
  • Inserted Article 257A for Union deployment of armed/other forces in States; omitted by Amendment 44.

Related chapters: 17. Emergency Provisions; 05. Preamble of the Constitution; 10. Fundamental Duties; 15. Center-State Relations; 13. Parliamentary System; 08. Fundamental Rights; 09. Directive Principles of State Policy; 23. Parliament; 12. Basic Structure of the Constitution; 21. Central Council of Ministers; 04. Salient features of the Constituion; 18. President; Articles Summary

42nd Amendment Act

Amendment 43 — 1977

  • Restored judicial-review powers curtailed by Amendment 42: omitted Articles 32A, 131A, 144A, 226A and 228A and reversed associated changes.
  • Repealed Article 31D's special protection for anti-national-activity laws.
  • Retained Part IVA/Fundamental Duties.

Related chapters: 10. Fundamental Duties

Amendment 44 — 1978

  • Removed property from Fundamental Rights: omitted Article 19(1)(f) and Article 31; inserted Article 300A as a constitutional legal-right protection.
  • Inserted Article 30(1A): acquisition of minority educational-institution property must not restrict or abrogate the protected institutional right.
  • Inserted Article 38(2): minimise inequalities in income, status, facilities and opportunities among individuals and groups.
  • Article 74: President may require reconsideration once, then must act on the reconsidered ministerial advice.
  • National Emergency: replaced internal disturbance with armed rebellion; required the Cabinet's decision to be communicated in writing and defined Cabinet in Article 352(3).
  • Article 352: approval within one month, by total-membership majority plus at least two-thirds present and voting in each House; renewal every six months with that majority, subject to dissolved-Lok-Sabha provisions.
  • Strengthened revocation/disapproval: President must revoke after Lok Sabha disapproval; one-tenth of LS members may trigger a special sitting within 14 days.
  • Article 358: automatic Article 19 relaxation only for war/external-aggression Emergency; emergency-related-law recital and connected executive-action safeguards apply.
  • Article 359: Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended through a presidential enforcement-suspension order; introduced emergency-related-law safeguards.
  • Removed Amendment 38's immunity for satisfaction underlying emergency proclamations and ordinances; judicial review is available.
  • Article 356: restored six-month continuation periods; continuation beyond one year requires the relevant Emergency and Election Commission certification, subject to the ordinary three-year maximum and historical special exceptions.
  • Restored ordinary Lok Sabha/State Assembly terms to five years; omitted Article 257A and Article 329A.
  • Inserted Article 361A: protected substantially true reports of legislative proceedings, excluding malicious publication and secret sittings.
  • Articles 105/194: replaced direct reference to House of Commons privileges with privileges existing immediately before the relevant change took effect on 20 June 1979.
  • Article 22: enacted a two-month preventive-detention threshold and altered Advisory Board safeguards, but the relevant amendment provision was not commenced; the operative constitutional threshold remains three months.

Related chapters: 17. Emergency Provisions; 10. Fundamental Duties; 13. Parliamentary System; 08. Fundamental Rights; 09. Directive Principles of State Policy; 23. Parliament; 21. Central Council of Ministers; 04. Salient features of the Constituion; 18. President; Articles Summary

44th Amendment Act

Amendment 52 — 1985

  • Inserted the Tenth Schedule and amended Articles 102/191: anti-defection disqualification for MPs and State legislators.
  • Originally included a one-third split exemption; Amendment 91 later removed it. The two-thirds merger exception remains.
  • Later judicial limit: Kihoto Hollohan (1992) upheld the principal scheme but invalidated paragraph 7's judicial-jurisdiction exclusion for want of required State ratification; Speaker/Chairman decisions are reviewable.

Related chapters: 04. Salient features of the Constituion

Amendment 58 — 1987

  • Inserted Article 394A in Part XXII: authorised presidential publication of the Constitution's Hindi translation and Hindi translations of constitutional amendments.
  • Texts published under Article 394A are authoritative in Hindi for all purposes; this does not declare Hindi a national language.

Related chapters: 02. Making of the Constitution; Articles Summary

Amendment 60 — 1988

  • Article 276: raised the annual professions/trades/callings/employment tax ceiling per person from ₹250 to ₹2,500.

Related chapters: 15. Center-State Relations

Amendment 61 — 1988

  • Article 326: reduced the voting age for Lok Sabha/State Assembly elections from 21 to 18; operative in 1989.

Related chapters: 23. Parliament; 04. Salient features of the Constituion

Amendment 63 — 1989

  • Repealed Article 359A, removing the special Punjab emergency regime; also reversed the associated special Article 356 provision introduced by Amendment 59.

Related chapters: Articles Summary

Amendment 65 — 1990

  • Article 338: replaced the Special Officer arrangement with a constitutional National Commission for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, with investigation, monitoring, advisory and reporting functions.

Related chapters: 09. Directive Principles of State Policy

Amendment 68 — 1991

  • Article 356: raised the special maximum duration for the Punjab President's Rule proclamation of 11 May 1987 from four to five years; this was a historical exception, not the general present rule.

Related chapters: 17. Emergency Provisions

68th Amendment Act

Amendment 69 — 1991

  • Inserted Articles 239AA/239AB: designated Delhi the National Capital Territory, with a Legislative Assembly and Council of Ministers; operational in 1992.
  • Assembly legislative power excludes public order, police and land; Parliament retains power, and Delhi did not become a full State.

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory

Amendment 70 — 1992

  • Articles 54/55: included elected MLAs of Delhi and Puducherry in the President's electoral college; operative from 1 June 1995.
  • Does not automatically include every Union territory with a legislature.

Related chapters: 18. President

Amendment 71 — 1992

  • Added Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali to the Eighth Schedule: scheduled languages increased from 15 to 18.

Related chapters: 04. Salient features of the Constituion

Amendment 73 — 1992

  • Inserted Part IX, Articles 243–243O, giving Panchayats constitutional status; operative from 24 April 1993.
  • Inserted the Eleventh Schedule: 29 subjects for possible devolution through State law.
  • Established the constitutional framework for village/intermediate/district Panchayats with exceptions, five-year terms, SC/ST and at least one-third women's reservation, State Election Commissions and five-yearly State Finance Commissions.
  • Added Article 280(3)(bb): Finance Commission recommendations to augment State funds supporting Panchayat resources.

Related chapters: 09. Directive Principles of State Policy; 04. Salient features of the Constituion

Amendment 74 — 1992

  • Inserted Part IXA, Articles 243P–243ZG, giving municipalities constitutional status; operative from 1 June 1993.
  • Inserted the Twelfth Schedule: 18 subjects for possible devolution through State law.
  • Provided the Nagar Panchayat/Municipal Council/Municipal Corporation framework, five-year terms, SC/ST and at least one-third women's reservation, election/finance safeguards, and district/metropolitan planning arrangements, with constitutional exceptions.
  • Added Article 280(3)(c): Finance Commission recommendations to augment State funds supporting municipal resources.

Related chapters: 15. Center-State Relations; 04. Salient features of the Constituion

Amendment 76 — 1994

  • Placed Tamil Nadu's 69% reservation law in the Ninth Schedule.
  • Later judicial limit: Ninth-Schedule placement does not confer absolute immunity; post-24 April 1973 insertions remain subject to basic-structure review.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights

Amendment 77 — 1995

  • Inserted Article 16(4A): enabled reservation in promotion for inadequately represented SCs/STs, subject to constitutional requirements.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights

Amendment 80 — 2000

  • Reworked Articles 269/270 and omitted Article 272: expanded the divisible pool of Union taxes shared with States.
  • Implemented the alternative devolution scheme linked to the Tenth Finance Commission, retrospectively from 1 April 1996; its historical 29% figure is not today's devolution rate.

Related chapters: 15. Center-State Relations; Articles Summary

Amendment 81 — 2000

  • Inserted Article 16(4B): allowed unfilled reserved vacancies to be treated as a separate backlog class, excluded from the current year's vacancies when applying the 50% reservation ceiling.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights

Amendment 82 — 2000

  • Added a proviso to Article 335: enabled relaxation of qualifying marks or evaluation standards for SC/ST promotion reservation.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights

Amendment 84 — 2001

  • Extended the freeze on census-based seat allocation until publication of the first census figures taken after 2026; retained the 1971 population base for inter-State Lok Sabha allocation and the President's electoral-college calculation.
  • Permitted constituency readjustment and reserved-seat revision using the 1991 census without changing the frozen allocation; Amendment 87 later substituted the 2001 base.
  • Does not automatically end the freeze on 1 January 2027.

Related chapters: 23. Parliament; 18. President

Amendment 85 — 2001

  • Article 16(4A): added consequential seniority to SC/ST promotion reservation, retrospectively from 17 June 1995.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights

Amendment 86 — 2002

  • Inserted Article 21A: State must provide free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14 as determined by law.
  • Replaced Article 45 with the early-childhood-care/education goal for children below six.
  • Added Article 51A(k): parent/guardian duty to provide educational opportunity to a child/ward aged 6–14.
  • Relevant provisions commenced on 1 April 2010 alongside the RTE framework; distinguish Act year 2002 from commencement year 2010.

Related chapters: 10. Fundamental Duties; 08. Fundamental Rights; 09. Directive Principles of State Policy; 04. Salient features of the Constituion; Articles Summary

Amendment 87 — 2003

  • Substituted the 2001 census for the 1991 census in constituency delimitation and SC/ST reserved-seat readjustment under Articles 81, 82, 170 and 330.
  • Retained the freeze on inter-State Lok Sabha seat allocation; boundary readjustment is different from reallocating seats among States.

Related chapters: 23. Parliament

Amendment 88 — 2003

  • Provided for Article 268A on service-tax levy/distribution, Union List Entry 92C and a consequential Article 270 change.
  • Important status: this amendment was not brought into force; the 101st Amendment later omitted the service-tax provisions. Do not describe it as an operative service-tax devolution scheme.

Related chapters: 15. Center-State Relations; Articles Summary

Supreme Court: commencement status

Amendment 89 — 2003

  • Separated the combined SC/ST Commission: Article 338 for the National Commission for Scheduled Castes and new Article 338A for the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes; separation became operative in 2004.

Related chapters: 09. Directive Principles of State Policy

Amendment 91 — 2003

  • Article 75(1A): Union Council of Ministers, including PM, capped at 15% of total Lok Sabha membership.
  • Article 164(1A): State Council of Ministers, including CM, capped at 15% of Assembly strength, with a minimum of 12 ministers; the Union has no corresponding minimum of 12.
  • Articles 75(1B)/164(1B) and 361B: barred defectors from ministerial and specified remunerative political posts for the constitutionally specified period.
  • Removed the Tenth Schedule's one-third split exemption; retained the two-thirds merger exception.

Related chapters: 21. Central Council of Ministers

Amendment 92 — 2003

  • Added Bodo, Dogri, Maithili and Santali to the Eighth Schedule: scheduled languages increased from 18 to 22; assent/operation in 2004.

Related chapters: 04. Salient features of the Constituion

Amendment 93 — 2005

  • Inserted Article 15(5): enabled special admission provisions for socially and educationally backward classes and SCs/STs, including private aided/unaided institutions.
  • Excluded Article 30(1) minority educational institutions; the Amendment Act's title year is 2005, although assent was in 2006.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights

Amendment 95 — 2009

  • Article 334: extended SC/ST legislative-seat reservation and Anglo-Indian nomination provisions from 60 to 70 years after constitutional commencement, carrying them to 2020.
  • Amendment 104 subsequently extended SC/ST reservation but did not extend Anglo-Indian nomination.

Related chapters: 23. Parliament

Amendment 96 — 2011

  • Changed Oriya to Odia in the Eighth Schedule; language count remained 22.

Related chapters: 04. Salient features of the Constituion

Amendment 97 — 2011

  • Article 19(1)(c): added the right to form cooperative societies.
  • Inserted Article 43B: promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of cooperatives.
  • Inserted Part IXB, Articles 243ZH–243ZT: constitutional governance framework for cooperative societies; operative from 15 February 2012.
  • Later judicial limit: Rajendra N. Shah (2021) invalidated Part IXB as applied to State-only cooperatives for lack of required State ratification; the multi-State/UT application and Articles 19(1)(c)/43B survive.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights; 09. Directive Principles of State Policy; 11. Amendment of the Constitution; 04. Salient features of the Constituion

Supreme Court: surviving provisions

Amendment 100 — 2015

  • Amended First-Schedule territorial descriptions for Assam, West Bengal, Meghalaya and Tripura to implement the India–Bangladesh 1974 Land Boundary Agreement and 2011 Protocol.
  • Enabled acquisition/transfer of territory, including exchange of 111 Indian enclaves in Bangladesh for 51 Bangladeshi enclaves in India.

Related chapters: 06. Union and its Territory

100th Amendment Act

Amendment 101 — 2016

  • Inserted Article 246A: special Union/State GST legislative powers; Parliament has exclusive power over inter-State supplies.
  • Inserted Article 269A: Union levy/collection and Union–State apportionment of inter-State GST; imports are treated as inter-State supplies.
  • Inserted Article 279A: GST Council, with recommendations on the common GST framework.
  • Changed related tax provisions and Seventh-Schedule entries; omitted the former Article 268A and Union Entry 92C service-tax provisions.
  • Added Article 279A to Article 368's State-ratification proviso. Constitutional framework enacted in 2016; GST rollout began on 1 July 2017.

Related chapters: 15. Center-State Relations; 11. Amendment of the Constitution; 04. Salient features of the Constituion; Articles Summary

Amendment 102 — 2018

  • Inserted Article 338B: gave the National Commission for Backward Classes constitutional status.
  • Inserted Article 342A on specification of socially and educationally backward classes and Article 366(26C) defining them.
  • The State-list issue arising from this framework was subsequently addressed by Amendment 105.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights; 09. Directive Principles of State Policy

Amendment 103 — 2019

  • Inserted Articles 15(6)/16(6): enabled special provisions and up to 10% additional reservation for economically weaker sections outside the classes covered by the existing specified backward-class/SC/ST provisions.
  • Article 15(6) covers admissions, including private aided/unaided institutions, excluding Article 30(1) minority institutions; Article 16(6) covers public employment.
  • Later judicial status: Janhit Abhiyan (2022) upheld the amendment by a 3:2 majority.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights; 09. Directive Principles of State Policy

Supreme Court: EWS ruling

Amendment 104 — 2019

  • Article 334: extended SC/ST reservation in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies to 80 years after commencement of the Constitution, i.e. 2030.
  • Did not extend Anglo-Indian nomination provisions beyond 70 years; that representation ceased on 25 January 2020. Act title year is 2019; commencement was in 2020.

Related chapters: 23. Parliament; 18. President

Amendment 105 — 2021

  • Restored/clarified States' and UTs' power to prepare and maintain their own lists of socially and educationally backward classes for their own purposes, which may differ from the Central List.
  • Amended Articles 338B, 342A and 366(26C); distinguished the Central List from State/UT lists and adjusted the NCBC consultation requirement accordingly.

Related chapters: 08. Fundamental Rights

Amendment 106 — 2023

  • Inserted Articles 330A/332A: reserve as nearly as possible one-third of Lok Sabha/State Assembly seats for women, including one-third within SC/ST reserved seats.
  • Amended Article 239AA to provide corresponding women's reservation in the Delhi Assembly; does not cover Rajya Sabha or Legislative Councils.
  • Inserted Article 334A: implementation follows delimitation after publication of the relevant first post-amendment census figures; the insertion did not immediately create reserved-seat elections.
  • Article 334A provides a 15-year reservation period from commencement of its operation, continuation as Parliament determines by law, rotation after subsequent delimitation as Parliament determines, and protection of existing Houses until dissolution.

Related chapters: 23. Parliament; 04. Salient features of the Constituion; Articles Summary
