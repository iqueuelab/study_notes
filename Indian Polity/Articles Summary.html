Articles Summary

Part I — The Union and its Territory

  • 1: India, that is Bharat, is a Union of States; name and territory.
  • 2: Admission or establishment of new States on terms Parliament thinks fit.
  • 2A (repealed): Sikkim’s association with the Union; inserted by 35th Amendment (1974), repealed by 36th (1975).
  • 3: Formation of States and alteration of areas, boundaries or names.
  • 4: Article 2/3 laws may amend First/Fourth Schedules and contain supplemental, incidental and consequential provisions; not Article 368 amendments.

Part II — Citizenship

  • 5: Citizenship at commencement: domicile plus prescribed birth/parentage/residence conditions.
  • 6: Citizenship of qualifying migrants from Pakistan to India.
  • 7: Migrants to Pakistan; specified permanent-return permit exception.
  • 8: Qualifying Indian-origin persons residing abroad; consular/diplomatic registration.
  • 9: Voluntary foreign citizenship excludes the specified commencement citizenship claims.
  • 10: Continuance of citizenship subject to parliamentary law.
  • 11: Parliament regulates citizenship by law.

Part III — Fundamental Rights

  • 12: Definition of State for Part III.
  • 13: Laws inconsistent with or derogating from Fundamental Rights.
  • 14: Equality before law and equal protection of laws.
  • 15: Prohibition of specified discrimination, with constitutional exceptions/protective provisions.
  • 16: Equality of opportunity in public employment, with constitutional qualifications.
  • 17: Abolition of untouchability.
  • 18: Abolition of titles; military/academic distinctions excepted; other constitutional conditions apply.
  • 19: Specified freedoms of citizens, subject to constitutionally permitted restrictions.
  • 20: Protection against retrospective penal conviction/heavier punishment, double jeopardy and compelled self-incrimination.
  • 21: Protection of life and personal liberty.
  • 21A: Free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14.
  • 22: Arrest/detention safeguards; distinct ordinary and preventive regimes.
  • 23: Prohibition of trafficking, begar and similar forced labour; public-service exception.
  • 24: Under-14 prohibition in factories/mines/hazardous employment.
  • 25: Freedom of conscience and profession, practice and propagation of religion, subject to constitutional limits.
  • 26: Religious denominations' affairs/institutions/property, subject to constitutional limits.
  • 27: No compulsory taxes specifically appropriated to promote/maintain a particular religion or denomination.
  • 28: Religious instruction/worship rules for educational institutions; distinguish wholly State-funded institutions, trust/endowment exceptions and consent requirements.
  • 29: Distinct language/script/culture of sections of citizens and specified educational-admission protection.
  • 30: Religious/linguistic minorities' right to establish/administer educational institutions.
  • 31 (repealed): Former compulsory-acquisition/property provision; omitted by 44th Amendment.
  • 31A: Specified estate/property/corporate/mining laws; limited 14/19 protection.
  • 31B: Ninth-Schedule validation, subject to basic-structure judicial review.
  • 31C: Qualifying 39(b)/(c) implementing laws; limited 14/19 protection, not all DPSPs.
  • 31D (repealed): Former anti-national-activities protective clause; inserted 42nd, omitted 43rd.
  • 32: Supreme Court remedies for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
  • 32A (repealed): Former restriction on considering State-law validity in Article 32 proceedings; inserted 42nd, omitted 43rd.
  • 33: Parliament may modify rights for constitutionally specified forces/services.
  • 34: Parliamentary indemnity/validation for prescribed martial-law acts.
  • 35: Parliament-exclusive legislation for specified Part III matters/offence punishments.

Part IV — Directive Principles of State Policy

  • 36: State in Part IV has the Part III meaning, unless context otherwise requires.
  • 37: DPSPs not enforceable by courts; fundamental in governance; State lawmaking duty.
  • 38: Welfare-oriented social order; minimise inequalities.
  • 39: Livelihood, resources/common good, wealth concentration, equal pay, worker/child protection.
  • 39(a): Adequate means of livelihood for men and women equally.
  • 39(d): Equal pay for equal work for men and women.
  • 39A: Equal justice and free legal aid.
  • 40: Organisation of village Panchayats.
  • 41: Work, education and public assistance within economic capacity/development.
  • 42: Humane working conditions and maternity relief.
  • 43: Living wage, decent life/leisure and rural cottage industries.
  • 43A: Worker participation in industrial management.
  • 43B: Voluntary, autonomous, democratic, professional cooperatives.
  • 44: Uniform Civil Code for citizens.
  • 45: Early childhood care and education for children below six.
  • 46: Educational/economic interests of weaker sections, particularly SCs/STs; protection from social injustice/exploitation.
  • 47: Nutrition/living standards/public health; harmful-intoxicant prohibition except medicinal purposes.
  • 48: Scientific agriculture/animal husbandry; specified cattle preservation/breed improvement.
  • 48A: Environment, forests and wildlife protection.
  • 49: Protection of nationally important monuments, places and objects.
  • 50: Separation of judiciary from executive in State public services.
  • 51: International peace/security, just relations, respect for international law and arbitration.

Part IVA — Fundamental Duties

  • 51A: Eleven Fundamental Duties of citizens.
  • 51A(a): Constitution, ideals/institutions, National Flag and Anthem.
  • 51A(b): Freedom-struggle ideals.
  • 51A(c): Uphold/protect India's sovereignty, unity and integrity.
  • 51A(d): National defence/service when called upon.
  • 51A(e): Harmony/common brotherhood across differences; renounce practices derogatory to women's dignity.
  • 51A(f): Composite cultural heritage.
  • 51A(g): Environment and compassion for living creatures.
  • 51A(h): Scientific temper, humanism, inquiry and reform.
  • 51A(i): Public property and rejection of violence.
  • 51A(j): Individual/collective excellence.
  • 51A(k): Parent/guardian educational opportunities for child/ward aged 6–14; 86th Amendment.

Part V — The Union

  • 52: There shall be a President of India.
  • 53: Union executive power vested in President; constitutional exercise and supreme command of defence forces.
  • 54: Presidential electoral college: elected MPs and specified elected Assembly members.
  • 55: Presidential election manner, weighted representation and secret-ballot transferable voting.
  • 56: Presidential term, resignation to Vice-President, removal and holdover until successor assumes office.
  • 57: Eligibility for presidential re-election.
  • 58: Presidential qualifications: citizenship, age 35, Lok Sabha eligibility and office-of-profit conditions.
  • 59: Conditions of presidential office, legislative-seat vacancy, emoluments and allowances.
  • 60: Presidential oath/affirmation before CJI or senior-most available SC judge.
  • 61: Impeachment for constitutional violation; notice, investigation and two-thirds total-membership thresholds.
  • 62: Presidential vacancy-election timing; casual-vacancy successor has full term.
  • 63: There shall be a Vice-President of India.
  • 64: Vice-President is ex-officio Rajya Sabha Chairman; does not perform that role while acting/discharging President.
  • 65: Vice-President acting as President or discharging presidential functions during vacancy/incapacity.
  • 66: Vice-Presidential election by members of both parliamentary Houses; qualifications and conditions.
  • 67: VP term, resignation, removal by RS then-member majority agreed by LS, notice and holdover.
  • 68: VP term-end/casual-vacancy election timing; casual-vacancy successor has full term.
  • 69: VP oath/affirmation before President or appointed person.
  • 70: Parliamentary provision for presidential-function discharge in other contingencies.
  • 71: SC determination of President/VP election disputes; vacancy and prior-act protections.
  • 72: Presidential clemency in specified Union-executive, court-martial and death-sentence cases.
  • 74: PM-headed Council advises President; reconsideration once, renewed advice binding; advice-inquiry bar.
  • 75: Appointment, tenure, oaths and salaries of ministers; collective responsibility to Lok Sabha; six-month membership rule; total including PM capped at 15% of Lok Sabha strength under 75(1A). Under 75(1B), defection-disqualified members are barred from ministership until the original term ends or, on an earlier contested election, they are declared elected, whichever is earlier.
  • 76: Attorney-General appointment, qualifications, duties, audience rights and tenure.
  • 77: Union governmental business, presidential-name action, authentication and business allocation.
  • 78: PM duties to inform President and submit specified ministerial decisions to Council.
  • 79: Constitution of Parliament: President, Council of States and House of the People.
  • 80: Rajya Sabha composition: maximum 250, including 12 presidential nominees; elected representation of States/UTs; State elections by elected MLAs using PR-STV.
  • 81: Lok Sabha composition: up to 530 State and 20 UT representatives; territorial representation and population-ratio rules, with specified exceptions and census provisos.
  • 82: Readjustment of seats and constituencies after census by authority/procedure prescribed by Parliament; census-based freeze provisos apply.
  • 83: Rajya Sabha is not dissolved; about one-third retire every second year. Lok Sabha normally lasts five years from first meeting; National Emergency extensions by law limited to one year at a time and six months after Emergency ends.
  • 84: Parliamentary membership qualifications: citizenship, prescribed oath, age 30 for RS/25 for LS and qualifications prescribed by Parliament.
  • 85: Presidential summoning, prorogation and LS dissolution; maximum six months between last sitting of a session and first sitting of next.
  • 86: President may address either/both Houses assembled together and send messages; Houses must consider matters in messages with convenient dispatch.
  • 87: Special presidential address at first session after each general LS election and first session of each year.
  • 88: Ministers and Attorney-General may participate in either House, joint sittings and a committee of which named a member; Article itself confers no vote.
  • 89: Vice-President is ex-officio RS Chairman; RS elects Deputy Chairman from its members.
  • 90: Deputy Chairman vacation/resignation/removal; effective-majority removal after at least 14 days notice.
  • 91: RS Deputy Chairman or presidential appointee performs Chairman duties when offices vacant; rules/House arrange presiding during absence.
  • 92: RS Chairman/Deputy cannot preside during consideration of own removal. VP may speak/participate in own-removal proceedings but cannot vote.
  • 93: LS elects Speaker and Deputy Speaker from its members as soon as may be, filling vacancies as required.
  • 94: Speaker/Deputy vacation, resignation and effective-majority removal with 14-day notice; Speaker continues after dissolution until immediately before new LS first meeting.
  • 95: LS Deputy Speaker performs duties during Speaker vacancy; President appoints member if both offices vacant; rules/House arrange presiding during absence.
  • 96: Speaker/Deputy cannot preside while own removal considered; Speaker may speak and vote initially on own removal, but not cast tie-breaking vote.
  • 97: Salaries/allowances of RS Chairman/Deputy and LS Speaker/Deputy determined by Parliament.
  • 98: Parliamentary secretariats, including separate staff, possible common posts, and recruitment/service regulation.
  • 99: MP oath/affirmation before taking seat, before President or appointed person, in Third Schedule form.
  • 100: Voting ordinarily by majority present/voting; chair casts tie vote. Houses may act despite vacancies; quorum ordinarily one-tenth of membership.
  • 101: Vacation of parliamentary seats: incompatible memberships, disqualification, accepted voluntary/genuine resignation and specified unauthorised absence.
  • 102: Parliamentary membership disqualifications including office of profit, court-declared unsound mind, undischarged insolvency, citizenship/allegiance, statutory grounds and Tenth Schedule.
  • 103: President decides post-election questions under 102(1), obtaining and acting according to ECI opinion; distinct from presiding-officer defection decision.
  • 104: Penalty of ₹500 per day for sitting/voting before required oath or knowingly while unqualified/disqualified/prohibited.
  • 105: Parliamentary speech/vote and authorised-publication immunity; House/member/committee privileges; extends to authorised participants. Bribery is not protected by speech/vote immunity.
  • 106: MP salaries and allowances determined by Parliament; pension arises under statute.
  • 107: Introduction/passage of Bills subject to financial exceptions; prorogation does not lapse Bills; LS dissolution rules determine which pending Bills lapse.
  • 108: Joint sitting for rejection, final disagreement or delay over six months, excluding specified non-sitting periods; not for Money/constitutional amendment Bills.
  • 109: Money Bill procedure: LS first, RS recommendations within 14 days; LS decides acceptance; no return in time means deemed passage in LS form.
  • 110: Money Bill contains only specified financial/incidental matters; excludes merely fines, service/licence fees or local taxes; Speaker certifies, subject to constitutional judicial review.
  • 111: Presidential assent/withholding/one return of non-Money Bill; ordinary Bill repassed by both Houses must receive assent.
  • 112: Annual Financial Statement; estimated Union receipts/expenditure, charged/voted spending and revenue/other expenditure distinctions.
  • 113: Charged expenditure discussable but not voted; LS votes demands for grants on presidential recommendation, approving/refusing/reducing demand.
  • 114: Appropriation by law authorises CFI withdrawal; Bill includes voted grants and charged spending; specified amount/destination amendments barred.
  • 115: Supplementary, additional and excess expenditure statements and grants, subject to prescribed financial procedure.
  • 116: Votes on account, votes of credit and exceptional grants; appropriation requirements still apply.
  • 117: Financial Bill safeguards: 117(1) specified fiscal-plus-other-matter Bill introduced only in LS with recommendation; 117(3) CFI-expenditure Bill may start in either House but recommendation for consideration required before passage.
  • 118: Each House makes procedure/business rules; President makes joint-sitting rules after consultation; LS Speaker/Deputy or prescribed person presides.
  • 119: Parliament may regulate financial-business procedure by law for timely completion; such law prevails over inconsistent House rules.
  • 120: Hindi/English parliamentary business; chair may permit mother tongue; statutory continuation of English.
  • 121: SC/HC judge judicial conduct cannot be discussed except upon removal-address motion.
  • 122: No judicial challenge solely for parliamentary procedural irregularity; substantive illegality/unconstitutionality is not shielded.
  • 123: Presidential ordinances during parliamentary recess; urgency, laying, withdrawal and six-week reassembly expiry.
  • 136: Supreme Court special leave to appeal, subject to constitutional terms.
  • 141: Law declared by Supreme Court binding on all courts within India.
  • 142: Supreme Court complete-justice decrees/orders and associated powers, subject to constitutional terms.
  • 143: Supreme Court advisory-reference jurisdiction under specified constitutional conditions.
  • 148: Comptroller and Auditor-General: appointment, tenure/removal and independence safeguards.
  • 149: CAG duties and powers as prescribed by parliamentary law.
  • 150: Form of Union/State accounts prescribed by President on CAG advice.
  • 151: CAG audit reports laid before Parliament or State legislature through President/Governor.
