November 2025 editorial: a basic income floor

  • UBI: regular cash payment to individuals, universal and unconditional with respect to income/work. A payment limited to women/elderly/poor can be a phased or categorical basic-income programme but is not yet universal UBI. The editorial advocates UBI as a welfare foundation; it does not report an enacted nationwide Indian UBI.
  • Rationale: income security amid informal/gig work and automation; reduced poverty-targeting exclusion/stigma; bargaining power and autonomy, including for unpaid carers; predictable household demand. DBT/Jan Dhan/Aadhaar may support delivery but cannot eliminate authentication, banking, connectivity and grievance failures by definition.
  • Trade-offs: large recurring fiscal cost; universal gross transfers include wealthy recipients unless progressive taxes recover them; opportunity cost for health/education/nutrition; local price/supply effects; labour incentives and political durability. Cash should complement public services and essential entitlements; universal payment is not proof that exclusion disappears in delivery.
  • Source cites Madhya Pradesh SEWA pilots, 2011–13, and Finland, Kenya, Iran/Alaska as comparative experiences; designs, populations, funding and durations differ. Positive pilot results on wellbeing, schooling or work do not prove a national programme has no labour-supply effects or financing constraints.
  • ₹7,620/person/year/~5% GDP is a historical illustrative estimate, not a November 2025 universal cost calculation. Affordability must specify population coverage, price year, transfer amount and the spending/tax package. Source's mixed wealth-Gini/top-wealth shares, 8.4% growth and WHR 2023 rank do not form a single current dataset; do not present them as 2025 measurements.
  • Automation's “800 million jobs by 2030” is a scenario estimate, not a realised count or firm forecast for India. Inflation is not caused only by collapse/external debt: transfer scale, financing, supply response and local market concentration matter. Claims of no work disincentive likewise require empirical design/context.
  • Options: phased pilots, transparent evaluation, progressive financing, subsidy review with distributional safeguards, reliable bank access and grievance redressal; retain PDS/MGNREGA and essential services through transition. Weigh citizenship/dignity and resilience against fiscal sustainability and public-goods needs.
  • Answer-writing practice (not PYQ): critically examine UBI at the centre of welfare in light of inequality and technological disruption (250 words). Define universality; assess benefits/costs; propose phased evaluation and complementary public services. The source supplies no exam year.
