Strawberry cultivation and Mahabaleshwar GI

Editable notes

27 April 2025: horticulture and rural livelihoods

  • Meghalaya's Strawberry Festival was announced for 2–3 May; promotion of local fruit, farm tourism and growers' market access is the context, not an already completed festival.
  • Strawberry is a high-value, perishable horticultural crop rich in vitamin C and antioxidants. Promotional graphics claiming anti-cancer, antiviral or disease-curing effects are not clinical evidence.
  • Indian cultivation includes Mahabaleshwar–Panchgani (Maharashtra), Himachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Uttarakhand and Sikkim; Nagaland is described as emerging. Mahabaleshwar Strawberry's GI registration certificate dates to 3 September 2010. GI Registry.

Cultivation anchors

  • Temperate/subtropical conditions, suitable variety and flower-inducing temperature/day length matter. Source indicative temperature 15–25°C and elevation 800–2,000 m are not universal limits for every cultivar/site.
  • Well-drained sandy-loam/loam soil, slightly acidic pH; NHB gives 5.7–6.5. Avoid drought and waterlogging; drip irrigation supports efficient water/fertiliser use. NHB cultivation guide.
  • Source cultivars: Chandler, Sweet Charlie, Winter Dawn, Camarosa, Festival and Ofra. ICAR/IARI research, quality planting material and locally tested varieties help productivity and climate resilience.

Value chain and policy

  • High input costs, powdery mildew, red spider mites, bruising and a short unrefrigerated shelf life make rapid handling and cold chains essential. Processing into jams/pulp can reduce wastage; packaging, refrigeration and reliable transport improve market access.
  • MIDH horticulture/protected-cultivation assistance and PKVY organic-farming support are relevant policy routes; eligibility and state implementation determine actual support, rather than automatic strawberry-specific benefits.
  • Source indicative cultivation costs ₹5–8 lakh/ha, yields 15–25 tonnes/ha, and receipts above ₹10 lakh/ha vary with inputs, prices, losses and processing. Receipts are not net profit.
  • Source unsourced estimates—India 2,000–2,500 ha, 24,000–26,000 tonnes, Mahabaleshwar over 80% share, and early-20th-century introduction—need year/definition checks before statistical recall.
  • Global source list identifies China, US, Mexico, Spain, Türkiye and Egypt as major producers; Mexico/Spain/US as exporters and US/Germany/France as importers. Its claimed 2023 tonnages and China's 40% share lack an underlying dataset and should not be memorised as verified rankings.
  • Mains: connect crop diversification, smallholder income and tourism with extension, producer organisations, market intelligence, affordable cold chains and water-efficient farming.
