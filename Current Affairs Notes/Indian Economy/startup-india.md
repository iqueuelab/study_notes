# Startup India: recognition and employment

## December 2025: recognised-startup snapshot
- **Startup India launched 2016**. DPIIT's 10 December year-end review reports **2,01,335 recognised startups** and **21 lakh-plus jobs**. These are cumulative administrative counts, not startups founded or net jobs created during December alone. Recognition is distinct from profitability, survival and technology depth.
- Fintech, agritech, logistics, healthtech and digital commerce illustrate innovation areas. Skills, finance, market access, research links and predictable regulation influence sustainable enterprises. [DPIIT review](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2201280&lang=2&reg=3).

## January 2026: ten years of Startup India
- **16 January 2016 launch → 16 January 2026 tenth anniversary**, National Startup Day. PM's anniversary address reported **nearly 44,000 new registrations in calendar 2025**, the initiative's highest annual addition; **over 2 lakh recognised startups**, third-largest ecosystem, and **nearly 125 active unicorns** versus four in 2014. These are dated official claims; a **unicorn is a private startup valued at US$1 billion or more**, not necessarily profitable. [PM address](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/pm-addresses-the-programme-marking-a-decade-of-startup-india-in-new-delhi/).
- PM cited **over ₹25,000 crore invested through the Fund of Funds route**. Distinguish underlying investments mobilised through supported funds from the original **₹10,000-crore government corpus**; the Fund of Funds supports SEBI-registered AIFs rather than directly choosing every startup investment.
- The issue discusses a **₹10,000-crore Fund of Funds 2.0** and a stronger deep-tech focus: AI/ML, quantum, defence and aerospace. **Budget 2025–26** announced a fresh ₹10,000-crore contribution and exploration of a Deep Tech Fund of Funds. The source's **April 2025 approval** is not independently established here; do not infer all proposed funds were operational/disbursed. [Budget announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2098353&lang=2&reg=48).
- Deep-tech enterprises need patient risk capital, research links and testing/procurement pathways because proof of concept and commercialisation can take longer. Benefits include strategic capability, skilled jobs and domestic value chains; recognition, valuation, successful IPOs and durable productivity are different measures.
