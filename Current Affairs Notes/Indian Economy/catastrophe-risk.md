# Natural catastrophe losses and insurance resilience

## Hazard × exposure × vulnerability

- Swiss Re report cited: India **2023 economic losses about US$12 bn**, versus **US$8 bn annual average over the prior decade**. Economic loss differs from insured claims; more exposed assets can increase losses without a matching increase in hazard frequency.
- Source attributes roughly **63%** of annual catastrophe economic losses over two decades to floods; **Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu 36.8% industrial output** cited, baseline unspecified. Industry concentration raises exposure, not identical risk everywhere.
- **Protection gap** concerns losses not covered by insurance. Source's **over 90% households/businesses uninsured or underinsured** should not be substituted for a verified census of households: original insurance analysis may use loss-based denominators.

## Resilience: prevention plus risk finance

- Southwest monsoon **June–September** (Kerala 2018/Uttarakhand 2013 examples), northeast monsoon **October–December** (Chennai 2015); Gujarat cyclone/earthquake/flood risks, Mumbai flood/cyclone exposure, Chennai coastal/urban-flood risk. Delhi, Ahmedabad and Himalayas face seismic hazards.
- Granular hotspot mapping → recent-data exposure/risk modelling → mitigation + insurance/reinsurance tailored to remaining risks.
- Land-use/drainage, resilient buildings, early warning/evacuation and ecosystem protection; accessible policies, affordable premiums, clear exclusions and reliable claims. Insurance transfers financial risk; it does not prevent deaths or physical damage.
