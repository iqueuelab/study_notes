Prices → sourcing → macro effects

  • Source Urals about $5.5/barrel below Arab Light, Basrah Medium about $0.80 above Dubai benchmark; different grades/benchmarks, time-specific spreads.
  • Landed cost includes freight, insurance, sanctions/payment constraints and refinery suitability; the largest discount alone does not determine supply.
  • Source India over 85% import dependence, third-largest oil consumer; distinguish crude-import dependence from all-energy dependence.
  • Oil prices/import bill → current account, rupee, inflation, fiscal/subsidy pressures and transport/industrial costs. Diversification, strategic reserves, efficiency and cleaner alternatives strengthen energy security.
  • Source predicts weak Russian supplies early 2025 and possible March–April 2025 rebound during refinery maintenance; forecasts, not verified subsequent outcomes.

Ballia: exploration is not a proven oilfield

  • March 2025 reporting concerned ONGC exploratory work near Sagarpali, Ballia, Uttar Pradesh. ONGC lists environmental clearance for two exploratory wells in the district's OALP-VI block. ONGC clearance register.
  • An exploration lease/drilling plan, a geological indication, a resource estimate and a commercially recoverable reserve are separate stages. Do not label the location a confirmed “massive” producing field from the news alone.
  • Exploration may help supply diversification but economics, reservoir assessment and environmental/community safeguards determine feasibility. Import-dependence percentages require a specified reporting year and denominator.

June 2025: West Asian escalation

  • Israel–Iran hostilities raised risks of oil-price spikes, disrupted Strait of Hormuz traffic and longer Red Sea/Suez shipping routes; distinguish direct purchases from Iran from indirect global price exposure.
  • Higher landed oil costs can widen the trade/current-account deficit, pressure the rupee, raise transport/input inflation and weaken investment/consumption. Tax cuts/subsidies can protect consumers but reduce fiscal space; none of these responses is automatic.
  • Current account includes goods, services, primary and secondary income; a merchandise import surplus alone is the trade deficit, not the full definition of CAD. Reserves do not necessarily fall because a CAD exists.
  • Supplier diversification, reserves, energy efficiency and producer diplomacy improve resilience; route disruption can also increase Indian exporters' freight/time costs.

Oil reserves versus production

  • Proved reserves are estimated economically recoverable volumes under specified conditions; production is a flow per unit time. Venezuela's over 300 billion barrels, largely extra-heavy crude, illustrate why large reserves do not ensure high output: sanctions, finance, refining technology and infrastructure matter. Canada's oil sands and US shale similarly show technology/economics shape supply.
  • Source table (rounded billion-barrel reserve snapshots, without common reference year): Venezuela 303, Saudi Arabia 267, Iran 209, Canada 163, Iraq 145, UAE 113, Kuwait 102, Russia 80, US 74, Libya 48. Its embedded graphic gives conflicting figures/order (e.g. Iran ~155/Russia ~107/Canada >170), includes Qatar and Brazil, and mixes gas reserves: do not memorise this as a harmonised current ranking. Libya has Africa's largest proved oil reserves.
  • Reject the “annual billion barrels” production table: the magnitudes/units are inconsistent and no comparable year/series is supplied. The US is a leading producer and Saudi Arabia/Russia are major suppliers, but reserve rank ≠ production rank; crude-only versus all-liquids definitions differ.

OPEC/OPEC+ revision

  • OPEC: founded September 1960, Baghdad by Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Venezuela; headquarters Vienna. Coordinates petroleum policies, market stability, producer returns and reliable consumer supplies.
  • June 2025 twelve members: Algeria, Republic of the Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Libya, Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Venezuela. Angola left January 2024; Ecuador/Qatar/Indonesia are former members or suspended participants, not current members.
  • OPEC+ / Declaration of Cooperation (2016) adds Azerbaijan, Bahrain, Brunei, Kazakhstan, Malaysia, Mexico, Oman, Russia, Sudan and South Sudan. Distinguish the 2016 Declaration from the later 2019 Charter of Cooperation; participation in the Charter or voluntary cuts is not identical to OPEC membership.
  • Output targets, conformity monitoring and additional voluntary adjustments influence Brent/WTI and importing countries' inflation/current accounts. Saudi/Russian influence is substantial but non-OPEC growth (US, Brazil, Guyana), demand, sanctions and compliance constrain coordination; market shares depend on year and crude/all-liquids denominator.
  • Correct source's generic “cuts extended into mid-2025”: by 31 May 2025, eight members had agreed another 411,000 b/d July increase while unwinding part of voluntary restraint. This was announced future supply, not July output already produced. OPEC May announcement archive.
  • 2024 Venezuela PYQ: (d)—large reserves do not imply rapid crisis recovery or halted emigration. Economic distress and migration persisted in the question's context.

July 11: Russian-oil tariff risk

  • Embedded clipping reports Trump's threat of a 500% tariff against countries importing Russian oil, alongside a claim that trade talks with India were nearing agreement. Treat this as a source-period political threat/proposal, not an enacted tariff already applying to every Indian export.
  • Such secondary pressure can affect supplier choice, payment/shipping costs and India–US negotiations even where Russian discounts lower the crude purchase price. Existing FY2025-first-half import-dependence figures remain historical comparisons.
