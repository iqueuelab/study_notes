Cobia and marine cage aquaculture

Editable notes

Species and habitat

  • Rachycentron canadum, also cobia/black kingfish/crab-eater; marine finfish found in tropical/subtropical seas and coastal/brackish waters, migratory within the sea (oceanodromous).
  • Naturally present in Indian waters: the source's claim it is not native to India is incorrect. MPEDA illustration specifically notes Tamil Nadu/Kerala coastal availability. Avoid an absolute pelagic-versus-demersal dichotomy because it occurs in different coastal habitats. CMFRI research catalogue.

Mariculture potential

  • Rapid growth (source 6–8 kg in a year under suitable farming conditions), high-value white meat and export demand make it attractive for sea cages; used in markets including Taiwan/Japan and sashimi preparations.
  • CMFRI has developed breeding/seed-production and farming protocols. Cage culture requires suitable sites, healthy seed, feed, biosecurity and environmental carrying capacity; fast growth alone does not assure profits. Link to India's mariculture/PMMSY and Blue Revolution context. CMFRI mariculture.
  • 2022 PYQ: golden mahseer is a fish (a); Indian nightjar, spoonbill and white ibis are birds. Cobia is a marine fish; mahseer is a freshwater fish.
