Indian Economy

Gini index and multidimensional inequality

Editable notes

Read the measure correctly

  • Gini describes distribution of a specified variable: 0 (equality) to 1 (inequality), or 0–100. Income, consumption and wealth Ginis answer different questions; a single aggregate cannot measure every form of inequality.
  • India's 25.5 cited in July debate is a consumption-based measure for 2022–23, not a comprehensive 2023 income/wealth Gini. World Bank derives it from household surveys, not only tax returns/formal wages. The PDF's assertion that informal workers are simply excluded is false; survey coverage, rich-household underrepresentation, top-end consumption and expenditure definitions can still bias inequality downward. World Bank methodology.
  • Consumption can be smoother/more equal than income or wealth; do not infer a low consumption Gini means low asset concentration. Source top 1% income share 22.6%,2022–23 is an income-share estimate, not a wealth share or mathematical contradiction of consumption Gini.

Overlapping disadvantages

  • Analyse gender, caste/location, employment quality, property/inheritance, health, education and digital access alongside distribution. Source reports school computer/internet access 52.7%/53.9%, household broadband 41.8%, rural female/male internet 25%/49%, women managers 12.7% and women-run startups 7.5%; these have unspecified survey periods/denominators and should not be treated as one verified July 2025 census. 'Women35.9% of workforce' is also not the same as female labour-force participation rate.
  • Digital exclusion compounds gender/education gaps through online banking, learning and job access. Combine disaggregated household surveys, administrative/tax and wealth evidence, service-access indicators and qualitative fieldwork; comparing countries requires matching variable, year and method.
