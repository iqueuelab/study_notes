Indian Economy

Core industries and industrial indicators

Editable notes

Eight core industries

  • Coal, crude oil, natural gas, refinery products, fertilisers, steel, cement, electricity.
  • Index of Eight Core Industries (ICI) is a weighted output index; monthly release by Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT. The source series uses 2011-12 as base year.

How to use

  • Measure sector output relative to the base period; weights represent the constituent sectors within the index, not each sector's share of the entire economy.
  • Infrastructure and manufacturing are linked through steel/cement, fuel and power; changes have downstream effects on construction, transport and finance.
  • Published near month-end for the previous month's output, before that month's IIP release; distinguish growth rate from index level.

Read the index

  • Monthly business survey comparing conditions with the previous month; a timely indicator of manufacturing/services activity, not a direct measurement of GDP growth.
ReadingMeaning
Above 50Expansion compared with previous month
50No change
Below 50Contraction
  • 58 → 54: slower expansion, not contraction. 46 → 49: slower contraction, not expansion. Distinguish level from direction of change.
  • A diffusion index uses respondents reporting improvement plus half those reporting no change, with relevant weighting/seasonal adjustment.

Components and comparison

  • S&P Global manufacturing headline: new orders 30%, output 25%, employment 20%, suppliers’ delivery times 15% (inverted), stocks of purchases 10%. S&P methodology.
  • Services business-activity and composite-output measures differ from simply averaging every manufacturing headline component.
  • Longer delivery times can reflect demand pressure or supply disruption; a headline alone can mislead. S&P interpretation.
  • PMI: survey signals on orders/output/jobs and other conditions, usually released early. IIP: official measured industrial production, covering mining, manufacturing and electricity with a later publication lag.
  • Useful to policymakers, firms and investors alongside inflation, credit, jobs and official output data; it does not mechanically determine RBI rates or guarantee stock-market gains. Indian PMI is compiled by S&P Global; IHS Markit is the predecessor name, not a separate current compiler.

Index of Industrial Production

  • IIP: measured industrial output in mining, manufacturing and electricity; monthly NSO, MoSPI release. Source-series base 2011–12; formerly associated with CSO, now part of NSO.
  • Weighted volume index using a fixed-base/Laspeyres approach; compare the same relevant period for growth.
  • Index 116 =16% above base output, not automatically 16% year-on-year growth. Positive index value ≠positive growth; falling positive values can indicate contraction.
  • Eight core industries contribute about 40.27% of that IIP series, not 40.27% of all GDP. IIP excludes most services and differs from value-added GDP and survey-based PMI.

IIP revision

  • IIP measures weighted changes in industrial production volumes: mining, manufacturing and electricity; excludes agriculture and most services. NSO publishes it.
  • The base in the March 2025 source period was 2011–12. Index 116 means 16% above the base level, not 16% year-on-year growth.
  • A positive index value does not prove production rose that month; compare the appropriate periods. IIP is an output indicator, not a direct inflation measure.

April 2025 IIP release

  • 28 May 2025 quick estimate: industrial production grew 2.7% year on year in April, against the 3.0% March quick estimate. It is a slowing growth rate, not a 2.7% fall in output. MoSPI release.
  • The issue's remaining IIP definition, sector coverage, volume-index method and 2011–12 base repeat existing notes. Compiler is NSO, MoSPI, not an independent current CSO; index 116 means 16% above the base, not automatically 16% annual growth. IIP is not a price index or a full GDP measure.
