# Nobel Economics 2025: innovation-driven growth

## October 2025: knowledge and creative destruction
- Joel Mokyr, Philippe Aghion and Peter Howitt received the 2025 Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel. One half went to Mokyr; the other half jointly to Aghion/Howitt. Total prize 11 million Swedish kronor; Academy announcement 13 October. [Official announcement](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2025/press-release/).
- Mokyr's economic history explains how scientific understanding and practical knowledge reinforce sustained technological progress; openness to new ideas and institutions matter. Aghion–Howitt's 1992 model formalises **creative destruction**: improved products/processes displace old ones, while temporary innovation returns encourage R&D and are challenged by subsequent entrants.
- Innovation is influenced by incentives, education, research, entrepreneurship, competition and institutions, rather than only an unexplained external trend. Distinguish Mokyr's historical account from Aghion–Howitt's formal growth model; they did not jointly originate every endogenous-growth theory.
- Policy tradeoffs: protect incentives to innovate without entrenching incumbents; support education/basic research, finance and entry while helping displaced workers acquire skills. India relevance: research–industry links, public/private R&D, competitive markets and diffusion to smaller firms; innovation does not automatically distribute gains equally or ensure escape from a middle-income trap.
- Economics prize began in 1969 and was established by Sveriges Riksbank in memory of Nobel; it is distinct in origin from the prizes specified in Nobel's will. Source's ~0.7% Indian R&D/GDP is an approximate earlier-period comparison, not an audited October 2025 release.
