# Farm ponds and decentralised water harvesting

## 10 June 2025: Kukas model
- **Kukas near Jaipur, Rajasthan/Amber block**: farm ponds capture monsoon runoff on farmers' land where groundwater stress constrains agriculture. Source layout **~5% farmland, 10 ft deep, plastic-lined, fenced**; sizing needs rainfall/catchment/soil and safety assessment.
- Stored runoff can supplement kharif/rabi irrigation, livestock and horticulture; evaporation, rainfall variability, irrigation efficiency and maintenance determine duration. Source **14–21 lakh litres per hectare**, **50 built/25 under construction**, **50,000 villagers expected to benefit** are project estimates with distinct units/status; not demonstrated year-round security for every village.
- **Correction: ** a plastic liner limits seepage, so direct recharge cannot simultaneously be its principal function. Storage ponds reduce pumping pressure; separate unlined recharge works/infiltration elsewhere can replenish aquifers.
- Benefits: drought buffering, diversification, potential processing/dairy jobs. Assess land opportunity cost, runoff diversion/downstream impacts, liner disposal, safe fencing and fair access; no-river/no-canal claim is site-specific, not true of all Rajasthan.
- **2025 Araghatta PYQ: (b)**, wheel with earthen pots at spoke ends lifts water into channels. Ancient device is distinct from a rainfall-storage pond; similar water-wheel concepts do not prove a single undisputed invention history.
