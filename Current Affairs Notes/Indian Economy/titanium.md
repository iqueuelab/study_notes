# Titanium and Odisha’s mineral value chain

## Titanium complex: April 2025 proposal
- Source reports approval of an **₹8,000-crore titanium complex in Ganjam, Odisha**, promoted by Orissa Metallurgical Industry Pvt. Ltd.; proposed outputs: titanium slag, pigment and sponge; estimated employment **8,000**. Approval and employment potential are not construction completion or jobs already created.
- Ilmenite processing can add local value and strengthen strategic supply chains. Power/water, transport, specialist skills, coastal ecology, effluent treatment and lawful land acquisition/R&R determine viability. The wider source industrial-approval package reports ₹1.65 lakh crore and 51,939 potential jobs; these are proposal estimates.
## Metal, ores and products
- **Ti; atomic number 22; transition metal**; discovered by William Gregor in **1791**. Fourth most abundant crustal metal after aluminium, iron and magnesium. Main ores: **ilmenite FeTiO₃; rutile TiO₂**; beach placers include Odisha, Chavara (Kerala) and Manavalakurichi (Tamil Nadu), with deposits also in Andhra Pradesh/Maharashtra.
- **Ore → titanium-rich slag/feedstock → separate pigment or sponge-metal routes.** White **TiO₂ pigment** serves paints, coatings and sunscreens; titanium metal/alloys serve aircraft, defence, marine equipment and biocompatible implants. Pigment is not aerospace metal.
- High strength-to-weight ratio, density about 60% of steel and a protective oxide film give useful corrosion resistance; resistance is not universal to every acid/base. Hot finely divided titanium can burn, including in nitrogen to form TiN. TiO molecular spectra help identify cool stars.
- Sponge production commonly uses the energy-intensive **Kroll process**: titanium tetrachloride reduced with magnesium; alloying with aluminium/vanadium creates specialised grades. India already has indigenous titanium capability: avoid the blanket claim of no processing technology.
- **Titanium is not a rare earth**, but is on India's **2023 list of 30 critical minerals**. Criticality concerns supply vulnerability and importance, not simply geological rarity. [PIB critical-mineral list](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1942027&lang=1&reg=3)
- Mains: connect domestic refining, technology/quality standards, reliable procurement, local skills and environmental safeguards rather than equating mineral abundance with manufacturing self-reliance.
