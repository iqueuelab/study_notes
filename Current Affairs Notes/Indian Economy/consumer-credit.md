# Household debt and consumer credit

## Debt, assets and demand
- RBI Financial Stability Report figures cited in the source: household debt/GDP rose from **36.6% in June 2021 to 42.9% in June 2024**.
- Household financial assets/GDP fell from 110.4% to 108.3% in the cited comparison. This excludes much physical wealth; a falling ratio does not prove absolute assets declined.
- Prime/subprime classifications measure credit risk, not income classes exactly. Source estimates link more super-prime borrowing to asset creation and more subprime borrowing to consumption; their scope and date matter.
- Unsecured personal loans, credit cards and BNPL can smooth consumption but raise debt-service stress. Existing repayments can reduce disposable income and future demand; effects depend on household circumstances.
- NPA classification follows applicable borrower-wise rules within a bank; every late payment does not automatically make every loan across all banks an NPA.
- Better underwriting must consider aggregate obligations, transparent charges, affordability, grievance redress and financial literacy while avoiding indiscriminate exclusion.
