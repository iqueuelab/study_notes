Indian Economy

Welfare versus freebies: design, fiscal cost and accountability

Editable notes

Evaluate design, not labels

  • “Freebie” lacks a single settled economic/legal definition. Merit services such as nutrition, education, health and safety nets can improve capabilities and equity; transfers can protect consumption during shocks.
  • Examples: PDS, school meals, MGNREGA, vaccination, Rythu Bandhu, KALIA, PM-KISAN. Source claims of one scheme directly causing another national scheme need historical evidence.
  • Tamil Nadu school meals predate the source's 1956 claim (earlier Madras municipal initiative); Kamaraj expanded provision. National programme followed in 1995.

Trade-offs

BenefitRisk if poorly designed
Poverty relief / human capitalUnsustainable recurring obligations
Access to essential servicesLeakage and weak targeting
Demand supportCrowding out productive spending
Affordable household energyCross-subsidy burdens / wasteful consumption
  • Electricity is rival/excludable but can have social benefits; private good ≠ zero public benefit. Universal programmes can reduce exclusion, while targeting can contain cost; assess both.
  • Disclose beneficiaries, recurring cost, funding and debt/contingent liabilities; evaluate outcomes and opportunity costs, safeguard fiscal sustainability without abruptly withdrawing essential protections.

Elections and institutions

  • ECI manifesto guidance asks for rationale and funding transparency. Court discussion of an expert/apex mechanism is not proof a new binding nationwide authority already exists.
  • State policy space, cooperative federalism, independent fiscal scrutiny and informed voters matter. Combine skills/jobs with necessary income/food protection; neither replaces the other in all circumstances.
