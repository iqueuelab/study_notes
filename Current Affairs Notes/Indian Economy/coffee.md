# Coffee cultivation, heritage and exports

## Idukki plantation history
- Pallikkunnu CSI Church near Kuttikkanam, Kerala, preserves an old coffee tree associated with colonial plantation history.
- Source account: British expansion in the early 19th century; coffee initially dominant around **Peerumade, Kuttikkanam and Thengakal**; leaf disease in **1875** encouraged a shift towards tea.
- The source reports a roughly 150-year-old tree and historical Thengakal yields of 2 tonnes/acre; these are local heritage claims requiring independent corroboration.
## Mains links
- Plantation economies show how disease, crop suitability and profitability reshape land use and livelihoods.
- Preserve agricultural heritage through cultural tourism; diversify crops and strengthen pest/climate resilience.
## Exports: January 2025 report
- **Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu** are major growing states; Karnataka leads. Europe is a key market: Italy, Belgium and Germany; other destinations include US, Russia and UAE.
- Higher Robusta prices and supply disruptions in Brazil/Vietnam can lift export value; distinguish price-driven receipts from volume growth. The source links pre-EUDR stockpiling to demand.
- Source's 'first ever $1 billion' claim and mixed April-November/FY labels are unreliable: official data show **$1.29 billion in FY2023-24**. [PIB coffee brief](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/jan/doc 2025120487201.pdf).
- **EUDR** means traceable deforestation-free supply chains; costs include documentation and monitoring. Avoid assuming every Indian exporter necessarily violates it or that the EU Foreign Subsidies Regulation applies uniformly to every coffee consignment.
- Mains: value-added exports, farmer incomes, diversification, climate/pest resilience, sustainable sourcing and trade compliance.

## Cultivation and Coffee Board
- Arabica/Robusta differ in environmental needs and product characteristics; Indian shade-grown coffee links production to tree cover but plantations do not automatically equal intact natural forest.
- Well-drained humus-rich soils, suitable rainfall/temperature and slope management matter; generic climate ranges are not universal specifications for both species.
- Baba Budan's introduction story is a historical tradition; source exact year should not be treated as conclusively established.
- **Coffee Act 1942; Coffee Board, Bengaluru**, 33 members. Chairperson and executive/Secretary roles should not be conflated.
- Source 65–70% export share is dated; contradictory production-rank lists should not be memorised. Quality, price volatility, pests, labour and climate resilience influence growers.

## Export update and production geography
- Source April–February **2024–25 export receipts: US$1.54 billion**, about 40% higher year on year. Revenue growth can reflect higher prices as well as quantities; it is not automatically a 40% production rise.
- Karnataka supplies roughly 70% of India's coffee; Kerala and Tamil Nadu are other major producers, with non-traditional areas including the Eastern Ghats.
- Shade cultivation, well-drained soils, rainfall and elevation matter; **Arabica and Robusta have different climatic tolerances**. Coffee rust is a fungal disease, not an insect pest.
- Source says more than two-thirds exported; destination shares/rankings require the same period. Brazil/Vietnam weather-related supply pressure can benefit receipts while global-price volatility remains a farmer risk.
- Coffee Board dates to **Coffee Act 1942**, Commerce Ministry. Do not infer a dedicated coffee PLI from a general manufacturing incentive, or an already operative India–EU FTA in March 2025.
- Value addition, speciality quality, resilient varieties, responsible shade management, market access and domestic demand support incomes; foreign-exchange earnings do not alone fix the current account.

## Araku Valley and tribal coffee
- **Eastern Ghats, Andhra Pradesh**, Araku landscape with **Anantagiri/Sunkarimetta forests**, hills including **Galikonda, Raktakonda, Sunkarimetta and Chitamogondi**. Source elevation **600–900 m** is a local range, not every surrounding hill's summit height; cooler highland conditions do not make the whole region a temperate climatic zone.
- **Araku Valley Arabica Coffee GI registered 2019**, official product name; GI protects qualifying geographic origin/reputation and use conditions, not a guarantee every bag is organic or sold by one commercial company. [GI Registry](https://search.ipindia.gov.in/GIRPublicSearch/Application/Details/607).
- Tribal farmers' coffee, forest produce and agriculture connect biodiversity, livelihoods and culture. “Araku tribes” is a geographical umbrella, not one named notified tribe; communities have distinct identities/knowledge.
- Cooperatives/social enterprises, suitable shade cultivation, processing and premium markets can improve incomes; measure farmer returns, control and certification rather than assume universal empowerment from branding.
- Bauxite **deposits/mining proposals and ecological conflict** must be separated from proof the whole valley is an operating mine. Forest/water protection, recognised land/community rights and meaningful consultation matter.
- Tourism nickname **“Ooty of Andhra Pradesh”** reflects scenic appeal, not scientific geography. Ecotourism/agro-tourism require carrying-capacity, waste/water management and community benefit sharing; scenic promotion alone is not sustainable development.
