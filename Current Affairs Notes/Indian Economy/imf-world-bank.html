Indian Economy

IMF and World Bank: institutions and reports

Editable notes

Bretton Woods institutions

  • 1944 Bretton Woods conference: IMF and IBRD institutional framework; IMF Articles entered into force 27 December 1945, also India’s joining date. Reconstruction was central to IBRD; IMF’s distinct purpose is international monetary stability, exchange/payment cooperation and balance-of-payments support. IMF Articles.
  • IMF had 191 members in April 2025, after Liechtenstein joined on 21 October 2024; source’s 190 is outdated. IMF announcement.
  • IMF reports: World Economic Outlook, Global Financial Stability Report (normally spring/autumn). The latter assesses financial-system risks; the source’s “Global Financial Stabilisation Report” wording is incorrect.

World Bank versus World Bank Group

  • World Bank = IBRD + IDA; World Bank Group = these plus IFC, MIGA and ICSID. IBRD lends to middle-income/creditworthy poorer countries; IDA provides concessional support; IFC serves private enterprise; Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency provides political-risk guarantees; International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes handles investment dispute settlement. World Bank distinction.
  • Source-era IBRD membership: 189; membership differs across Group institutions. India belongs to IBRD/IDA/IFC/MIGA but not ICSID. Do not imply every Group institution has identical membership.
  • Reports/products: World Development Report, Human Capital Index; Doing Business was discontinued. IMF focuses on macroeconomic/monetary stability; World Bank on longer-term development, finance and poverty reduction.
  • US is the largest IBRD shareholder; source percentages (US 16.41%, Japan 7.87%, etc.) omit reference date and mix shares/voting power, so do not memorise them as April 2025 figures.
  • PYQ recall (2016): Global Financial Stability Report → IMF; Ease of Doing Business ranking → World Bank.

Additional IMF revision points

  • Headquartered Washington, DC; financial operations began 1947. Existing note records 1944/1945 origins and corrects source's outdated 190 members to 191 in May 2025.
  • Fiscal Monitor complements WEO/GFSR with fiscal-policy analysis. Surveillance, policy advice, technical assistance/capacity development and balance-of-payments financing are distinct activities; crisis assistance commonly carries programme conditions.
  • India borrowed during the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis and later participated as a creditor/contributor; quota determines financial commitments and strongly influences voting power, but quota share is not identical to voting percentage. Sri Lanka/Pakistan illustrate programmes, not unconditional grants or automatic debt cancellation.

July 29 WEO update

  • IMF projected global growth 3.0% in 2025,3.1% in 2026, upgrades 0.2/0.1 percentage points from April. India projected 6.4% in both FY 2025–26 and FY 2026–27, also up0.2/0.1 points; India's figures use fiscal years, unlike the world aggregate's calendar years. IMF July table.
  • Better effective tariff assumptions, trade front-loading, improved financial conditions/weaker dollar and fiscal support explain the global upgrade; uncertainty, tariffs and geopolitics remain downside risks. Forecast is not realised output. IMF overview.
  • Existing note's 191 members remains correct for this source period; the PDF repeats stale 190. Existing bank already contains the 2016 Global Financial Stability Report→IMF PYQ.

August 2025 Executive Director appointment

  • Embedded clipping reports approval of former RBI Governor Urjit Patel as IMF Executive Director for three years, replacing K. V. Subramanian; distinguish government nomination/appointment approval from the start of board service.
  • India's constituency includes Bangladesh, Bhutan and Sri Lanka; Executive Directors represent countries/constituencies, while IMF staff and Managing Director have different roles. The Board has 25 Executive Directors, rather than treating every member country as holding its own seat.
  • Repeated institutional facts/reports add no new baseline material; the PDF's 190 members is stale: IMF had 191 after Liechtenstein joined on 21 October 2024. IMF accession record.

19 January 2026 WEO update

  • IMF revised India's FY2025–26 real GDP growth estimate from 6.6% to 7.3%, +0.7 percentage point, citing a better-than-expected third-quarter outturn and strong fourth-quarter momentum. These quarter references in the WEO discussion must not be read as completed Indian FY fourth-quarter results in January. Forecasts remain distinct from realised annual growth. January WEO.
  • India's table uses fiscal years starting April: 2025 column means FY2025–26; it projects 6.4% in FY2026–27 and FY2027–28 as temporary/cyclical supports fade. Source compares NSO first advance estimate 7.4% with IMF 7.3% for FY2025–26; differing estimates are not contradictory final outcomes.
  • Institutional history, reports, Urjit Patel appointment and the 2016 GFSR question repeat the common notes; 191 IMF members, not the recycled 190. 2011 PYQ: lending is to member countries; eligible members are not confined to developed economies. The central bank may act as fiscal agent/depository, which does not turn it into an independent non-sovereign IMF borrower.
