# Employment, unemployment and PLFS measures

## Measures and classification
- **Labour force =employed +unemployed**; unemployed means seeking/available for work under the applicable survey definition, not everyone without a salary.
- **UR =unemployed ÷labour force ×100**; **LFPR =labour force ÷population ×100**; **WPR =employed ÷population ×100**. Always specify age group, rural/urban and reference period.
- **PLFS, NSO/MoSPI**: source-era annual rural/urban estimates under usual/current weekly status, quarterly urban CWS estimates. **Usual status** reflects a longer reference period; **CWS** uses the preceding seven days.
- Sampling and student/individual employment classification matter; missing formal contracts does **not make informal workers statistically unemployed**.
## Types and traps
| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Disguised | Extra workers add little/no marginal output; may still count as employed |
| Seasonal | Work available only in specified seasons |
| Structural | Skill/location/sector mismatch |
| Frictional | Job-search or transition interval |
| Technological | Displacement following technology change; new jobs may also emerge |
| Cyclical | Demand downturn reduces employment |
- **Vulnerable employment** describes insecure categories such as own-account/contributing-family work; not an unemployment category. Source “69% year-on-year automation loss” misrepresents a risk estimate, not observed annual job destruction.
## Causes and response
- Weak labour demand, skill mismatch, seasonal agriculture, unequal access/discrimination and mobility barriers; population size alone does not mechanically determine unemployment.
- **MGNREGA** rural work guarantee, **PMKVY /PM-DAKSH** skills, **SMILE** targeted rehabilitation/livelihood support, Startup India; programme coverage and quality need assessment.
- Improve labour-intensive growth, apprenticeships, portable benefits, women's safe access/childcare, market-linked training and credible placement outcomes.
- Falling UR alone can reflect lower participation; assess job quality, earnings and underemployment alongside the headline rate.
## 15 May 2025 : survey redesign
- From **January 2025**, PLFS rotational-panel design covers **rural and urban** areas, with monthly CWS indicators and rural/urban quarterly coverage; **first monthly bulletin covers April 2025**. Source body repeats the older urban-only quarterly objective and misses this key clipping development. Annual usual-status/CWS estimates continue with the revised design. [Mo SPI redesign](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/PLFS_Changes-in-2025_rev.pdf).
- NSO/Mo SPI survey measures employment, participation, unemployment and work intensity; hours worked aid underemployment analysis. A survey redesign does not establish that hours had never been asked in any earlier employment survey.
- Assess sampling/reference-period changes before comparisons; more frequent estimates do not automatically mean every month-to-month change is statistically meaningful.

## July 2025: employability and job quality
- Editorial distinguishes **unemployment, underemployment and skill mismatch**. EPFO enrolment is not a complete measure of net new jobs, secure work or graduate employability; informal employment should not be counted as unemployment.
- Source attributes approximately **83% of unemployed people being youth**, and **about half of graduates being employable**, to dated reports. These are different populations/indicators; the uncited email/file/spreadsheet percentages lack age/sample denominators and should not be generalised to all Indians.
- **WEF Future of Jobs 2025** scenario to 2030: **170 million roles created, 92 million displaced, net 78 million**; forecasts depend on employers' assumptions and are not achieved outcomes.
- Proposals: assessed industry–college partnerships, placement/earnings accountability, digital and communication skills, apprenticeships, interdisciplinary learning and international qualification/language preparation. Idea/Tinker Labs and teaching by industry professionals can support practice. An **Indian Education Service** is an editorial proposal, not an established new service.

## October 2025: integrated employment policy proposal
- Editorial proposes an Integrated National Employment Policy: align labour, education, skill and MSME policies; Centre–State–industry coordination; an empowered secretaries' group, district planning and time-bound job-quality targets. These are recommendations, not notification of an enacted policy.
- Demand side: labour-intensive textiles, agro-processing, tourism, healthcare and MSMEs; supply side: practical curricula, vocational skills, apprenticeships, portable benefits and migration support. Housing near industry, rural BPOs/remote work and attention to lagging districts can improve mobility and regional access.
- Childcare/eldercare, safer travel, flexible/local work and decent conditions can support women's participation. The source's roughly 25% female participation lacks age/reference-period specification and is not comparable with every PLFS measure.
- Gig-worker proposals: registry, skills/work records, insurance/credit/social security, fair contracts and grievance channels. Reject its unqualified 9-crore-by-2030 claim: NITI's 2022 report estimated 77 lakh workers in 2020–21 and projected 2.35 crore by 2029–30. [NITI report](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-02/25th_June_Final_Report_27062022.pdf).
- Four 2019–2020 Labour Codes consolidate 29 laws; passage and commencement differ, and the 6 October editorial calls for implementation. Its CII 133-million additional working-age people/18% global increment and 2043 peak are source-attributed projections, not achieved jobs or necessarily a peak in the absolute population. Better integrate EPFO, PLFS, NCS and e-Shram while accounting for their different coverage.
