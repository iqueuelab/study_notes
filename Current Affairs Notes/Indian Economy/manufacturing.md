# Manufacturing, Make in India and PLI

## Policy framework
- **Make in India (2014)**: investment, manufacturing capability and employment.
- **PLI (introduced 2020)**: incentives linked to eligible incremental production/sales; coverage expanded across **14 sectors**. Defence and toys are not automatically PLI-covered merely because domestic manufacturing is promoted.
- Supporting levers: industrial corridors, logistics parks, National Infrastructure Pipeline, skills, infrastructure and business reforms.
## January 2025 source claims
- PDFs report Rs 1.28 lakh crore investment and 8.5 lakh jobs, mobile-phone production +600% and toy exports +239%; the measurement windows are unspecified, so use only after baseline verification.
## Opportunities and constraints
- Electronics, pharmaceuticals, renewable-energy equipment and defence; supply-chain diversification after COVID-19 can attract investment.
- Challenges: logistics, skills, technology, predictable regulation, environmental compliance and competition from Vietnam/Bangladesh.
- **India did not join RCEP in November 2019.** The source's claim that RCEP participation strengthened India's manufacturing position is wrong. [Commerce Ministry/PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=200213).
## Mains approach
- Combine productivity and scale with jobs, domestic supplier capacity, resilient trade links and sustainable production.
## Specialty steel: PLI 1.1
- Launched **6 January 2025**, second round; production period **FY 2025–26 to 2029–30**. First specialty-steel round notified **29 July 2021**, within the broader PLI initiative begun in 2020.
- Five categories: **coated/plated steel; high-strength/wear-resistant steel; specialty rails; alloy steel and wires; electrical steel**. Uses: automobiles, white goods, transformers and specialised industries.
- Changes: lower **CRGO** investment/capacity thresholds; excess production carried forward to the **immediately following year** for incentive eligibility; augmentation of existing mills eligible; quality/energy efficiency/process improvement.
- **Cold-Rolled Grain-Oriented steel**: transformer cores, high-tension power distribution; January 2025 ministry says domestic technology gap. Do not treat this as a timeless 'no Indian producer' claim.
- **PLI rewards eligible production, not simply any steel investment**. [Ministry release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2090683&lang=2&reg=48).

## May 2025 editorial: innovation-led manufacturing reset
- D. N. Gupta argues technical education, **core engineering/R&D** and production ecosystems matter alongside industrial incentives and shifting US tariffs. Liberalisation **1991**, **National Manufacturing Competitiveness Programme 2005**, Make in India 2014 are milestones; India's manufacturing efforts predate 1991.
- Source 2023 comparisons: manufacturing value added **India US$461bn, China US$4,658bn, US US$2,497bn**; per-capita **India ~US$320/world ~US$2,000**, productivity **India ~US$8,900/world ~US$32,000**. These are dated article indicators with methodology/price bases not fully given; per-worker productivity, per-capita value added and aggregate size differ, and they are not 2025 results.
- Reform entrance assessments/curricula towards concepts, creativity, problem-solving, collaboration and applied research; grades are useful but insufficient. Proposed **50% practical work** requires labs, tool rooms, workshops, assembly facilities and trained mentors—an editorial recommendation, not a mandatory national rule.
- Build **civil/mechanical/electrical/chemical/textile/biotech** capacity alongside AI/IT and electronics; equipment/machinery/process design underpin physical production. Avoid assuming service-startup growth supplies every manufacturing capability.
- State-specific parks with plug-and-play infrastructure, common prototyping/testing/certification/design, links to institutions/labs and supplier/startup finance. Lessons from Silicon Valley concern skills, research and networks, not a promise one copied park creates equivalent outcomes.
- Proposed **R&D 0.65%→2% GDP** and **additional 1% GDP industrial infrastructure** are spending recommendations; reference-year/scope of the 0.65% baseline must be specified. Combine educational capability, affordable finance, scale/quality, market access and dynamic evidence-based policy; check productivity/jobs and domestic technological depth.

## June clipping: PLI performance
- Newspaper reports **₹21,534 crore incentives disbursed** across sectors, investment **₹1.76 lakh crore** and production/sales **₹16.5 lakh crore**. The clipping dates production/sales to **March**; these cumulative administrative figures have different measures, so keep them distinct from annual output, net value addition or attributable employment.
- Source's smartphone **FY2017–18 imports US$3.6bn/exports US$0.334bn → FY2022–23 imports US$1.6bn/exports US$11bn** describes a shift toward net exports; its stated **US$9.8bn surplus is arithmetically inconsistent** with those rounded latter numbers (**US$9.4bn**). Do not use the pre-PLI baseline as proof every gain was caused solely by PLI.
- **2023 export-share/PLI PYQ: (d)**—3.2% share of global goods exports is incorrect in the question's context; domestic and foreign firms can use eligible PLI schemes. Sector-specific base-year/eligibility rules mean incentives are not universally calculated against the immediately preceding year's sales.

## White Goods PLI: reopened September window
- Official **14 September 2025** announcement reopened applications **15 September–14 October 2025**, inclusive, for components/subassemblies of **ACs and LED lights**. Both new applicants and specified existing beneficiaries seeking a higher investment category can apply under scheme conditions.
- Snapshot: **83 selected applicants; ₹10,406 crore committed investment**, not realised spending. Cabinet approval **7 April 2021**, outlay **₹6,238 crore**, implementation through **FY2028–29**. Eligible domestic component capacity aims to deepen supply chains and reduce import dependence. [Commerce Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2166458&lang=2&reg=48).
- General 14-sector PLI, cumulative incentive figures, smartphone trade comparison and **2023 PYQ (d)** repeat existing notes. The source's “seven years FY2021–22 to FY2028–29” wording is internally awkward; retain the official implementation endpoints without deriving an incorrect inclusive-year count.

## October 2025: China's WTO consultations on incentives
- **DS642:** China requested consultations **15 October**, circulated **20 October 2025**, over ACC battery PLI, auto/component PLI and the **Scheme to Promote Manufacturing of Electric Passenger Cars in India**. The third is not simply another scheme formally named “EV PLI”. This was an allegation at consultation stage, not an adverse ruling. [WTO October notice](https://www.wto.org/english/news_e/news25_e/ds642rfc_20oct25_177_e.htm).
- Alleged domestic-input discrimination engages **SCM Articles 3.1(b), 3.2; GATT III:4; TRIMs 2.1**, with **GATT I:1** also raised for the passenger-car scheme. [WTO dispute record, consultation details](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/dispu_e/cases_e/ds642_e.htm). Domestic value addition, domestic sourcing and production within India require examination of the actual scheme conditions; do not assume either automatic illegality or automatic compliance.
- SCM subsidy analysis distinguishes government financial contribution/income-price support and benefit, with specificity relevant to disciplines; export-contingent and domestic-over-imported-goods subsidies are prohibited categories. The PDF's permanent “non-actionable R&D/regional subsidies” category is outdated: those temporary provisions expired in 2000. [WTO subsidy overview](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/scm_e/subs_e.htm).
- Consultations may resolve disputes; otherwise panel adjudication may follow. The Appellate Body's impasse complicates review/enforcement; it is not a ruling that every challenged Indian measure remains lawful. Other countries' subsidies and policy goals do not themselves establish a legal defence.

- **10 December release**, using PLI results **through June 2025**: actual investment **over ₹1.88 lakh crore**, incremental production/sales **over ₹17 lakh crore**, **12.3 lakh direct/indirect jobs**, exports **over ₹7.5 lakh crore**, across **14 sectors**. June is the data cutoff, not the publication month. [DPIIT review](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2201280&lang=2&reg=3).
- Electronics, pharmaceuticals, telecom/network equipment and food processing contribute strongly. Keep **realised investment, cumulative sales, exports, incentive disbursement and jobs** separate; administrative attribution is not a controlled estimate of every job/output caused solely by PLI.
- Existing notes cover 2020 launch, incremental-sales/base-year conditions and the **2023 PYQ answer (d)**. The source again misstates the FY2022–23 smartphone trade surplus: **US$11bn−US$1.6bn = US$9.4bn**, not US$9.8bn. Eligibility is not universally measured against the immediately preceding year.
- Mains **2025, 250 words/15 marks**: rationale → achievements with dated figures → improve domestic value addition, supplier/R&D depth, transparent incentive evaluation, skills/logistics and competitiveness after incentives end.
