# Regulatory reform and business accountability

## May 2025 editorial diagnosis
- India's services-led path contrasts with manufacturing-led industrialisation. Source approximate **manufacturing GVA 16%→17.5% /services 33%→55% since 1980** mixes broad historical snapshots: sector shares depend on series/year, and regulation is one proposed explanation rather than a proven sole cause.
- Burdens: overlapping inspections, permissions/NOCs, unclear rules, obsolete requirements and discretionary/rent-seeking enforcement; anecdotal office “snake-pit” and electrical-fitting cases illustrate alleged misuse, not its prevalence across all agencies.
- **Budget 2025–26 announced a High-Level Committee** to review **non-financial-sector regulations, certifications, licences and permissions**, with recommendations expected within **one year**; this announcement is not completed deregulation. [Budget/PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2098373&lang=2&reg=48).
## Proposed microeconomic reforms
- Risk-based self-certification for lower-risk activity, competent third-party certification of building/fire/lift requirements, longer justified licence validity; publication of documents/checklists; coordinated inspections and predictable grievance/appeal timelines.
- Simplify land-use conversion, zoning/building rules and appropriate acquisition procedures; modernise labour rules without erasing worker safeguards; review disproportionate criminal penalties. Joint annual inspections are an **editorial suggestion**, not a universal legal cap, and serious hazards still require responsive enforcement.
- Automate suitable NOCs using geospatial/geotagged data (e.g. airport height restrictions); mining/forest/rail/defence approvals have distinct safety/environmental mandates and cannot be reduced to automatic permission in every case.
- Gig-work framework should recognise flexible work while securing dignity/social protection. The source's claim that States uniformly classify gig workers as full-time employees is too broad; welfare obligations do not automatically create that legal status.
- Reform administrative incentives towards lawful facilitation, published reasons and accountability. Contrast **1991 macro liberalisation** with today's proposed micro-level institutional reform; the source's **8%+ growth for 2047** is an ambition/scenario, not an assured outcome.
- Evaluate turnaround time/compliance cost alongside safety, environment, fair competition and worker protection; streamlined regulation needs credible enforcement, not merely fewer forms.
