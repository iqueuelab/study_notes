# Palmyra livelihoods and Udangudi palm jaggery

## Sap → sweeteners → livelihood
- **Palmyra (Borassus flabellifer)**, Tamil Nadu's State tree; Thoothukudi climbers tap **pathaneer** from inflorescences. Lime-coated collection pots inhibit fermentation; sap is boiled/concentrated, then cooled in coconut-shell moulds into **karupatti**; crystallisation produces **panangkarkandu**, sometimes flavoured with ginger/pepper/long pepper.
- Source describes a roughly **five-month season**, collections before sunrise, mid-morning and evening, and multi-day processing. Dangerous climbing without adequate safety equipment, low returns, seasonal employment and younger workers leaving threaten skill continuity.
- August source prices: producers **₹200–250/kg**, retail up to **₹380/kg**; these are local reported prices, not a national minimum or current quotation. Improve safety gear/training, insurance, producer collectives, direct marketing, quality assurance and off-season income.
## Udangudi GI
- **Udangudi Panangkarupatti**, Thoothukudi, is palm jaggery, not coconut jaggery. GI register **application 691; certificate 3 October 2023**—“recently” in the practice prompt does not mean first registered in August 2025. [GI Registry](https://search.ipindia.gov.in/GIRPublicSearch/Application/Details/691).
- Source links its character to red sand-dune soils and dry conditions; traditional processing avoids additives such as phosphoric acid/triple superphosphate. Source export destinations: Sri Lanka, Malaysia, Singapore. GI protects origin-linked reputation and authorised production; it does not guarantee every seller's purity, income or medical efficacy.
- Tamil Nadu source distinguishes permitted fresh-sap sweeteners from prohibited toddy fermentation. “Chemical-free” is marketing shorthand; jaggery remains sugar and the source's medicinal/health superiority claims are not clinical proof. Practice answers: palmyra **B (2,3)**; Udangudi **D (neither)**.
