# IBBI and flexible corporate insolvency resolution

## 26 May 2025 Fourth Amendment
- **IBBI (Insolvency Resolution Process for Corporate Persons) (Fourth Amendment) Regulations, 2025**, reported 30 May. **Resolution professional, with Committee of Creditors approval**, can invite expressions of interest for resolution of **the corporate debtor as a whole, sale of one/more assets, or both**. This facilitates parallel/part-wise options rather than waiting for value erosion; it does not guarantee every case resolves faster or authorise sale without process safeguards. [IBBI regulations/press-release register](https://ibbi.gov.in/en/).
- Potential broader bidding, viable-business preservation, realisation and flexibility; assess valuations, credible competition, creditor treatment, feasibility and actual timelines. EOI is a solicitation stage, not an approved resolution plan or completed sale.
## Institution and powers
- **Statutory IBC 2016 regulator**, established **1 October 2016**. Regulates insolvency professionals, professional agencies/entities and information utilities; registered-valuer oversight under delegated Companies-law framework. **NCLT adjudicates corporate cases**; IBBI does not personally adjudicate every insolvency or automatically apply every personal-insolvency provision universally.
- Board: chairperson; **three Central-government ex-officio members** representing Finance/Corporate Affairs/Law; **one RBI nominee**; **five nominated members, at least three whole-time**. Chairperson/other appointed-member tenure **five years or 65**, subject to statutory rules; do not apply it blindly to ex-officio representation.
- Distinguish **resolution** preserving viable value from **liquidation**, and regulator from professional/CoC/adjudicator. Better predictability/investor confidence are goals requiring evidence, not automatic consequences of notification.
