# Technology clusters, FDI and inclusive urban growth

## Hyderabad example
- January 2025 source describes Google's planned **Hyderabad campus**, **3 million sq ft / 7.3 acres**, billed as its largest outside the US. Embedded graphic projected **early 2026 completion / capacity for 18,000 employees**; projections are not proof construction or hiring was completed.
- **Gachibowli** IT corridor; institutions **ISB, IIIT Hyderabad**; Telangana **TS-iPASS**, industrial project approval/self-certification system, illustrates facilitation alongside infrastructure and skills.
- Source's blanket claims that Amazon, Microsoft and Facebook each have their “largest campus” here require company-specific comparison; Hyderabad is a major cluster without relying on those superlatives.
## Benefits, risks and response
| Channel | Benefit | Conditions / risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Investment + jobs | Construction, IT/services and suppliers | Jobs/capital inflows must be measured; office size alone is not FDI or GDP |
| Knowledge links | R&D, startup collaboration, global networks | Technology transfer is not automatic |
| Urban growth | Transport, housing, services | Congestion, unaffordable housing, water/power demand and construction pollution |
| Sector concentration | Scale and skilled-worker networks | Global downturn/visa exposure; diversify industries |
- Align transit, affordable housing and civic services with expansion; sustainable buildings, reliable utilities, satellite centres where suitable, industry-linked training in **AI/cloud/data**, and university–firm research.
- Source's **$5 trillion by 2025–26** is a dated ambition, not an achieved GDP statistic. Coaching Mains prompt: investment benefits → distribution/infrastructure constraints → sustainable cluster governance.
