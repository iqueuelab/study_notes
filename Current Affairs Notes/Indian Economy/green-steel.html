Indian Economy

Green steel and industrial decarbonisation

Editable notes

Policy status in December 2024

  • Ministry of Steel was preparing a Green Steel Mission with estimated cost Rs 15,000 crore. The January PDF overstates this as an already implemented investment.
  • Proposed elements: green-steel PLI, renewable-energy incentives and public procurement. Green-steel taxonomy released 12 December 2024; decarbonisation roadmap drew on 14 task forces. Ministry review.

Transition levers

  • Renewable electricity + energy efficiency; scrap recycling; hydrogen-based direct reduced iron (DRI); carbon capture where technically/economically suitable.
  • Related policies: National Steel Policy 2017 (300 Mt capacity ambition for 2030-31), Steel Scrap Recycling Policy 2019, Vehicle Scrapping Policy 2021 and National Green Hydrogen Mission.

Dated industry context and trade-offs

  • Source reports 139.15 Mt finished-steel production in 2023-24; distinguish finished-steel output from crude-steel production/capacity.
  • Gains: lower emissions/pollution, export competitiveness and green skills. Barriers: costly capital/hydrogen, reliable clean power, technology scale-up and financing for smaller producers.
  • Way forward: R&D, pilots, standards, demand creation, PPPs and international technology/finance cooperation; align expansion with net-zero 2070.

  • Coal gasification does not inherently produce low-carbon steel; electricity or hydrogen is low-carbon only with a suitable production pathway. Reduce emissions per tonne and total emissions as output grows.
  • India's 12 December 2024 taxonomy defines green steel below 2.2 tCO2e per tonne of finished steel: five stars below 1.6; four stars 1.6–2.0; three stars 2.0–2.2. Certification uses measured intensity; “green” does not mean guaranteed zero emissions. Steel Ministry brochure.

Industrial policy and constraints

  • Steel underpins infrastructure, vehicles, engineering and defence. Specialty-steel PLI has an approved ₹6,322-crore outlay (Cabinet approval); quality-control orders require specified BIS conformity for covered products. Logistics planning through PM Gati Shakti connects production centres to transport, ports and energy infrastructure.
  • Carbon prices, green public procurement and credible product standards can create demand; renewable power, affordable hydrogen and pilots can reduce production costs. Hydrogen storage/transport, furnace conversion, finance for smaller plants and geological storage assessment constrain scaling.
  • Source's 300-Mt National Steel Policy goal means crude-steel capacity by 2030–31, not guaranteed actual production in 2030. Its 2050 output, cost and export-loss figures are model-dependent projections.

PYQ revision

  • 2015, coking coal (c): India imports suitable metallurgical coal for coke-based steelmaking; saltpetre and rock phosphate are not the answer.
  • 2014, pollutants (d): steelmaking can emit all four listed gases—sulphur oxides, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide. Air-pollution controls, wastewater treatment and waste management complement carbon reduction.

April 23 additions: steel governance and scenario reading

  • National Metallurgist Award, Ministry of Steel: recognises metallurgical contributions. Source describes 25 minimum safety guidelines, informed by ILO iron/steel safety practice and World Steel Association principles; occupational safety accompanies emission reduction.
  • Source's FY2022–23 output snapshots (125.32 Mt crude; 121.29 Mt finished) and FY2021–22 ~2% GDP are dated figures, not April 2025 production totals. Major producing belts include Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat and West Bengal; rankings need a stated production measure/year.
  • National Solar Mission began January 2010; renewable electricity can lower steelmaking emissions. Kalyani FeRRESTA is a company green-steel brand, not a government steelmaking programme or proof that all production is fossil-free.
  • Model claims in the source—435 Mt output in 2050, $500 billion cumulative coking-coal forex savings, 58% EU export loss without decarbonisation, hydrogen break-even at $1/kg, or competitiveness at $2/kg with a $50/tCO2 carbon price—are conditional projections, not policy targets or guaranteed outcomes.
  • Likewise quoted green/grey hydrogen prices and ₹3.2 lakh per tonne plant-cost figures are dated assumptions (the latter lacks a clear capacity denominator); compare full system costs, financing, ore quality, electricity and carbon-price conditions.
  • Its steel-emissions figures mix 2018 direct sector emissions (~270 MtCO2e) with other years/boundaries; do not add its 9%, 10% and 11% shares. Purchased-electricity emissions, national greenhouse gases and energy-related CO2 have different denominators.

May 14 additions: product categories and data correction

  • Flat steel: plates/sheets; long steel: bars/rods/sections; specialty grades meet particular performance needs. Tata Steel, SAIL and JSW illustrate major producers; company capacity rankings require current geographic/consolidation scope.
  • Correct the source's 2023 “120 Mt crude steel”: World Steel Association gives 140.8 Mt in calendar 2023. FY, calendar-year, finished/crude output and installed capacity differ; the source's company capacities and >150-Mt national capacity are dated snapshots. World Steel in Figures 2024.
  • India imports substantial suitable coking coal but has large domestic iron-ore production; avoid equating iron-ore and metallurgical-coal import dependence. Global competition, energy costs and decarbonisation affect export competitiveness.
  • Housing/infrastructure and manufacturing can expand demand, but future plant expansions and policy targets are not realised output. Generic references to Steel Development Fund/export incentives need scheme/date verification, not assumed universal subsidies.

February 2026: procurement as an anchor market

  • Editorial proposes that public infrastructure buyers, including Railways, create dependable demand for certified low-emission steel. Its illustrative arithmetic: if steel is 18% of project cost, a 30% steel premium raises total cost by about 5.4% (rounded ~5.5%), assuming other costs unchanged; this is not a universal construction estimate.
  • Proposed implementation: verifiable product certification/traceability (including QR systems and accreditation), low-carbon criteria in schedules/tenders, value-for-money/lifecycle evaluation, phased pilots and coordination across Steel, Finance and Environment ministries. These are recommendations, not evidence a mandatory procurement regime has already commenced.
  • Existing taxonomy and decarbonisation routes remain the basis; certification capacity and tender usability need strengthening. Avoid reading the editorial's verification criticism as proof that India's green-steel taxonomy/certification framework does not exist.
