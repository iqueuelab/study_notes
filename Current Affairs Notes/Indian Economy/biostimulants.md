# Biostimulants: plant processes and product regulation

## December 2025: sustainable-agriculture explainer
- Biostimulants stimulate plant physiological processes to improve nutrient-use efficiency, growth, crop quality or stress tolerance. They act on plants/seeds/rhizosphere; benefits depend on product, crop, dose and conditions. They do not replace all fertilisers or guarantee yield/soil-carbon gains.
- Examples/categories include seaweed/botanical extracts, **humic/fulvic acids**, protein hydrolysates/amino acids, vitamins, antioxidants, anti-transpirants and eligible microbial products. Better roots, enzyme activity and plant–microbe interactions can support nutrient/water use; biomass-derived inputs connect to the circular bioeconomy.
- **Correction:** statutory definition says **“regardless of its nutrient content”**, rather than the PDF's absolute “without supplying nutrients”. Regulated under **Fertiliser Control Order, 1985**, **clause 20C/Schedule VI**, following **23 February 2021 notification**; excludes pesticides/plant-growth regulators under the Insecticides Act and distinguishes live microbes from biofertilisers/biopesticides. [Agriculture Ministry clarification, 9 September 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2165081&lang=2&reg=48).
- Bioefficacy, toxicology, chemistry and quality/label compliance are essential. Provisional certificates expired after **16 June 2025**; ministry reported **146 Schedule VI products as of 9 September 2025**, a dated count. “Natural” does not itself establish safety or legal registration. Specific strains such as Azospirillum/Bacillus/Trichoderma cannot be classified solely from the genus without product function/registration.
