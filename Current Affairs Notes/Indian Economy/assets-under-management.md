# Assets under management: funds and NBFC portfolios

## Meaning and limits
- **AUM** is the value of assets managed; for mutual funds, these are investor portfolios, not the fund manager's own corporate assets. Stocks, bonds/cash and other permitted investments may be included. In NBFC reporting, AUM commonly refers to the managed lending portfolio; distinguish it from mutual-fund AUM.
- Market changes, subscriptions and redemptions alter fund AUM. Reinvestment retains value within a fund compared with a payout; it does **not independently create wealth or mechanically raise AUM by the dividend amount**, since NAV adjusts for distributions.
- High AUM can permit scale/diversification but does not guarantee performance, low fees, good liquidity, investor trust or redemption resilience. Asset quality, concentration and liability/liquidity management matter.
