# India–US trade negotiations and WTO rules

## Legal distinctions
- bilateral trade agreement negotiations are not a signed FTA. A label“BTA”does not itself exempt preferential tariffs from WTO obligations.
- **MFN:GATTArticle I(goods); GATSArticle II(services)**; relevant exceptions differ. Non-discrimination is not a requirement everycountryset identicaltariffs.
- **GATTXXIV** permits qualifyingcustomsunions/FTAs covering substantiallyalltrade; interimagreements need aplan/schedule. Understanding ordinarilylimits reasonabletransitionto 10 years, with exceptionalcases explained.
- **Enabling Clause 1979** supports specified developing-country preferences/GSP and developing-country regional arrangements; it does not broadly authorise a reciprocal India–USselectiveagreement.
- **GSP** is a preferencescheme operating through applicable legal basis, not a separate treatyarticle identicalto XXIV.
## Negotiation trade-offs
- market access, services, supply chains andtechnology opportunities; farming/dairy sensitivities, standards andpolicy space need assessment.
- bound tariffs are ceilings; applied rates can differbelowthem. Demandforreciprocalrates does not automatically prove a breach—actual measures, bounds, MFN/exceptions matter.
- Safeguard WTOcompatibility andtransparent sectoral impact analysis; potentialbenefits are not guaranteedby announcement.
## WTO relevance and reform: April 2025 debate
- **Established 1 January 1995** following the Uruguay Round: wider coverage than GATT, including services/IP, negotiations, dispute settlement, trade-policy monitoring and capacity building. WTO members account for about **98% of world trade**; membership share is not proof 98% of transactions comply with every WTO rule. [WTO profile](https://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/whatis_e/who_we_are_e.htm).
- **Doha Round launched 2001:** prolonged disagreement over agriculture, subsidies, market access and development; do not infer it produced no outcomes except fisheries. **Trade Facilitation Agreement** was concluded at Bali **2013** and entered into force **22 February 2017**. [WTO TFA](https://www.wto.org/englIsh/tratop_e/tradfa_e/tradfa_e.htm).
- **Appellate Body unable to hear new appeals since December 2019**, following blocked appointments. Panels, consultations and arbitration continue; appeals into an unavailable appellate process can obstruct finality. This serious impairment is not the complete disappearance of dispute settlement or enforcement. [WTO Appellate Body](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/dispu_e/appellate_body_e.htm).

## Pressures and legal qualifications
- Unilateral tariff measures, geopolitical rivalry, subsidy/state-enterprise concerns, overcapacity and opaque notifications strain predictability. US **Section 232** steel measures and **Section 301** China measures are examples; WTO compatibility depends on the specific measures, obligations and claimed exceptions. Trump was President again in April 2025; historical Biden policies belong to an earlier administration.
- Regional arrangements such as **RCEP/CPTPP** can fragment rules and divert negotiating attention; qualifying FTAs are recognised exceptions within WTO law, not automatically breaches of MFN. WTO disciplines on subsidies/state trading exist; the challenge is adequacy, transparency and enforceability, not a total absence of rules concerning state-led economies.
- Source's “India may subsidise agriculture only up to 10%” is misleading: **10% de minimis** applies to relevant trade-distorting product-specific/non-product-specific support; Green Box, specified developing-country measures and other qualifying exemptions differ. Historical AMS entitlements and public-stockholding/food-security concerns create asymmetries. [WTO agriculture explanation](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/agric_e/ag_intro03_domestic_e.htm).
- India seeks food-security safeguards, a durable public-stockholding solution and meaningful special/differential treatment. Labour/environment provisions negotiated in particular bilateral agreements do not create a universal Indian acceptance or rejection of all such provisions in the WTO.
- Transparency duties and committee scrutiny matter; weak/incomplete notifications and enforcement do not mean reporting obligations do not exist.

## Value, outcomes and reform priorities
- A common rules framework, negotiated tariff commitments, transparency and a negotiating forum matter especially to smaller/developing economies. Formal member equality does not eliminate differences in bargaining power, legal capacity or market size; decisions usually use **consensus**.
- **MC12, June 2022:** Geneva Package included fisheries-subsidy disciplines, pandemic response, a targeted COVID-19 vaccine TRIPS decision, food security and protection of WFP humanitarian food purchases. The IP outcome was not a blanket waiver of all intellectual property. Fisheries adoption in 2022 and entry into force are different milestones; do not assume the agreement was already operative in April 2025. [WTO Geneva Package](https://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/minist_e/mc12_e/geneva_package_e.htm).
- Reform: restore effective appellate review, improve notifications/monitoring and dispute access, address subsidy/market-access concerns, and reconcile new issues with development and food security. Negotiated proposals are not enacted reforms.
- Mains balance: institutional dysfunction weakens WTO credibility, but abandoning its common framework can leave weaker economies more exposed to unilateral power. Assess negotiation, adjudication and monitoring separately; pursue reform with transparent, inclusive implementation.

## Dairy certification and India–US negotiations: July 2025
- “Non-veg milk” is a debate label for milk from animals fed certain animal-origin ingredients, not a distinct biological milk category. Market access involves feed certification, cultural preferences, sanitary regulation and small-producer livelihoods.
- India's veterinary certification has restrictions on source-animal feed; the published certificate refers to internal organs, blood meal and ruminant-origin tissues, and also animal rennet in products. Check the applicable certificate rather than generalise every animal-derived ingredient identically. [DAHD certificate](https://www.dahd.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-07/OMdated10August2020onVHCforimportofMilkandMilkProducts.pdf).
- US regulation prohibits specified mammalian proteins in ruminant feed to prevent BSE, with exemptions and additional high-risk-material controls. It is misleading to imply US cattle universally or unrestrictedly eat other cattle. [FDA feed rules](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/bovine-spongiform-encephalopathy/feed-ban-enhancement-implementation-questions-and-answers).
- Source **239.3 million tonnes Indian milk output, 2023–24**; producer-income/price-loss scenarios from import opening are estimates, not realised losses. Balance negotiation gains with livelihood impacts, credible certification and food safety; July deadline discussions did not establish a signed deal.
