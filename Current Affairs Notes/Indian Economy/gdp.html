Indian Economy

GDP: measurement, growth and potential output

Editable notes

Measurement

  • GDP measures final production within domestic territory over a period. Nominal at current prices; real at constant prices; neither is a complete welfare measure.
  • GDP at market prices = GVA at basic prices + product taxes − product subsidies. Output minus intermediate consumption is value added, requiring the tax/subsidy adjustment for GDP; income sums compensation/operating surplus/mixed income plus appropriate net taxes; expenditure C + I + G + X − M, with statistical discrepancies where reported.
  • NSO, MoSPI quarterly/annual accounts. Informal production is estimated, not wholly ignored; coverage/measurement gaps persist. GDP does not directly show distribution, unpaid care, health, education or environmental depletion. Nominal growth may reflect inflation rather than additional real output.

30 May 2025 provisional estimates

  • Source-series 2011–12 base: FY 2024–25 real GDP growth 6.5%; Q 4 7.4%. Provisional release differs from later revised/base-year estimates. Annual slowdown can coexist with faster final-quarter growth. NSO release.
  • Embedded clipping: Q 4 agriculture 5.4%, manufacturing 4.8%; annual manufacturing 4.5%, construction 9.4%, Q 4 construction 10.8%; annual household/PFCE growth 7.2% versus 5.6% previous year. Compare annual with annual and quarter with quarter. Construction/consumption support contrasted with manufacturing weakness and global uncertainty.

Potential GDP and PYQ reasoning

  • Potential GDP = sustainable output at normal resource utilisation without persistent inflation pressure; labour/skills, capital, technology/productivity and institutional capacity determine it. Infrastructure, regulatory unpredictability, investment/finance, low farm productivity and skill deficits can constrain India; reforms must improve productive capacity, not only demand.
  • 2015 GDP trends: (b): real growth rate did not steadily rise in the preceding decade, while nominal rupee GDP level did. Do not use post-2015 pandemic/demonetisation examples as evidence available to that question.
  • Tax/GDP ratio is receipts relative to GDP; a fall can reflect rates/exemptions/compliance, composition or cyclical changes. It does not logically prove slower growth or inequality. Source's answer (a) is its given exam interpretation; avoid turning it into a universal economic identity. The question has no separate year label here, so held for review/practice rather than assigning a guessed year.

June 2: fourth-largest economy claim and welfare

  • May 2025 NITI Aayog statement refers to IMF April 2025 projected nominal-dollar GDP: India approximately US$4.187tn, narrowly above Japan US$4.186tn in the 2025 projection. Forecast ≠ already completed national-accounts result. Comparisons depend on fiscal/calendar conventions, exchange rates and revisions; PPP rankings are different. IMF April database.
  • Total output helps assess market scale, but per-capita GDP, distribution, job quality, health, education and ecological costs determine living conditions. Embedded India/Japan/Poland charts compare agricultural versus industrial/service work, vulnerable employment, female participation, health and HDI; total-GDP rank does not imply corresponding welfare rank.
  • Drivers: services/IT/telecom/finance; domestic demand; UPI/JAM/ONDC; investment/manufacturing facilitation through PLI, Gati Shakti, Make in India. Programme announcement is not proof every gain is caused by that programme.
  • Constraints: large farm employment relative to value added, underemployment, skills/job mismatch, weak manufacturing depth, informal work, inequality and low research investment. Source shares ~45% farm employment/~18% GDP, ~16–17% manufacturing, >85% informal, youth UR15–18% need year/age/reference definitions; farm share may be of GVA rather than GDP.
  • ~$2,500 per capita/rank141/behind Bangladesh, wealth shares top1%=40% /bottom50%=3%, and R&D international ratios are dated/source-specific estimates; do not mix income with wealth, or infer every comparison uses 2025. Its “2004–14 tripled /2014–24 doubled” nominal-dollar trajectory does not isolate real productivity or establish causes of slowdown.
  • Source reports ~96% net FDI decline in FY2024–25. Net flows deduct repatriation/disinvestment and outward investment; strong gross inflows can coexist with small net receipts. Repatriation need not mean only profit transfer or flight caused by policy uncertainty. RBI annual report.
  • Trade slowdown/tariffs, oil-import prices and financial uncertainty create external risks. Priorities: farm productivity/agro-processing, labour-intensive MSMEs/textiles/tourism, apprenticeships, health/education, competitive clusters/logistics, research and private R&D, predictable rules and efficient public investment.
  • UBI pilot and subsidy/tax/land/labour reforms are editorial recommendations, not implemented national entitlements. Assess fiscal space, social protection and distribution alongside aggregate growth.

July 2025 state-led growth forecast

  • Source reports Morgan Stanley's 23 July 2025 “India's State-Led Economic Transformation” forecast: India could become the third-largest nominal-dollar economy by 2028, reaching $10.6 trillion by 2035 and contributing about 20% of global growth over a decade. These are conditional forecasts, not achieved ranks. $4.19 tn→$10.6 tn exceeds doubling, correcting the PDF's arithmetic description.
  • Reported leading state scorecard: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Telangana; most-improved over five years: UP, MP, Chhattisgarh. A projected 3–5 states above$1 tn GSDP by 2035 is distinct from an official realised GSDP estimate.
  • Mechanisms: infrastructure/capital spending, supply-chain diversification, manufacturing/PLI, financial access and competitive federalism. Source's public capex comparison 1.6% GDP(FY 2014–15)→3.2%(FY 2024–25) is its dated metric, not every government's combined expenditure.
  • Risks: geopolitical/energy shocks, climate damage, insufficient quality jobs, inequality, volatile capital and poorly managed urbanisation. Existing notes' IMF projection qualification resolves the PDF's conflicting fourth/fifth-rank labels.

October 2025: scale, skills and self-reliance debate

  • “Hanuman leap” is the editorial's metaphor for confidence through domestic productive capacity: demographic scale, skills, entrepreneurship, digital infrastructure and manufacturing can help respond to protectionism and ageing abroad. A young population becomes an advantage only with productive jobs, education, health and participation.
  • Link PLI/infrastructure/GST and UPI/JAM to productivity, access and resilience while assessing implementation, competition and distribution. Self-reliance can include diversified international supply and technology partnerships; it need not mean insulation from trade.
  • Source statistics mix periods and forecasts: $600-billion-plus reserves, $765-billion exports, 220-GW renewable capacity, $135-billion remittances and ₹3.17-lakh-crore festive sales are not one comparable October dataset. Its fourth-largest-economy claim incorrectly names Germany as the economy to overtake: the existing 2025 nominal-dollar projection comparison is with Japan. GDP rank/forecasts do not measure per-capita welfare. The supplied 250-word/15-mark essay is unyearred practice, not a CSE PYQ.
