Indian Economy

Consumer Price Index and inflation measurement

Editable notes

What CPI measures

  • CPI tracks retail price changes in a representative, weighted household consumption basket of goods and services. It helps assess inflation, purchasing power and living costs; a population average need not match every household's expenses.
  • For this April 2025 coverage, NSO/MoSPI publishes monthly CPI-Rural, CPI-Urban and CPI-Combined, base 2012=100; weights derive from the NSS 68th-round 2011–12 consumption survey. MoSPI CPI portal.
  • Fixed-basket/Laspeyres intuition: CPI = current cost of base-period basket ÷ base-period cost × 100. Official compilation aggregates weighted price relatives; inflation is the percentage change in the index, not the index itself.

Monetary policy and PYQ logic

  • RBI's flexible inflation-targeting agreement dates to February 2015; the 2016 RBI Act amendment gave statutory backing. Target: headline all-India CPI inflation, 4% with ±2 percentage-point tolerance for the period relevant to these notes. Earlier policy monitored multiple indicators, so avoid treating the transition as a simple statutory WPI-to-CPI substitution. RBI framework.
  • 2020 Prelims: answer (a), 1 and 2 only. CPI has greater food weight and includes services; WPI measures goods and is not RBI's inflation-target benchmark.
  • For the WPI 2011–12 series, 24.38% is the broader Food Index (food articles plus manufactured food products), not food articles alone. Do not confuse this with CPI's food-and-beverages group. Office of Economic Adviser release.
