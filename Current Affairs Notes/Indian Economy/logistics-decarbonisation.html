Indian Economy

Decarbonising India’s logistics sector

Editable notes

Why green logistics matters

  • Freight transport, storage and distribution connect producers with markets. Cleaner logistics supports competitiveness, energy security, urban air quality and India's net-zero 2070 ambition.
  • Road dependence and diesel trucks, energy-intensive warehouses and difficult-to-electrify aviation are major challenges. Rail and water transport can lower emissions per tonne-kilometre where routes, load factors and first/last-mile connections are suitable.
  • The source reports logistics at 13.5% of national GHG emissions, road transport at 88% of that, and trucks at 38% of CO₂ emissions; these have different or unspecified denominators. Treat them as source estimates requiring dataset/year verification rather than comparable shares. The source also reports roads handling about 70% of freight and 90% of passenger movement; passenger and freight shares are distinct metrics. Its 2022 modal-split image reinforces road dominance, with rail next and shipping/air much smaller.

  • PM Gati Shakti supports integrated infrastructure planning; National Logistics Policy supports efficient logistics systems and coordination. Government logistics overview.
  • The source's Delhi–Jaipur overhead-electric-highway example and tripling waterborne cargo by 2030 are proposals/source-reported targets; implementation and the cargo baseline require verification.
  • LNG, biofuels, hydrogen and ammonia differ in lifecycle carbon impact and hazards; LNG remains a fossil fuel. Solar assistance may reduce vessel energy demand but does not automatically power all ship operations.

International benchmark and implementation

  • IMO 2023 strategy: net-zero international-shipping GHG emissions by or around 2050; indicative reductions against 2008 of at least 20%, striving for 30%, by 2030 and at least 70%, striving for 80%, by 2040. The source's 50%-by-2050 figure belongs to the older ambition. IMO strategy.
  • Rail use in China/US illustrates modal choice; the source's China 50% share needs a comparable metric before adoption.
  • Constraints: capital costs, charging/fuel networks, reliability, technology maturity and inter-ministry coordination. Use PPPs, targeted incentives, green finance and R&D, while tracking actual emissions per tonne-kilometre and total emissions.
  • Efficient logistics → less empty running and energy use → lower costs and emissions, provided growing freight demand does not outweigh efficiency gains.

10 May 2025: hydrogen truck in mining logistics

  • Adani Enterprises announced a hydrogen fuel-cell truck flagged off in Raipur, Chhattisgarh, by Chief Minister Vishnu Deo Sai, for coal movement from Gare Pelma III to the State's power plant. The company's “India's first” label is an attributed claim; this launch is not evidence of a completed nationwide fleet conversion. Company announcement.
  • Announced configuration: three hydrogen tanks, up to 40-tonne cargo, about 200-km range. Adani Natural Resources (ANR) and Adani New Industries (ANIL) cooperate; ANR sources hydrogen cells from ANIL. The mine is State-owned, with Adani as mine developer/operator.
  • Fuel cell pathway: H₂ + O₂ → electricity + water + heat, powering an electric drivetrain without combustion of the hydrogen. Low exhaust pollution/noise differs from proven zero life-cycle emissions: hydrogen production, compression, transport and electricity source determine climate performance; tyre/road particulates remain.
  • Decarbonising coal transport does not decarbonise coal combustion. The announcement does not itself establish that every kilogram of supplied hydrogen is green.
  • Barriers: truck/fuel-cell cost, hydrogen availability, refuelling/storage networks, flammability/pressure safety and operational reliability. Compare cost and emissions per tonne-km with battery-electric freight, rail and other feasible options.
  • Green-hydrogen supply, domestic equipment, freight refuelling corridors, PPPs and suitable incentives are proposed scaling levers. Source suggestions about FAME/PLI inclusion are recommendations, not verified eligibility or newly sanctioned benefits; retain India's net-zero 2070 goal as a target.
