Indian Economy

Fertilisers, DAP and subsidy reform

Editable notes

DAP and January 2025 package

  • Di-ammonium phosphate supplies nitrogen + phosphorus; produced from ammonia and phosphoric acid. Phosphorus supports roots, flowering and seed formation.
  • 1 January 2025 Cabinet decision extended special DAP support ₹3,500/tonne over and above NBS, from that date until further orders; previous package covered 2024. Cabinet release.

Subsidy architecture

MechanismDistinction
UreaStatutorily notified farmer MRP; government supports eligible cost gap
NBSFixed nutrient-based subsidy for phosphatic/potassic fertilisers; not urea
Fertiliser DBTSubsidy paid to companies based on eligible verified retail sales, rather than ordinary cash transfer to every farmer
  • The PDF's '100% subsidy' means release of the approved subsidy amount under the payment mechanism, not that fertilisers are free or 100% of sale price is subsidised.

Efficiency and sustainability

  • Neem-coated urea reduces diversion and improves nitrogen-use efficiency; avoid indiscriminate overuse.
  • IFFCO nano urea/DAP are promoted for efficient nutrient delivery; assess field evidence and agronomic recommendations before assuming complete replacement of conventional fertilisers.
  • PM-PRANAM promotes balanced use/alternative nutrients and better soil health. Organic/biofertilisers and soil-informed dosing complement chemical nutrients.
  • Trade-off: affordable inputs and food security versus fiscal cost, imbalanced N:P:K use, import exposure, pollution and degraded soil health.
  • Link excessive nitrogen application with the common groundwater nitrate note; retain one main explanation of contamination there.

Standing Committee: 2025–26 allocation

  • Source compares ₹1.84 lakh crore departmental proposal with ₹1.71 lakh crore allocation, about 7.38% lower; this is not necessarily a 7.38% cut from the previous year's final expenditure.
  • Reported 2024–25 utilisation gaps differed by subsidy category and reporting cutoff. Underutilisation can reflect timing/claims and should be investigated, not automatically labelled corruption.
  • NBS covers specified P&K fertilisers; urea has a separate subsidy/pricing framework. Subsidies to producers/importers and point-of-sale-linked payments differ from unrestricted cash directly to every farmer.
  • Nano urea/DAP expansion was recommended; reported trial yield gains are crop/site-specific and do not prove universal replacement of conventional fertiliser without agronomic validation.
  • Secure raw materials through diversified supply and investments; combine adequate timely funding, soil testing, balanced nutrition and credible outcome monitoring. Committee recommendations inform scrutiny but are not executable law by themselves.
