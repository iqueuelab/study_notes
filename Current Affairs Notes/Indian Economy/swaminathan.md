# M. S. Swaminathan: Green Revolution and sustainable farmer welfare

## Productivity, sustainability and inclusion
- **M. S. Swaminathan**, a central figure in India's Green Revolution, helped adapt semi-dwarf high-yielding wheat in collaboration with **Norman Borlaug** and Indian institutions. Source varieties include **Sonora 64 and Lerma Rojo 64**; irrigation, fertiliser, extension, procurement and credit complemented seed technology. This was a collective transformation, not one scientist's invention of all varieties.
- Rice breeding drew on **IRRI** germplasm and Indica–Japonica work. The source's claim that he personally developed **Pusa Basmati 1 (1989)** is not retained; later institutional breeding achievements should not be attributed to one person without support.
- **Evergreen Revolution**: sustained productivity gains with ecological protection—soil health, biodiversity, water efficiency and reduced harmful input dependence. Contrasts in emphasis with an exclusively yield/input-led approach, while preserving food-security gains.
- Chaired the **National Commission on Farmers (constituted 2004)**; associated recommendation: MSP at least **50% above weighted average production cost**, with the cost definition an important policy debate. It is **not 25%**. Land/water rights, credit, insurance, technology, marketing and recognition of women farmers connect productivity to livelihood security.
- Advocated farmers' seed rights and gender inclusion; the **Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001** is the relevant legal framework, not proof that one individual drafted its entire text. Food self-sufficiency evolved over time; avoid treating a precise single year as the end of dependence on food aid.
- Honours in issue: **Bhatnagar 1961, Magsaysay 1971, Albert Einstein World Award 1986, first World Food Prize 1987, Padma Vibhushan 1989, Indira Gandhi Prize 2000**. Source's separate UNESCO Gandhi Prize assertion is unsupported and omitted. **2008 Borlaug PYQ: A, United States**, despite his Mexican research base; Borlaug is the global Green Revolution figure, Swaminathan the Indian figure.
- Mains revision: **seed + irrigation/institutions → productivity → environmental constraints → evergreen productivity + farmer welfare + gender inclusion**. Source Swaminathan mains question has no exam year and remains practice.
