Indian Economy

SIDBI–AFD and green MSME finance

Editable notes

Institutions

  • SIDBI: statutory SIDBI Act 1989, operational 2 April 1990; promotion, financing and development of MSMEs, with direct finance, refinance, risk capital and institutional support.
  • AFD: French development institution, founded 1941; loans, grants and technical cooperation across sustainable development, not a commercial Indian bank.
  • Collaboration directs long-term development finance towards green MSME investment, energy efficiency, low-carbon technology and resilience.

Financing mechanism

  • External development credit →SIDBI/intermediaries →eligible MSME investments →technical support/monitoring.
  • US$100 million AFD co-finance appears in SIDBI's FY2024–25 account; distinguish sanctioned funds, on-lending and final disbursement. SIDBI annual report.
  • Source AFD 108-country /€1.7-billion figures lack a clear programme/period denominator; not reliable descriptions of the institution's entire current worldwide annual lending.

Benefits and safeguards

  • MSMEs face credit, collateral, technical-assessment and technology-upgrade barriers; blended finance/capacity building may lower transition costs.
  • Verify additional investment, energy/emission savings, repayment viability and small-firm access; guard against greenwashing, currency risk and subsidising projects that would proceed anyway.
  • Green finance is complementary to cleaner production skills, reliable electricity, market demand and proportionate regulation.
