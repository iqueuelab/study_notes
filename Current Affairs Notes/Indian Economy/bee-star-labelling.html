Indian Economy

BEE star labelling and appliance energy efficiency

Editable notes

Programme and institutions

  • Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE): statutory body under the Energy Conservation Act, 2001, established in 2002, Ministry of Power. The Standards and Labelling programme began in May 2006. BEE programme.
  • 1–5 stars indicate comparative energy efficiency within a product category and applicable rating period; more stars generally mean greater efficiency. Compare capacity, annual consumption and label validity as well as stars: a larger five-star appliance need not consume less absolute energy than a smaller one.
  • Labels communicate efficiency/energy consumption, brand and model, enabling lifecycle-cost comparison. Benefits: lower bills and peak demand, lower emissions and more informed consumer choice; actual savings depend on usage and baseline.
  • Both mandatory and voluntary categories exist. Ceiling fans, electric geysers and tubular fluorescent lamps are all within the historical scope relevant to the 2016 PYQ: (d), all three. The PDF's entire long appliance list should not be memorised as uniformly mandatory; legal status depends on the product-specific notification and effective date. BEE reported 39 categories: 16 mandatory/23 voluntary in November 2024, a dated count rather than a January 2026 total. BEE review.

Compliance and broader functions

  • Energy Conservation (Compliance Enforcement) Rules, 2025 provide detection/verification and adjudication of non-compliance. Distinguish final notified rules from the earlier consultation draft; manufacturer/importer responsibility and accurate energy claims protect consumer trust. The daily source highlights penalties for divergence from label declarations, but does not establish a universal penalty amount or identical procedure for every appliance. Final Gazette rules.
  • BEE also works on minimum energy-performance standards, designated consumers, energy audits, certification of energy managers/auditors and building efficiency codes. ECBC originated for commercial buildings; don't describe all large residential complexes as automatically covered by ECBC. State notification/enforcement and separate residential-code provisions matter.
