# Regional Rural Banks: inclusion and financial health

## Institutional anchors
- Began under a **1975 ordinance**, followed by **RRB Act, 1976**, on Narasimham working-group recommendations: combine rural familiarity with commercial-bank resources.
- Original ownership **Union 50% : sponsor bank 35% : State 15%**; later amendments permit capital flexibility, so this historical structure should not be treated as an immutable prohibition on other capital.
- **RBI regulation; NABARD supervision**. Lending/deposits and government-payment delivery serve rural households, farmers, artisans and small enterprises.
- Priority-sector target: **75% of ANBC or credit-equivalent off-balance-sheet exposure, whichever is higher**, not an undefined 75% of all assets.
## Read ratios carefully
| Indicator | Meaning / caution |
|---|---|
| CRAR | Capital relative to risk-weighted assets; resilience measure |
| Credit–deposit ratio | Loans relative to deposits; higher is not always safer/better |
| NPA ratio | Asset-quality stress; decline may reflect recovery, write-offs or denominator growth |
| Profit | Sustainability; FY 2023–24 aggregate ₹7,571 crore is a dated result |
- Challenges: geographic concentration, technology/cyber capacity, operating costs, staff and local credit appraisal. Consolidation may improve scale but must preserve last-mile service and accountability.
