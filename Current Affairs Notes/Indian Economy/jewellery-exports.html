Gems and jewellery exports: tariffs and West Asian diversification

Editable notes

September 2025: diversification towards West Asia

  • US tariff pressure on Indian gems/jewellery prompted exporters to seek markets in Saudi Arabia and the UAE. The issue cites the US at around 30% of the export basket; this is a dated sector estimate, not the share of all Indian exports.
  • Saudi Arabia Jewellery Exposition (SAJEX), Jeddah Superdome, 11–13 September 2025, organised with GJEPC participation: a B2B platform for Indian exhibitors and regional buyers. On this issue date it was forthcoming; do not claim completed orders. Saudi Press Agency's event confirmation.
  • Source plans: 100+ exhibitors, 200 booths, 2,000+ buyers, diamond/coloured-gem jewellery, gold/platinum jewellery, lab-grown diamonds and manufacturing technology. Prospective attendance is not realised sales.
  • India is a major cutting/polishing hub, with employment concentrated around Surat and Mumbai. Processing dominance does not mean India mines the diamonds it exports; the source's “14 of 15” is an industry estimate, not a geological production share.
  • West Asian purchasing power, wedding/gifting demand and commercial ties offer opportunities. Source market forecast Saudi US$4.56 billion (2024) → US$8.34 billion (2030) is a projection. Local designs, established suppliers, competition and distribution relationships constrain rapid substitution for lost US sales.
  • India–UAE CEPA already supported market access; the India–UK CETA signed in July 2025 was not yet in force. The coaching claim that UK FTA benefits had already produced export success is premature. Diversification can reduce concentration but cannot guarantee immediate replacement of US demand.
