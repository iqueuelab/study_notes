# Electric cooking: energy security, affordability and grid readiness

## March 2026: electrifying kitchens
- Editorial proposes induction cooking to diversify household energy away from imported LPG and reduce combustion inside kitchens. **332 million LPG connections** and reported traditional-fuel use illustrate that a connection is not exclusive sustained clean-fuel use; households may stack fuels.
- **Cost comparison requires a tariff/subsidy baseline.** IEEFA's FY2024–25 Delhi family-of-four table estimated annual costs **electric ₹5,844; PNG ₹6,667; unsubsidised LPG ₹8,024; universally subsidised LPG ₹6,424; PMUY LPG ₹4,024**. Its headline 37%/14% use electricity as denominator; from these costs electricity is about **27% cheaper than unsubsidised LPG and 12% cheaper than PNG**. PMUY LPG was cheaper in that example. Appliance/utensil capital cost is additional. [Original calculations](https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/2025-10/India%27s%20clean%20cooking%20strategy-%20E-cooking%20the%20next%20frontier%20%20%281%29.pdf).
- Typical source efficiencies **~85% induction versus ~40% LPG** describe heat delivery at the stove, not whole-system electricity-generation efficiency. Induction avoids a local combustion flame; lifecycle emissions depend on the grid and cooking still produces food aerosols.
- Constraints: upfront devices/compatible cookware, reliable electricity, household wiring/transformer capacity and Indian multi-pot cooking. Morning/evening cooking can coincide with system peaks; **automated demand response**, smart meters and time-of-use signals can shift flexible loads, not every meal.
- Pair efficient appliances with distribution upgrades, consumer finance and renewable power/storage. Rooftop solar mainly generates in daylight; evening cooking needs stored energy or grid supply. P2P trading requires metering and regulatory arrangements. PM Surya Ghar supports rooftop generation; **300 units is a scheme-linked potential benefit, not unconditional free grid electricity for every household**.
- National Efficient Cooking Programme/BEE labelling support appliance adoption. Redirecting some LPG subsidy or starting with reliable urban grids is an editorial proposal, not an enacted compulsory switch. The issue's **$26.4bn** refers loosely to cooking-gas import exposure; do not treat it as a verified LPG-only annual bill without a commodity/year breakdown.
