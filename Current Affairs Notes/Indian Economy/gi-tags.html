Indian Economy

Geographical indications: protection and producer livelihoods

Editable notes

Meaning and legal anchors

  • GI: identifies goods whose quality, reputation or other characteristic is essentially attributable to their geographical origin. Covers agricultural/natural/manufactured goods, handicrafts and food products.
  • GI Act, 1999, effective 2003; Registry Chennai; Registrar is the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks, under the DPIIT/Commerce and Industry framework.
  • Registration lasts 10 years, renewable. Registered proprietors and authorised users have statutory protection; authorised-user registration matters for producer rights. GI is a collective origin-linked right, not an individual transferable brand. IP India FAQs.
  • Darjeeling Tea: India’s first registered GI (2004–05). Global framework: industrial-property protection under the Paris Convention (1883) and GI provisions of WTO TRIPS. Indian registration does not automatically confer identical protection in every foreign jurisdiction.

Turning recognition into income

  • Origin and skill → registration + authorised users → quality control and traceability → branding/market access → producer value. A tag alone does not guarantee premiums or exports.
  • Problems: imitation/misuse, weak awareness, unequal regional registration capacity, boundary disputes, fragmented producers and poor post-registration marketing; an imitation’s infringement depends on the protected indication and legal facts, not simply machine production.
  • Responses: producer collectives, registration assistance, authentic labelling, enforcement, quality standards, affordable logistics, digital selling and benefit-sharing with artisans/tribal communities.
  • GI logo/tagline “Invaluable Treasures of Incredible India” supports recognition. APEDA facilitates agricultural exports: Naga Mircha, Manipur black rice and Assam lemon are cited examples.
  • ODOP ≠ GI: district product promotion may support registered products, but ODOP selection is not a GI certificate. ONDC can improve digital market access; neither guarantees global sales.
  • Mains value: rural entrepreneurship, cultural preservation and export differentiation; ensure premiums reach original producers rather than only intermediaries.

June 27: artisan recognition and differentiated craft

  • News list records Kolhapuri chappal makers' objection to Prada's “replica” sandals. Kolhapuri chappal is a 2019 GI, a traditional leather-footwear craft linked to Maharashtra/Karnataka; the headline is a dispute, not an adjudicated finding of infringement. Official craft account.
  • Repeated Kannadippaya clipping adds its distinctive soft inner reed-bamboo layers and mirror-like patterns, associated with winter warmth/summer cooling; tribal craft skills and producer benefit matter alongside registration. Named communities include Oorali, Mannan, Muthuva, Malayan and Kadar, in Idukki, Thrissur, Ernakulam and Palakkad districts; do not treat GI as restricted to a single household or as proof therapeutic effects.
  • Existing 10,000-by-2030 target, legal duration, authorised users, examples and marketing constraints cover the repeated body.

Prada–Kolhapuri dispute and overseas protection

  • The July discussion develops the earlier Prada–Kolhapuri dispute into a distinction between cultural attribution and territorial legal rights.
  • Protection is territorial: domestic registration alone creates no universal international monopoly over a design. Overseas national applications, mutual-recognition agreements and appropriate certification/collective marks can help. The WIPO Lisbon system facilitates protection among contracting parties; India is not a member in this issue's timeframe. WIPO GI explanation.
  • Paris Convention, WTO TRIPS and India's GI Act supply distinct international/domestic legal layers. Right to prevent misleading origin claims differs from a patent over an invention or a design right.
  • Traditional-knowledge patent examples: turmeric wound-healing patent revoked after prior-art challenge; neem antifungal patent revoked in Europe; RiceTec/Basmati controversy concerned patent claims and naming issues. Avoid describing all as identical GI disputes or implying the RiceTec patent was wholly cancelled.
  • Responses: searchable authentic-product databases, producer/authorised-user capacity, foreign-market registration where useful, enforcement, ethical brand–artisan collaboration and benefit-sharing. The issue's 658 GI figure lacks a clear matching date/count definition; do not replace the existing dated registry figures with it.
