# India: installed power capacity and energy transition

## Capacity snapshot at 31 January 2026
- During **April 2025–January 2026**, India added **52,537 MW**, exceeding the previous full-year record **34,054 MW in FY2024–25**. Renewables contributed **39,657 MW**, including solar **34,955 MW** and wind **4,613 MW**. [Power Ministry, 15 February 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2228348&lang=2&reg=48).
- Total installed generation capacity: **520,510.95 MW (~520.51 GW)**. Fossil **248,541.62 MW (~47.75%)**; non-fossil **271,969.33 MW (~52.25%)**, comprising renewable **263,189.33 MW (~50.56%)** and nuclear **8,780 MW (~1.69%)**. Nuclear is non-fossil but is separately classified from renewables.
- **MW/GW measure capacity; MWh/GWh measure energy generated.** Installed renewable share does not mean the same share of actual electricity generation or continuous dependable supply. Grid expansion, storage/flexibility, land, finance and demand management determine reliable integration; coal dependence cannot be inferred directly from the combined fossil-capacity share.
