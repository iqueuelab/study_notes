# Stablecoins, CBDCs and monetary sovereignty

## October 2025: preparedness debate
- Source reports Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman at the **Kautilya Economic Conclave** urging countries to prepare for stablecoins and changing global financial flows. Engagement is a policy discussion, not an announcement that private coins became Indian legal tender.
- **Stablecoin:** cryptoasset designed to track a reference asset, commonly the US dollar. Fiat/collateral-backed and algorithmic designs differ; a peg can fail through weak reserves, liquidity stress or loss of confidence. Cryptoassets are not all equally decentralised.
- **CBDC:** central-bank liability in digital form; RBI's **e₹ pilots began in 2022**. Unlike a private stablecoin, it represents sovereign currency and is exchangeable at par with cash. “No volatility” means nominal rupee parity, not immunity from inflation or exchange-rate movement. [RBI concept note](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Publications.aspx?id=1218).
- Potential uses: faster payments, programmable transfers and cross-border settlement; risks include cyber/privacy concerns, runs, currency substitution/dollarisation and monetary-policy transmission. Actual international operation requires technical interoperability and legal cooperation; CBDCs do not automatically bypass every existing cross-border system.
- **2023 Prelims:** both statements correct—digital-currency payments can occur without US dollars/SWIFT, and spending conditions can be programmed. Possibility is distinct from every CBDC having those features. Tax/AML coverage of cryptoassets does not confer legal-tender status or prove all possession/trading is categorically illegal.
