Indian Economy

India–US trade negotiations and WTO rules

Editable notes

Legal distinctions

  • bilateral trade agreement negotiations are not a signed FTA. A label“BTA”does not itself exempt preferential tariffs from WTO obligations.
  • MFN:GATTArticle I(goods); GATSArticle II(services); relevant exceptions differ. Non-discrimination is not a requirement everycountryset identicaltariffs.
  • GATTXXIV permits qualifyingcustomsunions/FTAs covering substantiallyalltrade; interimagreements need aplan/schedule. Understanding ordinarilylimits reasonabletransitionto 10 years, with exceptionalcases explained.
  • Enabling Clause 1979 supports specified developing-country preferences/GSP and developing-country regional arrangements; it does not broadly authorise a reciprocal India–USselectiveagreement.
  • GSP is a preferencescheme operating through applicable legal basis, not a separate treatyarticle identicalto XXIV.

Negotiation trade-offs

  • market access, services, supply chains andtechnology opportunities; farming/dairy sensitivities, standards andpolicy space need assessment.
  • bound tariffs are ceilings; applied rates can differbelowthem. Demandforreciprocalrates does not automatically prove a breach—actual measures, bounds, MFN/exceptions matter.
  • Safeguard WTOcompatibility andtransparent sectoral impact analysis; potentialbenefits are not guaranteedby announcement.

WTO relevance and reform: April 2025 debate

  • Established 1 January 1995 following the Uruguay Round: wider coverage than GATT, including services/IP, negotiations, dispute settlement, trade-policy monitoring and capacity building. WTO members account for about 98% of world trade; membership share is not proof 98% of transactions comply with every WTO rule. WTO profile.
  • Doha Round launched 2001: prolonged disagreement over agriculture, subsidies, market access and development; do not infer it produced no outcomes except fisheries. Trade Facilitation Agreement was concluded at Bali 2013 and entered into force 22 February 2017. WTO TFA.
  • Appellate Body unable to hear new appeals since December 2019, following blocked appointments. Panels, consultations and arbitration continue; appeals into an unavailable appellate process can obstruct finality. This serious impairment is not the complete disappearance of dispute settlement or enforcement. WTO Appellate Body.

Pressures and legal qualifications

  • Unilateral tariff measures, geopolitical rivalry, subsidy/state-enterprise concerns, overcapacity and opaque notifications strain predictability. US Section 232 steel measures and Section 301 China measures are examples; WTO compatibility depends on the specific measures, obligations and claimed exceptions. Trump was President again in April 2025; historical Biden policies belong to an earlier administration.
  • Regional arrangements such as RCEP/CPTPP can fragment rules and divert negotiating attention; qualifying FTAs are recognised exceptions within WTO law, not automatically breaches of MFN. WTO disciplines on subsidies/state trading exist; the challenge is adequacy, transparency and enforceability, not a total absence of rules concerning state-led economies.
  • Source's “India may subsidise agriculture only up to 10%” is misleading: 10% de minimis applies to relevant trade-distorting product-specific/non-product-specific support; Green Box, specified developing-country measures and other qualifying exemptions differ. Historical AMS entitlements and public-stockholding/food-security concerns create asymmetries. WTO agriculture explanation.
  • India seeks food-security safeguards, a durable public-stockholding solution and meaningful special/differential treatment. Labour/environment provisions negotiated in particular bilateral agreements do not create a universal Indian acceptance or rejection of all such provisions in the WTO.
  • Transparency duties and committee scrutiny matter; weak/incomplete notifications and enforcement do not mean reporting obligations do not exist.

Value, outcomes and reform priorities

  • A common rules framework, negotiated tariff commitments, transparency and a negotiating forum matter especially to smaller/developing economies. Formal member equality does not eliminate differences in bargaining power, legal capacity or market size; decisions usually use consensus.
  • MC12, June 2022: Geneva Package included fisheries-subsidy disciplines, pandemic response, a targeted COVID-19 vaccine TRIPS decision, food security and protection of WFP humanitarian food purchases. The IP outcome was not a blanket waiver of all intellectual property. Fisheries adoption in 2022 and entry into force are different milestones; do not assume the agreement was already operative in April 2025. WTO Geneva Package.
  • Reform: restore effective appellate review, improve notifications/monitoring and dispute access, address subsidy/market-access concerns, and reconcile new issues with development and food security. Negotiated proposals are not enacted reforms.
  • Mains balance: institutional dysfunction weakens WTO credibility, but abandoning its common framework can leave weaker economies more exposed to unilateral power. Assess negotiation, adjudication and monitoring separately; pursue reform with transparent, inclusive implementation.
