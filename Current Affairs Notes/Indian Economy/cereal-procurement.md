# Cereal procurement, food stocks and crop diversification

## November 2025: the rice surplus and procurement debate
- Editorial uses **Tamil Nadu Kuruvai paddy procurement**, TNCSC's FCI-linked role, reported delays/corruption allegations and a roughly **two-lakh-acre cultivation increase** to examine how assured procurement influences crop choice. Allegations require investigation; procurement incentives are one cause among water, markets and agronomy.
- Source **31 October 2025** rice-equivalent procurement snapshot **119.86 lakh tonnes versus 82.08 lakh tonnes** a year earlier. **1 October** rice stock **356.1 lakh tonnes versus norm 102.5 lakh tonnes**; stock is a point-in-time quantity, procurement an inflow. Do not label this rice norm as the combined wheat-plus-rice norm.
- Source annual rice procurement **525–547 lakh tonnes** compared with distribution/offtake **392–427 lakh tonnes**, while wheat offtake exceeded procurement in two of the preceding three years. The editorial's broad “surplus cereals” label should not obscure crop/year-specific wheat tightness. Food subsidy around **₹2 lakh crore/year** is a dated fiscal-scale estimate.
- Pulses/oils paradox: source cites pulses output **252.4 lakh tonnes for 2024–25**, imports near **₹30,000 crore**, and edible-oil imports around **55% of demand**, import bill **₹1.2 lakh crore in 2023–24**. These estimates use different periods and should not be treated as the final revised 2024–25 production series or one common-year balance sheet. Source's claim oilseeds crossed 400 lakh tonnes only once since 2014 requires a consistent estimate series and is omitted.
- Assured cereal procurement can crowd out pulses/oilseeds, stress water resources and enlarge carrying/storage costs; it also protects farmer incomes and food access. Global oil prices, trade policy, yields, irrigation and processing affect import dependence, so 1990s import liberalisation is not a complete single-cause explanation.
- Editorial cites **28% PDS leakage** from an ICRIER study. Treat this as a study/method/reference-period estimate, not an official measured November diversion rate; the PDF's “2023 report” dating is not established.
- Proposals: region-specific crop-demand planning, transition finance/extension, reliable buyers and procurement for suitable pulses/oilseeds; **FPO/SHG/cooperative** aggregation, direct farmer–processor links (e.g. black gram for papad), soil health/rotation and local processing/storage. Rebalance procurement without abruptly removing income/food safeguards.
- Decentralised procurement already exists in participating States; reform would deepen local capacity rather than invent it. More predictable trade rules, transparent TNCSC/FCI operations, social audits and reduction of post-harvest loss complement diversification; the author's freer-rice-export recommendation is a proposal, not a verified repeal of every export restriction.
