Foreign portfolio investment: flows, returns and regulation

Editable notes

Calendar 2025: equity outflows

  • Source attributes to NSDL net foreign equity selling of about ₹1.6 lakh crore in 2025, including ₹22,611 crore in December; net selling in 8/12 months, over ₹1.16 lakh crore January–March and ₹74,000 crore January. These concern equity flows, not all FPI debt/equity combined or India's total foreign investment. February Nifty ~6% decline is market context, not proof a single cause.
  • Article links exits to expensive valuations (about 22× earnings), rupee depreciation lowering dollar returns and competing AI/technology opportunities in Japan/Taiwan. These are dated explanations, not a demonstrated decomposition of every investor's decision. A ₹91/US$ forecast is not an observed exchange-rate close; the appended “inflows ↑” recap conflicts with the outflow article.
  • FPI is the regulatory category; “FII” persists in market reporting for institutional flows. Mutual/pension/insurance/hedge funds can provide liquidity and price discovery while reversals pressure equities/currency. Institutional origin does not guarantee long holding periods or superior stability to all retail flows.
  • Correct the PDF's obsolete blanket 24% combined FII/NRI/PIO ceiling: individual FPI equity holding is below 10% (fully diluted basis); the aggregate FPI limit generally follows the applicable sectoral cap from 1 April 2020, subject to permitted lower thresholds/conditions. NRI/OCI limits are a separate framework. RBI framework, 2020 FDI policy.
  • SEBI registration/securities rules and FEMA/foreign-investment conditions operate together. Do not generalise an old eligibility list/“five-year trust” condition or unconditional permission for unlisted securities as current universal FPI rules. FDI denotes lasting interest under the applicable framework; every FDI investment need not confer management control, and every FPI investment is not short-term.
