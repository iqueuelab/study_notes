Indian Economy

Women-led MSMEs and access to finance

Editable notes

Credit gap behind enterprise ownership

  • Embedded analysis reports women-led firms at 20.50% of registered MSMEs, but 18.73% of employment, 11.15% of investment and 10.22% of turnover. These are shares of different totals, not firm-level productivity measurements.
  • The cited SIDBI comparison puts the gap between requested and received credit at 35% for women versus 20% for men. Limited property collateral, financial literacy, documentation, bank access and lender bias restrict formal finance; informal high-interest loans can deepen vulnerability.
  • MUDRA, 2024: women held 42,492,281 of 66,777,013 loan accounts (~64%), receiving ₹2,25,887.08 crore of ₹5,41,012.86 crore (~42%; article rounds to 41%). Account share is not loan-value share, and accounts are not necessarily unique borrowers.
  • Udyam Assist: article reports over 1.86 crore informal micro-enterprises, with women-led units 70.5% and their employment share 70.8%. Its universe differs from all registered MSMEs; formal recognition supports access but does not itself guarantee a loan.
  • MSMEs' roughly 30% GDP contribution is a dated sector estimate; 35% is the article's ambition, not an achieved share. RBI's June repo reduction to 5.5% and announced CRR easing can support liquidity, but phased CRR cuts were not already fully implemented in July.
  • Improve credit appraisal, outreach, financial capability, documentation and collateral substitutes; measure loan size, costs and successful utilisation as well as registrations/accounts.
