Indian Economy

Tea cultivation, Tea Board and export performance

Editable notes

Crop and regions

  • Camellia sinensis; origins in the China–northeast India–northern Myanmar region. International Tea Day: 21 May.
  • Warm humid tropical/subtropical climate, approximately 20–30°C, 150–300 cm well-distributed rain, slightly acidic well-drained porous soils; frost/extreme heat are harmful. Management and cultivar affect these ranges.
  • Assam: Brahmaputra/Assam Valley and Cachar; West Bengal: Darjeeling, Dooars, Terai; southern production: Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, notably Nilgiris. India is a major producer/consumer and black-tea supplier; most output is consumed domestically.
  • Darjeeling Tea was India's first GI-registered product; premium origin protection is distinct from a generic variety name.

Institutions and 2024 exports

  • Tea Board, statutory body under the Tea Act 1953, constituted 1954, Commerce Ministry; HQ Kolkata. Functions include industry development, producer support and export promotion.
  • Source/Akashvani March 2025 reporting places 2024 exports at 255 million kg, about 10% above 231.69 million kg in 2023, and describes India as second-largest exporter after Kenya, surpassing Sri Lanka. Preserve this as a dated reported ranking, with product coverage/China comparison requiring care. Akashvani report.
  • Main markets include Iraq/West Asia, UAE, Iran, Russia, US and UK; exports predominantly black tea. The source's percentages/overseas-office list have no clear data date and are not fixed institutional facts.

September 2025: value rather than export volume alone

  • At the India International Tea Convention, Kochi, International Tea Committee Executive Director James Suranga Perera highlighted scope for better quality, premium branding, new markets in South America/Africa/West Asia, and domestic demand.
  • Clipping's 2024 global output 7.074 billion kg / consumption 6.97 billion kg; India output 1.303 billion kg / consumption 1.22 billion kg are source-period estimates. Its 255 million kg/$800 million Indian exports versus 245 million kg/$1.4 billion Sri Lankan exports illustrate that volume and price realisation differ; ensure matched product/year/currency definitions before comparison.
  • 840 g/person/year in India versus ~3 kg in Turkey are source estimates; “1 kg would absorb all production” is an illustrative domestic-demand argument, not a guaranteed market forecast. Labour welfare, productivity, climate resilience, packaging/organic certification and origin branding affect competitiveness.
  • The same issue calls India both second- and third-largest exporter. Existing notes preserve the dated reported 2024 second-place claim with scope caveats; neither contradictory sentence establishes a fresh verified September global ranking. Source's fixed Tea Board office/member/State-share figures remain period-dependent.

24 December 2025: FSSAI clarification on “tea”

  • FSSAI directs that tea must derive from Camellia sinensis, under Food Product Standards Regulations 2011,2.10.1. Calling non-Camellia herbal/plant infusions rooibos/herbal/flower “tea” is misleading/misbranding; labelling must describe the food's true nature under Labelling and Display Regulations 2020,5(1). Applies to relevant food businesses including e-commerce; it is not a ban on consuming herbal infusions. FSSAI order.
  • C. sinensis, family Theaceae, is an evergreen shrub/tree normally pruned in plantations; young leaves/buds are processed. Flowers are bisexual, with stamens and carpels. Different tea categories chiefly reflect processing, rather than four separate botanical source species.
  • Green: oxidation curtailed by heating; oolong: partial oxidation; black: extensive oxidation; white: minimal processing/withered-dried leaves/buds. “Fermentation” in conventional black-tea processing commonly refers to enzymatic oxidation, not necessarily microbial fermentation.
  • Existing climate/drainage facts repeat. Source's 15–23°C is an indicative optimum, not a universal fixed growing limit; its “calcium-free soil” is too absolute. Do not infer tea grows only on hills: drainage, moisture, cultivar and soil acidity matter. Its short country list omits major producers such as Kenya/Sri Lanka, so is not a world ranking.
  • Accurate product identity supports consumer choice and legitimate tea producers. Herb blends containing genuine tea must be assessed under applicable composition/labelling rules, not treated identically to infusions with no Camellia ingredient.
