# Potato cultivation, productivity and food security

## 21 May 2025: CIP outlook
- Scientists associated with **International Potato Center (CIP / Centro Internacional de la Papa), Peru**, projected Indian potato production could reach **100 million tonnes annually by 2050**, potentially overtaking China. India was described as **second-largest after China**; projection is not an achieved ranking.
- Context: symposium on **Root and Tuber Crops Research and Development in India and South Asia**, and **50 years of India–CIP cooperation**; source quotes board chair Helen Hambly Odame. Research, improved seed, storage and processing support food security and livelihoods; growth must remain climate/resource-resilient.
## Crop and geography
- Potato is botanically a **modified underground stem tuber**, not a root. Primarily **rabi** in Indian plains; cooler hill/southern conditions allow other seasons, including limited kharif production in source-listed Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra.
- **UP and West Bengal** lead; other producers include Bihar, Gujarat, MP, Punjab, Haryana, Assam, Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh. Source 2021–22 figures: UP **161 lakh tonnes**, West Bengal **124 lakh**, India **533 lakh**: combined **~53.5%**, rather than exactly 50%.
- Source series **1991–92 → 2020–21**: area **11 → 22 lakh ha**; output **181.95 → 561.72 lakh tonnes**; yield **~16 → 25 tonnes/ha**. Keep these dated rounded figures together; do not compare them directly to a forecast as actual growth.
- Cool conditions favour tuberisation; heat stress reduces yield. Source indicative day **18–29°C**, night **13–18°C**, growth optimum **~24°C**, tuber development **~20°C**: cultivar and stage matter, so “above 30°C tuberisation always stops” is too absolute.
- Loose, well-aerated, organic-rich loam/sandy loam and good drainage aid tuber growth; salinity/alkalinity and waterlogging constrain it. Adequate light matters; the source's six-hour rule is a general horticultural guide, not a universal agronomic threshold.
## Value chain and PYQ links
- Quality disease-free seed, extension, water management, cold storage, processing and export access can lift supply and income. More output alone does not guarantee better prices/net income; post-harvest losses and gluts require market planning.
- **2023 India–China agriculture PYQ: (b), only two**—India has more arable area and a higher irrigated proportion, but not higher average productivity; compare like-for-like land definitions.
- **2019 rice exporter: India (b)** in the question's five-year context, not a permanent ranking guarantee. Source rice export shares/destination lists are period-dependent; policy is not invariably export-friendly.
- **2011 lower Gangetic plain: paddy and jute (c)** fit humid, warm, alluvial conditions. Wheat is still cultivated in eastern India; cotton can grow beyond black soils, so alternative-option explanations should not become blanket impossibility claims.
