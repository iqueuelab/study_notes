# Gold monetisation and domestic savings

## October 2025: mobilising household gold
- Editorial proposes financial self-reliance through voluntary gold mobilisation: transparent assaying, reliable collection/refining logistics, bank-led flows and digital gold-account records. Tax/scrutiny relaxation, “no-questions-asked” deposits and 4.5–6.5% funding costs are proposals/estimates, not enacted exemptions or guaranteed investment returns. Digital account records here are not automatically the same as commercial unregulated digital gold.
- Source estimates private holdings at 25,000 tonnes/$2.4 trillion and about 55% of FY26 GDP; valuation depends on price/date, while FY26 GDP is prospective in this issue. Its import dependence, 8% import-bill share and 2010–13 trade-deficit comparison use different periods. Do not combine them into a current audited balance sheet or assume gold value instantly becomes bank credit.
## Scheme and March 2025 change
- GMS launched November 2015 to mobilise idle household/institutional gold for productive use and reduce imports. Deposit → purity testing/refining → gold-denominated account/claim → permitted use by banks; original jewellery is melted, so emotional attachment and trusted valuation affect participation.
- **26 March 2025:** new medium/long-term government deposits, including renewals, discontinued; existing deposits continue under their terms. Short-term bank deposits remain at individual banks' discretion. Terms: short 1–3 years; medium 5–7; long **12–15**, rejecting the source table's 7–12. Short-term maturity redemption may be gold or rupee equivalent according to option/terms; government-deposit interest/redemption rules differ. [RBI updated directions](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=10084).
- The source's SLR practice explanation is wrong: eligible gold stock on a bank's books can count toward general SLR requirements; lending use does not logically exclude SLR eligibility. [Official launch explanation](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/printrelease.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=130202).
- Benefits depend on uptake: reduce imported physical demand, formalise savings, improve liquidity and external balance. Constraints: collection costs, assaying/refining reach, low awareness/returns, trust and attachment. Source treats SGBs as discontinued; lack of new issuance should not be read as cancellation of existing bonds and their obligations.
- Its 2019 question asks objectives: monetise holdings/reduce import bill, not export promotion/fiscal-deficit targets; printed label is **UPSC ESE 2019 Paper 1**, so excluded from CSE banks. Unyearred practice MCQs remain revision material only.
