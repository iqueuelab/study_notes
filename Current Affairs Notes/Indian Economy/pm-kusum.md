# PM-KUSUM: solar agriculture and groundwater trade-offs

## Scheme structure and corrected targets
- **Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthaan Mahabhiyan**, launched **8 March 2019**, **MNRE**; de-dieselise agriculture and support farmers as renewable-energy producers.
- **January 2024 guidelines**, applicable before this issue: target **34,800 MW by March 2026**, Central support **₹34,422 crore**; these are programme targets, not installed achievements.
| Component | Target/design |
|---|---|
| A | **10,000 MW** decentralised grid-connected renewable plants, generally up to **2 MW** each |
| B | **14 lakh** stand-alone solar agricultural pumps |
| C | Solarisation of **35 lakh** grid-connected agricultural pumps, including individual-pump and feeder-level approaches |
- PDF's **20-lakh B /15-lakh C** table reflects an earlier configuration. Use the revised targets and distinguish **sanctioned / installed / solarised**. [MNRE January 2024 guidelines hosted by State agency](https://mprenewable.nic.in/uploads/2024/MNRE%20Guideline%20PM%20KUSUM%20dated%2017.01.2024.pdf).

## Benefits depend on implementation
- Barren/fallow-land generation, reduced diesel use/grid demand, decentralised clean power, possible electricity-sale/lease income and local installation/maintenance jobs. Sales to DISCOMs require an appropriate connection, agreement and tariff; not every stand-alone pump automatically exports power.
- Source **32-million-tonne annual CO₂ reduction** is a full-implementation projection, not measured savings in May 2025. Solar generation does not guarantee uninterrupted irrigation through nights, clouds or equipment failures.
- Constraints: upfront farmer contribution, credit, timely subsidies, domestic supply/quality, distribution connection, DISCOM finances, maintenance and skills. Pump upgrades require matching motor, PV capacity and hydraulics; difficulty varies rather than applying uniquely to every solar pump.
- **Water–energy trade-off:** low marginal pumping costs may increase groundwater extraction. Solarisation does not itself conserve water; pair metering/groundwater budgets, efficient irrigation, suitable pump sizing and credible incentives to sell/save electricity with aquifer protection.
- Mains: assess energy access, farmer net income, reliable maintenance and groundwater outcomes together; capacity sanctioned alone is an inadequate measure of success.
