Indian Economy

Wheat cultivation, production estimates and food security

Editable notes

Cultivation

  • Wheat is a major rabi cereal, second to rice in India's cereal-production volume; central to northern/northwestern diets, procurement and PDS food security.
RequirementRevision cue
SeasonUsually sown October–December; harvest spring/early summer varies by region
ClimateCool growing season; warm, dry, sunny ripening
Source teaching rangesAbout 10–15°C sowing, 21–26°C ripening; 50–100 cm rainfall
SoilWell-drained loam/clay loam; irrigation important in major producing belts
Main statesUttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan; also Bihar, Gujarat and Maharashtra
  • Terminal heat shortens grain filling; rain/hail/winds near maturity can damage grain and cause lodging. Timely sowing, appropriate varieties, irrigation and ICAR/IMD advisories matter.

April 2025 estimates and satellite monitoring

  • March second advance estimate placed 2024–25 wheat output around 115.4 million tonnes, about 1.9% above 2023–24; the supplied article rounds it to 115.3. It is an estimate, not a final harvest total. Agriculture Ministry series, 10 March 2025.
  • The page image highlights ISRO's crop forecast: NRSC's CROP framework combines remote sensing for near-real-time sowing/harvest monitoring. ISRO separately estimated 122.724 million tonnes across eight major states as of 31 March 2025; that pilot estimate must not be substituted silently for the ministry's national estimate. ISRO framework.

Food security and sustainability

  • Production affects MSP procurement, buffer stocks, PDS availability, food inflation and rural incomes. Global production rank does not establish export rank; trade shares/destinations change with year and policy.
  • 13 May 2022 wheat-export prohibition included exceptions for specified prior commitments and government-authorised food-security requests. The source's export destinations/share describe a dated trade situation, not unrestricted exports in April 2025. DGFT notices via APEDA.
  • Response: heat-tolerant/short-duration varieties, zero tillage, crop rotation, water efficiency, soil-health measures, extension/KVKs and crop monitoring. Improve dry-grain storage and logistics; a general cold-chain prescription is not essential for ordinary wheat grain.
  • Balance domestic affordability, predictable trade policy and farmer incentives; diversify intensive monocultures to reduce groundwater pressure and pest/nutrient risks.
