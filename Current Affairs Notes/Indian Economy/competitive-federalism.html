Competitive federalism and investment attraction

Editable notes

November 2025 editorial: compete on competence

  • The editorial uses Google's planned Visakhapatnam AI hub, Andhra Pradesh, to discuss interstate competition for investment. Google's 14 October 2025 announcement committed approximately US$15 billion over 2026–2030 to its first Indian AI hub; this was an investment plan, not a fully operating facility or proof of the world's largest data centre. Google announcement.
  • 1991 liberalisation reduced industrial licensing/central allocation and gave State infrastructure, skills, clearances, land and policy stability greater importance. The source's “permission to persuasion” captures an interpretation of the shift, not the disappearance of central policy or regulation.
  • Benefits: investment/employment, productivity, supply-chain clusters, faster approvals and diffusion of single-window, skills, EV/startup policies. EoDB/startup/export-readiness comparisons can incentivise performance; rankings alone do not demonstrate outcomes.
  • Risks: opaque subsidies, discounted land, contingent liabilities and fiscal “race to the bottom”; displacement between States may produce limited net national benefit. Judge projects by additional investment, durable jobs, skills, environmental costs and subsidy cost per outcome, not only announced project value.
  • International examples: US Amazon HQ2 bidding; German Länder innovation competition; Australian/Canadian regional investment promotion. Institutional settings differ. The sheet's Micron/State list is imprecise, and the Vedanta–Foxconn proposal must not be described as a completed joint semiconductor plant after its partnership collapse.
  • Better federal compact: transparent incentive disclosure, reliable utilities/logistics, fair land processes, stable rules, human capital and inter-State cooperation. Competitive and cooperative federalism can coexist; poorer States need enabling capacity. Practice key States improving governance/attracting investment (b); no exam year.
