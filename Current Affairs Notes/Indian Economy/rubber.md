# Natural rubber: cultivation, industry and governance

## Crop and production
- **Hevea brasiliensis**, native to Amazonian South America; latex is tapped from bark. A warm humid tropical crop: roughly **200–300 cm rainfall, 25–34°C, ~80% humidity**, deep well-drained acidic soils (approximately **pH 4.5–6**); flooding/high winds damage plantations.
- Kerala leads Indian production; Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Tripura, Assam and Meghalaya also contribute. Demand, especially from tyres/automotive industry, exceeds domestic output and requires imports. Source's **India third-largest producer/fourth consumer** and **Kerala >90%** claims are unreliable without a dated statistical table; do not memorise them.
- Other uses: gloves, footwear, seals/hoses, conveyor belts and consumer goods. Rubber's American origin is distinct from its later colonial transfer and present Asian production concentration.
## Constraints and response
- Untapped trees, labour shortages, weak prices and maintenance reduce output; source estimates **20–25% untapped** need a survey base. Heavy rain/dry spells affect tapping/yield; adapt clones, labour-saving techniques and training.
- EUDR raises traceability/deforestation-compliance requirements for relevant exports; expansion in the Northeast must assess ecological suitability and land/community rights.
- **Rubber Board:** statutory body under **Rubber Act 1947**, Ministry of Commerce and Industry, headquarters **Kottayam**; research, extension, training and development. Nutrient deficiency, including phosphorus deficiency, is a management problem—not a requirement that soil must be low in phosphorus.
- Old World = Europe/Asia/Africa; New World = Americas in historical European terminology. Indigenous American societies existed long before European arrival. 2019 PYQ group **tobacco, cocoa, rubber (a)** is American-origin.

## Kerala: beetle–fungus plantation threat
- July report identifies **Euplatypus parallelus**, a wood-boring ambrosia beetle of Central/South American origin, with **Fusarium ambrosium and F. solani** (the PDF writes “F. ambrosia”) in rubber plantations around **Iritty–Kannur**. It reports first Indian detection in **Goa cashew in 2012**; the new association is reported locally, not proof all Indian plantations are affected.
- Beetle tunnels carry fungal inoculum; fungi grow inside wood and provide food for beetles/larvae (**mutualism**). Tissue/xylem damage can cause leaf fall, trunk drying, reduced latex and tree death; deep galleries/systemic infection complicate treatment.
- Monitoring, sanitation/removal of infected material, appropriate traps and evaluated plant-protection measures need region-specific coordination among farmers, scientists and government. Antagonistic fungi/beneficial bacterial consortia are **proposed biological approaches**, not proven universal cures. Other woody hosts cited include cashew, coffee, mango, coconut and teak.
- The source's possible opportunistic Fusarium risk in immunocompromised people does not establish a demonstrated plantation-worker outbreak; assess occupational exposure separately.
- Taxonomic spelling: **Fusarium ambrosium**, also classified as *Neocosmospora ambrosia*; do not confuse ecological “ambrosia” with the printed species epithet. [EPPO taxon record](https://gd.eppo.int/taxon/FUSAAM/distribution/IN).
