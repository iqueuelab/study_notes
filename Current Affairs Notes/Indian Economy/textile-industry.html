Source-era trade and policy

  • SourceFY2023–24 textile/apparel exports~US$34.1 billion versusUS$33.4 billionFY2019–20; garment and cotton/home-textile segments moved differently. Values, volumes and growth periods must match.
  • Raw-material availability/cost, quality-control orders, logistics, skills and preferential trade access affect competitiveness; QCOs need quality benefits balanced with input availability.
  • Traceability, cleaner processing, renewable power, water efficiency and recycling help respond to buyer sustainability requirements. PMMITRA infrastructure and production incentives require implementation/results assessment.

-2030 global fibre-demand figures and projected market values are forecasts, not achieved production.

Circular fashion

  • Source examples: Panipat textile recycling, Bengaluru waste-management innovations and Tiruppur cleaner production/wastewater work.
  • Repair → reuse/rental → upcycling → fibre recycling, where feasible; match each route to material quality and demand.
  • Mixed fibres, sorting, dyes, water/energy and transport affect actual benefits. Recycled textiles are not automatically impact-free; support worker safety, viable markets and responsible collection.

June 2025: competitiveness debate and targets

  • Industry report says export value has remained around US$37–42 billion, with volume shrinking; US$100 billion exports/US$350 billion industry by 2030 are ambitions, not realised output. Source's 17% CAGR and US$100 billion capacity investment are industry calculations/estimates, dependent on base year and scope.
  • Raw-material/power/labour/finance costs, lead times, fragmented inter-State fibre-to-fashion chains and detailed compliance limit scale. Compare productivity, quality and logistics, not wages alone. Cotton-price stabilisation fund, cheaper MSME loans, freer input trade and labour-compliance reform are recommendations in this issue, not newly enacted schemes.
  • Recall PM MITRA, textile PLI, TUFS and SAMARTH have differing mandates/status; programme name does not prove every firm receives incentives or every park is operating. QCOs support quality but can constrain competitively priced polyester/viscose imports.

Complementary source data: keep dates/definitions

  • 4.5 crore employment concerns the broad textile sector, not only cotton; 60 lakh cotton growers separate. Graphic cites 130.61 lakh ha/40% world cotton area, world 324.16 lakh ha; undated area estimates do not establish June production share or yield. India cultivates Gossypium arboreum, herbaceum, barbadense, hirsutum; cotton zones broadly north/central/south.
  • 24% cotton share/second-largest producer, second MMF producer, MSME 80%, MMF consumption 3.1 kg/person versus China 12/North America 22.5, all-fibre 5.5 vs global 11.2 are source-era figures with unspecified matching reference years. Reliance—polyester; Grasim—viscose; “sole domestic viscose producer” is product/category-dependent.
  • Source growth FY2015–19 textiles 0.4% CAGR/apparel 7.7%; FY2020–24 −1.8%/−8.2% lacks an explicit volume/value series. Its 2/2.3% GDP, 11/12% exports, 13% industrial output use mixed snapshots; do not combine as one current measure.
  • FY2020→FY2024 source exports: apparel US$15.5→14.5 billion; cotton/home textiles US$10.2→12.3 billion; total 33.4→34.1 already recorded. US/EU major markets; brand examples JCPenney/Gap/Decathlon, home-textile firms Welspun/Trident. Claimed Vietnam US$40 billion apparel (2023) may conflate apparel with wider textiles.
  • Sustainability: traceability, clean power, recycling and responsible sourcing; source 16 EU rules in 2021–24 needs instrument-level verification. Recycling-market India US$400 million/global US$7.5 billion, 148 million tonnes waste by 2030 and under 25% recycled are projections/undefined-scope estimates, not guaranteed outcomes. Assist MSMEs with standards/finance while measuring actual environmental performance.

September GST reform and man-made fibre inputs

  • Inverted duty structure: higher tax on inputs than outputs leaves accumulated input credit and can strain working capital, especially MSMEs. Clipping highlights PTA, MEG and PET scrap, recycling into polyester/MMF and slow refunds; estimated 1.25 lakh tonnes/month PET-recycling capacity is a source industry estimate, not verified actual monthly output.
  • The PDF's generic 18% inputs/5% finished textiles simplifies product-specific HSN rates. It predates implementation of the September reform; 56th GST Council, 3 September 2025, recommended MMF 18%→5%, MMF yarn 12%→5%, effective with relevant notifications from 22 September. At this issue date, these changes were announced/prospective. Textiles Ministry response, September 17 backgrounder.
  • Lower fibre/yarn rates reduce one inversion; they do not prove every upstream chemical/recycled-feedstock mismatch or refund delay disappears. Distinguish fibre, yarn, fabric and chemical inputs before assessing tax neutrality, competitiveness and circular-economy effects.

December 2025: PMMITRA and regional value chains

  • The Gwalior summit links PM Mega Integrated Textile Regions and Apparel (PMMITRA) Parks to cotton demand and incomes in Malwa/Chambal. Better farm prices and employment are expected benefits, not guaranteed results for every farmer.
  • Cabinet's 2021 scheme: seven parks, ₹4,445 crore original outlay; integrates spinning, weaving, processing/dyeing, garments, testing, logistics and common infrastructure. 5 F: farm→fibre→factory→fashion→foreign. Source graphic describes State/Centre SPVs and PPP development; a planned park is not an already completed end-to-end ecosystem.
  • Large integrated sites aim to reduce fragmented logistics and improve competitiveness. Combine anchor investment/MSME links with cotton quality, worker skills/welfare, water and effluent safeguards; physical co-location alone does not ensure global competitiveness.

April–October 2025: technical-textile export decline

  • Council data reported US$1.95bn versus US$1.97bn, a 1.2% dollar decline; rupee-denominated exports +2.3%. Currency basis can change the sign of growth. The PDF links weakness to US tariffs; this is industry attribution, not a quantified causal estimate.
  • Source destinations US 25.9%, Germany 6%, UK 5%; exports to US −15% year-on-year. FIBC (flexible intermediate bulk container) bags, slings, ropes, gauze/wipes etc ~60%; technical fabrics 27.7%. Embedded article reports October US$249.7m versus US$253.5m; keep month and seven-month comparisons separate.
  • Technical textiles are defined by performance/function, not just fibre: geotextiles for roads/erosion control, medical/protective textiles, packaging, industrial/agro/transport applications. They can use natural or synthetic fibres, woven/nonwoven/knitted structures; cotton/silk/wool are not inherently excluded, contrary to the PDF's comparison.
  • Client-specific specifications limit rapid redirection to other markets; diversification needs certification, design/quality, market intelligence, trade negotiations and domestic demand, alongside scale/logistics. Vietnam/Bangladesh facing weak demand does not by itself mean they could absorb India's exports.
