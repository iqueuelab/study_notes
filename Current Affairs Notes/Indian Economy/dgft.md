# DGFT and foreign-trade administration

## Institutions and trade facilitation
- **DGFT**, attached office under the **Ministry of Commerce and Industry**, headquarters **New Delhi**; evolved from Chief Controller of Imports and Exports during post-1991 reforms. Implements Foreign Trade Policy, authorisations, notifications, norms and exporter obligations alongside its facilitation role.
- Source describes **24 regional offices**, also documented in a June 2024 DGFT circular; this is a dated administrative count, not an immutable number.
- **IEC** is the importer-exporter identifier issued online by DGFT, aligned with PAN. Generally required for goods trade **subject to exempt categories**; services/technology have specified requirements including FTP-benefit eligibility. Source's “mandatory for all importers/exporters” omits exemptions. [FTP 2023](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/dgftprod/61d61bc2-272e-4880-b96c-c8f685a3b244/Foreign%20Trade%20Policy%202023.pdf).
## 17 May restrictions; 21 May garment-industry response
- **Notification 07/2025–26**, effective immediately: Bangladesh ready-made garments barred through land ports; imports permitted through **Nhava Sheva and Kolkata seaports**. Other specified products face specified land-port restrictions; this is **not a blanket ban on all Bangladesh trade**. [Commerce Ministry notification summary](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2129380&lang=2&reg=48).
- Clipping: Indian/Tiruppur garment MSMEs hoped higher logistics costs and lead times would shift orders domestically; importers' response and realised order gains were still uncertain. Source estimates imported garments **12–15% cheaper**, land-to-sea shift could add **~10% costs**, imports **$634 million in 2024/~19% ten-year CAGR**, and overall 2024 textile/apparel trade **India→Bangladesh $3.2 billion vs reverse $1.07 billion**. These are industry/trade-data claims with distinct denominators, not realised benefits of the notification.
- Mains: trade facilitation and border regulation must weigh domestic manufacturing/inputs, consumer costs, logistics, lawful origin verification and neighbouring-country relations; alleged rerouting of Chinese fabric is an industry concern requiring evidence.
