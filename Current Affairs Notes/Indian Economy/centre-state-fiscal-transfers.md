# Centre–State fiscal transfers: devolution, equity and contribution

## Fiscal transfers and the GSDP-proxy debate
- Union-to-State transfers include **tax devolution, grants-in-aid and Centrally Sponsored Schemes**. **Article 280 Finance Commission** recommends vertical sharing and horizontal distribution; constitutional and scheme transfers have different conditions and purposes.
- **15th Finance Commission: 41% of the divisible pool, not 41% of gross Union tax revenue**, for 2021–26. Cesses/surcharges excluded from the pool and collection costs affect the denominator. **Article 270** sharing and **Article 271** surcharges matter for fiscal-autonomy debates. [Official XVFC summary](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1693868&lang=2&reg=3).
- Horizontal formula: **income distance 45%, population 2011 15%, area 15%, demographic performance 12.5%, forest/ecology 10%, tax/fiscal effort 2.5%**. The issue's loose reference to both 1971/2011 population should not be mistaken for the actual XVFC weights.
- Editorial proposal: use a larger **GSDP share** weight as a proxy for economic contribution. Tax booked in a State can reflect corporate headquarters/PAN jurisdiction rather than the place of production/consumption; collection is not the same as final tax incidence. GSDP also has limitations and cannot itself measure every central-tax liability.
- Source correlations **GSDP/direct tax 0.75, GST 0.91, combined collections 0.81; transfers/collections 0.24**, and **₹75.12 lakh crore transfers over 2020–25**, are the article's dataset-specific calculations, not proof of causality or adopted FC criteria. Maharashtra's **40.3% collections /6.64% transfers**, UP **15.81%**, Bihar **8.65%**, WB **6.96%** use that reported aggregate, not necessarily statutory horizontal tax-devolution shares.
- Proposed GSDP weighting could favour Maharashtra/Gujarat/Karnataka/Tamil Nadu and reduce relative shares elsewhere, depending on weights. This remains a proposal; balance **equalisation, expenditure needs, demographic performance, fiscal effort and incentives** rather than treating redistribution to poorer States as inherently illegitimate. Transparent transfer denominators and greater predictable untied finance can improve cooperative federalism.
