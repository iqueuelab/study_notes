# UJALA and efficient lighting

## Design
- **Unnat Jyoti by Affordable LEDs for All**, launched **5 January 2015**; earlier **Domestic Efficient Lighting Programme (DELP)**. Energy efficiency, not a renewable-generation scheme.
- **EESL with DISCOMs**, Ministry of Power; large-scale **zero-subsidy** LED distribution, bulk procurement/e-procurement and competitive pricing.
- Source eligibility: households with metered electricity connections; upfront payment or instalment arrangements where offered; **SHGs** participate in distribution.
- LED price cited **₹300–350 → ₹70–80** through scale and market transformation; programme-era comparison, not a current retail quote.
## 6 January 2025 snapshot
| Indicator | Source / official snapshot |
| --- | --- |
| Distributed LED bulbs | **36.87 crore** |
| Annual electricity saved | **47,883 million kWh** |
| Annual monetary saving | **₹19,153 crore** |
| Peak demand reduced | **9,586 MW** |
| Annual CO₂ reduction | **3.87 crore tonnes** |
- [Power Ministry's ten-year review](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2090639&lang=1&reg=1). Savings estimates are annual, not cumulative generation.
- Source's **85 lakh kWh / 15,000 tonnes CO₂** target lacks a usable timeframe and is inconsistent in scale with reported achievements; don't memorise it as the national annual target.
- **77 crore conventional bulbs** is a replacement ambition; **3.5 crore streetlights** belongs to the separate street-lighting programme, not household bulbs already distributed under UJALA.
- Mains mechanism: pooled demand → lower unit cost → wider adoption → lower electricity demand/bills/emissions; verify quality, disposal and access.
