PM E-DRIVE and electric-truck incentives

Editable notes

Scheme and July 2025 truck incentive

  • Correct expansion: PM Electric Drive Revolution in Innovative Vehicle Enhancement; Ministry of Heavy Industries scheme, notified September 2024, ₹10,900 crore. Original source-period implementation was 1 October 2024–31 March 2026, subsuming earlier EMPS spending; the sheet's “Electric Demand Reduction…” expansion is wrong. Covers electric 2/3-wheelers, ambulances, trucks, buses, charging infrastructure and testing-agency upgrades; private electric cars are excluded. Scheme portal.
  • E-truck incentive was announced 11 July 2025, not July 12 as the sheet says: ₹500 crore, about 5,600 trucks, maximum ₹9.6 lakh/truck depending on weight. Categories N2: above 3.5–12 tonnes; N3: above 12–55 tonnes. Delhi allocation: ₹100 crore/about 1,100 trucks. Purchase-price reduction is reimbursed to OEMs; scrapping an old truck is required. Warranty: battery five years/5 lakh km; vehicle/motor five years/2.5 lakh km, whichever earlier. Launch release.
  • Earlier FAME-I (2015–2019) supported hybrid/EV adoption, pilots and charging; FAME-II (2019–2024) initially had ₹10,000 crore, including public/commercial e-buses, 3-wheelers, 4-wheelers and 2-wheelers. Original FAME-II targets included 7,000 buses, five lakh 3-wheelers, 55,000 4-wheelers and ten lakh 2-wheelers; targets are not actual sales.
  • Truck incentives address high upfront cost and diesel freight pollution; charging reliability, battery life, domestic supply, electricity mix and total cost of ownership determine results. Cleaner urban air, energy security and green jobs are intended benefits; lifecycle emissions and implementation must be measured. Source's mains prompt is a practice question, not a PYQ.
