# Global Capability Centres and India–UK services cooperation

## GCC model
- **Global Capability Centres**, also called captive/global in-house centres, are MNC units abroad handling IT, BPM, R&D, AI, analytics, cybersecurity, finance, tax/legal and other core functions. Their role increasingly includes innovation/centres of excellence, beyond cost-saving back-office work.
- India's talent, English skills, lower costs, IT/startup ecosystem and established clusters (Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Gurugram, Chennai, Noida) support growth. Source **1,500+ centres/1.9 million employees** are dated, unharmonised industry estimates, not a fresh national census.
## July 2025 FTA editorial
- The editorial argues India–UK trade cooperation can support a knowledge corridor through investment, professional mobility, clear data/cybersecurity standards, IP protection and resolution of tax/regulatory uncertainty. These are potential benefits/negotiating priorities, **not proof each is a binding FTA obligation**.
- Source's final-negotiation description should be dated: negotiations had been concluded inMay 2025, while signature was still forthcoming inJuly.
- Budget 2025 proposed a national framework guiding states to promote GCCs in emerging tier 2 cities; the source discusses MeitY expert consultation and state incentives/conclaves. Proposed national support differs from an already enacted uniform GCC law.
- Policy trade-offs: state competition versus national coordination; infrastructure/talent diversity; cross-border mobility, double taxation, data localisation and legal predictability. Measure value addition, skills and innovation, not simply number of foreign-owned offices.
- [Official 6 May conclusion announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2127321&lang=2&reg=48) distinguishes conclusion of negotiations from signature/entry into force.
