# Cassava and crop diversification in Punjab

## February 2026: Jalandhar demonstration
- Jalandhar farmers reported harvesting **cassava/tapioca (*Manihot esculenta*)**, a South American-origin tropical starchy-root crop, as a diversification experiment beyond Punjab's water-intensive paddy. An experimental harvest is not proof statewide climatic/economic suitability.
- Warm establishment, planting time, a long frost-free growing period, market access and processing capacity matter. Source describes March planting and roughly ten-month cultivation under local conditions; drought tolerance does not eliminate establishment water or protect against frost.
- Potential uses: food/tapioca pearls, industrial starch and ethanol. Mature roots may remain in the ground for flexible harvest, but indefinite rot-free storage is not guaranteed. Cyanogenic compounds require appropriate variety selection and processing; raw roots should not be treated as automatically safe food.
- Compare irrigation under equivalent soil/weather/yield conditions; the source's **one-tenth paddy water** is a local reported comparison, not a universal crop coefficient. Its claim India produces half of Asia's cassava is unsupported and contradicted by the major Southeast Asian producers. Diversification needs viable procurement/markets and farmer returns alongside groundwater benefits.
