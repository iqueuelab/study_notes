# Food loss and waste: food security and climate

## 29 September: awareness and supply-chain priorities
- International Day of Awareness of Food Loss and Waste is **29 September**. **Food loss** occurs before retail, including harvest/storage/transport; **food waste** concerns retail/food-service/household stages. Saving food also saves embedded water, energy, land, labour and emissions.
- **SDG 12.3:** by 2030 **halve per-capita global food waste at retail/consumer levels**, while **reducing food losses along production/supply chains**. It does not prescribe an identical 50% reduction for every category. Indicator 12.3.1(a) Food Loss Index differs from (b) Food Waste Index. [FAO measurement framework](https://www.fao.org/platform-food-loss-waste/food-loss/food-loss-measurement/en).
- Source estimates annual Indian losses ~₹1.5 trillion/~3.7% agricultural GDP, fruit/vegetables 10–15%, paddy 4.8%, wheat 4.2%, and an FAO–NIFTEM–GCF assessment of 30 crop/livestock categories yielding ~33 million tonnes CO₂-equivalent. These are source-reported study estimates with unharmonised reference periods, not new exact 2025 national inventory totals. “One-third globally” is a longstanding broad shorthand, not directly interchangeable with stage-specific modern FAO/UNEP estimates.
- Fragmented chains, poor handling, storage/roads/cooling gaps and limited finance/technology increase losses. Solutions: pre-cooling/cold chains and refrigerated transport, suitable crates/packaging, silos/moisture control, solar cooling, demand forecasting and IoT monitoring; **PM Kisan SAMPADA Yojana** supports processing/value-chain infrastructure.
- Safe surplus redistribution/food banks first; unavoidable waste to suitable animal feed, compost or bioenergy with safeguards. FAO's **FLAPP** supports food-loss reporting/analysis; source's 2023/30-country claims need programme-specific verification. Government, businesses, researchers, civil society and consumers share responsibility; integrate measured loss reduction into food/climate policy.
