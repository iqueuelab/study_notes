# Fisheries: livelihoods, sustainability and governance

## Sector
- Marine capture, inland capture and aquaculture are distinct; source's production rank is dated and should not be treated as a permanent ranking.
- Fisheries support food, exports and millions of livelihoods. Estimated resource potential is not a sustainable annual catch entitlement.
- Constitution: State List 21 covers fisheries; Union List 57 covers fishing/fisheries beyond territorial waters.
## Policies and constraints
- **PMMSY2020; National Policy on Marine Fisheries 2017. National Fisheries Policy 2020 was a draft**, not automatically a final enacted framework.
- Cold chains, landing/processing facilities, credit, skills and traceability support value addition; fishers' safety/social protection matter with export growth.
- Climate change, habitat degradation, illegal/unreported/unregulated fishing, bycatch and excessive effort need ecosystem-based management.
- Bottom trawling and deep-sea fishing are not synonymous; assess gear, habitat and stock impacts. Neighbouring-country maritime disputes require lawful coordination.

## October 2025: MSC certification and export diversification
- Source reports preparations for roughly ten Indian marine fisheries/seafood varieties to seek Marine Stewardship Council certification by 2026, with PMMSY support for certification costs. This is a reported target, not proof ten fisheries already certified; a species name alone is not the certified unit of assessment.
- MSC is an independent international non-profit, founded in 1997 by WWF/Unilever. Its wild-capture sustainability assessment concerns healthy stocks, ecosystem effects and effective management; independent assessors, surveillance and traceability/chain of custody support the blue ecolabel. Do not apply wild-capture MSC certification indiscriminately to all farmed shrimp.
- Stock assessments, bycatch/turtle safeguards, fishing-effort data and management improvements can open premium markets and reduce single-market dependence. The issue's 30% price/income benefit is an expectation, not a guaranteed return for every fisher, and certification cannot automatically remove US tariffs or separate import restrictions.
- Costs, fragmented small-scale operations, limited stock data, audits, cold chains and market access can exclude small fishers; shared certification support and transparent benefits matter. Source $8-billion exports, GDP and employment shares are dated approximations, not one harmonised sector account. Avoid assigning every US tariff to environmental non-compliance.
- [MSC standard and independent assessment](https://www.msc.org/en-us/standards-and-certification/fisheries-standard).

## November 2025: value addition and FAO cooperation
- **World Fisheries Day, 21 November**: India's 2025 celebration theme **“India’s Blue Transformation: Strengthening Value Addition in Seafood Exports”**. It is the Indian event theme, not necessarily one universal FAO theme. [Department of Fisheries](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2191658&lang=2&reg=48).
- Historical reference points: Indian fish production **17.54 million tonnes in 2022–23**; FAO SOFIA 2024 data for **2022** give **92.3 million tonnes global capture** and **130.9 million tonnes aquaculture including algae**. India's **10.23 million-tonne aquatic-animal aquaculture** figure is a differently scoped series; avoid direct comparison with an algae-inclusive total. Source's roughly **30 million livelihoods** is an estimate, not a census count.
- FAO collaboration: **Bay of Bengal Programme** for small-scale fisheries, safety and post-harvest improvement; **BOBLME** ecosystem-based management and IUU control; GEF-supported sustainable aquaculture work in **Andhra Pradesh**; fishing-port technical cooperation at **Jakhau, Gujarat**, and **Vanakbara, Diu**. These add specific examples to existing PMMSY/credit/cold-chain notes.
- **IUU = illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing** (not “unreporting”). Stock assessments, appropriate gear/effort, community co-management, monitoring-control-surveillance, reduced disease/antibiotic misuse, traceability and certification support sustainability. Vessel transponders, weather tools, digital markets and Matsya Seva Kendras can improve safety/access if small fishers can afford and use them. The National Fisheries Policy 2020 remains labelled **draft** in this source.

## January 2026: diversify species and export markets
- Government advisory reported in the source responds to **tariff uncertainty, climate stress and concentration of disease risk**. Spread production/markets rather than relying predominantly on **Litopenaeus vannamei** shrimp and a few destinations; retain biosecurity, market access and farmer viability.
- Source options: inland **scampi (freshwater prawn), tilapia, pangasius**; indigenous **Penaeus indicus /P. monodon**; brackish-water **seabass and pearl spot (karimeen)**, marine cages and seaweed. This is diversification, not an instruction to abandon rohu/catla or introduce non-native fish without ecological controls.
- **Integrated Multi-Trophic Aquaculture (IMTA): fed species + organic extractive species (shellfish/deposit feeders) + inorganic extractive species (seaweeds)**; wastes/nutrients from one component support another. Match species and carrying capacity; it can improve nutrient use but does not eliminate all disease/climate risk.
- Saline inland areas in **Punjab, Haryana, UP and Rajasthan** offer site-dependent brackish-water opportunities; avoid degrading groundwater/agricultural land. Source reports outreach to **40+ countries**, including China, Russia, Iran, Vietnam and Saudi Arabia; engagement is not completed export access.
- **2 January official indigenous-species initiative** prioritised native candidates and clusters, including scampi, murrel, pabda, pengba, trout, pearl spot, marine cages and ornamental fish. [Department of Fisheries](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2210861&lang=1&reg=1).
- Embedded infographic's **India 10.23 million tonnes /world aquatic-animal aquaculture rank 2** refers to **2022 data in FAO SOFIA 2024**, not 2026 output. Source fish production **FY2020 141 lakh tonnes → FY2024 over 184 lakh tonnes**, FY2024 exports **₹60,500 crore**, per-capita consumption **5–6 →12–13 kg**, aquaculture yield **3 →4.7 tonnes/ha**, and ten-year **₹38,500-crore investment** are dated series, not present annual entitlements. Its “white-spot wiped out black-tiger farming in 2006–07” is overbroad; retain disease vulnerability without claiming the species ceased being farmed.
