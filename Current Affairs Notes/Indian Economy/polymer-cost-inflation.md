# Polymer feedstocks and cost-push inflation

## March 2026: West Asian supply shock
- Manufacturers reported polymer input prices rising about **50–60%** amid conflict-related crude/gas and logistics disruption. The clipping's projected **up to 60% plastic-goods price increase** is an industry warning, not an observed uniform retail inflation rate.
- **Petrochemical feedstock disruption → polymer costs → packaging/finished-goods costs → possible consumer-price pass-through.** Old inventory can delay repricing; margins, demand, contracts and competition affect the amount/timing passed on. Higher packaging costs can spill into food/FMCG prices.
- Key plastic families: **PE**—films/bottles; **PP**—containers/fibres; **PVC**—pipes/construction; **PS**—packaging/appliances. These are mostly fossil-derived commercial plastics, but **not all polymers are fossil-derived**: cellulose/proteins and bio-based polymers are counterexamples.
- Source bottle-price examples and hosiery increases illustrate possible pass-through, not nationwide administered prices. Exporters also face freight/input pressure even if overseas competitors face similar shocks. Undated global/Indian polymer ranks and the circular “polymers are 80% of plastics” claim are not retained as verified statistics.
- Feedstock diversification, efficient material use and recycling improve resilience; plastic-waste EPR and restrictions cover specified categories, not every plastic product.
