# GST, GST Council and appellate tribunal

## GST and appellate resolution
- **GST began 1 July 2017**, enabled by **101st Amendment Act, 2016**; destination-based indirect tax with input-tax credit, subsuming many earlier Union/State levies. The supplier generally collects/remits tax; the economic burden usually passes to consumption.
- **CGST + SGST/UTGST** generally apply to intra-State supplies; **IGST** to inter-State supplies and imports. Exports are **zero-rated**, so “IGST on exports/imports” without qualification is misleading. June source recalls nil/5/12/18/28% principal bands; exemptions/special rates exist and bands are date-dependent.
- **Article 279A GST Council:** Union Finance Minister chairs; Union Minister of State in charge of Revenue/Finance and State finance/taxation or nominated ministers participate. Quorum **half the total membership**; decisions need **three-fourths weighted votes of members present and voting**, Centre weight **one-third**, States together **two-thirds**. The graphic's simple head-count interpretation is incorrect.
- **GSTAT**, constituted under **CGST Act section 109**, hears appeals from first appellate/revisional authorities, providing a common Centre–State forum and consistent interpretation. It is the second appellate level, not a substitute for the first appeal. [GSTAT](https://gstat.gov.in/).
- Source's “tribunal may be headed by a former SC judge/HC Chief Justice” repeats an old proposal: former Jharkhand Chief Justice **Sanjaya Kumar Mishra had already become the first President in May 2024**. Appointment/constitution does not establish every bench was operational by June 2025. [President biography](https://gstat.gov.in/president).

## 3 September Council decision: prospective rate changes
- **56th GST Council** recommended **5% merit + 18% standard rates**, with a special **40% demerit rate** and exemptions; main rollout planned **22 September 2025**, with specified tobacco products deferred. This supersedes August's proposal stage, not an already operative 4 September rate schedule. [Council release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2163555&lang=2&reg=3).
- Examples: cement **28→18%**; specified toiletries **18→5%**; individual life/health policies exempt; specified medicines/foods exempt or reduced. EVs remain **5%**. Not all goods, insurance or medicines become tax-free.
- Corrective spectacles **12→5%**, corrective goggles **18→5%**; non-corrective eyewear remains at its prescribed rate. The prose's blanket **28→5% spectacles** is wrong; its embedded table gives the earlier corrective-spectacle rate as 12%. [Official FAQ](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2163560&lang=2&reg=48).
- Policy goals: fewer classifications, household relief and correction of inverted duties in textiles/fertiliser inputs. Practical risks: price pass-through, business transition, state revenues and uncertain consumption/compliance response. Existing GST Council/GSTAT and 2017/2018 historical PYQ explanations apply.


## 5 September editorial: demand, pass-through and federal revenues
- Rate cuts can lift disposable purchasing power when exports/private investment are weak; impact depends on firms passing savings to consumers and household response. Budget income-tax relief is a separate policy channel.
- Source estimates **₹48,000 crore annual revenue implication**, based on **2023–24 consumption data**; the editorial questions adequacy of the estimate. It is an ex-ante static estimate, not a measured loss already incurred or the fiscal deficit itself.
- Fewer slabs can reduce classification disputes/inverted-duty distortions, while transition, state finances and compliance remain important. Council consensus illustrates cooperative federal decision-making; a PM speech alone does not change GST schedules.
- Editorial suggestion to revive the **National Anti-Profiteering Authority** is a proposal for price-benefit enforcement, not proof that body was revived. Monitor actual prices and revenue rather than assume tax cuts fully finance themselves through buoyancy.
