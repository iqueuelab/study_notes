Indian Economy

Creative economy and grassroots innovation

Editable notes

Concept and economic role

  • Creative economy draws on creativity, skills, cultural expression and intellectual property: design, crafts, architecture, publishing, audiovisual content, music and related services. Creativity generates ideas; innovation applies and scales them.
  • UNCTAD reports 2022 creative-services exports of US$1.4 trillion and creative-goods exports of US$713 billion, respectively 29% and 19% above 2017. Export totals are not total worldwide industry revenue. Source's Indian valuation, employment and export figures use inconsistent years/definitions and should not be combined into one benchmark. UNCTAD outlook.

Grassroots innovation pathway

  • Mitticool clay refrigeration illustrates electricity-free evaporative cooling (NIF); pedal-operated washing devices and amphibious bicycles illustrate locally adapted ingenuity. Their commercial potential depends on safety, usability, design and demand.
  • Scouting → prototype/testing → appropriate IP and finance → incubation/design → production → market access. NIF and GIAN can support scouting, mentoring and commercialisation.
  • Barriers: funding gaps, weak visibility and infrastructure, costly/complex IP processes, fragmented markets and limited scaling support. Indigenous knowledge also requires consent, attribution and fair benefit-sharing; formal patents do not cover every creative activity.
  • Policy options: proof-of-concept funds, PPP incubation, creative clusters, simpler IP assistance, design education, digital market access and district-level support. “One District, One Innovation” is an editorial proposal, distinct from the existing ODOP initiative.
  • World Intellectual Property Day: 26 April 2025, theme IP and Music: Feel the beat of IP. WIPO.

1 May 2025: WAVES and the orange economy

  • PM inaugurated World Audio Visual and Entertainment Summit (WAVES) at Jio World Centre, Mumbai; scheduled 1–4 May 2025, with global media/creative-industry participation. Orange economy is another framing of culture/creativity/IP-based economic activity, popularised in Latin American/IDB work. PIB inauguration.
  • “Create in India, Create for the World”: film/TV/OTT, music, performing/visual arts, crafts, design/advertising, animation, gaming and digital storytelling. India's multilingual heritage, youth and digital distribution can expand employment and cultural reach; subtitles widen audiences and small screens can have global reach.
  • Address stressed openness to world cultures/creators, human sensitivity, ethical content and creativity over robotic uniformity. These are policy messages, not proof projected jobs or revenue have already materialised. PM address.
  • Opportunities: talent/skills, production investment, exports, cultural tourism and soft power. Constraints: fair IP/licensing and creator remuneration, finance/platform access, responsible AI and preserving cultural diversity; international collaborations and awards are instruments, not outcome measures.
