# Pulses: cultivation, imports and farmer incentives

## Crops and geography
- **Gram/chickpea/chana**, lentil/masoor and dry peas are chiefly **rabi**; **tur/arhar/pigeonpea**, moong and urad are important **kharif** crops, with cultivar/region-dependent summer/rabi cultivation. Gram and chana are the same crop, not separate pulse species.
- **Arhar also fixes atmospheric nitrogen** through root-nodule symbiosis: the source's exception is false. Pulse rotation can improve soil fertility, but actual fixation depends on plant–microbe/environmental conditions. [ICAR biofertiliser crop list](https://icar.gov.in/sites/default/files/2022-06/ICAR-technologies-Biofertilizers.pdf).
- Main producing States in source map: **Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka**. Its uniform **20–27°C / 25–60 cm rainfall** cannot describe every pulse/cultivar. India is a major producer/consumer/importer; map's **25% production/27% consumption/14% trade** are dated approximations with different denominators.
- **Yellow peas = dry Pisum sativum L.**, legumes used as dal substitutes/protein ingredients; rabi sowing approximately **Oct–Nov**, harvest **Mar–Apr**, well-drained loam. Source **15–25°C / 20–40 cm** indicative, **20–25% protein** approximate. Canada/Russia are important suppliers, with Ukraine, US, Australia, France/China also cited; exact producer rankings need year and dry-pea definition.
## 31 May 2025 yellow-pea extension
- Clipping announces free import **without minimum import price/port restrictions**, subject to online import-monitoring registration, for consignments with **bill of lading on/before 31 March 2026**; previous window ran to **31 May 2025**. This supersedes rewritten “February 2025/reimposed in 2024” account. Import-policy “free” and customs **zero duty** are distinct measures. [USDA report reproducing Indian notifications 31/2025 and 16/2025–26](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/newgainapi/api/Report/DownloadReportByFileName?fileName=India+Extends+Unrestricted+Imports+of+Yellow+Peas_New+Delhi_India_IN2025-0039.pdf).
- Source reports duty-free tur extended to **31 March 2026**; chickpea/urad/lentil decisions differ by notification/date. Do not treat its blanket “no decision yet” as a verified current status for every crop.
## Import–price trade-off and policy
- Source import totals **3.31 Mt (2023), 6.63 Mt (2024)** are stated all-pulse totals, not yellow peas alone; calendar/FY labels need reconciliation. Reported prices **urad down 25% from₹100/kg, turUS$1,400→800/t, yellow peasUS$700→450/t** are market/time-specific, not universal farmgate realisations.
- Imports may contain food inflation/bridge shortages yet depress local prices and planting incentives; cereal procurement/irrigation, rainfed vulnerability and weak seed/storage/processing can discourage pulses. Ethanol-driven maize competition is an incentive concern, not proof every farmer switched.
- **NFSM pulses:** demonstrations, seed production/hubs; source **28 States+2 UTs /150 hubs /343 varieties 2014–23** are programme-period claims, not new June achievements.
- **PM-AASHA/PSS** MSP procurement and price-support instruments have scheme-specific scope; listing PDPS/private procurement does not mean all apply identically to every pulse. **Budget 2025 six-year self-reliance mission** focuses **tur, urad, masoor**, resilient/productive seeds and procurement via **NAFED/NCCF**. Announcement/budget allocation differs from full field implementation.
- Recommended stable, consulted import policy, calibrated duties/quotas where justified, MSP access, storage/processing and location-specific R&D; weigh consumer affordability and farmer returns rather than assuming protection alone guarantees self-sufficiency.

## Green gram (moong)
- **Vigna radiata, Fabaceae**: short-duration pulse, commonly **60–75 days**, fitting rotations/intercropping with maize, sorghum, cotton or sugarcane. Kharif, rabi or summer/zaid cultivation varies by region.
- Warm conditions, well-drained loam/sandy loam; the source's **25–35°C, 600–750 mm rainfall and pH 6.2–7.5** are indicative agronomic ranges, not universal requirements. Waterlogging impairs growth/nodulation.
- Rhizobial fixation and residues can improve soil nitrogen; actual fertiliser savings depend on conditions. MSP/NFSM-pulses support is relevant; irrigation/organic programmes are complementary instruments, not a guarantee of doubled income.
- PYQ revision: urad can be both kharif and rabi; moong does **not** account for nearly half of Indian pulse output. The 2020 question's historical trend statement about declining rabi pulses is false; do not present its three-decade window as a newly measured July 2025 trend.

## NITI Aayog roadmap: September 2025
- **Strategies and Pathways for Accelerating Growth in Pulses towards the Goal of Atmanirbharta** recommends region-specific clusters, agroecological technology, quality seeds and **One Block–One Seed Village** through FPOs, focusing on **111 high-potential districts**. Its domestic supply projections **30.59 Mt in 2030 /45.79 Mt in 2047** are forecasts, not achieved output. [Official release](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2163828&lang=2&reg=48).
- Source report comparison **16.35 Mt (2015–16) →26.06 Mt (2022–23)**, productivity up about 38%, import dependence **29%→10.4%** uses an earlier assessment window; it does not show dependence continued falling through FY2025. The same issue later uses **25.7 Mt** for 2022–23: preserve the reporting distinction rather than merge inconsistent estimates.
- **FY2025 6.7 Mt imports/2.04 Mt yellow peas** are source-period figures/expectations; demand growth, rainfed crop shocks and liberal import policy explain resurgence. Different year/estimate windows make these compatible with an earlier decline without proving a continuing trend.
- Climate-resilient seeds, local extension/irrigation, storage/processing, predictable procurement/import policy and FPO seed hubs address the supply gap. Existing notes cover the six-year tur/urad/masoor mission and farmer–consumer trade-off.
- **2020 PYQ revision:** urad can be kharif/rabi; moong is not half of pulse output; the question's claim of falling rabi production is false. Gram/chickpea/chana are the same crop. Annual State rankings require a specified crop year, not a timeless “2025 top five”.

## September 2025: yellow-pea imports before the Supreme Court
- The clipping reports the **Supreme Court sought the Centre's response to a petition seeking restrictions on yellow-pea imports**, alleging cheap imports depressed domestic pulse prices below MSP. The court considered recommendations from **CACP** and **NITI Aayog**; seeking a response is **not a court-imposed import ban**.
- Source petitioner cites imported peas around **₹3,500/quintal** and claimed farmer losses. These are allegations/market-period examples, not a finding that every domestic pulse price or farmer return is identical. Consumer affordability, procurement, farmer planting incentives and supply security must be considered together.
- Existing notes retain the corrected yellow-pea import window to **31 March 2026**, subject to the notification's conditions, and distinguish free import policy from zero customs duty. The repeated **December 2023–February 2025 → duties reimposed** timeline is outdated; crop-specific notifications govern. Canada's/Russia's supplier importance is not a timeless producer ranking.
- Repeated Budget mission is **six years**, with separate four-year procurement assurances; do not replace it with a four-year mission. Source **₹1,000 crore** is its budget-period allocation claim, not total mission cost. Stable calibrated imports, effective MSP procurement, resilient seeds and storage/processing support the farmer–consumer balance.

## October 2025: regenerative agriculture and self-reliance
- Editorial by **Ashok Gulati and Ritika Juneja** argues for nutrition-sensitive diversification into pulses/oilseeds, alongside soil/water restoration. Regenerative practices include rotations/intercropping, legumes' nitrogen fixation, organic matter/compost, livestock integration, soil cover and reduced avoidable chemical use; assess local yields, transition cost and ecological outcomes.
- Correct the issue's mission arithmetic: Cabinet approved **Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses**, **2025–26 to 2030–31**, **₹11,440 crore**, with **350 lakh tonnes pulse production** target by 2030–31—not 30 lakh tonnes or one combined pulses-and-oilseeds mission. Target area **310 lakh ha**, yield **1,130 kg/ha**; **four-year** procurement assurance for registered/agreement farmers' tur/urad/masoor through NAFED/NCCF in participating States differs from six-year scheme duration. [Cabinet decision](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2173547&lang=2&reg=48&s=09).
- Source estimates around **60% edible-oil** and **10–15% pulse** import dependence, **52% arable land degradation** and **0.3% soil organic carbon**. These are editorial-period claims with denominator/location/method limitations; 0.3% is not every Indian soil, and the issue's 29% Earth-land/arable equivalence is unreliable.
- Rebalance incentives/procurement toward suitable pulses/oilseeds; resilient seeds, ICAR/NARS research, Soil Health Cards, precision inputs, FPO aggregation, storage/processing, extension and predictable trade policy connect farmer returns with nutrition. AIM/agritech/private partnership examples illustrate possible support, not verified membership or investment by every named company.
