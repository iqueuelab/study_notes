# Credit ratings and rating agencies

## Meaning and regulator
- Assessment of a borrower's/debt instrument's creditworthiness and ability to pay principal/interest; applies to corporates, governments and specified debt instruments.
- India's securities credit-rating agencies are regulated by **SEBI under the CRA Regulations, 1999**, not RBI. [SEBI regulations listing](https://sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/home/HomeAction.do?cid=21&doListingAll=yes).
- Global examples: **S&P, Moody's, Fitch**. Source's 95% market-share claim needs a dated market definition.
- Source list includes CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, SMERA/Acuité, Fitch India/India Ratings and Brickwork; avoid a fixed 'six agencies' assertion or outdated names without checking the applicable registry/date.
## PYQ distinction
- **ICRA is a public limited company; Brickwork Ratings is an Indian agency.** UPSC Prelims 2022 answer **(b), 2 and 3 only**; preserve the exam's historical statements rather than infer today's licence status.
- Ratings express credit-risk opinions; they are not guarantees against default and are different from a borrower's personal credit score.
## May 2025 diagram clarifications
- Ratings combine **quantitative and qualitative analysis** and express credit-risk opinions, not guaranteed repayment or a purely quantitative score. Better perceived creditworthiness can lower borrowing costs, but market rates/liquidity also matter.
- Diagram scale: **AAA → AA → A → BBB** investment-grade bands; **BB and below** speculative, with near-default/default grades at the bottom. Agencies use different symbols; Moody's **Baa3** roughly corresponds to S&P/Fitch **BBB−**, not identical notation.
- The India sovereign-rating picture references pandemic-era risks and an undated Fitch “negative” outlook; **do not treat it as a verified May 2025 snapshot**. Rating and outlook are separate judgments.
- **2022 PYQ answer (b), 2 and 3** remains: SEBI regulates securities CRAs; ICRA public limited; Brickwork Indian. RBI recognition of rating agencies for banking prudential purposes does not transfer the CRA-registration regime away from SEBI. Source blanket accreditation across all listed instruments should not replace the applicable RBI rules.

## 14 August 2025 sovereign upgrade
- **S&P: long-term BBB− → BBB; short-term A−3 → A−2; stable outlook**, the first long-term upgrade since January 2007, an **18-year gap**. Drivers include growth resilience, fiscal consolidation, improved expenditure quality/capex and strong external/financial/corporate balance sheets. [Finance Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2156501&lang=2&reg=48).
- **BBB− remains the lowest investment-grade notch**; BBB is one notch higher. AAA is strongest. An outlook is separate from a rating; the source repeatedly miscalls BBB the lowest rung.
- Source-period macro context: Centre's deficit **9.2% of GDP in FY 2020–21 → budgeted 4.4% in FY 2025–26**; FY 2024–25 real growth **6.5%** and July 2025 CPI inflation **1.55%**. These are dated estimates/targets, not current outcomes. The source's **49–51% debt/GDP aspiration for FY 2030–31** concerns Central-government debt, unlike combined Centre/State measures.
- Better credit perception can reduce risk premia and widen capital access for government/corporates; actual borrowing rates, rupee movement and bond yields also depend on global rates, liquidity and market expectations. S&P's cited sub-6% combined deficit scenario is an assessment criterion, not a legal threshold guaranteeing an upgrade.
- Retain SEBI regulation and historical **2022 PYQ answer B (2 and 3)**. Avoid reusing obsolete fixed lists of “six” agencies or treating a rating as repayment insurance. Source practice **B (1, 2 and 4)** after correcting the rating-scale explanation.
