Indian Economy

LPG pricing and household affordability

Editable notes

How the cost builds up

International LPG benchmark → import/landed cost → rupee conversion → domestic distribution and taxes → retail price → eligible DBT → effective household cost.

  • LPG consists mainly of propane and butane, liquefied under pressure; CNG is predominantly methane stored as compressed gas. LPG chiefly serves cooking/heating; CNG commonly serves transport. Both are fossil fuels unless produced through alternative pathways.
  • Import-parity pricing represents equivalent imported supply cost: overseas price, ocean freight, insurance/port-related charges and applicable duties. Saudi Aramco's Contract Price (Saudi CP) is an important benchmark.
  • Rupee depreciation raises the rupee cost of dollar-priced imports, other things equal; appreciation has the opposite effect.
  • Bottling, domestic freight, marketing, distributor commission and applicable taxes affect delivered cost and location-wise differences.

Price is not the same as cost

  • International costs influence domestic supply economics, but domestic cooking-cylinder retail prices are modulated by government policy. They do not automatically change every month with the benchmark.
  • Commercial and domestic cylinders have different pricing patterns; do not infer one from the other.
  • PMUY's targeted DBT refill support reduces the eligible consumer's effective cost. This differs from an OMC's under-recovery—the gap between supply cost and realised regulated price.
  • February 2025 example: government reported rising Saudi CP alongside a reduction in effective PMUY prices since August 2023, illustrating incomplete pass-through. These are dated comparisons, not today's cylinder price. PIB parliamentary reply

Policy trade-off: protect clean-cooking affordability while controlling fiscal cost, targeting benefits and limiting prolonged financial stress on oil marketing companies.
