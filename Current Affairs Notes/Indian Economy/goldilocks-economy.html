Indian Economy

Goldilocks economy and household welfare

Editable notes

Aggregate balance versus lived welfare

  • Goldilocks economy: sustainable growth without overheating/recession, with low/stable inflation and supportive macroeconomic conditions. A favourable aggregate reading does not establish that all households benefit equally.
  • PDF cites May 2025 CPI2.82% versusMay 2024 4.80%; these are dated readings, not August inflation. RBI target was 4%±2 percentage points, not “a2–6% target” with no central aim. NSO June release with May final data.
  • October 2024 CPI6.21% /CFPI10.87% illustrates a period when food inflation exceeded headline inflation; food carries larger budget weight for poorer households. This does not mean food inflation was above headline in every subsequent month.
  • Real wage growth adjusts nominal gains for inflation; source examples 2023 nominal9.2%/real2.5%;2020 real−0.4% are dataset-dependent, not proof about every Indian worker. Weak real incomes constrain consumption and savings despite headline GDP growth.
  • K-shaped recovery describes divergent outcomes between better-off/organised groups and vulnerable workers/households. Taxable-income Gini, consumption Gini and wealth concentration measure different populations/variables; a decline in a tax-return Gini does not settle national inequality.
  • FY 2025–26 Union fiscal-deficit BE4.4% of GDP differs in scope from ~81% general-government debt/GDP. Debt service can squeeze fiscal space; borrowing can crowd out private credit, while well-designed public investment may also crowd investment in. Fiscal consolidation does not automatically prove that every welfare/public-investment outlay fell.
  • The source's old FY 2023–24 growth7.6%/$3.6 tn snapshot and FY25 forecast are not the latest releases available by August 2025; use the existing GDP topic's dated national-account estimates. Evaluate employment, real wages, distribution, health/education and fiscal composition together.
