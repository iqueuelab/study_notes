# Economic Survey 2024–25: indicators and policy signals

## Purpose
- Annual economic assessment prepared by **Department of Economic Affairs, Finance Ministry**, led by **Chief Economic Adviser**; usually presented before Union Budget. Policy recommendations are advisory, not spending authorisation.
- First Survey **1950–51**, separated from Budget **1964**. Chapter structure/statistical appendix can vary; “Part A/Part B” is not a permanent constitutional rule.

[Survey overview](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2025-26/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap01.pdf), [external-sector chapter](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2025-26/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap03.pdf).

- Insurance source **₹11.2 lakh crore / 7.7%** has insufficient segment/period clarity; do not treat it as a verified common total.
## Mains connections
- Private investment and infrastructure → productivity; skilling and employment → inclusive demand; prudent fiscal/financial policy → resilience.
- Disinflation helps purchasing power but aggregate growth need not imply equal household gains. Assess jobs, regional distribution, social outcomes and environmental costs alongside GDP.

## Economic Survey 2025–26: January 2026 assessment
- **DEA, Ministry of Finance; Chief Economic Adviser V. Anantha Nageswaran**. Survey recommendations diagnose policy choices; they do not themselves enact laws or authorise spending. [Official Highlights](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/Infographics%20English.pdf).
- Read **FAE = first advance estimate; PE = provisional estimate; PA = provisional actual; BE = budget estimate**. FY 2026–27 projections differ from FY 2025–26 estimates; monthly, half-year and annual figures below retain their periods.

### Growth, fiscal policy and taxation
| Indicator | Survey-era observation |
|---|---|
| Growth outlook | **FY 2026–27 real GDP projected 6.8–7.2%**; potential growth assessed around **7%** |
| FY 2025–26 FAE | Real GDP growth **7.4%**; nominal GDP **₹357.1 lakh crore**; real GVA growth **7.3%**: agriculture **3.1%**, industry **6.2%**, services **9.1%** |
| Demand composition | FY 2025–26 FAE private consumption **61.5%**, investment **30.0% of nominal GDP** |
| Central fiscal deficit | **9.2% GDP FY 2020–21 →4.8% FY 2024–25 PA; FY 2025–26 BE 4.4%**; primary-deficit BE **0.8%** |
| Revenue/tax base | FY 2021–22 to FY 2024–25 average revenue receipts/GDP **9.1%**, gross tax/GDP **11.5%**, non-corporate tax/GDP **3.3%**, versus pre-pandemic FY 2015–16 to FY 2019–20 **8.5%,10.8%,2.4%** |
| Returns filed | Income-tax returns **6.9 crore in 2021–22 →9.2 crore in 2024–25** |
| Effective capital expenditure | Union capex plus grants creating capital assets: **₹3.27 lakh crore FY 2014–15 →₹13.30 lakh crore FY 2024–25 PA; ₹15.48 lakh crore FY 2025–26 BE** |
- Global headwinds: geopolitical conflicts, weaponisation of energy/finance, technology export controls, critical-mineral competition and weaker confidence in free trade. Productivity reforms work through **capital accumulation, labour/skills/formalisation and total-factor productivity**, rather than guaranteed growth from spending alone.
- Survey links credible fiscal consolidation, revenue buoyancy, transparency and capex to **2025 sovereign-rating upgrades by Morningstar DBRS, S&P and R&I**. Centre's effective capex rose from pre-pandemic average **2.7% GDP to 4% in FY 2024–25**; **SASCI** supported States' capex around **2.4% GDP**.
- **GST rationalisation:** main rates **5% merit /18% standard**, with **40% demerit rate**; exemptions/special schedules still matter. Intended channels: simpler compliance, consumption, correction of inverted duties, working capital and formalisation. April–December FY 2025–26 **e-way bills 115 crore**, compared with **70.3 crore in FY 2022–23**, indicate transactions, not real GDP or unique firms.

### Monetary transmission, finance and inclusion
- Chart shows easing policy/liquidity conditions and expanding micro/small-enterprise credit. **September 2025 SCB GNPA 2.2%, NNPA 0.5%**, versus **March 2018 peaks 11.2% /6.0%**. Lower ratios can reflect recoveries, write-offs and advances growth; they do not establish every loan is safe.
- **Pensions, FY 2015–16 →FY 2024–25:** APY subscribers **24.6→641.3 lakh**, AUM **₹0.51→44.8 thousand crore**; NPS subscribers **97.5→198.6 lakh**, AUM **₹118.3→1,398.7 thousand crore**. Subscriber counts and assets are different measures.
- Insurance FY 2024–25: AUM **₹77.4 lakh crore**, total premium income **₹11.9 lakh crore** versus **₹8.3 lakh crore FY 2020–21**; life benefits **₹6.3 lakh crore**, non-life net incurred claims **₹1.9 lakh crore**. The life/non-life rounded chart totals need not exactly equal the rounded overall total.
- IPOs **260 in April–November 2025**, versus **320 in full FY 2024–25**: partial-year and annual counts are not directly comparable growth rates. Survey charts also show increasing primary-market resource mobilisation; equity/debt scope must match before comparing series.
- Financial reform examples: RBI consultation/periodic-review framework and consolidation of **9,000+ circulars/guidelines**, PSB MSME credit assessment, **FREE-AI** responsible-AI approach; SEBI investor tools **SEBI Check /SEBI vs SCAM**, ESG debt and electricity derivatives.
- **Legal-status correction:** the **Securities Markets Code Bill,2025** proposed consolidating **SCRA 1956, SEBI Act 1992 and Depositories Act 1996**; it had been referred to the **Standing Committee on Finance**, rather than already repealing those Acts as the Highlights imply. [Parliamentary Affairs year-end record,5 January](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2211578).
- **Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act,2025** received assent **20 December**, published **21 December 2025**; provisions include a higher FDI ceiling and regulatory/policyholder reforms. **Publication is distinct from commencement**, which section 1 leaves to notification. [Gazetted Act](https://financialservices.gov.in/beta/sites/default/files/2026-01/The-Sabka-Bima-Sabki-Raksha-Act-2025.pdf).
- **PMMY to October 2025:** disbursement **₹36.18 lakh crore across 55.45 crore loan accounts**; **69% of microloans accessed by women**, **₹15.6 lakh crore /33.4 crore accounts** for SC/ST/OBC/minority categories. Accounts/loans do not equal unique entrepreneurs or jobs.
- **PM SVANidhi 2025 impact assessment:** borrower annualised business income **+20% between 2023–25**; women **33%**, SC/ST/OBC **64%**; estimated NPA below 10%. Reported housing/food/health/education improvements are assessment findings, not guaranteed effects for every borrower.

### External sector and inflation
- **April–December 2025 merchandise exports US$330.3 bn, imports US$578.6 bn, deficit US$248.3 bn**, versus 2024 **US$322.4 bn /546.4 bn /224.0 bn**. Non-petroleum/non-gems-and-jewellery exports **US$266.9 bn**, imports **US$371.9 bn**; these are narrower categories.
- Estimated services exports **US$304.0 bn**, imports **US$152.2 bn**, surplus **US$151.7 bn** in April–December 2025; separately, **H1 FY 2025–26 CAD 0.8% GDP**, versus **1.3%** a year earlier. Services/remittances cushion the merchandise deficit; trade balance is not the whole current account.
- **Foreign reserves US$701.4 bn at 16 January 2026**; import cover **11.1 months at 9 January**; external debt/GDP **19.2% at end-September 2025**, versus **23.8% FY 2014–15**. Dates differ across indicators.
- Gross FDI **US$64.7 bn April–November 2025**, versus **US$55.8 bn** a year earlier; distinguish gross receipts, repatriation and net FDI. **US$114 bn greenfield digital project announcements,2020–24**, cover digital services/data centres/IT infrastructure; announcements are not automatically realised investment.
- Trade agreements may be **under negotiation, negotiations concluded, signed or implemented**. Survey lists eight recent agreements but explicitly labels UK/Oman awaiting implementation and NZ/EU negotiations concluded; that list is not eight simultaneously operational FTAs. Supply-chain integration needs domestic technological/value-addition depth.
- **Headline CPI inflation averaged 1.7% April–December 2025**; December chart **headline 1.3%, food−2.71%, core 4.62%**. Vegetables **−18.5%**, pulses **−15.1%** year-on-year; favourable supply, stocks, base effects and GST pass-through helped. Falling inflation is not necessarily a fall in the general price level.
- Precious metals explain much of core pressure: **core excluding gold/silver 2.27% in December**, versus core 4.62%. Manufacturing's relative output prices/terms of trade fell against agriculture/services, influencing nominal GVA shares; nominal share changes need not equal real-output changes. India's reported 2025 inflation reduction **1.8 percentage points** should not be read as 1.8% of the earlier rate.

### Agriculture, services, manufacturing and innovation
- **FY 2019–20 to FY 2023–24 average GVA growth:** agriculture **4.7%**, crops **4.0%**, livestock **6.1%**, fishing/aquaculture **7.2%**. Diversification should match local soil, water, rainfall and markets; calibrate ethanol prices/crop incentives with food/nutrition security and fertiliser support with agronomic needs. Per-acre/per-quintal transition support is a policy option, not a universal enacted entitlement.
- Support map: **NFSNM, edible-oil/oilseed/oil-palm missions, KVKs, FPOs/cooperatives, PMFBY, Krishonnati, Digital Agriculture Mission; irrigation, soil-health cards, fertiliser/KCC/custom hiring, livestock foot-and-mouth control and PM Matsya Kisan Samridhi Sah-Yojana; PM-KISAN, MSP and PM Maandhan**. Correct Highlights' “food and mouth” to **foot-and-mouth** disease.
- Services share of **nominal GDP 53.6% in H1 FY 2025–26**, **30% of total employment /61.9% urban employment**; services account for **51.7% EPFO employment additions** in the cited snapshot. GDP/GVA shares and payroll additions/unique jobs are different denominators.
- **FY 2022–23 to FY 2024–25 growth rates:** software-service exports **13.5%**, professional/management consulting **25.9%**, compared with **4.7% /11.9% in FY 2015–16 to FY 2019–20**. April–December 2025 port cargo **672.9 million tonnes**, air cargo **2.6 million tonnes**, air passengers **275.5 million**, rail freight **1,070.8 million tonnes**.
- Space services: **393 foreign satellites launched 2015–24**, export earnings **about US$433 million**; NSIL revenue **₹322 crore FY 2019–20 →₹3,246 crore FY 2024–25 estimate**; satellite-data-services market **US$495 million 2024**. PPPs and Earth-system-data products are proposed growth areas.
- Tourism 2024: domestic visits **+17.5%**, international arrivals **+8.9%**; medical/wellness arrivals **12% CAGR 2009–24**. Long-distance trails/marinas are proposals. Media/entertainment 2024 **about ₹2.5 trillion**, digital media roughly one-third; gaming **₹232 bn**, animation/VFX **₹103 bn**, live entertainment **over ₹100 bn**. Different components must not be summed without checking scope.
- **GCCs 1,430 FY 2018–19 →1,700 FY 2023–24**; GenAI startups **66 H1 2023 →240 H1 2024 →890 H1 2025**. Industry GVA grew **7.0% H1 FY 2025–26**, manufacturing **9.13% Q2**; medium/high-tech activity contributes **46.3% manufacturing value added**.
- Electronics FY 2024–25: production **₹11.3 lakh crore**, imports **₹8.4 lakh crore**, exports **₹3.3 lakh crore**. Mobile production **₹0.18 lakh crore FY 2014–15 →₹5.45 lakh crore FY 2024–25**, nearly 30-fold. Cement capacity/output, steel production/consumption, coal domestic supply, pharmaceuticals and vehicles show expansion in the separate sector charts; dates/units differ.
- EV-registration chart reports **62.5% CAGR FY 2019–20 to FY 2024–25**; its axis labels should not be used to confuse millions with thousands. **GII rank 81 in 2015 →38 in 2025; 1.97 lakh DPIIT-recognised startups by 31 October 2025**. Patent applications nearly doubled and trademark registrations about 1.5 times FY 2019–20 to FY 2024–25; applications/registrations differ from commercialised innovation. ASPI's **top-five in 45/64 critical technologies** is a research-output assessment, not demonstrated leadership in every production technology.

### Infrastructure, environment and climate finance
- PPP Appraisal Committee: **28 projects /₹1,04,686 crore to November FY 2025–26**, versus 15 /₹69,583 crore full FY 2024–25: approvals, not completed construction.
- National highways **91,287 km FY 2013–14 →1,46,572 km to December 2025**; operational high-speed corridors **550→5,364 km**. Average annual rail commissioning **1,499 km 2004–14 →3,118 km 2014–24**. Port total capacity **1,561→2,771 million tonnes**, cargo **1,052→1,602 million tonnes**, average container-vessel turnaround **43.44→30.08 hours**, FY 2014–15 to FY 2024–25; inland-water cargo **18 million tonnes 2013–14 →146 million 2024–25**.
- Power generating-capacity additions **40,938.6 MW April–November 2025**, versus 15,007.9 MW year earlier; rural daily supply **12.5 hours FY 2013–14 →22.6 FY 2024–25**, urban 22.1→23.4. Distribution AT&C losses **22.62→15.04%**; aggregate PAT **₹67,962 crore loss →₹2,701 crore profit** across those fiscal endpoints. Aggregate profits do not prove every distribution company profitable.
- BharatNet December 2025: **2.15 lakh connected gram panchayats, 6.95 lakh km fibre,14.07 lakh high-speed broadband connections**. Internet connections **101.78 crore /broadband 99.56 crore by September 2025**; subscriptions need not equal unique people. Wireless data realisation **₹268.97/GB 2014 →₹8.27 September 2025**, monthly use **0.06166→25.24 GB per subscriber**; rural tap-water access **over 81%**, with service functionality separately important.
- Renewables require mining, materials and embedded energy: illustrative per-GW solar **18.5 t silver,2–3 thousandt polysilicon,10,252 t aluminium**; wind **2,866 t copper**. Material assumptions are technology/ore-grade dependent, not universal lifecycle emissions coefficients. Low-carbon/non-fossil **nuclear is not renewable**.
- Existing nuclear notes retain **₹20,000 crore SMR mission, at least five indigenous SMRs by 2033,100 GW by 2047 target**; Survey capacity snapshot **8,780 MW**. **SHANTI Act 2025** assent/commencement/licensing must remain separate; a Highlights statement about private participation is not an unconditional operating licence.
- Storage need **around 411 GWh by 2031–32**, distinct from installed capacity. Policy framework recognises storage/ancillary services/infrastructure status; **₹18,100 crore ACC PLI for 50 GWh manufacturing** differs from **VGF targetingabout 43 GWh BESS deployment**. Waivers and support remain subject to scheme conditions.
- Environmental reform map: **PARIVESH 3.0**, post-clearance monitoring, consent-guideline reform, critical-mineral rules, **Environment Audit Rules 2025**, updated industry categories/green-belt norms, EPR/circular-economy plans across 10 waste categories and selected single-use-plastic restrictions. Decriminalising specified offences does not remove substantive standards; Water Act amendments must not be indiscriminately attributed to Jan Vishwas 2023.
- Sustainable-finance sequence: **SEBI green-bond disclosures 2017; BRSR 2021 (top 1,000 listed firms; voluntary FY 2021–22/mandatory FY 2022–23); sovereign-green-bond framework 2022; BRSR Core/RBI green deposits 2023; 2025 ESG debt/BRSR Core adjustments**. State climate plans increasingly stress district risks, adaptation, budgets, local institutions and measurable resilience, rather than isolated mitigation projects.

### Human capital, employment, villages and cities
- NEP access targets: **school GER 100% by 2030; higher-education GER 50% by 2035**. Snapshot **about 25 crore pupils,14.7 lakh schools, over 1 crore teachers**; GER **90.9 primary /90.3 upper-primary /78.7 secondary /58.4 higher-secondary**. Higher education **4.46 crore students**, GER **29.5%2022–23**, versus 27.1%2019–20; enrolment ratios are not completion/learning measures.
- Equity/learning examples: **500 PM-JANMAN hostels approved; 2,682 KGBVs upgraded; 692 Dharti Aaba hostels sanctioned**; multilingual content/material in 22 languages, NIPUN/PARAKH foundational learning, **5+3+3+4**, DIET/SCERT capacity, APAAR/Vidya Samiksha Kendra. Fifteen foreign institutions **expected** to establish campuses are not fifteen already operating campuses.
- Life expectancy **70.3 years in 2023**, IMR down **37% over a decade**. Daily per-capita intake 2009–10→2023–24: rural **2,147→2,212 kcal /59.3→61.8 gprotein**, urban **2,123→2,240 kcal /58.8→63.4 g**. Averages do not eliminate undernutrition/obesity or distribution gaps.
- Prevention/health tools: Eat Right/Fit India/oil-reduction awareness, AI screening/clinical support, e-Sanjeevani, **14.32 crore U-WIN registrations**; **Tele-MANAS 14416 /NIMHANS SHUT clinic**, school screen-time/safety guidance. **1.8 lakh+Ayushman Arogya Mandirs,42.78 crore Ayushman cards** are service/administrative snapshots, not unique successful treatments.
- **December 2025 PLFS(age 15+, current weekly status):LFPR 56.1%, UR 4.8%**; annual female usual-status LFPR **23.3%2017–18 →41.7%2023–24**, UR **5.6→3.2%**. Do not mix monthly CWS and annual usual-status measures.
- Four Labour Codes were **enacted 2019–20; made effective 21 November 2025**, not enacted on that date. Benefits/requirements depend on rules, coverage and implementation; fixed-term work, portability/gig recognition, floor-wage framework, safety/creches and consent-based women's night work must retain safeguards. **77 lakhpotential jobs** is a citedSBI projection, not employment already generated. [Labour Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2194018&lang=1&reg=1).
- Participation/skilling: safe transport, Sakhi Niwas/Thozhi housing, care services, returnships and WE-Hub/MAVIM/Kudumbashree; **1,000 ITI upgrade programme**, Flexi-MoU, **PMKVY 4.0**, NSQF/sector councils and apprenticeships. **NAPS 2.0** supports 25% stipend via DBT; **NATS 2.0 over 12 lakhstipend transfers** are transfers, not unique jobs. Skill loans/vouchers/NSDC Skill Impact Bond link choice/finance with verified placement/retention.
- Rural: World Bank extreme poverty **5.3%2022–23 at US$3/day(2021 PPP)**, not a nominal-dollar wage measure; **99.6% eligiblehabitations connected,2.9 crore+houses** in cited programme snapshots. **SVAMITVA 3.28 lakhvillages /2.76 crorepropertycards; 500 Namo Drone Didi drones 2023–24; 629 RSETIs across 616 districts/33 States-UTs**. DDU-GKY, women SHGs/DAY-NRLM, community resource persons and Samarth/Meri Panchayat/eGramSwaraj connect livelihoods, local revenue and accountable planning.
- AI choices: frontier vs applications, rapid scale vs labour inclusion, proprietary vs open models, central data centres vs distributed compute, regulation vs innovation. Develop earn-and-learn/credit-bearing experience, adaptable skills and human-centred occupations. Proposed data framework seeks **openness, auditability and domestic value** through local training, upskilling/R&D and incentives; this is not a newly enacted universal localisation law.
- Urban agglomeration improves matching, shared infrastructure and knowledge spillovers. Higher FSI/land productivity and Bhu-Aadhaar/registration reform require accompanying mobility, sanitation and services; manage sprawl and distributional impacts. Survey cites 2024 rush-hour losses **Bengaluru 117 h /Mumbai 103 h /Delhi 76 h**.
- Priorities: reliable/digital buses, financed e-bus fleets, shared/last-mile mobility, TOD/station value capture, parking/congestion management. **Municipal waste 2024–25:door-to-door collection 98% urbanwards, processing 80%**, versus 16% processing 2014–15; coverage is not proof all waste safely disposed. Indore combines segregation, leadership, community communication, nudges and penalties. Civic order requires predictable rules, inclusive service design, participation and integration of informal livelihoods.
