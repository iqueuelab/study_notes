# LPG pricing and household affordability

## How the cost builds up

**International LPG benchmark → import/landed cost → rupee conversion → domestic distribution and taxes → retail price → eligible DBT → effective household cost.**

- LPG consists mainly of **propane and butane**, liquefied under pressure; CNG is predominantly methane stored as compressed gas. LPG chiefly serves cooking/heating; CNG commonly serves transport. Both are fossil fuels unless produced through alternative pathways.
- Import-parity pricing represents equivalent imported supply cost: overseas price, ocean freight, insurance/port-related charges and applicable duties. Saudi Aramco's **Contract Price (Saudi CP)** is an important benchmark.
- Rupee depreciation raises the rupee cost of dollar-priced imports, other things equal; appreciation has the opposite effect.
- Bottling, domestic freight, marketing, distributor commission and applicable taxes affect delivered cost and location-wise differences.

## Price is not the same as cost

- International costs influence domestic supply economics, but **domestic cooking-cylinder retail prices are modulated by government policy**. They do not automatically change every month with the benchmark.
- Commercial and domestic cylinders have different pricing patterns; do not infer one from the other.
- PMUY's targeted **DBT refill support** reduces the eligible consumer's effective cost. This differs from an OMC's **under-recovery**—the gap between supply cost and realised regulated price.
- **February 2025 example:** government reported rising Saudi CP alongside a reduction in effective PMUY prices since August 2023, illustrating incomplete pass-through. These are dated comparisons, not today's cylinder price. [PIB parliamentary reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2118208&lang=1&reg=37)

**Policy trade-off:** protect clean-cooking affordability while controlling fiscal cost, targeting benefits and limiting prolonged financial stress on oil marketing companies.

## March 2026: supply shock and diversification
- West Asian disruption exposed India's dependence on imported cooking gas. The issue reports commercial shortages affecting restaurants/hotels and officials seeking alternative supplies from the US/Norway; longer voyages create a transitional supply gap. Its roughly two-month estimate is a reported round-trip logistics estimate, not a universal one-way voyage time or a guaranteed recovery date.
- Refineries were directed to maximise LPG and prioritise household cooking requirements; the issue's initial output increase of about 10% is a dated estimate. Households and essential institutions take priority when supply is constrained, leaving commercial users exposed.
- **LPG and LNG are different supply chains:** LPG is chiefly propane/butane, stored as liquid under pressure; LNG is predominantly methane, liquefied near −162°C. Qatar's importance for LNG must not be converted into a claim that all Indian LPG comes primarily from Qatar. Diversify suppliers/routes, expand storage/terminals and preserve affordable clean cooking.
- Image background: PSU oil companies concluded a **one-year 2.2 MTPA US Gulf Coast LPG contract for 2026**, announced **17 November 2025**. Officially this was about **10% of annual LPG imports**, not 10% of total demand as the graphic implies; it predates the March crisis. [Petroleum Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190755&lang=1&reg=3).


## 13 March 2026: refinery response and structural import dependence
- The issue reports FY2024–25 LPG production about **13 million tonnes** against consumption about **31 million tonnes**—approximately **40% domestic/60% imported**. Temporary higher output does not remove the structural production gap; limited buffer storage increases vulnerability but does not itself cause the entire import requirement.
- LPG is recovered in natural-gas processing and petroleum refining; **fluid catalytic cracking** converts heavier hydrocarbons into lighter products including LPG/gasoline. Adjusting refinery yields competes with propylene/petrochemical and other product streams, so capacity cannot instantly be redirected without trade-offs.
- The **8 March order** diverted relevant propane/butane/propylene/butene streams towards the LPG pool. By **11 March**, government reported domestic LPG production about **25% higher** and household priority, with hospitals/educational institutions prioritised among non-domestic users and an IOCL–HPCL–BPCL committee reviewing commercial allocation. These are dated government supply claims, not proof shortages had disappeared. [PIB briefing](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2238617&lang=1).
- Stocks, terminals, transport, supplier diversification and predictable allocation complement refinery measures. Strategic crude-oil reserves are not interchangeable with dedicated LPG storage.
