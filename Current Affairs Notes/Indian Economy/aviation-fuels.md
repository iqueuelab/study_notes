# ATF and sustainable aviation fuel

## Fuel and emissions
- Conventional **ATF** is jet fuel, generally kerosene-based. **SAF** uses approved waste/biomass or synthetic pathways and certified blends; arbitrary bio-oils cannot be put directly into aircraft.
- SAF can reduce **life-cycle emissions**; combustion still emits CO₂. Feedstock, land/water use, renewable energy, transport and processing determine net savings.
- Challenges: cost, reliable sustainable feedstock, competing uses, certification, scale and refinery/airport compatibility.
- India's announced **indicative international-flight blending targets: 1% in 2027, 2% in 2028, 5% in 2030**, not 1% in 2025. CORSIA concerns international aviation emissions; a target is not evidence all flights already comply. [Civil Aviation Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2255021&lang=1&reg=3).
- Mains: combine efficient aircraft/operations, credible life-cycle accounting, domestic SAF scale-up and safeguards against food/land competition.
