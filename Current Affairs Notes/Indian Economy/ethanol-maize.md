# Ethanol blending: maize demand and food–fuel balance

## Programme and dated source estimates
- **E20 = 20% ethanol blended petrol**, by volume; policy advanced target to **2025–26** from original 2030. February source forecast “within two months” is not proof then achieved.
- Source annual requirement **~1, 100 crore litres**, installed capacity **~1, 600 crore litres**: capacity ≠ realised production/OMC procurement or achieved blend.
- Proposed supply: FCI/broken rice **~110 crore litres**, sugar/high-grade molasses **~400**, maize **350–400**; these figures do not sum to total requirement and are not a complete reconciled supply balance.
- Sugar-diversion restrictions increased maize interest; source FCI rice price **₹28 → ₹22.50/kg**, maize **42 million tonnes FY2024–25**, **9 million tonnes** ethanol demand are contemporary estimates/policy context, not final harvest data.
## Supply-chain effects
- Growing States include **Karnataka, MP, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Rajasthan, Bihar, UP**. Stable demand may increase farmer returns but alter acreage and prices for poultry, livestock and starch users.
- Source maize imports **US$33 million FY2023–24 → US$188 million April–November 2024**; unequal periods. April–June ₹100 crore is a separate rupee snapshot.
- **DDGS**, distillers' dried grains with solubles, recovers feed value; not a complete one-for-one substitute for every maize feed formulation.
- Domestic fuel can reduce crude import exposure; source **₹6, 000 crore per 100 crore litres** is a scenario-sensitive claim, dependent on petroleum/ethanol prices, energy content and imported inputs.
## Sustainable response
- Diversify suitable feedstocks; cellulosic/second-generation agricultural-residue ethanol, flexible distilleries, fair procurement and transparent stock assessments.
- Assess water use, food/feed prices, land competition and lifecycle emissions; biofuel is not automatically carbon neutral. Ensure engine/fuel compatibility and quality standards.
- Later confirmation: **July 2025 monthly blending ~19.93%**, not evidence February already E20. [Programme chronology](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2283118&lang=1&reg=3).
## Ethanol's environmental trade-offs
- First-generation ethanol uses food/feed crops or sugar/molasses; second-generation uses lignocellulosic biomass. EVs and hydrogen are not biofuels.
- Source 8–12 litres water/litre ethanol refers to reported plant use, not automatically total crop-plus-factory water footprint. Feedstock, location and recycling change results.
- Require effluent treatment, air-pollution controls, water monitoring and meaningful applicable public consultation; allegations near a plant need investigation.
- Residue logistics, stubble management and DDGS feed recovery can help. Tailpipe CO reductions in trials do not prove identical lifecycle GHG savings everywhere.

## May 2025: Andhra Pradesh environmental concerns
- The issue revisits local opposition to ethanol plants over water depletion and pollution in the Krishna-region agricultural economy. Claims of effluent contamination, ineffective Pollution Control Board action and Lokayukta findings are **source-reported allegations/observations**, not proof every distillery breaches its consent conditions.
- NITI-linked vehicle-test figures of **30% lower CO for four-wheelers /50% for two-wheelers** are not equivalent to lifecycle CO₂ reductions. Aldehyde emissions require pollutant-specific assessment; the source incorrectly treats acetaldehyde, formaldehyde and acrolein as interchangeable carcinogens.
- Ethanol-only project clearance relaxations/exemptions have defined conditions; a bypassed public hearing does not abolish effluent, emissions or water-consent obligations. Evaluate applicable project rules instead of assuming every clearance omits pollution limits.
- Pages4–6 repeat earlier maize figures and a stale “E20 within two months” forecast: installed capacity, intended annual supply and realised blending remain distinct. Do not re-date February-era estimates as May achievements. Pilot projects preceded the **2003 programme rollout**; **CCEA advanced the target on 21 December 2020**, followed by the **June 2021 roadmap/public announcement** and **2022 National Biofuels Policy amendment to 2025–26**. Distinguish these steps. [NITI roadmap](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-07/Roadmap-For-Ethanol-Blending-In-India-2020-25.pdf).

## August 2025: vehicle compatibility and consumer confidence
- Ethanol has lower energy per litre than petrol; blending can reduce mileage, with the size of the effect depending on engine tuning and vehicle. Fuel-system materials, corrosion resistance, calibration and manufacturer certification matter; a Bharat Stage emissions label alone does not certify every vehicle's E20 compatibility.
- Source distinguishes older-vehicle concerns from E20 material-compatible vehicles introduced from2023; avoid its blanket claim that every BS2 vehicle is universally safe at E15. Flex-fuel capability has a specified certified blend range, not automatically every ratio for every model.
- Editorial seeks clear manufacturer guidance, fuel-quality enforcement, transparent pricing/savings, older-vehicle mitigation and insurance clarity. E27 is described as a standards proposal, not proof universal rollout; source's **US$10billion annual import savings** and offsetting fertiliser bill are separate claims, not a verified net foreign-exchange account attributable solely to ethanol.
- C-heavy molasses, broken/FCI rice and maize illustrate feedstock choices; “surplus” depends on stocks and competing food/feed demand. Practice **A (1 only)**; the PDF swaps the numbered statements in its explanation, despite the correct final answer.
