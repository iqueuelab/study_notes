# Tamil Nadu Space Industrial Policy

## Policy status and aims
- **Tamil Nadu Space Industrial Policy 2025**; Cabinet approval **17 April 2025**, correcting the source’s **2024** label. Source describes it as the third State policy after Karnataka/Gujarat. Aim: **₹10,000 crore investment and 10,000 direct/indirect jobs over five years**—targets, not realised investment/jobs. [Official policy](https://storage.investingintamilnadu.com/Guidance/Uploads/Others/indus_policy.pdf), [official policy listing](https://www.investingintamilnadu.com/business-in-tamil-nadu/policy-notifications/tamil-nadu-space-industrial-policy-2025).
- Manufacturing/R&D, launch/satellite systems, space-grade electronics and services; supports startups/NGEs within **Indian Space Policy 2023** framework. IN-SPACe promotes/authorises non-government activity; ISRO research and NSIL commercial roles remain distinct.
## Why the State has an industrial base
- **ISRO Propulsion Complex, Mahendragiri, Tirunelveli:** storable/cryogenic engine and stage testing/development. **Kulasekarapattinam, Thoothukudi:** forthcoming second spaceport, not already fully operational in this issue.
- Existing precision-engineering/electronics vendor ecosystem, source **over 250 ISRO vendors**; **Space Technology Incubation Centre at NIT Tiruchirappalli**; **TIDCO–IN-SPACe MoU** links firms/startups and space-grade component manufacture.
- Local startups span launch/reusable-launch technology, refuelling, data fusion and satellite applications; examples do not prove all capabilities have completed flight qualification.
## Source-listed incentive tools and applications
- Payroll subsidies for R&D/global-capability centres; **Space Bay** districts with special packages for projects below **₹300 crore**; industrial housing support **10%, up to ₹10 crore**; green capital support **25%, up to ₹5 crore**. Eligibility/conditions govern entitlement, not automatic payment to every firm.
- Training for space-grade manufacturing/electronics/data systems; applications in agriculture, fisheries, disaster management, health, urban/revenue administration and transport.
- Assess targets against actual project commitments, skilled jobs, testing infrastructure, supply-chain quality, regulatory safety and research outcomes. The source’s “Aerospace and Defence Policy 2021” reference needs title/version checking rather than a new unquestioned historical anchor.
