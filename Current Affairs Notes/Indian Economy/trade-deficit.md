# Trade deficit and external-sector resilience

## Concepts: keep the balances separate
- **Merchandise trade balance = goods exports − goods imports**; negative means a merchandise deficit. Goods + services balance differs; **current account** also includes primary income and transfers/remittances. **Trade deficit ≠ fiscal deficit ≠ current-account deficit**.
- Causes: crude oil dependence (**over 85%**), electronics/semiconductor inputs and pharmaceutical **APIs**, notably from China; domestic investment/demand, relative prices, competitiveness and global demand.
- Imported capital/intermediate goods can raise productivity/exports; consumers gain choice, competition can stimulate efficiency. Benefits of imports do not prove that any size of persistent deficit is sustainable.

## Effects: conditional, not automatic
- Import competition can displace some jobs; inputs can create others. Persistent external imbalances may pressure rupee/import prices and create financing risk.
- **FDI/equity inflows, borrowing or reserves** can finance external imbalances; a trade deficit does not automatically mean foreign borrowing or reserve depletion. Services/remittances can offset goods deficits. [RBI balance-of-payments framework](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=9479).
- Source's emphasis on lost **export-duty revenue** is misleading as a general fiscal mechanism: most exports are not subject to export duty. Broader growth/tax effects vary.
## Policy and mains structure
- Competitive logistics/ports, reliable power, quality/innovation, skills and diversification; domestic capability in critical inputs; export-market access through negotiated FTAs, including **India–UAE CEPA**.
- Public procurement and carefully targeted import substitution can help; blanket high tariffs can raise input costs, harm exports and invite retaliation. FTA benefits depend on rules of origin/utilisation, not automatic bilateral balance.
- Answer sequence: define correct balance → explain composition/drivers → weigh productive imports against vulnerability → address competitiveness, energy diversification and stable financing.
- The source's daily mains prompt is **coaching practice**, not a dated UPSC PYQ.
## Accounting distinctions
- Merchandise trade deficit: goods imports exceed goods exports. Goods-plus-services balance differs; the current account additionally includes primary and secondary income such as remittances.
- A bilateral deficit is not automatically India's overall current-account deficit. Importing capital goods or intermediate inputs can support future production and exports.
- Financing can involve FDI, portfolio/debt flows and reserves. A deficit does not inevitably mean immediate reserve collapse or borrowing of the same amount.
- Risks depend on composition, persistence, exchange rates and financing stability. Rupee depreciation can raise import costs, but export responses depend on imported inputs and demand.
- Improve competitiveness, logistics, skills, quality standards and market diversification. Higher import tariffs can also raise exporters' input costs; assess value chains and sector effects.
- Export duties are not generally India's main fiscal-revenue source; avoid confusing trade balance with government budget balance.
