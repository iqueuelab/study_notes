# Biofortified potatoes and micronutrient security

## Nutrition through crop development
- **Biofortification** raises micronutrient density during crop growth through breeding, agronomic methods or biotechnology; **food fortification** adds nutrients during processing. Biofortified does not automatically mean genetically modified.
- **CIP (International Potato Center), Lima, Peru**, develops iron-rich potatoes through **conventional crossing/selection**, combining nutrition with acceptable yield, taste and disease resistance. August coverage describes testing/adaptation for India; it does not establish nationwide commercial availability or identical yield everywhere. [CIP breeding explanation](https://cipotato.org/potato/biofortified-potatoes/).
- Iron-rich potatoes target hidden hunger/iron deficiency; orange-fleshed sweetpotatoes supply **beta-carotene (provitamin A)**. Source reports their availability in Karnataka, Assam, West Bengal and Odisha; dietary diversity, bioavailability and seed access still matter.
- **CIP South Asia Regional Centre, Singna, Agra**, Cabinet-approved in 2025; potato/sweetpotato/root-crop research, quality seed, climate resilience, pests and value chains. Construction/establishment should not be assumed completed just from approval. [CIP26 June 2025 announcement](https://cipotato.org/pressreleases/cip-announces-launch-south-asia-regional-centre-agra/).
- Correct the PDF's “61 biofortified varieties”: the **11 August 2024 release involved109 varieties of 61 crops—34 field and 27 horticultural crop species/categories**; all 109 were not necessarily biofortified. Salt/pest resistance, e.g.CR Dhan 416, is not itself proof of nutritional biofortification. Durum wheat composition depends on the named variety; “12%protein” is not a universal biofortification criterion. [2024 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2044052&lang=2&reg=48).
