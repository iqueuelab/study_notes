Pokkali, integrated coastal farming and GIAHS

Editable notes

Pokkali: saline rice–prawn rotation

  • Indigenous salinity-tolerant rice, coastal Ernakulam, Thrissur and Alappuzha, Kerala; monsoon freshwater supports rice and drier brackish season supports prawns/shrimps on the same fields. GI recognition 2008; nutrient/tidal recycling reduces external inputs. Traditional organic practice does not itself establish every marketed lot has formal organic certification or medicinal efficacy.
  • Tall rice and retained organic residues support the integrated cycle; protect local seed diversity, wetland habitat, food/income diversification and adaptation to salinisation. Source describes protein/fibre value without a comparative nutritional dataset.
  • Challenges: scarce skilled labour, tools unsuitable for marshy fields, low farm-gate returns, encroachment, pollution and altered estuaries. Options: adapted tools, fair incentives/procurement, GI branding, youth skills and wetland restoration/protection.

  • Kuttanad diagram: major rivers Pamba, Meenachil, Achankovil, Manimala feed the Vembanad landscape; Karappadam alluvium, Kari organic-rich dark soils and Kayal reclaimed lake beds are local field categories. Below-sea-level farming here does not mean all Kerala rice fields lie below sea level.

GIAHS and June 2025 snapshot

  • FAO Globally Important Agricultural Heritage Systems, launched 2002: safeguard living agricultural landscapes, biodiversity, knowledge, cultural heritage and resilient livelihoods through farmer/community participation, technical support and market opportunities.
  • 95 systems in 28 countries after May 2025 designations, a dated snapshot. FAO, 21 May.
  • India’s three sites at the June issue: Kashmir saffron heritage (Karewa soils), Koraput traditional agriculture, Odisha (millets, pulses, native paddy and tribal knowledge including Kondh/Paroja/Bonda and other communities), Kuttanad below-sea-level farming, Kerala.
  • 2016 PYQ: (b) 2 only—protect eco-friendly traditional farming, associated landscapes, biodiversity and knowledge. GIAHS does not automatically confer GI registration or mean replacement by modern methods; technical assistance/innovation compatible with living heritage remains possible.
