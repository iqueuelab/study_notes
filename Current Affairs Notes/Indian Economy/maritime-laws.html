Indian Economy

Maritime law reform and port-sector development

Editable notes

6 August 2025: distinguish the Houses

  • Lok Sabha passed the Merchant Shipping Bill (introduced in 2024; passed as the 2025 Bill), replacing the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958; modernises ship registration, safety, seafarer welfare, environmental compliance and alignment with IMO conventions. On this issue date it had not completed both Houses; Rajya Sabha passage came on 11 August.
  • Rajya Sabha passed the Carriage of Goods by Sea Bill, 2025, completing parliamentary passage; replaces the Indian Carriage of Goods by Sea Act, 1925, updating cargo-carriage rights, duties/liabilities and contractual clarity. Passage is distinct from assent and commencement. 6 August Ministry release.

Sector opportunities and constraints

  • Sea routes carry approximately 95% of Indian trade by volume/70% by value; India's position between Suez and Malacca supports ports, transshipment, shipbuilding/repair, recycling and marine services. Source lists 13 major/200+ other ports; administrative designation and operational status differ.
  • MIV 2030: port/logistics modernisation; Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision 2047 supplies the longer horizon. Sagarmala: port-led development and hinterland/coastal connectivity; Major Port Authorities Act, 2021: greater port-board autonomy. National Logistics Portal (Marine)/Sagar Setu reduce paperwork and coordinate logistics.
  • Vadhavan and Great Nicobar/Galathea Bay are planned developments, not completed operating container hubs. JNPA's 10+ million TEU milestone is capacity, not evidence of annual throughput above 10 million; retain the existing JNPA distinction.
  • Source 820 MMT handled/1,630 MMT capacity are dated major-port metrics; 10,000 MMT by 2047, ₹5 lakh crore by 2030/$1 tn by 2047 investment needs are planning estimates, not present output/funds already spent. Its “16 th maritime nation,” turnaround 0.9 days, dwell 3 days, nine ports in a performance index require the named metric/year; do not treat them as universal current rankings.
  • LPI 2023 international-shipment rank22 vs44 is a sub-index, not India's overall LPI rank. Capacity, cargo handled, container TEUs and efficiency rankings measure different things.

Greening and safeguards

  • Green Tug Transition Programme: all tugs at Indian major ports envisioned green by end 2040, a scope-specific transition goal. Green-hydrogen hub ports include Paradip, V.O.Chidambaranar (Tuticorin), Deendayal (Kandla). GTTP announcement.
  • Hong Kong Convention, adopted 2009, entered into force 26 June 2025; safe/environmentally sound ship recycling, hazardous-material inventories and authorised facilities. Alang's role does not certify every yard automatically compliant.
  • Mains: resolve berth/equipment bottlenecks, last-mile rail/road gaps, skills shortages and financing; combine digitisation/clean fuels with oil-spill control, coastal ecosystems, community consultation and fair rehabilitation.
