# World Food Prize and sustainable soil microbiology

## 2025 laureate
- **Mariangela Hungria**, Brazilian microbiologist, named 2025 World Food Prize laureate for biological nitrogen fixation/soil-microbiology advances and deployment of microbial inoculants. Soybean/bean symbiosis and beneficial microbes in other crops support yield with lower synthetic-fertiliser needs; not every inoculant fixes nitrogen equally in every crop. [Foundation laureate](https://www.worldfoodprize.org/en/laureates/20202026_laureates/2025_hungria/).
- Potential: lower input costs, fertiliser emissions and water pollution, healthier soils. Productivity gains do not by themselves prove reduced Amazon deforestation or that Brazil’s entire soybean leadership was caused by one technique.
## Prize recall
- Conceived by **Norman Borlaug**, established 1986 ; **M.S.Swaminathan first laureate 1987**. Des Moines, Iowa-based Foundation recognises improvements in food’s quality, quantity or availability across agriculture, nutrition, technology and rural development.
- Annual international award; “Nobel Prize for Food and Agriculture” is an informal description. The source’s **US$250,000** is outdated for 2025 : the prize increased to **US$500,000**. Announcement and later award ceremony are distinct events.
- Source associates ceremony with October/World Food Day and a Saul Bass sculpture; scheduling is year-specific, not always 16 October.
