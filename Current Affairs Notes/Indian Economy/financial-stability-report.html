Indian Economy

Financial Stability Report and systemic resilience

Editable notes

June2025 report, discussed in July6 material

  • RBI's half-yearly FSR, normally June/December, reflects the FSDC Sub-Committee's collective assessment of systemic risks and financial resilience; covers banks, NBFCs, markets and connected financial institutions.
  • 30June2025 assessment: Indian macroeconomic fundamentals and domestic financial balance sheets remained resilient despite global trade/policy uncertainty, volatile markets, debt/asset valuations and geopolitical risks. Banks' capital, earnings and asset quality and NBFC buffers improved; weather/climate shocks remain risks.
  • Stress tests are scenario exercises, not forecasts or guarantees. RBI says most SCBs maintain adequate capital under specified adverse scenarios; the PDF overstates this as every bank safe even in any worst-case event. RBI release reproduced by FIDC.
  • GNPA ratio2.3%,March2025 is an observed aggregate; the PDF's 2.5%March2026/2.6%March2027 under stress table is not a reliable reproduction of the scenario results and should not be memorised. Use baseline/adverse labels, horizon and bank sample from the original report.
  • Capital adequacy measures eligible capital against risk-weighted assets, not idle cash set aside; regulatory thresholds/buffers vary. Scheduled banks are included in the RBI Act'sSecondSchedule, not simply any RBI-approved bank. NBFCs provide specified financial services under a distinct regulatory framework.
  • Lower food inflation means prices rise more slowly, not necessarily fall; good harvests can improve supply without guaranteeing higher farmer incomes. RBI/MPC, rather than the government directly, sets the monetary-policy repo rate.
