# CBAM: carbon leakage and Indian industrial exports

## 31 December 2025: exporter transition concerns
- **EU CBAM** aligns embedded carbon costs of covered imports with the **EU ETS**, aiming to limit carbon leakage. Initial sectors: **iron/steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, electricity, hydrogen**. It is product/emissions based, subject to legal coverage/exemptions, not a blanket tariff on all Indian exports.
- At issue date the **October 2023–December 2025 transition** was reporting-focused; **definitive regime from 1 January 2026** was prospective. Under October 2025 amended rules, certificate purchase for 2026 imports begins in 2027; **financial obligation for 2026 imports is distinct from same-day cash payment at the border**. [EU consolidated regulation,20 October 2025](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02023R0956-20251020).
- Carbon intensity and compliance cost can compress Indian steel/aluminium export margins; impact depends on actual/default emissions, ETS prices, free-allocation adjustments, carbon price paid abroad and buyer/producer contracts. The PDF's 'second-largest exporter' rank combines sectors/timebases without proof; 'most Indian steel uses blast furnaces' also overgeneralises a mixed production-route industry.
- Options: reliable emissions measurement/verification, energy efficiency, cleaner power, scrap/electric arc where appropriate, hydrogen research and market diversification. Shifting to Africa/West Asia is an industry response, not guaranteed higher profits or eliminated carbon risk.
- India–EU FTA exemption/adjustment was a negotiation request, not granted relief. Assess WTO consistency, developing-country costs and climate justice alongside decarbonisation. Proposed downstream scope expansion is not automatically operative coverage.
