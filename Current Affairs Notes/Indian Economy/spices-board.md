# Spices Board and export quality

## Institution
- Statutory **Spices Board Act 1986**; Board established 1987 by merger of Cardamom Board and Spices Export Promotion Council. **Commerce and Industry Ministry; Kochi headquarters.**
- Export development for 52 scheduled spices and development of small/large cardamom; quality testing, market promotion, research and exporter support.
- Food-safety regulation and export promotion are distinct functions; FSSAI and other competent agencies retain their respective roles.
- Traceability, pesticide residues, ethylene oxide and importing-country standards influence market access. Testing must connect to corrective action across the supply chain.
## Value addition
- Source 2030 US$10 billion export target/~70%value-added share is an ambition; reported US$4.5 billion, 1.5 million tonnes, 48%processed share use dated/possibly different denominators.
- Raw spices→seasonings/extracts/essential oils→branding/standardisation; global seasoning-market share differs from India's overall spice-export share.
- FPOs, storage, packaging, traceability, IPM and farmer training; ICAR/seed-spice research supports disease/climate-resilient varieties.
- Nutraceutical/pharmaceutical opportunities require evidence, quality and applicable approvals; traditional use alone does not establish clinical efficacy. Extending PLI is a recommendation, not automatic existing eligibility.

## SPICED and commodity-board recall: May 2025
- Spices Board constituted **26 February 1987**, merging **Cardamom Board (1968)** and **Spices Export Promotion Council (1960)**. **Indian Cardamom Research Institute** undertakes small/large cardamom research; the Board's scheduled-spice export work differs from general food-safety regulation. [Board constitution](https://www.indianspices.com/about-us/constitution.html).
- **SPICED: Sustainability in Spice Sector through Progressive, Innovative and Collaborative Interventions for Export Development**. Supports productivity/quality, cardamom rejuvenation, post-harvest processing, GI/organic/value-added exports, food-safety/phytosanitary compliance, capacity building and MSME/startup market access.
- May issue describes **2025–26 implementation**; SPICED already operated in **2024–25**, so this is not proof of a first-ever May 2025 launch. Approved through the remaining Fifteenth Finance Commission period to FY 2025–26. Programme objectives do not demonstrate every supported plantation/export project is complete. [Board programmes](https://www.indianspices.com/box5_programmes_schemes.html), [2024–25 report](https://www.indianspices.com/sites/default/files/Spices%20Annual%20Report%202024-25%20final%20%282%29%20%281%29.pdf).
