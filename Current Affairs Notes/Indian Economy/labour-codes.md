# Four labour codes: coverage and implementation debate

## Enacted framework as of July 10, 2025
- **29 central laws → four Codes** enacted in 2019–20. Their principal consolidated provisions were **not yet brought into force in July 2025**; enactment, draft rules, commencement and actual benefit delivery are distinct stages. Labour is in the Concurrent List; Centre–State rules and enforcement capacity matter.
| Code | Laws consolidated | Main scope |
|---|---|---|
| Code on Wages, 2019 | 4 | Minimum/floor wage framework, timely wage payment, bonus and equal remuneration |
| Industrial Relations Code, 2020 | 3 | Trade unions, industrial disputes, standing orders, retrenchment/closure and fixed-term employment |
| Code on Social Security, 2020 | 9 | PF, ESI, maternity, gratuity and frameworks/schemes for unorganised, gig and platform workers |
| Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 | 13 | Workplace safety/health, hours and welfare; factories, mines, plantations, contract and inter-State migrant labour |
- Universalising minimum-wage coverage beyond scheduled employments differs from already paying every worker a common wage. Social-security recognition does not automatically give every gig worker all EPF/ESI benefits; scheme design, eligibility, contributions and implementation matter. OSH coverage/obligations have establishment-specific thresholds.
## Strike and implementation concerns
- **July 9 nationwide strike:** ten central trade unions claimed over **25 crore participants**, protesting labour codes, prices, insecure work and unemployment. Participation is a union claim, not a verified official headcount. The clipping points to a roughly decade-long gap since the **2015 Indian Labour Conference**.
- Article's 2024 committee account of draft rules in 32 States/UTs and historical shortages must retain that reference period. Priorities: tripartite consultation, clear rules, inspection/grievance capacity, informal-worker inclusion and outcome monitoring.
- Women's **worker-population ratio, age 15+, usual status: 28.7% (2019–20) →40.3% (2023–24)**; this is not female labour-force participation or proof all new work is formal/decent. National Career Service, rural/tribal skills and social protection complement legal simplification.
- PYQ revision: **Factories Act 1948 and Plantations Labour Act 1951 → OSHWC Code 2020**.

## November 2025: commencement changes the July position
- Government made the four Labour Codes effective from **21 November 2025**; their **2019/2020 enactment years** differ from commencement. This supersedes the earlier July note's then-correct non-commencement status. Centre–State rules, transition arrangements and enforcement still govern delivery. [Labour Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2194018&lang=1&reg=1).
- Existing **4+3+9+13 = 29 laws** table remains the structure. Floor wage sets a lower benchmark for appropriate-government minimum wages; **it does not make every occupation/region's wage identical**. Gig/platform-worker recognition and schemes do not instantly confer all employee EPF/ESI entitlements.
- Fixed-term employees receive statutory benefits proportionate to comparable permanent work. IR Code expands strike notice requirements and raises the prior-permission threshold for specified lay-off/retrenchment/closure provisions to **300 workers**; this does not abolish compensation, notice or all protections below that threshold. Two-member Industrial Tribunals are intended to improve dispute resolution.
- Women's night work requires consent and prescribed safety safeguards. Annual health checks and OSH duties depend on prescribed ages/classes and rules; avoid treating the PDF's **40+** shorthand as an unconditional Code-text entitlement for every worker. Single-registration/licensing/returns and inspector-cum-facilitator provisions simplify specified compliance processes.
- Trade unions contest weakened bargaining/greater employer flexibility; industry supports simpler compliance and investment. Assess coverage, wages, injury prevention, collective voice and actual social-security access instead of assuming legal consolidation alone creates universal protection.

## February 2026: wage definition and benefit delivery
- Editorial links uniform wage measurement with PF, pension/gratuity and financial inclusion. The Codes' **21 November 2025 commencement** and consolidation of 29 laws are already covered; rules, eligibility and implementation determine actual benefits.
- **50% rule**: specified excluded remuneration above half of the total is added back to statutory “wages”. This does not universally require **basic pay itself to be 50% of CTC**; wages include applicable components, and statutory exclusions matter. Thus the PDF's **₹50,000 CTC → ₹25,000 basic** example is not an automatic legal calculation. [Labour Ministry FAQs, December 2025/January 2026](https://www.labour.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/01/de4758d5bfeffc456d7de97a801891b0.pdf).
- A higher statutory wage base can increase covered contributions/benefits and affect take-home pay, but caps, remuneration structure and eligibility prevent one universal result. **Fixed-term employees' one-year gratuity rule** does not automatically apply after one year to every contractor, gig worker or permanent employee.
- Recognition of gig/platform/unorganised workers and schemes does not instantly give all the same EPF/ESI coverage as employees. Portable migrant benefits require operating schemes and registration; formalisation does not follow merely from bank payment.
- The author's demand-led-growth/social-stability benefits are expectations; trade unions raise bargaining-power, threshold and enforcement concerns. Assess coverage, net income, claims processing, workplace safety and grievance redress alongside compliance simplification.
