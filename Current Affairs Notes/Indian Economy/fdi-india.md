# Foreign direct investment: routes, flows and net investment

## Gross inflows versus net investment
- **FDI:** non-resident equity investment in an unlisted Indian company, or **at least 10% of fully diluted post-issue equity** of a listed company; portfolio investment is distinguished by regulatory criteria, not merely how long shares are held. [RBI definition](https://systemhealth.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx_id%3D11200%281%29.html).
- Gross inward FDI can rise while net FDI falls: **net = gross inward flows − repatriation/disinvestment − outward direct investment** under the reported flow convention. Repatriation/disinvestment should not be equated solely with dividend/profit transfers.
- August clipping reports June gross FDI at a four-year high while net fell 50%; retain this as a **June 2025 source-period comparison**, not a full-year trend or proof of flight of all foreign capital. Source's $12.5 billion outward figure lacks a clear like-for-like annual flow definition.
- Capital, technology, management, jobs and export links can help; domestic linkages, competitive conditions, infrastructure and skills determine gains. Outward expansion and mature-investment exits can lower net inflows without proving a collapse in investment confidence.
## Entry routes and qualifications
- **Automatic route:** no prior government approval, subject to sectoral caps/conditions, FEMA reporting and other laws; **government route:** prior approval. Do not treat “automatic” as regulation-free.
- The source's blanket “100% agriculture/trading” and “all railways prohibited” are overbroad: permitted activities and conditions differ; specified railway infrastructure activities allow investment. Lottery/gambling, chit funds, Nidhi and atomic energy are key prohibited areas, subject to the operative policy text.
- The source's April 2025 export-PMI “worst slump” assertion is not verified; omit it as an established explanation for falling FDI.
