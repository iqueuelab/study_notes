# Foreign direct investment: routes, flows and net investment

## Gross inflows versus net investment
- **FDI:** non-resident equity investment in an unlisted Indian company, or **at least 10% of fully diluted post-issue equity** of a listed company; portfolio investment is distinguished by regulatory criteria, not merely how long shares are held. [RBI definition](https://systemhealth.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx_id%3D11200%281%29.html).
- Gross inward FDI can rise while net FDI falls: **net = gross inward flows − repatriation/disinvestment − outward direct investment** under the reported flow convention. Repatriation/disinvestment should not be equated solely with dividend/profit transfers.
- August clipping reports June gross FDI at a four-year high while net fell 50%; retain this as a **June 2025 source-period comparison**, not a full-year trend or proof of flight of all foreign capital. Source's $12.5 billion outward figure lacks a clear like-for-like annual flow definition.
- Capital, technology, management, jobs and export links can help; domestic linkages, competitive conditions, infrastructure and skills determine gains. Outward expansion and mature-investment exits can lower net inflows without proving a collapse in investment confidence.
## Entry routes and qualifications
- **Automatic route:** no prior government approval, subject to sectoral caps/conditions, FEMA reporting and other laws; **government route:** prior approval. Do not treat “automatic” as regulation-free.
- The source's blanket “100% agriculture/trading” and “all railways prohibited” are overbroad: permitted activities and conditions differ; specified railway infrastructure activities allow investment. Lottery/gambling, chit funds, Nidhi and atomic energy are key prohibited areas, subject to the operative policy text.
- The source's April 2025 export-PMI “worst slump” assertion is not verified; omit it as an established explanation for falling FDI.

## Outward investment and intermediary jurisdictions
- September clipping reports a large share of **FY2024–25 outward investment** routed initially through Singapore, Mauritius, UAE, Netherlands, UK and Switzerland, with a reported **56%** share and **63% in Q1 FY2025–26** for its selected low-tax group. These are source classifications/first-destination shares, not proof every jurisdiction or investment is a tax haven or unlawful.
- First destination can differ from ultimate operating location: holding companies/SPVs facilitate fundraising, joint ventures, treaty planning and global expansion. Risks include opaque beneficial ownership, profit shifting, round-tripping and regulatory arbitrage; GAAR, treaty anti-abuse rules, reporting and global BEPS cooperation respond.
- The source's **₹3,488.5 crore total /₹1,946 crore subset** lacks a consistent unit/flow definition and is not retained as verified RBI outward-FDI totals. Its **Singapore 22.6%, Mauritius 10.9%, UAE 9.1%** and July **~60% JVs** are clipping-specific figures with limited classification detail.
- Routing exports through another jurisdiction does not itself change origin: substantial transformation and applicable origin rules matter. OECD's **15% global minimum tax** concerns an agreed framework for covered multinationals, not a universal tax imposed on all Indian overseas investment.
- **PYQ recall:** 2020—FDI is largely non-debt-creating capital; 2011—FII expands capital availability generally while FDI targets particular enterprises/sectors. Existing notes explain routes, the **at least 10%** listed-company threshold and gross/net flows; repeated background adds no new annual series.

## August 2025 flows, reported in October
- Source RBI-data snapshot: **gross inflows US$6,049 million**, **repatriation/disinvestment $4,928 million**, **outward direct investment $1,736 million**, and **net FDI about −$616 million**. Rounded components yield −$615 million; the $1-million difference should be treated as rounding/reporting precision, not silently forced into an exact identity.
- It reports gross inflows **−30.6% year-on-year /−45.5% month-on-month**, repatriation **−5.4% year-on-year /+30% month-on-month**, outward investment **−29.7% year-on-year**, and net FDI **−159% year-on-year**, crossing below zero. A fall exceeding 100% is possible when a formerly positive net flow becomes negative. May 2025 was another small net-outflow month in this series.
- **April–August 2025:** net **$10.1 billion (+121% YoY)**; gross **$43.7 billion (+18.2%)**, repatriation **$21.2 billion (−6.1%)**, outward **$12.4 billion (+26%)**. A negative August does not reverse the positive five-month cumulative result.
- Lower gross investment, mature-investment exits and Indian firms' overseas expansion affect net flows differently; the source's geopolitical/project-delay explanations are possible drivers, not causation established by the flow table. Existing notes retain sectoral policy qualifications and correct the repeated overbroad agriculture/railway claims.
- **PYQ recall, already banked:** 2020 **(b)**—largely non-debt-creating capital; 2011 **(b)**—FII broadens capital availability while FDI targets enterprises/sectors. These contrasts are revision heuristics: FDI need not mean buying only newly issued shares, and portfolio investors need not have zero influence or universally short holding periods.

## January 2026: UNCTAD's preliminary 2025 estimates
- **Global Investment Trends Monitor No.50**, January 2026, estimated global FDI **US$1.6 trillion in calendar 2025, +14%**. Much of the increase reflected financial-centre flows: **excluding conduits, about +5%**; greenfield/project-finance activity remained weak. Do not read the headline as uniform new factory investment. [UNCTAD release, 20 January](https://unctad.org/news/global-investment-growth-resumes-finance-leading-rebound).
- Source country estimates: **India US$47 billion, +73%**; **China US$107.5 billion, −8%, third consecutive decline**. These are preliminary annual inward-FDI measures, distinct from India's fiscal-year equity-only figures and RBI net FDI after repatriation/outward flows. [Monitor](https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/diaeiainf2026d1_en.pdf).
- **Developed economies +43% to US$728 billion; developing economies −2% to US$877 billion** in the report. India's rise is not the experience of every developing country. Services/digital investment and selected large projects matter; policy incentives or China+1 are possible explanations, not causation proved by aggregate flows.
- **UNCTAD/UN Trade and Development**, established **1964**, Geneva, publishes this periodic investment monitor; it is distinct from the annual **World Investment Report**. Provisional estimates can later be revised.
- Source **2010 PYQ: Brazil, Mexico and South Africa—(d), all three**, in its historical emerging-economy classification. Country grouping is not an automatic ranking of investment safety or a universal legal status.

## 10 March 2026: Cabinet approval of PN3 changes
- **Press Note 3, 17 April 2020** required government-route approval for investment by an entity from a land-border country or where the beneficial owner is situated in/is a citizen of such a country; transfers creating that beneficial ownership were also covered. Purpose: curb opportunistic pandemic-era takeovers.
- Cabinet approved a **beneficial-owner definition based on PML Rules 2005**, applied at the **investor-entity level**. Investors with **non-controlling land-border-country beneficial ownership up to 10%** may use the otherwise applicable automatic route, subject to sectoral caps/conditions and investee reporting to **DPIIT**. This is not blanket permission for a company directly based in China to buy any 10% Indian stake without approval.
- **60-day decision window** for specified manufacturing proposals: capital goods, electronic capital goods/components, polysilicon and ingot-wafer. Resident Indian citizens/Indian entities owned and controlled by resident citizens must retain majority shareholding/control in these cases; the Cabinet-Secretary-led Committee of Secretaries can revise specified sectors.
- Distinguish the **Cabinet decision** from subsequent operative FEMA/policy notification and individual approvals. Aim: ease global PE/VC/start-up investment and technology/supply-chain collaboration while retaining scrutiny of control/security-sensitive investment. [Cabinet release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2237806&lang=2&reg=48).
