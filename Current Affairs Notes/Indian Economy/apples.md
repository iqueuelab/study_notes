# Apple cultivation: Himalayan horticulture and trade

## September 2025: rain disruption in Kashmir
- Relentless rain disrupted Kashmir's apple harvest/marketing; transport, grading, cold storage and resilient orchards link farmer incomes to horticultural value chains. Jammu and Kashmir is the leading producing region, followed by Himachal Pradesh; Uttarakhand and parts of the Northeast also grow apples.
- Apples are temperate fruit grown in Himalayan climates. NHB guidance: **1,500–2,700 m**, **21–24°C during growth**, **1,000–1,250 mm rain**, well-drained organic-rich loam; winter chilling matters. These are cultivation guidelines, not universal limits for every cultivar. Northern India extends beyond the tropics: the PDF's blanket claim that Indian apple regions are outside the temperate zone is misleading. [NHB cultivation guidance](https://nhb.gov.in/model-project-reports/Horticulture%20Crops/apple/apple1.htm).
- Varieties: Red/Royal/Golden Delicious; **Ambri** of Kashmir, Tydeman's Early and McIntosh. Reduced chilling, erratic rain/snow and post-harvest losses constrain output; MIDH, horticulture clusters and Operation Greens value-chain support are relevant policy links.
- Source's **~70% J&K/~20% Himachal, 3 lakh ha, 2.3–2.5 Mt, world fifth/2%** use unspecified crop/reference years; do not learn them as a verified 2025 statistical set.
- **2024 PYQ recall: (d)**—India imports US apples; GM-food import requires competent-authority approval. The existing common bank already holds the complete question under horticulture; do not duplicate. This does not mean every imported apple is genetically modified.

## December 2025: New Zealand tariff concession concerns
- Himachal growers oppose the proposed **apple tariff 50%→25%** under the concluded FTA negotiations. This reports a prospective arrangement and protest, not an immediately operative universal 25% duty on every imported apple.
- Cheaper imports could pressure orchard-gate prices/incomes; alternative crops are constrained by terrain, skills and sunk orchard investment. Climate-driven chilling/snowfall shifts, input costs, labour shortage, transport/cold storage and marketing also affect competitiveness.
- Source **6.5 lakh tonnes/~25% national output,1.5 lakh families, ₹5,500 crore annual economy** are article-specific estimates with no clearly aligned crop year; retain as dated context rather than current statistics. Earlier notes preserve crop/variety/agroclimate distinctions.
- Balance consumer choice/export diversification with farm adjustment: quotas, minimum import price/seasonal conditions, safeguards, origin verification, storage/grading and productivity. Exact FTA schedules govern concession scope. Agriculture is a State subject while external trade/customs are Union competences; consultation helps align national bargaining with regional livelihoods.

## February 2026: rootstocks and high-density orchards
- **Rootstock** provides the root system onto which the chosen apple variety/scion is grafted. **Clonal rootstocks** are vegetatively propagated for relative uniformity; seedling rootstocks vary genetically. Rootstock and fruit variety are not the same thing.
- The Kashmir report describes **M9/M26 and MM-series dwarf or semi-dwarf stocks**, alongside research on newer **Geneva-series** stocks, enabling smaller canopies, closer spacing, earlier fruiting and easier pruning/harvest. ICAR–Central Institute of Temperate Horticulture, Srinagar supports rootstock/planting-material technologies; ICAR's earlier demonstrations show M9 can induce fruiting about two years after planting under appropriate management. [ICAR horticulture account](https://icar.org.in/sites/default/files/2022-06/IH-Sep-Oct-2021.pdf).
- **Higher yield per hectare does not automatically mean higher yield per tree.** Benefits depend on healthy planting material, site/rootstock compatibility, canopy light interception, drip irrigation/fertigation, trellis/support, pruning and pest/disease management. Dwarfing stocks with shallow roots are **not automatically drought-resilient**.
- Barriers for small growers: initial investment, imported/quality planting-material access, technical knowledge, irrigation and specialised management. Develop local nurseries, extension, suitable credit/support and varieties/stocks tested across Kashmir's altitude, chilling and moisture conditions; avoid assuming one rootstock suits every orchard.
- The issue's **11 lakh tonnes, 1.08 lakh hectares and 27 lakh livelihoods** are report-specific context without a harmonised crop/reference year. Earlier notes already cover agroclimate, trade and the **2024 apples/GM-food PYQ (d)**; the question is not new and does not imply every imported apple is genetically modified.

## February 2026: Kashmir growers and proposed tariff concessions
- Source describes concern over **US 25%** and **EU 20% within a TRQ** versus the usual **50%** duty. These are reported negotiated/proposed concessions; do not present them as an already effective blanket tariff cut for every shipment. The source's EU illustration is **50,000 tonnes**, with agreement conditions/commencement governing access. A **17 February Agriculture Ministry statement** also describes US sourcing as quota-based and cites an **₹80/kg import value floor**; political assurance about farm protection is not a measured price outcome. [Ministry statement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2229322&lang=9&reg=1).
- **TRQ:** lower duty within the specified quota, ordinary/out-of-quota duty beyond it; **minimum import price** and seasonal/origin conditions are separate safeguards. Their presence does not alone prove either complete farmer protection or inevitable price collapse.
- **Controlled-atmosphere storage** regulates oxygen/carbon dioxide and temperature to extend marketing. Cheaper off-season imports may erode the premium supporting stored domestic apples and returns on sunk storage investment; orchard productivity, grading and distribution also determine competitiveness.
- Article estimates **J&K 7–8 versus Western 40–70 tonnes/ha**, average holding **0.40 ha**, **₹10,000 crore economy/35 lakh livelihoods/seven lakh families** lack consistent crop/reference years. Treat them as source context rather than a harmonised official statistical series; improvements need local cost/yield evidence. Earlier notes and 19 February additions retain cultivation, varieties, rootstocks and high-density planting.
