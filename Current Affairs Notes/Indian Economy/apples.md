# Apple cultivation: Himalayan horticulture and trade

## September 2025: rain disruption in Kashmir
- Relentless rain disrupted Kashmir's apple harvest/marketing; transport, grading, cold storage and resilient orchards link farmer incomes to horticultural value chains. Jammu and Kashmir is the leading producing region, followed by Himachal Pradesh; Uttarakhand and parts of the Northeast also grow apples.
- Apples are temperate fruit grown in Himalayan climates. NHB guidance: **1,500–2,700 m**, **21–24°C during growth**, **1,000–1,250 mm rain**, well-drained organic-rich loam; winter chilling matters. These are cultivation guidelines, not universal limits for every cultivar. Northern India extends beyond the tropics: the PDF's blanket claim that Indian apple regions are outside the temperate zone is misleading. [NHB cultivation guidance](https://nhb.gov.in/model-project-reports/Horticulture%20Crops/apple/apple1.htm).
- Varieties: Red/Royal/Golden Delicious; **Ambri** of Kashmir, Tydeman's Early and McIntosh. Reduced chilling, erratic rain/snow and post-harvest losses constrain output; MIDH, horticulture clusters and Operation Greens value-chain support are relevant policy links.
- Source's **~70% J&K/~20% Himachal, 3 lakh ha, 2.3–2.5 Mt, world fifth/2%** use unspecified crop/reference years; do not learn them as a verified 2025 statistical set.
- **2024 PYQ recall: (d)**—India imports US apples; GM-food import requires competent-authority approval. The existing common bank already holds the complete question under horticulture; do not duplicate. This does not mean every imported apple is genetically modified.
