Indian Economy

Rice: seasons, growing conditions and exports

Editable notes

Cultivation and distribution

  • Major staple, predominantly kharif, needing high temperature (source >25°C), humidity and ample water; rainfall above 100 cm is favourable, while irrigation permits growing in drier regions.
  • Suitable climates allow two/three annual crops: Aus pre-monsoon, Aman principal monsoon and Boro winter/irrigated crop in West Bengal. Crop calendar terminology varies by region.
  • Important producers include West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh and Punjab; high-yield regions also include Haryana, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. PDF production map is explicitly 2016–17, not a June 2025 State ranking; its ~25% cropped-area statement is only a rounded background estimate.
  • For this historical question context India is the second-largest producer after China and leading exporter of basmati/non-basmati rice. 2019 Prelims: India (b) was largest exporter over the preceding five years; avoid converting a dated question into an eternal ranking.

November 2025: procurement imbalance and diversification

  • Tamil Nadu's Kuruvai paddy procurement controversy—reported delays, corruption allegations and roughly two lakh additional acres—illustrates how assured procurement can favour paddy over pulses/oilseeds. Allegations are not adjudicated findings.
  • Dated source figures: paddy procurement in rice equivalent 119.86 lakh tonnes by 31 October 2025, versus 82.08 lakh tonnes a year earlier; 1 October rice stock 356.1 lakh tonnes versus 102.5 lakh-tonne norm. These are rice figures, not total rice-plus-wheat stocks. Procurement flow and buffer stock are different measures.
  • Editorial's recent annual rice procurement 525–547 lakh tonnes exceeds reported PDS/NFSA offtake 392–427 lakh tonnes; wheat offtake exceeded procurement in two of three years. Avoid describing every cereal as an identical surplus. Food subsidy roughly ₹2 lakh crore/year is a dated fiscal approximation.
  • Source contrasts rice stocks with pulses/oil import dependence: pulses 252.4 lakh tonnes (2024–25 source estimate), imports valued around ₹30,000 crore; edible-oil imports ₹1.2 lakh crore in 2023–24, about 55% of demand. Production estimates and import values require their reference periods; neither is a current universal ratio. Source's 28% PDS leakage is a study estimate with methodological limits, not an officially established share of every year's grain.
  • Editorial proposals: demand-led local crop plans, buyer assurance, suitable irrigation/R&D and MSP operations for pulses/oilseeds; FPO/SHG/cooperative aggregation, processing and decentralised procurement; stronger storage, prompt payment and leakage control. Black-gram farmer–papad-maker linkages illustrate assured markets. Freer exports are a recommendation requiring consumer-price and food-security assessment, not proof restrictions were removed by this article.

January 2026 editorial: rice exports and virtual water

  • India's leading rice exports support farmers, PDS and foreign exchange but can export virtual water from stressed aquifers. Do not retain the explainer's unsupported claim India overtook China in 2023: rankings depend on crop year and paddy versus milled-rice basis. Official 20 November 2025 final estimate: 150.184 million tonnes rice in 2024–25, versus 137.825 Mt previous year. Agriculture Ministry.
  • Source's 3,000–4,000 L/kg rice and 20–60% above global average are water-footprint estimates, varying by location, crop and green/blue-water methodology; do not equate the entire footprint with groundwater pumping. >20 Mt exports is a dated magnitude, not an invariant annual level.
  • Punjab/Haryana: assured procurement/MSP, cheap electricity and irrigation infrastructure make paddy privately attractive even in water-stressed areas; deeper pumping raises energy/cost and depletes aquifers when withdrawals exceed replenishment. Reported 30 ft→80–200 ft examples and 35–57% excess extraction cannot classify every block or substitute for dated CGWB assessments; State-wide labels “over-exploited/critical” are oversimplified.
  • Existing procurement notes cover surpluses. Add water-sensitive crop diversification with assured buyers for millets/pulses/oilseeds; agronomic water saving such as appropriate direct seeding/alternate wetting and drying, aquifer monitoring, targeted power incentives and transition support. Drip/sprinklers are not universally suitable for all paddy conditions.
  • Article's Haryana ₹7,500 per hectare diversion incentive appears a unit/status claim requiring verification; do not reproduce as an established entitlement. Food security, farmer income and water security require joint policy, rather than blaming farmers responding to procurement incentives.
