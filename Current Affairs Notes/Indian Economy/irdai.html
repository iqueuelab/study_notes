Indian Economy

IRDAI: insurance regulation and policyholder protection

Editable notes

Institution and July appointment

  • Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India: statutory authority under IRDA Act1999; headquarters Hyderabad. Section4 composition: Chairperson, up to five whole-time and up to four part-time members, appointed by Central Government; statutory maximum is not evidence all seats filled.
  • 24 July2025: government notified Ajay Seth's appointment as Chairperson. Notification and taking charge are distinct events. Finance Ministry appointment.
  • Regulates/develops insurance and protects policyholders: registration, solvency, investments, reporting, inspections, intermediary qualifications/conduct, grievance/claim standards and rural/social obligations.
  • Covers life/general/standalone-health/reinsurance entities and authorised intermediaries such as agents, brokers, TPAs, surveyors and loss assessors. A TPA supports health-claim services; it is not itself an insurer.
  • Product/pricing oversight varies by insurance class and regulatory framework; avoid the PDF's implication that IRDAI individually pre-approves every premium/policy in every market. Protection, competition and financial soundness must be assessed together.

Regulatory enforcement clipping

  • Embedded report: IRDAI imposed ₹50 million (₹5 crore) on Policybazaar, PB Fintech's insurance-broking/aggregation arm, for regulatory lapses, including managerial-personnel directorship issues. A regulatory penalty is distinct from a court conviction and should not imply every company product invalid.
  • The Ajay Seth appointment clipping repeats the July appointment already retained; statutory composition, insurer/intermediary coverage and policyholder mandate add no separate new institutional facts.

17 December 2025: insurance Bill passes Parliament

  • Rajya Sabha passed Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Bill, 2025, after Lok Sabha's 16 December passage. Raises the proposed foreign-investment ceiling in Indian insurers 74% → 100%; amends Insurance Act 1938, LIC Act 1956 and IRDA Act 1999.
  • As of this 18 December issue, parliamentary passage is not assent/commencement: do not say foreign companies could already rely on the new law that day. Subsequent Gazette records assent 20 December and commencement by separate government notification. Gazette title/commencement clause.
  • Rationale: attract capital, expand competition/coverage and improve oversight/policyholder protection. Lower premiums or stronger inclusion are potential outcomes, not automatic consequences. 74% foreign ownership did not require Indian majority shareholding, so the PDF's blanket old-regime “shared management control” claim oversimplifies regulatory safeguards.
  • Government emphasised domestic investment of policyholder funds and rural/social obligations; these are distinct from prohibiting every lawful dividend or profit repatriation. Opposition concerns include data protection, fraud, foreign profit flows, public-insurer capacity and parliamentary scrutiny. An attributed US$600-billion market figure lacks a precise premium/assets/valuation denominator and is not retained as a verified market-size total.
  • IRDAI's solvency, investments, claims and rural/social requirements remain central; opening ownership does not exempt foreign-owned Indian insurers from domestic regulation.
