# Budget 2025–26: income-tax relief and demand

## Proposal and limits

- **1 February 2025 Budget**, FY2025–26 / AY2026–27 new-regime proposals: resident individuals with normal taxable income up to **₹12 lakh** receive rebate; eligible salaried income **₹12.75 lakh** after **₹75,000 standard deduction**.
- **₹12 lakh is a rebate threshold, not the nil-rate slab**. Special-rate income such as capital gains/lottery is excluded from this rebate; marginal relief applies under specified conditions just above threshold. [Income-tax FAQ](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/Documents/Budget/budget-2025/faqs-budget-2025.pdf).

| Income slab (₹ lakh) | Marginal rate |
| --- | --- |
| 0–4 | Nil |
| 4–8 | 5% |
| 8–12 | 10% |
| 12–16 | 15% |
| 16–20 | 20% |
| 20–24 | 25% |
| Above 24 | 30% |

- Rates apply to each slab, not the whole income at the highest applicable rate. [Budget highlights](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2025-26/doc/bh1.pdf).
- **~₹1 lakh crore direct-tax revenue forgone**, Budget estimate for direct-tax proposals, not proof of an equal deficit increase or realised annual loss. [Budget speech](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2025-26/doc/Budget_Speech.pdf).

## Economic transmission

- Tax relief → disposable income → consumption **and/or** savings; consumption can stimulate output/jobs, savings may finance investment. Response depends on income group, confidence, credit and supply capacity.
- Risks: fiscal opportunity cost, imports as leakage, inflation where supply constrained; crowding-in through investment is contingent. Investment does not have a universally larger multiplier in every setting.
- Combine private consumption/investment with effective public spending; judge distribution, employment and revenue outcomes rather than assuming tax cuts always finance themselves.
