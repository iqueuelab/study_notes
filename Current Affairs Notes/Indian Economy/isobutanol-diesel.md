# Isobutanol–diesel blending trials

## Proposed blending, not a rollout
- Transport Minister Nitin Gadkari reported **ARAI trials of 10% isobutanol with diesel**, following unsuccessful ethanol–diesel blending trials. Trial announcement is not a notified nationwide retail mandate; source criticism of mileage is not a measured fleet-wide outcome.
- **Isobutanol: C₄H₁₀O**, a flammable alcohol used as a solvent; higher energy density and lower water affinity than ethanol motivate fuel research. Feedstock, lifecycle emissions and engine compatibility determine its practical benefits.
- Diesel compression ignition depends on ignition quality/cetane behaviour; the source's **higher octane** claim does not by itself establish better diesel performance or suitability as a neat replacement. Validate stability, ignition delay, emissions, lubricity, durability and cold-flow performance before adoption.
- Source reports ISMA demands for higher sugarcane **FRP**, sugar **MSP**, and exports, plus a **349 lakh-tonne 2025–26 sugar forecast**; distinguish an industry forecast/demand from output or an approved policy. Maize price changes cited are not proof every farmer's net income doubled.
- Biofuel diversification can reduce petroleum exposure, but lifecycle water/land use, food/feed competition, costs and fuel-quality assurance remain central; existing ethanol notes cover E20 separately.

## Trial design and fuel-quality questions
- The repeated source describes an **18-month ARAI feasibility study**. Treat its duration as issue-reported; not evidence trials succeeded, nationwide commercialisation occurred or India established an independently verified world first.
- Lower ignition quality can lengthen diesel ignition delay; blending/additive costs, durability and emissions need engine-specific testing. The issue's **10% threshold** is a study/blend context, not a universal physical limit or notified retail standard. Higher flash point than ethanol does not make isobutanol non-flammable.
