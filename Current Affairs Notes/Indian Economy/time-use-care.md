# Time use, unpaid care and the care economy

## Reading Time Use Survey 2024
- NSO survey records a 24-hour activity diary; 2019 and 2024 comparisons require matching ages, activity categories and **participant versus all-person averages**.
- Source employment participation, ages 15–59: women 21.8%→25%, men 70.9%→75%; these measures are not interchangeable with PLFS annual LFPR.
- Unpaid domestic work remains strongly gendered; reported female 15–59 participant time 305 minutes/day versus 315 in 2019. Do not present participant minutes as time spent by every woman.
- Caregiving, learning, leisure and self-care have different age/participation denominators; one table's numbers cannot be applied to all ages. [NSO release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2106113&lang=2&reg=48).
## Care and policy
- **Recognise→Reduce→Redistribute** unpaid care; represent care workers in decisions and provide fair reward/conditions in paid care.
- Water access, reliable energy, nearby childcare/Palna, elder care and transport reduce time burdens; a registered tap is not proof reliable daily supply.
- Unpaid household care generally falls outside GDP's production boundary; unpaid work in a family enterprise/agriculture is a different category and cannot all be called excluded care.
- Gender Budget 2025–26₹4, 49, 028.68 crore/~8.86% of expenditure is an allocation classification, not money entirely dedicated to childcare or proof all women benefit.
- Bogotá care blocks illustrate linked neighbourhood services. Measure service quality, time saved and women's choices, not merely facility counts.

## November 2025: professional childcare and worker recognition
- **International Day of Care and Support: 29 October**, designated by the UN General Assembly in July 2023; links unpaid-care redistribution with dignity and decent work for paid care workers. **SDG 5.4** covers recognising and valuing unpaid care through services, infrastructure, social protection and shared responsibilities.
- Indian early-childhood pioneers **Tarabai Modak and Gijubhai Badheka** emphasised play and developmentally appropriate learning. Source links the **1972 Mina Swaminathan study group** with holistic care—health, nutrition and early learning—and ICDS expansion from **1975**.
- Source's **India Childcare Champion Awards 2025**, organised by **Mobile Creches and FORCES**, recognise crèche workers, supervisors, local leaders, NGOs, CSR contributors and a Mina Swaminathan jury award. Recognition complements, but cannot replace, wages, paid leave, social protection, training and career progression.
- Rural-oriented Anganwadi provision leaves urban migrant families and children below three particularly vulnerable. Climate shocks, displacement and male migration can intensify women's care load. Improve urban crèches/Palna, local-body participation, worker representation and convergence of health, nutrition, labour and WCD services.
- November editorial snapshots—**1.4 million centres, 2.4 million workers/helpers, 23 million children; 2,500 operational crèches against 10,000 approved; wages ₹8,000–15,000; 0.4% GDP spending**—have differing scopes and are not harmonised administrative totals. Its **60 million children/2.6 million centres/5 million workers by 2030** and **1–1.5% GDP investment** are projections/recommendations. Unpaid care's **15–17% GDP** is an imputed valuation, not a Time Use Survey GDP contribution; the **426 versus 163 minutes** needs age/activity/participant denominators before direct use.
