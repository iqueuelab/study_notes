# Time use, unpaid care and the care economy

## Reading Time Use Survey 2024

- NSO survey records a 24-hour activity diary; 2019 and 2024 comparisons require matching ages, activity categories and **participant versus all-person averages**.
- Source employment participation, ages 15–59: women 21.8%→25%, men 70.9%→75%; these measures are not interchangeable with PLFS annual LFPR.
- Unpaid domestic work remains strongly gendered; reported female 15–59 participant time 305 minutes/day versus 315 in 2019. Do not present participant minutes as time spent by every woman.
- Caregiving, learning, leisure and self-care have different age/participation denominators; one table's numbers cannot be applied to all ages. [NSO release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2106113&lang=2&reg=48).

## Care and policy

- **Recognise→Reduce→Redistribute** unpaid care; represent care workers in decisions and provide fair reward/conditions in paid care.
- Water access, reliable energy, nearby childcare/Palna, elder care and transport reduce time burdens; a registered tap is not proof reliable daily supply.
- Unpaid household care generally falls outside GDP's production boundary; unpaid work in a family enterprise/agriculture is a different category and cannot all be called excluded care.
- Gender Budget 2025–26₹4, 49, 028.68 crore/~8.86% of expenditure is an allocation classification, not money entirely dedicated to childcare or proof all women benefit.
- Bogotá care blocks illustrate linked neighbourhood services. Measure service quality, time saved and women's choices, not merely facility counts.
