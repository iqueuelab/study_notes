# Chincha Kingdom: guano and agricultural surplus

## Guano and Chincha expansion
- Research in Peru's arid **Chincha Valley** links seabird-guano fertilisation of maize to agricultural intensification by at least **1250 CE**, before Inca incorporation. Nitrogen-isotope evidence from archaeological maize and radiocarbon dating underpin the interpretation; they do not prove that fertiliser alone caused political expansion. [PLOS One study, 11 February 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0341263).
- **Guano** is accumulated animal droppings, notably seabirds and bats; nitrogen and phosphorus make seabird deposits valuable fertiliser. Coastal aridity helps preserve deposits. Peruvian booby, Peruvian pelican and Guanay cormorant are important guano-producing birds.
- Revision chain: nutrient transfer from sea to farms → higher maize productivity/surplus → trade, population support and political influence. Resource access may help explain Chincha's unusual position in its later relationship with the Inca Empire; this is an archaeological interpretation.
