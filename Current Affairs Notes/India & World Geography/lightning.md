# Lightning, thunder and disaster risk

## Mechanism
Water/ice collisions + convective updrafts → charge separation in a thundercloud → electrical discharge when the field is sufficient → air heats rapidly and expands → shock wave heard as **thunder**.
- Typical cloud structure has positive charge higher up and negative charge below, though real storms are more complex. Discharge may be intra-cloud, inter-cloud or cloud-to-ground; cloud-to-ground strikes are a direct surface hazard. In-cloud lightning is still hazardous to aviation.
- A properly earthed conductor offers a protected current path; protection is not a guarantee against every strike or a reason to shelter under an isolated tree.
## India and prevention
- Northeast/eastern India have high activity; deaths are also concentrated in exposed rural populations in central/eastern States. Frequency of lightning and number of deaths are different risk measures. Source cites >2,800 deaths in 2021, about 40% of deaths attributed to forces of nature in that dataset.
- Warming, convection, urban heat, land use and aerosols can influence storms; the source's **12% per °C** is a study-based projection, not a universal measured rule for India.
- IMD warnings/nowcasts and the Lightning Resilient India Campaign support early action; warning lead time and local accuracy vary. Improve last-mile alerts, safe shelters, school/farm-worker awareness and building earthing.
- **2013 Prelims: (d)**. Thunder comes from rapidly expanding lightning-heated air, not clouds colliding/separating or upward motion alone.
