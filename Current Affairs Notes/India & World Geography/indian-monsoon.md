# Indian monsoon: circulation, rainfall and resilience

## Seasonal circulation
- Monsoon derives from Arabic **mausim** (season): a large-scale seasonal reversal of winds, with marked wet/dry seasons. Land–sea thermal contrast, pressure gradients, shifting tropical convergence, ocean conditions and relief act together.
| Season | Circulation / rainfall |
| --- | --- |
| Southwest, June–September | Moist oceanic inflow; Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal branches; roughly 75% of annual Indian rainfall |
| Northeast, October–December | Seasonal retreat and northeasterly flow; moisture gained over the Bay of Bengal supplies southeastern peninsular India, especially Tamil Nadu and coastal Andhra Pradesh |
- Normal southwest onset over **Kerala around 1 June**; onset dates vary. Active/break spells and uneven distribution matter alongside seasonal totals. [IMD monsoon information](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/responsive/monsooninformation_monsoon_activity.php).
## Mechanism and controls
Summer heating of land and northwest India  
↓  
Thermal low + northward tropical convergence/monsoon trough  
↓  
Cross-equatorial inflow turns southwesterly  
↓  
Ocean moisture + convergence + mountain lifting → rainfall.
- **ITCZ:** convergence/low-pressure belt shifts north in boreal summer; the established monsoon trough and embedded disturbances organise rainfall.
- **Tibetan Plateau and Himalayas:** elevated heating and a barrier to cold/dry extratropical air help shape circulation; their roles cannot be reduced to one heat-source explanation.
- **Subtropical westerly jet:** lies south of the Himalayas in winter and shifts north in summer. **Tropical easterly jet:** upper-level summer circulation associated with the monsoon; its presence alone does not determine onset or rainfall totals.
- **Somali/Findlater jet:** strong low-level cross-equatorial flow near East Africa and over the Arabian Sea helps transport moisture toward India. [IMD research on mountains and jets](https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/view/3089).
## Regional contrasts
- Western Ghats' windward slopes: heavy orographic rain; interior Deccan: rain-shadow effects.
- Northeast, including Meghalaya's Mawsynram/Cherrapunji: exceptionally wet because moist inflow meets relief; northern plains: generally moderate to high monsoon rain with strong regional variation.
- Western Rajasthan and parts of Gujarat: lower, erratic rainfall; **Gujarat as a whole is not uniformly arid**. Eastern coastal areas gain important autumn/winter rain as northeasterlies cross the Bay.
## Why distribution matters
- Kharif rice, pulses and cotton: onset controls sowing; dry spells/excess rain affect yields and food security. Rural incomes, consumer demand and food inflation respond; good rainfall is one contributor to growth, not a guarantee.
- Rivers, aquifers, lakes and reservoirs recharge; irrigation and hydropower depend on stored water and inflows.
- Floods, landslides and poor urban drainage damage roads, railways, buildings and livelihoods. Cholera is waterborne; **dengue and malaria are mosquito-borne**, with vector risks influenced by standing water and weather. [WHO vector-borne diseases](https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/vector-borne-diseases).
- Rain supports vegetation and biodiversity; intense events can erode soil and degrade habitats.
## Forecasting and adaptation
- **India Meteorological Department (IMD)** monitors onset, advance, rainfall and warnings using satellites, Doppler radar, weather balloons and numerical models. National Monsoon Mission strengthens dynamical forecast capability.
- Forecasts → timely crop decisions and flood warnings → storage/drainage management → insurance and recovery.
- PM Fasal Bima Yojana addresses eligible crop-loss risks; watershed development, rainwater harvesting, water conservation and urban drainage/resilient infrastructure reduce exposure. The source's generic “National Watershed Project” label should be read as watershed initiatives, without inventing a specific notified scheme.
- Crop diversification, drought-tolerant varieties and climate-resilient infrastructure complement early warning. Assess **where, when and how intensely** rain falls, not just whether the national total is above/below normal.
