# Snowball Earth: varves and ancient climate variability

## Cryogenian glaciation and climate rhythms
- **Snowball Earth** describes extreme glaciations with ice reaching tropical latitudes during the **Cryogenian, about 720–635 million years ago**. Cryogenian is a period in the Neoproterozoic Era/Proterozoic Eon; **Precambrian is not an eon**, despite the source table.
- A study of laminated sediments in Scotland's **Garvellach Islands/Port Askaig Formation** reports annual, decadal and centennial variability during one interval of the Sturtian glaciation. The issue describes **2,640 varves**, with coarser melt-season sediment and finer winter deposits providing an annual interpretation.
- Thickness variations resemble roughly **9–11-year solar cycles**, longer **Gleissberg-scale variability**, and short interannual oscillations. An **El Niño-like periodicity is an analogy**, not proof that the modern ENSO system existed unchanged.
- Simulations suggest that some open ocean could allow atmosphere–ocean interaction. The researchers stress that this may have been a **brief exceptional disturbance**, against a generally cold, stable background; it does not prove all Snowball Earth intervals had today's climate variability. [University of Southampton study account](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/oes/news/2026/02/ancient-rocks-reveal-annual-climate-cycles-during-snowball-earth.page).
- Revision chain: **sediment layers → inferred annual record → periodicities → comparison with climate models**. Separate observed layering, annual interpretation and model-based explanation.
