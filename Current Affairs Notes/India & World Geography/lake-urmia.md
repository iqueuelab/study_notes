# Lake Urmia: salinity and basin restoration

## February 2026: Iran's shrinking saline lake
- **Northwestern Iran**, an **endorheic hypersaline lake**: water leaves mainly through evaporation rather than a river outlet. Tributaries include **Talkheh/Aji Chay, Zarrineh and Simineh**. Ramsar wetland and UNESCO Biosphere Reserve; supports Artemia and migratory waterbirds, including flamingos.
- **Drought/warming + irrigation withdrawals and dams/diversions** reduce inflow. Shrinkage concentrates salts and exposes lakebed → windblown saline dust → soil salinisation, crop damage and respiratory-health risks. Water decline cannot be attributed to climate alone. [UNEP assessment](https://wedocs.unep.org/items/7e426336-b3c7-4c39-b993-9aa80d8376c3).
- Reported rain prayers/cloud-seeding attempts illustrate drought response; they do not establish that cloud seeding restored the lake. Durable restoration needs basin-wide environmental flows, efficient irrigation with actual withdrawal limits, groundwater control and livelihood-sensitive land/water planning. [Integrated basin management plan](https://www.ramsar.org/document/wetland-management-plan-ir-iran-integrated-management-plan-lake-urmia-basin).
- **Great Salt Lake—USA** is the western-hemisphere saline-lake recall; the supplied JKSSB question is excluded. Avoid the source's “Dead Sea is the world's saltiest water body”: salinity rankings vary and more saline waters exist.
