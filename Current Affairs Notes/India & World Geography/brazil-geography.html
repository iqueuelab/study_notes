Brazil: physical geography and regional links

Editable notes

Location and map

  • South America's largest country and fifth-largest globally by area; capital Brasília, Atlantic coast, crossed by the Equator and Tropic of Capricorn. Neighbours: Argentina, Bolivia, Peru, Colombia, Venezuela, Paraguay, Uruguay, Guyana, Suriname, plus French Guiana (France). It borders neither Chile nor Ecuador; French Guiana is not an independent country.
  • North: Amazon lowlands/rainforest and Guiana Highlands; centre/southeast: Brazilian Highlands and Cerrado savanna; west-central: seasonally flooded Pantanal in the Paraguay basin; northeast: semi-arid Caatinga; east: Atlantic coastal plains/forest. Southern Brazil is chiefly subtropical; occasional snow occurs in southern highlands, so the source's blanket no-snow claim is wrong.
  • Amazon: largest discharge/basin; absolute longest-river ranking is disputed. Major mapped tributaries: Negro, Solimões, Madeira, Purus, Juruá, Tapajós and Xingu; Marajó lies at the mouth. São Francisco serves the northeast; Paraná links to Itaipu on the Brazil–Paraguay border; Tocantins–Araguaia is another major system. Locate Mato Grosso, Patos Lagoon, Manaus, Belém, São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro and Salvador.

Economy and cooperation

  • Coffee, soybeans, sugarcane, oranges, cotton and cattle are important; iron ore, bauxite, gold, oil and hydropower support industry. Petrobras is the national oil-company example.
  • Amazon deforestation/mining/cattle expansion, Pantanal fires and habitat degradation create conservation pressures. Brazil participates in Mercosur, IBSA, G4, G20 and BRICS; BRICS is no longer limited to the original five. India–Brazil cooperation includes agriculture, space, pharmaceuticals, energy and UN Security Council reform.

February 2026: soil, peak and resource revision

  • Pico da Neblina, near Venezuela in the northern Amazon region, is Brazil's highest peak, 2,995.3 m; the source's 2,999 m is an older rounded height. Official Brazilian conservation record.
  • Terra roxa denotes fertile reddish/purplish soils associated with weathered basalt, especially coffee-growing areas; selvas denotes equatorial rainforest. Terra roxa is not simply the black regur soil category. Brazilian Geological Survey. The supplied two-statement question is explicitly UPPCS/State PCS, so it is excluded from CSE banks.
  • Pyrochlore is an important niobium-bearing mineral; niobium's alloying uses differ from bauxite's aluminium role. Iguaçu is a Paraná tributary. Existing notes retain borders, Atlantic outlet, Pantanal/Paraguay-basin geography and major agricultural commodities; do not conflate Amazon-drainage boundaries with the wider Amazon biome/political region.
