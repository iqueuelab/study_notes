# Mount Makalu and high-altitude expeditions

## Himalayan map recall
- **World's fifth-highest peak**, Mahalangur Himalaya on **Nepal–Tibet (China) border**, about **23 km south-east of Everest**. ITBP uses elevation **8,485 m**; some Nepal official pages give **8,463 m**, so retain the stated source convention rather than imply every survey agrees.
- Four-sided summit pyramid, steep rock/ice pitches and exposed ridges make ascent technically demanding. Nearby subsidiary summit **Kangchungtse/Makalu II** lies north-north-west; the main peak is Makalu/Makalu I. The PDF's “Makalu I and Makalu II are both subsidiary peaks” is misleading.
- **Makalu Barun National Park/Barun Valley**, eastern Nepal: sharp altitude gradients, gorges, waterfalls and rich biodiversity; communities include Sherpa and Rai/Kirat groups. [Nepal park account](https://dnpwc.gov.np/content/41/makalu-barun-national-kunj/).
- **15 May 1955:** Lionel Terray and Jean Couzy made the first ascent as part of the French expedition led by Jean Franco; follows Everest's **1953** first ascent.

## ITBP achievement: April 2025
- **19 April 2025:** ITBP conducted simultaneous expeditions on **Makalu (8,485 m)** and **Annapurna (8,091 m)**, first such two-above-8,000-m expedition by an Indian CAPF. Correct the source's **2024** label. [ITBP](https://itbpolice.nic.in/Home/AdventureActivities).
- High-altitude climbing tests acclimatisation, endurance, technical rescue and logistics; weather, hypoxia, avalanches and exposure remain risks. An expedition demonstrates skills, not territorial control or an Indian patrol entitlement on the Nepal–China boundary.
