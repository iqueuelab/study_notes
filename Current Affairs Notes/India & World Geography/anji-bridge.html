Anji Khad Bridge and USBRL

Editable notes

Map and engineering

  • India's first cable-stayed railway bridge, Reasi, Jammu and Kashmir, between Katra and Reasi, over Anji River, Chenab tributary.
  • Part of Udhampur–Srinagar–Baramulla Rail Link (USBRL); Kashmir Valley connection across difficult Himalayan geology. Indian Railways with IIT Roorkee/Delhi site investigations.
  • Asymmetric cable-stayed design: cables transfer deck loads to main pylon/foundation, not a self-supporting arch like the nearby Chenab railway bridge.

Chenab rail bridge: June 2025

  • Bakkal–Kauri, Reasi, Jammu and Kashmir, USBRL: steel railway arch bridge 359 m above Chenab riverbed, total 1,315 m, principal arch 467 m; distinguish riverbed clearance from structural height or arch-span length. “World's highest railway arch bridge” concerns elevation, not the world's longest arch.
  • 6 June 2025: inauguration of Chenab and Anji bridges/USBRL connectivity; the issue's clipping anticipates that day's programme. These milestones differ from earlier completion/trials. Official overview.
  • Source engineering: 17 spans, concrete-filled steel arch boxes, 93 deck segments ~85 tonnes each, 120-year design life, wind design 266 km/h, high-seismic/blast-resistance provisions. These are design/fabrication specifications, not a guarantee against every earthquake, blast or maintenance failure.
  • IIT structural input, DRDO blast studies and GSI geological assessment illustrate cross-disciplinary engineering in difficult rock/terrain. Arch: loads transfer through arch towards supports; Anji cable-stayed bridge: loads carried via stays/pylon—different structural systems.
  • Improved Valley access supports passengers, freight, tourism, local livelihoods and strategic logistics; potential gains depend on reliable operations, complementary facilities and resilient maintenance.

Chenab basin map

  • Chandra + Bhaga meet at Tandi, Lahaul–Spiti, Himachal Pradesh → Chandrabhaga/Chenab → Jammu region → Pakistan Punjab → Panjnad → Indus → Arabian Sea. Sutlej joins the downstream combined system; Chenab does not flow directly to the sea as an independent coastal river.
  • Source length 974 km is an approximate convention, not uniquely established by every geographic source. Tributaries include Miyar, Thirot, Bhut, Marusudar and other Himalayan streams; basin networks matter for floods/hydropower.
  • Treaty western river, alongside Indus/Jhelum; allocation/use rights and April 2025 abeyance developments remain in the common Indus Treaty topic. India's bridge inauguration does not amend water allocations.
