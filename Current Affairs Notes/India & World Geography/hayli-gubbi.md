# Hayli Gubbi: volcanic ash and aviation risk

## 23–25 November 2025: Ethiopian eruption and distant ash
- **Hayli Gubbi**, southernmost volcano of the **Erta Ale Range**, in Ethiopia’s **Afar Rift**, erupted explosively on **23 November 2025**. It is a **shield volcano in a rift setting**; shape does not exclude explosive activity. [Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=221091).
- The November 25 clipping reports ash carried across **Yemen and Oman toward India via western Rajasthan**, with a projected route toward Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and the Northeast. These were moving-plume reports/forecasts, not a permanent route or proof of damaging ground-level deposition in every named State.
- **Volcanic ash is abrasive rock/mineral/glass particulate**, not ordinary combustion smoke. In hot jet engines it can soften/melt and deposit on turbine components, causing power loss/flameout; it also abrades windscreens and damages sensors. Winds at altitude can transport ash thousands of kilometres after local eruptive activity subsides.
- Source reports **DGCA ash-avoidance advisories**, airline monitoring/reporting of engine abnormalities, odour/smoke or visibility problems, aircraft inspection and airport checks for runway contamination. Restriction/suspension depends on actual ash exposure and safety assessment; proximity to an ash forecast does not automatically close every airport.
- Hazard chain: **eruption → high-altitude plume → long-range atmospheric transport → flight-route exposure**. Use volcanic-ash advisories, satellite observations and operational coordination. Avoid the unsupported claim that the volcano had certainly never erupted during an exact 12,000-year interval; “first recorded explosive eruption” is narrower than geological proof of inactivity.
