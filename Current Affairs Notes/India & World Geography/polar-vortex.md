# Polar vortex and cold-air outbreaks

## Concept and distinctions
- Broad polar **low-pressure/cold-air cyclonic circulation**. Distinguish **stratospheric polar vortex** from upper-tropospheric circulation/jet patterns; they interact but are not one identical vertically uniform feature.
- Winter strengthens the stratospheric vortex; summer weakening/breakdown is part of its seasonal cycle. Source's 'always exists unchanged from tropopause through mesosphere' is too sweeping across both definitions.
- Normal cyclonic circulation: **anticlockwise in Northern Hemisphere**, **clockwise in Southern Hemisphere**; not anticlockwise at both poles.
- **Jet streams**: narrow fast winds in upper troposphere near tropopause; source approx **30,000 feet / 9.1 km**, height varies.
## Strong containment → disturbance → possible surface effects
- Strong circulation helps contain polar air; disturbances/weakening/displacement can alter jet patterns and favour some midlatitude cold outbreaks.
- A stratospheric disruption is **not required for every cold spell**, nor a guarantee all regions turn cold; coupling, timing and local weather matter. [NOAA distinctions](https://prod-01-asg-www-climate.woc.noaa.gov/news-features/blogs/enso/polar-vortex-going-make-you-put-sweater-be-afraid-be-very-afraid), [NWS cold-air explanation](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-polar-vortex).
- Source January 2025 US cold outbreak; Europe/Asia also affected in relevant events. Southern circulation tends to be more symmetric, with ocean-dominated surroundings; Southern Hemisphere cold outbreaks still occur.
- Impacts: hypothermia/frostbite, transport disruption, agricultural losses and energy demand; forecasts and protection for vulnerable people matter.
