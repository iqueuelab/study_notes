# Mount McKinley–Denali and Alaska geology

## January 2025 naming context
- **20 January US executive direction** to restore **Mount McKinley** name for **Denali**, **North America's highest peak**, **Alaska Range, south-central Alaska**. Mountain renaming differs from renaming **Denali National Park and Preserve**.
- Two summits, higher southern summit; mainly granitic massif with tectonic uplift/erosion. Not a volcano and not on the US–Canada boundary.
- Source uplift history **about 60 million years** describes long tectonic history, not instantaneous formation of the present summit.
- Large vertical relief above surroundings, source **~5,500 m**; **relief is not summit elevation above sea level**. Snow/ice feed **Kahiltna, Muldrow, Peters, Ruth, Traleika** glaciers.
## Denali Fault
- Major **strike-slip fault system**; long-term horizontal movement rearranges geological units. Source study correlates three formerly connected **terminal-suture-zone** sites and estimates **~483 km displacement over millions of years**, not one earthquake or annual movement.
- **Suture**: zone of past terrane/plate joining; later strike-slip offsets can separate once-contiguous features. Map locations and differentiate vertical mountain uplift from lateral fault displacement.
