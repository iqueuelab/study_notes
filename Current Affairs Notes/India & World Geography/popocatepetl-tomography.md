# Popocatépetl: seismic tomography and volcanic hazards

## January 2026: imaging a complex magma system
- **Popocatépetl, Mexico**, active stratovolcano; UNAM team led by **Marco Calò** combined field seismographs and machine learning to reconstruct its interior. Source reports five years' fieldwork, sampling **100 times/second**, model depth **18 km below crater**, multiple magma accumulations separated by rock, particularly southeast. These are **inferred subsurface structures**, not photographs of liquid chambers or direct measurements of every temperature/state.
- “First” concerns this project's detailed whole-volcano 3D model, not the first-ever imaging of any volcano. UNAM described the approach in **July 2025**, so January publication is reporting an ongoing research development. Seismic velocities/ambient noise constrain structure; machine learning helps identify/classify signals. [UNAM research account](https://www.gaceta.unam.mx/como-es-el-popocatepetl-por-dentro/).
- Source estimates **25 million people within 100 km**, with cities, airports and hospitals exposed; renewed persistent activity since **1994**, current edifice about **20,000 years old**. Domes, gas/ash, pyroclastic flows, lahars and ashfall create different hazards. Dome growth/collapse is one mechanism, not a universal rule that every dome must seal the vent and cause the same eruption.
- Better models can improve monitoring, repeated comparisons, warnings and preparedness; they do **not guarantee exact eruption prediction**. Access risks, weather and equipment failures limit data.
