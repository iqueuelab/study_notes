India & World Geography

Arabian Desert and the Green Arabia hypothesis

Editable notes

Geography and ecology

  • Arabian Desert: about 2.33 million km², largely Saudi Arabia and adjoining Arabian Peninsula States; among the largest hot deserts, not the second-largest desert if polar deserts are included.
  • Rub al-Khali/Empty Quarter: vast sand sea; An-Nafud in the north; Ad-Dahna links major sand regions. Hyper-arid interiors, extreme heat and seasonal northwesterly shamal winds shape the landscape; rainfall varies, so <33 mm is not universal.
  • Drought-adapted vegetation includes acacia, ghaf, saltbush and date palms in suitable sites; fauna include Arabian oryx, gazelles, camels, wolves, hyenas, caracals, dhub lizards, vipers and falcons.

Evidence for wetter phases

  • An April 2025 speleothem study found recurring humid intervals over approximately 8 million years in central Arabia, rather than continuous desert conditions. Mineral deposits and fluid-inclusion isotope evidence indicate past infiltration and changing moisture sources.
  • Dating used uranium–lead for older samples and uranium–thorium for younger ones; the entire multi-million-year sequence cannot be dated by U–Th alone. Wet intervals could facilitate plant/animal dispersal; they do not imply humans lived throughout all 8 million years. Original study.
  • Complementary evidence: Baynunah Formation, UAE, fossils of water-dependent/savanna fauna; Jubbah, Saudi Arabia, palaeolakes, tools and early-human occupation evidence.
  • Size revision: Great Sandy < Gobi < Arabian < Sahara. The printed UPPSC 2023 question lacks its full alternative answer sets; retain this explanation without reconstructing missing options.
