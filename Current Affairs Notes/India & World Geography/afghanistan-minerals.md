# Afghanistan mineral resources: potential and constraints

## Resources, diplomacy and feasibility
- Afghanistan has iron ore (Hajigak, Bamiyan), copper (Mes Aynak, Logar), gold, chromite, industrial minerals and gemstones including lapis lazuli (Badakhshan), emeralds (Panjshir) and rubies. Lithium/cobalt/rare-earth potential is discussed in the source; occurrence or exploration potential is not the same as commercially recoverable reserves.
- The often-cited trillion-dollar valuation is a historical gross in-ground estimate, not annual revenue, proven bankable wealth or actual extraction. The issue's 2.2-billion-tonne iron/60-million-tonne copper numbers and “Saudi Arabia of lithium” analogy lack consistent resource/reserve and survey definitions; do not memorise them as audited reserves or imply parity with Bolivia's lithium resources.
- Copper and critical-mineral interest attracts Chinese and regional investors. Mining contracts are commitments, not completed mines; the source's $6.5-billion figure is source-period reporting. Security, infrastructure/power/rail gaps, water/environmental impacts, finance, governance, human rights and sanctions complicate production; a universal 7–10-year lead time is not guaranteed.
- India considers mineral security and access through Iran's Chabahar alongside historic Hajigak interest. Proposed connectivity or investments do not demonstrate an operating Indian mine or that Afghanistan is itself a formal INSTC member. Revenue transparency, local benefit, ecological safeguards and stability determine whether resources support development or conflict.
