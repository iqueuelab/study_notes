# Mount Elbrus and Caucasus mountain geography

## Map anchors
- Source reports an Arunachal Pradesh mountaineer's ascent of **Mount Elbrus, 5,642 m**, southwestern **Russia**, in the **Caucasus between the Black and Caspian Seas**. Conventionally Europe's highest summit and one of the Seven Summits; Europe–Asia boundaries are geographic conventions.
- Twin-summit volcanic massif; distinguish volcanic Elbrus from the wider Caucasus mountain belt formed in the Arabia–Eurasia collision zone. The source's “formed over 2.5 million years ago” is not a verified date for the present volcanic edifice. It is a dormant volcano, not a frequently erupting twentieth-century volcano.
- Glaciers including Bolshoi Azau and Irik sustain the **Baksan, Malka and Kuban drainage**; Prielbrusye National Park protects alpine ecosystems. Cold, altitude illness and crevasses require preparation even when a route has relatively modest technical difficulty.
- **2024 mountain-range PYQ: B, only two**. Vosges are European block mountains; Alps European fold mountains; Appalachians North American fold mountains; Andes South American fold mountains. Source Elbrus practice **C (1, 3 and 4)**; its option B is misprinted.
