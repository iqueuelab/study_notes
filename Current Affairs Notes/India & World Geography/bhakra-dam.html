Bhakra Dam and interstate water sharing

Editable notes

Location, engineering and reservoir

  • Concrete straight gravity dam on the Sutlej, near Bhakra village, Bilaspur, Himachal Pradesh, close to Punjab. Its weight resists water pressure; the gorge provides the foundation setting.
  • BBMB specifications: height 225.55 m above deepest foundation, top length about 518 m; source gives 207.26 m without a measurement datum and 518.25 m length. Do not compare dimensions with different datums or retain “world's highest/Asia's second tallest” as reliable rankings.
  • Gobind Sagar reservoir: BBMB gross storage 9,621 million m³ = 9.621 billion m³, surface area 168.35 km². Source's 9.34-billion-m³ and second-largest-in-India ranking are not the same verified engineering series; storage rankings depend on gross/live capacity and sedimentation. Source reservoir length about 90 km, crest width 9.1 m. BBMB.
  • Bhakra ≠ Nangal: Bhakra is the principal storage/hydropower dam; the smaller downstream Nangal dam regulates releases within the integrated project.

Nation building and water governance

  • Source dates preparatory construction to 1948; BBMB identifies the 1951–63 construction phase. Nehru's first concrete-pouring ceremony was 17 November 1955, not 1948; project dedicated/completed 1963. His temple metaphor conveyed infrastructure-led nation building. BBMB history, Nehru's contemporary speech.
  • Bhakra Beas Management Board (BBMB) operates/manages the system and coordinates water/power distribution. Irrigation, drinking water, hydropower and moderated downstream floods support Punjab/Haryana/Rajasthan and Green Revolution agriculture; flood moderation is not a guarantee of zero flood risk.
  • May 2025 dispute: the issue reports Punjab political opposition to additional Bhakra water releases for Haryana. This is a contested allocation/operation issue, not a new settled legal entitlement established by the PDF.
  • Mains: cooperative federalism, transparent reservoir/inflow data, agreed seasonal allocation and drought priorities, drinking-water security, efficient irrigation, dam safety and ecological flows. Separate engineering storage from political water entitlements.
