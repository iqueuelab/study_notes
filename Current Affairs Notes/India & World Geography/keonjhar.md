# Keonjhar: geography, tribal culture and mineral economy

## Location and population
- **Keonjhar/Kendujhar**, northern **Odisha**, is an inland district; the source map places it within Odisha, not on the coast. Forested uplands and the **Baitarani**, associated with **Gonasika**, connect ecology, sacred geography and livelihoods.
- **Census 2011**: population **18,01,733**; Scheduled Tribes approximately **45%**. These are dated district figures, not current Odisha-wide proportions. [District demography](https://kendujhar.odisha.gov.in/en/about-district/demogrpahy), [Census ST abstract](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/42194/study-description).
- Source-listed communities include **Munda, Kolh/Ho, Bhuiyan, Juang, Saanti/Sounti, Bathudi, Gond, Santhal, Oraon and Kondh**. **Juang is a PVTG**; this classification does not apply to every tribe in the district. [Tribal Affairs official list](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1577166&lang=2&reg=48).
## Festivals and dances
| Tradition | Revision cue |
|---|---|
| **Sarhul** | Munda spring festival; sal flowers and sacred grove worship |
| **Sohrai** | Munda cattle/agricultural prosperity festival |
| **Karmapuja** | Adivasi community worship and agrarian associations |
| **Chaitra/Uda Parab** | Seasonal festival associated locally with Chhau |
| **Changu** | Named for accompanying drum; prominent among Bhuiyans |
| **Juang and Ho dances** | Community celebration, song and percussion |
- Other source-listed festivals include **Nuakhai, Raja Parab, Baruni Jatra and Rath Jatra**; their presence does not make each unique to Keonjhar. [District culture account](https://kendujhar.odisha.gov.in/en/tourism/culture-Heritage).
- **Chhau** entered UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage Representative List in **2010**. Styles: **Seraikella (Jharkhand), Purulia (West Bengal), Mayurbhanj (Odisha)**. **First two use masks; Mayurbhanj does not**—correcting the source's blanket mask description. This is a living performance tradition, separate from UNESCO documentary heritage. [UNESCO Chhau](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chhau-dance-00337).
## Mineral economy and inclusion
- Keonjhar is especially associated with **iron-ore mining**, alongside forest-resource use and agriculture. The source's broad iron-ore/chromite wording should not displace **Sukinda, Jajpur** as Odisha's principal chromite-location cue. [Government mining annexure](https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/173/AU1616.pdf?source=pqals).
- Mining employment/revenue coexist with displacement, forest/water pressures and livelihood disruption. Development requires community participation, recognised land/forest rights, rehabilitation, accessible education/healthcare and environmental monitoring; mineral output alone does not demonstrate inclusive prosperity.
