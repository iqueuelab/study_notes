# Cyclone Tauktae, intensification and INCOIS

## Ocean–atmosphere coupling
- Tauktae was an extremely severe cyclonic storm in the **Arabian Sea, May 2021**. Warm surface/subsurface water, moisture, vorticity and favourable wind shear support intensification.
- INCOIS-associated research estimated atmospheric/ocean contributions for Tauktae at approximately **53.08%/46.92%** under its method; the source's 54/46 rounds/confuses another comparison. These are study-specific attributions, not universal cyclone weights. [Research paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-92460-4).
- Negative IOD means a relatively cooler western and warmer eastern equatorial Indian Ocean. ENSO/IOD influence conditions probabilistically; La Niña does not automatically intensify every Arabian Sea cyclone.
- Distinguish storms' basins: Amphan and Mocha were Bay of Bengal systems, not Arabian Sea examples.
- Forecast skill, shelters, evacuation, warnings reaching communities and resilient infrastructure matter; warming can increase intensity without every regional frequency trend being identical.
## INCOIS recall
- Established **1999**, autonomous under **Ministry of Earth Sciences**, based in Hyderabad; an ESSO organisation.
- Services: **Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre; Potential Fishing Zone advisories; ocean-state forecasts; observing/data services**, including Argo and ocean-analysis inputs.
- Indian Ocean tsunami-service-provider role operates within UNESCO-IOC arrangements. Ocean forecasts support navigation, fisheries, offshore activity and coupled climate models.
- INCOIS ocean/tsunami services complement **IMD's cyclone-warning responsibility**; they are not interchangeable agencies.
