# Cloudbursts and Himalayan flash-flood risk

## Hazard and August 2025 context
- Embedded report describes flash floods through **Dharali, Uttarkashi**, from **Kheer Ganga**, with early deaths/missing-person reports. Treat casualties as preliminary; a devastating flash flood alone does not prove a measured cloudburst at the site.
- IMD definition centres on **100 mm or more rainfall in an hour over a small local area**, often cited **20–30 km²**; NDMP 2019 uses roughly **less than 100 km²**. Area descriptions are not competing rainfall thresholds. [IMD study](https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/view/5084), [NDMP 2019](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/images/pdf/NDM_Plan_2019.pdf).
- Moisture-rich air rises/cools rapidly in strong convection; mountain uplift can intensify rainfall. **Not exclusive to orographic lifting or July–August/18–28°C**, despite the source graphic. Warmer air's moisture potential can intensify extreme rain; it does not establish a uniform measured increase in every Himalayan valley.
- Steep slopes, unstable sediment, constricted drainage and exposed settlements convert intense rain into flash floods, debris flows and landslides; deforestation/poor construction amplify vulnerability rather than directly defining a cloudburst.
## Reduce exposure and impacts
- Local rain/radar monitoring and impact-based warnings, catchment/landslide hazard zonation, protected drains, slope-sensitive building/road siting, resilient bridges, evacuation planning and community drills. Insurance assists recovery but cannot replace safe siting.
