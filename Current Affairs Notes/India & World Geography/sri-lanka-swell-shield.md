# Sri Lanka and Southern Ocean swell shielding

## October 2025: INCOIS wave study
- Sri Lanka's landmass blocks/redistributes long-period Southern Ocean swell energy approaching parts of India's southeast coast. Swells originate in distant storms and can travel thousands of kilometres, unlike locally generated wind waves; they can produce coastal inundation/erosion without a local storm.
- INCOIS comparison used **2019** waverider-buoy data off Kollam and Puducherry: about **96% of long-period-swell events observed at Kollam did not reach Puducherry**. This is an event-comparison result for those records, not 96% of all Indian Ocean waves or all coastlines. WAVEWATCH III 6.07 experiments removing Sri Lanka supported the shielding mechanism. [INCOIS research abstract](https://figa2025.incois.gov.in/iiosc2025/report_details.jsp?reg_id=ABS-05-0128).
- Puducherry is a Union Territory location; do not label it simply a Tamil Nadu buoy site. Protection is direction/period/geography dependent and does not remove cyclone, tsunami or all swell hazards. The hypothetical removal experiment does not establish imminent disappearance of Sri Lanka under sea-level rise.
- Coastal hazard mapping, swell alerts, ports, fisheries and tourism planning should incorporate landmass shielding, bathymetry and exposure alongside changing climate/sea level. Practice statements 2/3 are correct; local-wind/short-distance definition is false.
