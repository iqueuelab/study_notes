# Chile: geography, resources and India ties

## Map recall
- Long, narrow country on **South America's Pacific coast**; land neighbours **Peru (north), Bolivia (northeast), Argentina (east)**; Andes form much of the eastern boundary. **Santiago** is the capital.
- **North → south:** Atacama Desert → central Mediterranean-climate zone → temperate southern forests and Patagonia. The Valdivian region supports temperate rainforest.
- **Humboldt/Peru Current** flows northward along western South America. Cold water, subtropical subsidence and the Andes rain shadow help explain Atacama aridity; nutrient-rich upwelling supports fisheries.
- **Drake Passage** separates southern South America from Antarctica; Cape Horn lies in Chile's far south. Chile's southern ports are gateways for Antarctic research/logistics.
## Resources and economic geography
- Important copper producer; **Chuquicamata** is a major copper mining complex, not an unqualified permanent “world's largest open-pit mine” ranking.
- **Salar de Atacama:** lithium-bearing brines. Copper and lithium connect Chile to electricity infrastructure and battery supply chains; mining has water/ecosystem trade-offs. [USGS Chile](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/national-minerals-information-center/chile).
- Historical **sodium nitrate (NaNO₃)** deposits: fertilisers/explosives; other resources include gold, silver and iron ore.
- Wine, fruits such as grapes/apples/berries, forestry products and fisheries diversify exports; Atacama's high solar resource supports renewable-energy opportunities.
## India–Chile trade: correct the timeline
- Original **PTA signed 8 March 2006**, effective **September 2007**. Expansion signed **6 September 2016**, effective **16 May 2017**; 2016 was not the original agreement year. [Commerce Ministry](https://www.commerce.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/MOC_636338205640368585_Expansion_India-Chile_PTA.pdf).
- Mineral supply cooperation, pharmaceuticals, textiles, vehicles, renewables and Antarctic science offer complementarities. Preferential tariff concessions are narrower than an unrestricted free-trade regime.
