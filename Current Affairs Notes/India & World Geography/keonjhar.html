Keonjhar: geography, tribal culture and mineral economy

Editable notes

Location and population

  • Keonjhar/Kendujhar, northern Odisha, is an inland district; the source map places it within Odisha, not on the coast. Forested uplands and the Baitarani, associated with Gonasika, connect ecology, sacred geography and livelihoods.
  • Census 2011: population 18,01,733; Scheduled Tribes approximately 45%. These are dated district figures, not current Odisha-wide proportions. District demography, Census ST abstract.
  • Source-listed communities include Munda, Kolh/Ho, Bhuiyan, Juang, Saanti/Sounti, Bathudi, Gond, Santhal, Oraon and Kondh. Juang is a PVTG; this classification does not apply to every tribe in the district. Tribal Affairs official list.

  • Other source-listed festivals include Nuakhai, Raja Parab, Baruni Jatra and Rath Jatra; their presence does not make each unique to Keonjhar. District culture account.
  • Chhau entered UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage Representative List in 2010. Styles: Seraikella (Jharkhand), Purulia (West Bengal), Mayurbhanj (Odisha). First two use masks; Mayurbhanj does not—correcting the source's blanket mask description. This is a living performance tradition, separate from UNESCO documentary heritage. UNESCO Chhau.

Mineral economy and inclusion

  • Keonjhar is especially associated with iron-ore mining, alongside forest-resource use and agriculture. The source's broad iron-ore/chromite wording should not displace Sukinda, Jajpur as Odisha's principal chromite-location cue. Government mining annexure.
  • Mining employment/revenue coexist with displacement, forest/water pressures and livelihood disruption. Development requires community participation, recognised land/forest rights, rehabilitation, accessible education/healthcare and environmental monitoring; mineral output alone does not demonstrate inclusive prosperity.
