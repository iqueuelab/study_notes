# Arunachal Himalayan glaciers: retreat and GLOF risk

## Study and corrected arithmetic

- *Journal of Earth System Science*, **2025**, Ritse/Hazarika/Kulnu/Jamir: Landsat and Sentinel-2A imagery, semi-automatic mapping/GIS, **Randolph Glacier Inventory / GLIMS** reference, **1988–2020** Arunachal Himalayas.
- **756 → 646 glaciers**, net **110 fewer**. Initial area **585.23 ±3.4 km²**; published university abstract gives final area **275.381 ±2.9 km²**, implying loss **309.849 km² ≈52.9%**. Source swaps final area/loss and prints **47%**. [Author institution abstract](https://cottonuniversity.ac.in/index_research_highlights_details?lang=ASM&p=elN5S25McE1zYTRuQm1WcStNYmZmQT09?).
- Abstract also states **16.94 km²/year**, which does **not equal** the simple endpoint average (**309.849/32 ≈9.68 km²/year**). Method/period needs resolution; do not memorise 16.94 as endpoint arithmetic.
- Survey spans Tawang–Lohit, including West Kameng/Kurung Kumey/Upper Siang/Upper Dibang Valley; inventory boundaries and counting fragmented glaciers affect comparisons.
- Source temperature **0.1–0.8°C/decade**, future **5–6°C / precipitation +20–30%** lack scenario/baseline; projections are conditional, not assured forecasts.

## Why retreat matters

- Melting/retreat may form unstable glacial lakes → **GLOF**, threatening settlements, roads and hydropower. Not every glacier loss creates a lake or outburst.
- **2023 South Lhonak–Teesta disaster**, Sikkim, damaged **1,200 MW Teesta III**; source 55 deaths is a dated reported count, not final verified total.
- Changing seasonal runoff affects water security and irrigation; immediate meltwater increase does not guarantee long-term water abundance.
- Monitor lake/glacier change, assess moraine stability, install warning/evacuation systems, use hazard-sensitive infrastructure siting and coordinated catchment planning. Afforestation alone cannot stop glacier retreat/GLOF.
