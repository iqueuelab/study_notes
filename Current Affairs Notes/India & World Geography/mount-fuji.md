# Mount Fuji: volcano, sacred landscape and tourism

## Map and cultural anchors
- Japan's highest mountain, **3,776 m**, on Honshu between Yamanashi and Shizuoka, south-west of Tokyo; an active **stratovolcano**, last eruption **1707–08, Hōei**.
- Its setting involves a complex plate junction/subduction system, not a complete explanation by one simple Pacific–Eurasian boundary.
- With Mount Tate and Mount Haku, one of Japan's three holy mountains; Shinto/Buddhist traditions and Hokusai's art contribute to its significance.
- UNESCO inscription **2013, cultural category**, as Fujisan; within Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park. Natural scenery does not make the UNESCO category natural.
- 2025 climbing arrangements introduced **¥4,000** fees across the four trails; dollar equivalents fluctuate. [Official regulations](https://www.fujisan-climb.jp/l62q6e0000000457-att/Regulations-Mt-Fuji-2025.pdf).
- Overcrowding, waste, rapid overnight “bullet climbing” and altitude exposure require booking/access rules, preparedness and carrying-capacity management. Hut exceptions and restrictions vary by trail; it is not a universal requirement that every climber must stay overnight.
