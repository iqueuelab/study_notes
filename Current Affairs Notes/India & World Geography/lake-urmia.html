Lake Urmia: salinity and basin restoration

Editable notes

February 2026: Iran's shrinking saline lake

  • Northwestern Iran, an endorheic hypersaline lake: water leaves mainly through evaporation rather than a river outlet. Tributaries include Talkheh/Aji Chay, Zarrineh and Simineh. Ramsar wetland and UNESCO Biosphere Reserve; supports Artemia and migratory waterbirds, including flamingos.
  • Drought/warming + irrigation withdrawals and dams/diversions reduce inflow. Shrinkage concentrates salts and exposes lakebed → windblown saline dust → soil salinisation, crop damage and respiratory-health risks. Water decline cannot be attributed to climate alone. UNEP assessment.
  • Reported rain prayers/cloud-seeding attempts illustrate drought response; they do not establish that cloud seeding restored the lake. Durable restoration needs basin-wide environmental flows, efficient irrigation with actual withdrawal limits, groundwater control and livelihood-sensitive land/water planning. Integrated basin management plan.
  • Great Salt Lake—USA is the western-hemisphere saline-lake recall; the supplied JKSSB question is excluded. Avoid the source's “Dead Sea is the world's saltiest water body”: salinity rankings vary and more saline waters exist.
