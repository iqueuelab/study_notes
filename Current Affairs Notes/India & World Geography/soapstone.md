# Soapstone / steatite

## Rock → mineral → formation
- **Soapstone / steatite**: talc-rich **metamorphic rock**; **talc is a mineral**, not every soapstone specimen is pure talc. Soft, dense and heat-resistant; talc contributes the soapy feel.
- Associated minerals vary: **mica, chlorite, amphibole, quartz, magnesite and carbonates**.
- Heat/pressure and alteration of **peridotite, dunite or serpentinite**; chemically active hot fluids can alter **siliceous dolostone**, an example of **metasomatism**. Convergent settings favour metamorphism; not the only formation setting.
- Graphic recall: temperature/pressure change existing rocks without requiring complete melting; melting and solidification would instead produce igneous rock. [NPS rock classification](https://home.nps.gov/subjects/geology/metamorphic.htm).
## Locations, uses and regulation
- January 2025 source: Uttarakhand High Court scrutiny of unregulated mining in **Bageshwar**, cited **over 160 mines**; slope stability, river pollution and compliance are governance concerns.
- Source lists **China, India, Brazil, USA and Finland** among producers; Indian Bureau of Mines reserve snapshot **Rajasthan 57%, Uttarakhand 25%**, reference year unspecified; not current market shares.
- Slabs: countertops, sinks, hearths and sculpture. Processed talc: ceramics, paint, roofing, cosmetics/pharmaceutical formulations, lubricating/filling/thickening uses.
- Source describes talc/corn-starch powders; this is an industrial-use fact, not advice to apply mineral dust to infants. Product purity and inhalation/contamination risks require appropriate standards.
