# Tapti Basin Mega Recharge Project

## 10 May 2025: inter-State MoU
- **Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra** signed an MoU in Bhopal for the proposed **Tapti Basin Mega Recharge Project**. The newspaper clipping identifies **Mohan Yadav** as MP Chief Minister and **Devendra Fadnavis** as Maharashtra Chief Minister; the summary's “Deputy CM” designation is wrong for this event.
- **Tapti/Tapi** rises near **Multai, Betul district**, in the Satpuras, flows west south of the Narmada, and reaches the **Gulf of Khambhat/Arabian Sea**. Length about **724 km**; basin covers MP, Maharashtra and Gujarat. [CWC basin record](https://www.cwc.gov.in/sites/default/files/admin/9_N%26TBO_Gandhinagar_WestFlowingRivers_SYB_2015-16.pdf).
- **31.13 TMC** planned water use: **11.76 TMC MP + 19.37 TMC Maharashtra**. The source’s 19.36 is inconsistent with the Chief Minister’s release. TMC means thousand million cubic feet.
- Proposed irrigation: **123,082 hectares in MP (Burhanpur/Khandwa)** and **234,706 hectares in Maharashtra (Jalgaon/Akola/Buldhana/Amravati)**. The plan includes a dam at **Khariya Guti Ghat** and an estimated **₹19,244 crore cost at 2022–23 prices**.
- **Nagpur’s long-term drinking-water benefit concerned the separate Jamghat project discussed at the same meeting**, not a verified Tapti recharge allocation to Nagpur. [Chief Minister’s 10 May release](https://www.devendrafadnavis.in/press-release/tapi-mega-recharge-prakalpababat-maharashtra-aani-madhyapradeshat-aitihasik-karar/).
- MP's land requirement reported at **3,362 hectares**, with officials claiming no village displacement/rehabilitation needed. This is a project claim requiring social-impact verification, not an independently established guarantee of no livelihood effects.
- Leaders sought **national-project recognition and 90% central funding**; these were requests/expectations, not confirmed sanction or money released. The MoU was finalised after an inter-State Control Board meeting reportedly held for the first time in **25 years**; the concept dates to the **1990s**.
- “World's largest recharge project” is an attributed political claim, not an independently verified global ranking. Signing an MoU does not mean recharge/irrigation benefits have already materialised.
- Assess aquifer geology, recharge rates, water quality, downstream/environmental flows (including Gujarat), hydrological climate variability, EIA/SIA, community participation, financing and regular joint-board oversight. Ken–Betwa and Parvati–Kalisindh–Chambal illustrate cooperation, not identical project design.
