Shipki La: border tourism and trade

Editable notes

Location and June 2025 development

  • Himalayan pass in Kinnaur, Himachal Pradesh, on the India–Tibet (China) border; elevation about 3,930 m. Source reports domestic border tourism opened with Aadhaar-based identification and removal of the earlier permit requirement; this is a June 2025 development, not proof of a 2024 opening.
  • Trade was historically linked to Bushahr/Rampur and Guge. Wool, livestock, hides, yak hair, borax, turquoise and religious objects came from Tibet; grain, pulses, spices, vegetables, utensils, timber and iron goods went from India.
  • Oral agreements, shared Tibetan Buddhist traditions, monasteries, festivals and pastoral ties sustained local relationships. Small national trade volumes can still matter greatly to border livelihoods and cultural continuity.
  • The 1962 war interrupted trade, but later border trading resumed; COVID-19 closed it again in 2020. The source's claim of no commerce for decades is misleading. Tourism reopening is distinct from resuming international trade.
  • Potential gains: border employment, reduced outmigration and people-to-people links. A shorter Kailash–Mansarovar route remains a proposal requiring bilateral arrangements, not an operational pilgrimage route.
  • 2007 PYQ: Nathu La (Sikkim) reopened for India–China trade in July 2006; distinguish it from Shipki La.

August 2025: proposed trade resumption

  • Embedded clipping reports China agreed in principle during Wang Yi's recent India visit to the proposal to resume trade through Shipki La; the Union government initiated discussion concerning Shipki La, Lipulekh and Nathu La. This is diplomatic progress, not evidence traders have already crossed or customs operations restarted.
  • Trade had been suspended in 2020 because of COVID-19; the body's claim of no commercial activity for decades is stale. Domestic tourism access remains distinct from cross-border trade and from a proposed Kailash–Mansarovar route through Shipki La. Existing location, traditional exchange and 2007 Nathu La PYQ notes apply.
  • Official 7 August 2025 parliamentary reply confirms designated trade arrangements: Lipulekh 1992, Shipki La 1995 and Nathu La July 2006; all disrupted in 2020, with resumption under discussion then. MEA parliamentary answer.
