# Mullaperiyar Dam and interstate safety governance

## Dam and dispute
- **Idukki, Kerala**, on **Periyar near Mullayar confluence**; Tamil Nadu operates/maintains it under interstate arrangements for diverted water supporting irrigation/drinking supply and power. Completed **1895**; “126 years old” is stale for 2025. Source dimensions **53.66 m height/365.85 m length** need engineering datum consistency.
- **Periyar: about 244 km**, Kerala's longest river, Sivagiri/Western Ghats headwaters; tributaries **Mullayar, Muthirapuzha, Cheruthoni, Perinjankutti**; reservoir/river landscape within Periyar protected area. Source categorically locates origin in Tamil Nadu without adequate boundary evidence; use geographic headwaters rather than assert that State label. [Kerala statistics](https://ecostat.kerala.gov.in/publication-detail/compendium-of-environment-statistics-kerala-2013).
- **1979** safety concerns lowered level from **152 ft to 136 ft** while strengthening. Supreme Court **2006/2014** allowed **142 ft**, with **2014 supervisory mechanism**. Kerala seeks replacement/decommissioning for safety; Tamil Nadu argues strengthening/continued supply and eventual 152-ft restoration. Demands are not automatically permitted operational levels.
## 19 May 2025: repair directions
- Kerala to forward tree-felling application/recommendations **within two weeks**; Union **MoEFCC sanction within three further weeks**, subject to conditions. **No felling before formal sanction**.
- Material transport: Kerala had consented for six of nine repairs; remaining permissions **within one week**, with Kerala Executive Engineer present. **Vallakadavu Ghat Road** repairs: Kerala executes with ecologically suitable materials/Tamil Nadu engineer; Tamil Nadu pays; **four-week** completion direction.
- Dormitory repairs permitted under supervision; second boat conditional on **Inland Vessels Act 2021/Wildlife Protection Act 1972** compliance. Supervisory Committee addresses remaining matters **within six weeks** and grouting recommendations **within four weeks**.
- These are **court directions**, not proof works finished or environmental statutes waived. [Supreme Court order](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2006/8520/8520_2006_2_301_61943_Order_19-May-2025.pdf).
- Cooperative engineering assessment, transparent monitoring, downstream preparedness and predictable access are necessary; nearby landslides alone do not establish imminent structural failure.

## October 2025: heavy rain and downstream preparedness
- The issue reports northeast-monsoon flooding and mudslides in **Idukki (Kerala) and Theni (Tamil Nadu)**, road/power disruption, a landslide death in Idukki, temporary closure of **Suruli waterfalls**, and increased Mullaperiyar releases. **139.15 ft** is the reported reservoir snapshot, not a newly authorised operating limit or proof the dam failed.
- Named flood-affected localities include **Vallakkadavu, Ayyappancoil, Vandiperiyar and Manjumala**; Kumily–Kattappana/Kumily ghat roads faced obstruction. The source also reports Kochi urban flooding and a Tirunelveli flash-flood fatality; these are contemporaneous reports rather than a final statewide casualty total.
- Authorities issued downstream warnings, advised avoiding night travel on hill roads until **24 October**, cautioned fisherfolk and moved exposed people to safer ground. These are issue-period advisories. Early-warning communication, evacuation/shelters, slope/road management and coordinated Kerala–Tamil Nadu reservoir operations reduce risk.
- Existing notes retain the **1895 Periyar dam**, Kerala location/Tamil Nadu operation and court-permitted **142-ft** level. Rain, saturated slopes and releases can compound downstream risk; neighbouring landslides alone do not establish imminent structural failure. Source height figures use inconsistent datums and should not replace engineering measurements.
- Northeast monsoon **October–December** brings Bay-of-Bengal moisture to southeastern peninsular India, especially Tamil Nadu; wind/relief/local weather control actual rain. A localised episode should not automatically be equated with the geographic severity of the 2018/2019 Kerala floods.
