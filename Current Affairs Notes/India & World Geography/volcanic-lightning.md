# Volcanic lightning: plume electrification and hazard monitoring

## Ash, ice and charge separation
- Explosive eruptions generate electrified plumes; **ash collisions/friction, fragmentation and charge separation**, plus **ice charging** in cold upper regions, can produce discharge. Near-vent dense ash and high-altitude plume lightning need not have identical mechanisms. [USGS explanation](https://www.usgs.gov/news/science-snippet/hazard-guess-riskiest-science-quiz-you-will-ever-take-14).
- Magmatic water and entrained/external water may contribute to ice/hydrometeor formation. Correct the source's simplistic “not a thunderstorm/only ash, not water” contrast: volcanic plumes can have thunderstorm-like ice processes. Not every plume has more water than every ordinary storm, and not every eruption produces detectable lightning.
- Discharges may occur near the gas-thrust vent, rising convective column and umbrella cloud; they threaten people and electrical infrastructure and can help detect ash-producing eruptions remotely. Lightning frequency is a **context-dependent proxy**, not an exact universal eruption-strength meter. [USGS plume regions](https://volcanoes.usgs.gov/volcanic_ash/lightning.html).
- Source historical examples: **Pliny the Younger’s account of Vesuvius, AD 79**, and nineteenth-century observations associated with **Luigi Palmieri**. Volcanic electrification occurs beyond a single tectonic setting; explosive subduction-zone volcanoes are useful examples, not an exclusive requirement.
