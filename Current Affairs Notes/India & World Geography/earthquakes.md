# Earthquakes, seismic waves and risk

## Focus, fault and waves
- Earthquake: sudden release of stored energy, often through **fault slip**; tectonic movement, volcanic processes and some human activities can trigger seismicity.
- **Focus/hypocentre**: origin inside Earth; **epicentre**: surface point vertically above it.
| Wave | Medium / motion |
| --- | --- |
| P, primary body wave | Fastest; compression; solids, liquids and gases |
| S, secondary body wave | Transverse; solids; cannot pass through liquid outer core |
| Love, surface wave | Horizontal side-to-side motion |
| Rayleigh, surface wave | Elliptical motion in a vertical plane |
- **P-wave shadow zone roughly 103°–142° angular distance from epicentre**; refraction through the core. Direct S waves absent beyond about **103°** because the outer core is liquid.
- Love waves generally faster than Rayleigh waves; surface waves can be highly destructive. Shaking damage also depends on depth, distance, soil and construction.
## Measurement and distribution
- **Seismometer** records motion; **magnitude** measures event size, while **Mercalli-type intensity** describes local effects (I–XII).
- Source's Richter '0–10' is not a fixed upper-bounded scale; modern large-earthquake reporting commonly uses moment magnitude.
- Major belts: **Circum-Pacific, Alpine-Himalayan, mid-ocean ridges**. Source approximate **81%/17%** shares are broad distribution claims with differing event definitions, not timeless exact proportions.
- Plate boundaries: convergence/subduction, transform sliding and divergence/spreading; earthquakes also occur within plates.
## January 2025 Indian map and interior
- Source-era BIS zones **II, III, IV, V**, with V highest; about **59%** land classified in moderate-to-very-high hazard zones. The graphic's older **Zone I** key should not be combined with the four-zone map. [NIDM explanation](https://nidm.gov.in/safety_earthquake.asp).
- These are the **January 2025 source-era classifications**, not a claim that later standards cannot change.
- **Crust + uppermost mantle = lithosphere** over weaker asthenosphere; mantle extends to about **2,900 km**; **liquid outer core**, **solid inner core**.
- Risk reduction: enforce appropriate building codes, retrofit critical facilities, map local soils, plan drills/emergency response and protect lifelines; hazard maps do not predict exact earthquake dates.
## February 2025 preparedness discussion
- **17 February Delhi/Bihar tremors** renewed concerns; impact depends on depth, local soils, building quality and exposure, not magnitude alone.
- India–Eurasia convergence drives Himalayan seismic hazard. Source-era zoning **II, III, IV, V**; “15 major plates” is not a universally fixed geological classification.
- Source Türkiye–Syria **17,000+** deaths was an early count; Tibet **126–400** mixes reports/definitions. Do not present these as reconciled final tolls.
- **Prediction ≠early warning**: exact time/place/magnitude prediction remains unavailable; early warning detects an earthquake already beginning and may provide seconds before strong shaking.
- Enforce applicable building codes, retrofit vulnerable buildings, use microzonation and risk-sensitive land/infrastructure assessment; dams/tunnels can pose site-specific risks, not proof all infrastructure weakens Earth's crust.
- Redundant lifelines, NDRF/local readiness, drills, shelters, trained medical response and accessible alerts. Improve affordable insurance/risk finance without assuming no existing earthquake cover is available.
- Mains: **hazard cannot be prevented →reduce exposure/vulnerability →prepare response and recovery**; the supplied 150-word prompt is coaching practice.

## Seismic recall
- Focus/hypocentre is the underground origin; epicentre lies above it on the surface. **P waves** travel through solids/liquids; **S waves** through solids; Love/Rayleigh surface waves often cause damaging motion.
- Magnitude describes event size and is logarithmic; intensity describes local effects and varies by location. Depth, buildings, soils and distance shape damage.
- India's seismic zones are **II, III, IV and V** in the standard map, with V highest hazard; the current scheme has no zone I. Intraplate earthquakes also occur, not only boundary events.
