Indian Polity

Surrogacy: regulation and reproductive autonomy

Editable notes

November 2025: challenge to the existing-child restriction

  • Supreme Court agreed to examine the Surrogacy (Regulation) Act, 2021, section 4(iii)(c)(II) restriction on intending couples with a surviving biological, adopted or surrogate child. Exception: a child with mental/physical disability or life-threatening disorder/fatal illness without permanent cure, subject to the statutory medical certification/approval. Pending examination is not a judgment striking the rule down.
  • Petitioners invoke Article 21, reproductive autonomy and secondary infertility, arguing that inability to conceive a second child should not defeat access automatically. The Centre argues that surrogacy is regulated by statute, entails another woman's bodily autonomy and should not be an unrestricted entitlement. Reported population concerns and judicial observations are not a final constitutional ruling.
  • Gestational surrogacy uses an embryo without the surrogate's own egg; this definition does not require both gametes invariably to come from the intending couple. Commercial surrogacy is prohibited; altruistic surrogacy permits prescribed medical expenses and insurance. Couple age conditions: wife 23–50, husband 26–55, with medical indication and eligibility/essentiality requirements.
  • Correct the source's blanket exclusion of all single women: the Act recognises an Indian widow or divorcee aged 35–45 as an intending woman. Nor should medical indication be reduced to a universal first-child infertility test. Surrogate consent and withdrawal before implantation, insurance and protection against exploitation remain distinct safeguards. Act text.
  • Practice intends 1 and 2 only (A); its claim that an unrestricted right to surrogacy is already recognised as fundamental is unsupported. Distinguish reproductive liberty from a claim on another person's reproductive labour; assess proportionality, access and surrogate protection.
