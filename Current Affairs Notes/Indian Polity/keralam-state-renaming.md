# Kerala to Keralam: constitutional renaming process

## February 2026: Cabinet approval is one stage
- **24 February 2026:** Union Cabinet approved the proposal to change **Kerala to Keralam**. President was to refer the **Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026** to the State Assembly for views under **Article 3**, followed by the required presidential recommendation for introduction in Parliament. Cabinet approval alone did not change the constitutional name. [Cabinet release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2232093).
- Parliament can alter a State's name by ordinary law, with **simple majority**. State views must be sought within the specified/extended period but are not binding; a State resolution is a political initiative, not a constitutional veto or universal prerequisite for every Article 3 bill.
- **Article 4:** consequential First/Fourth Schedule changes and supplemental provisions can be included; such a law is not treated as an Article 368 constitutional amendment. Effectiveness follows the enacted law's commencement provisions, not automatically Cabinet announcement.
- Malayalam usage “Keralam” motivates linguistic/cultural recognition; the Assembly's **24 June 2024** resolution is confirmed in the release. Kerala formed **1 November 1956**, after Travancore–Cochin's **1949** union and linguistic reorganisation. The **Fazl Ali Commission was appointed 1953**, reporting 1955, rather than created in 1956.
- Ashokan “Keralaputra”/Chera associations and competing etymologies are historical explanations, not a conclusively proved derivation. Federal participation, cultural identity, administrative transition and costs are useful analytical links; do not treat the source's West Bengal rejection claim as an established constitutional precedent.
