# Assisted dying, living wills and dignity

## England and Wales: position on June 21, 2025
- Commons passed the **Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill** at third reading on **20 June 2025, 314–291**. It still required further parliamentary passage; assisted dying was **not already legalised**. [Parliament record](https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/2025-06-20/debates/525F3344-9DEB-4821-8F8D-01BE7B5E80B0/TerminallyIllAdults%28EndOfLife%29Bill).
- Proposed eligibility: adult 18+, decision-making capacity, terminal illness with six months or less expected life, England/Wales residence for at least 12 months; disability/mental disorder alone does not qualify. Proposed assistance requires a voluntary, informed request and patient self-administration.
- Medical assessments by two doctors and reflection periods are safeguards. **June version used Assisted Dying Review Panels, replacing the original mandatory High Court approval**; PDF mixes the original and amended versions. Panels include legal, psychiatry and social-work expertise. [Commons Library bill history](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10256/).
- Suicide Act 1961 criminalises assistance in England and Wales, with up to 14 years' imprisonment; Northern Ireland has separate legislation. Do not assign that Act identically to the whole UK.
## India and ethical distinctions
| Term | Meaning / June 2025 Indian position |
| --- | --- |
| Active euthanasia | Direct intentional lethal intervention; not authorised |
| Assisted suicide | Another facilitates patient's lethal self-administration; not authorised |
| Withholding/withdrawing life-sustaining treatment | Permissible under judicial safeguards in appropriate terminal/irreversible circumstances |
| Advance medical directive / living will | Adult records future treatment wishes for incapacity; implementation requires safeguards and medical assessment |
- **Aruna Shanbaug (2011)** recognised a framework for withdrawal; **Common Cause (2018)** recognised dying with dignity within **Article 21** and advance directives; **2023** order simplified procedures. This is not an unrestricted general right to suicide.
- Compare autonomy/dignity and relief of suffering with coercion, prognostic uncertainty, disability discrimination, unequal care and the duty to protect vulnerable people. Strengthen palliative care and meaningful informed consent.
- Netherlands/Belgium/Luxembourg, Switzerland and some US jurisdictions have differing assisted-death rules; no single international model. **France's National Assembly passed a proposal at first reading on May 27, 2025; it was not operative assisted-dying law in June.** [French legislative record](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/dossiers/fin_de_vie_17e).
