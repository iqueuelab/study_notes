# Digital Personal Data Protection: rights and implementation

## November 2025: rules and phased commencement
- **DPDP Act 2023** and **DPDP Rules 2025**: November notification starts phased implementation; the source's **February 2025 final-notification date is wrong**. Draft rules were consulted on in January 2025. The final framework follows **Puttaswamy 2017** privacy recognition, **B.N. Srikrishna committee 2018**, and the revised 2022 draft, preceding the 2023 Act. [Official November announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014&lang=2&reg=3).
- **Data Principal = individual to whom personal data relates; Data Fiduciary = person determining processing purpose/means**. They are not simply “citizen” and “company”: government bodies can be fiduciaries; statutory scope concerns digital personal data, including covered data digitised later and specified overseas processing linked to goods/services for individuals in India.
- Core duties include lawful processing, notice/consent or permitted legitimate uses, reasonable security, breach reporting and retention/deletion rules. Access, correction/erasure, grievance and nomination rights have statutory qualifications. Significant Data Fiduciaries have additional obligations, including DPO/audit; not every fiduciary must appoint a DPO by November 2026.
- **Phased schedule:** immediate institutional provisions; Consent Manager framework after one year; principal operational duties after eighteen months. The source's November 2026 “DPO deadline” confuses the one-year tranche; May 2027 is the broad eighteen-month implementation horizon, not an exemption unique to large technology firms. Board constitution does not establish that all substantive duties were enforceable in November 2025. [MeitY notification collection](https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa?pageTitle=Digit).
- Source describes a **four-member Data Protection Board**; distinguish notified composition from completed appointments. The framework provides monetary penalties for specified breaches and an adjudicatory Board; maximum penalties are not automatic for every infringement.
- State exemptions concern specified statutory grounds/notifications and should be assessed through necessity, proportionality and safeguards; the PDF's blanket claim that every government activity is exempt is too broad. Analyse surveillance risk, regulator independence, children's consent, accessible redress and business readiness alongside privacy protection.
- **RTI section 8(1)(j)** is replaced through **DPDP section 44(3)** in the November commencement framework; concern is wider personal-information withholding and accountability. **RTI section 8(2)'s public-interest override remains**—the source's statement that disclosure is impossible even in public interest is overbroad. Apply severability, reasoned decisions and appeals; earlier February/September notes correctly distinguish enactment from later commencement.
