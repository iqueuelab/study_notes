# Narco-analysis: consent, evidence and constitutional rights

## June ruling discussed in December 2025
- **Amlesh Kumar v State of Bihar, 9 June 2025 (2025 INSC 810)** set aside the Patna High Court's **9 November 2023** order permitting investigative narco-analysis. This is a June judgment revisited in December, not a new December verdict.
- Reaffirming **Selvi 2010**, involuntary narco/polygraph/brain-response techniques violate **Articles 20(3) and 21** protections against compelled self-incrimination and intrusion into personal liberty. Narco-analysis alters mental control through drugs such as sodium thiopental; it is neither a truth guarantee nor simply a harmless ‘non-violent’ procedure.
- Even voluntary test reports are not directly admissible. Subsequent discoveries may be admitted under applicable discovery rules (**Evidence Act Section 27 in the judgment**), and cannot alone establish guilt without supporting evidence. It cites **Manoj Kumar Soni**, not the PDF's ‘Saini’.
- Voluntary application at the defence-evidence stage remains subject to judicial assessment, free consent, legal advice, Magistrate-recorded consent and medical/independent-agency safeguards; there is **no unconditional right** to demand the test. The source's **‘BNSS Section 253’** does not accurately state the judgment's discussion of **CrPC Section 233**.
