Model Code of Conduct: welfare and election fairness

Editable notes

November 2025: Bihar welfare transfers and the MCC

  • Model Code of Conduct (MCC) is a consensual code administered by ECI, applying from election-schedule announcement until completion of the election process. Its purpose is a level playing field: prevent official-machine misuse, campaign inducements, communal appeals, inflammatory attacks and misuse of government publicity.
  • MCC is not itself a standalone penal statute; overlapping conduct can attract election/criminal laws and ECI directions. “Voluntary” does not mean the Commission lacks enforcement powers. Use RPA 1951 and applicable criminal law, rather than treating the replaced IPC as the sole 2025 framework. Ongoing work/essential services are not universally frozen.
  • Bihar Mukhyamantri Mahila Rojgar Yojana transferred initial ₹10,000 livelihood support to women; official launch/first transfer was 26 September 2025, correcting the source's August launch. PM launch address. The editorial questions continuing transfers around 6/11 November polling and whether pre-poll scheme timing circumvents the code's spirit; this criticism is not a judicial finding that every disbursement was illegal.
  • Debate: give MCC statutory backing and predictable sanctions versus the time needed for litigation and possible election delays. The source reports a 2013 parliamentary recommendation and ECI opposition; its 45-day argument is contextual, not an invariant constitutional limit for all elections.
  • Editorial proposal to remove routinely unenforced restrictions on welfare/project announcements is a recommendation, not an adopted rule. Alternatives: clear objective criteria for ongoing versus new benefits, transparent schedules, uniform enforcement, reasoned decisions and monitoring of campaign misuse. Frequent MCC periods feature in simultaneous-election arguments, but do not prove that all public administration must stop.
