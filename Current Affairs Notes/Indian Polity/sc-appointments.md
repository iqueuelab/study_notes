# Supreme Court judges: appointments and independence

## Constitutional recall
- **Article 124**, originally **CJI + 7 judges**; statutory sanctioned strength **CJI + 33 = 34**, distinct from actual filled seats. January 2025 news: **Justice K. Vinod Chandran sworn in**.
- **124(3)**: Indian citizen + **5 years HC judge**, or **10 years HC advocate**, including successive qualifying HCs, or **distinguished jurist in President's opinion**. No express general minimum age in this clause.
- **124(2)**: President appoints; constitutional consultation operates through judicially developed collegium rules. Oath under **124(6)/Third Schedule**, before President/appointed person; Constitution, sovereignty/integrity, impartial duties.
- Retirement **65**; resignation to **President**. Salaries under **125**, expenditure charged to Consolidated Fund; parliamentary determination subject to constitutional protection.
- **124(7)** bars a former SC judge from pleading/acting in any court or before any authority in India. **128** recall requires CJI request, President's prior consent and **judge's consent**, not compulsory recall.
- Removal **124(4)**: President after address by **each House in same session**, majority **total membership + at least two-thirds present and voting**, **proved misbehaviour/incapacity**. Do not confuse with simple majority. [Official Constitution](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/08/7af1daa22d65f9d04c00ae9b9aa5a799.pdf).
## Collegium timeline
| Case | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **First, 1981** | Executive primacy; consultation not binding CJI veto—source's contradictory “CJI primacy” incorrect |
| **Second, 1993** | Judicial primacy/institutional CJI opinion with two senior colleagues |
| **Third, 1998** | SC-appointment collegium **CJI + 4 senior-most** |
| **2015 NJAC judgment** | Invalidated **99th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2014**, and **NJAC Act, 2014**; collegium restored |
- “Collegium” is not constitutional text; SC appointment and HC recommendation collegia are not identical in size/function.
- Mains: independence with transparency, diversity, recorded selection criteria/conflicts and timely appointments; executive participation must respect constitutional/judicial framework.
## Distinguished jurists: proposal versus existing law
- **Article 124(3)(c)** permits a citizen whom the **President considers a distinguished jurist** to be appointed to **Supreme Court**. January article reports the route unused; this is a dated observation, not a restriction to sitting judges or practising advocates.
- **High Courts differ**: current **Article 217(2)** requires citizenship plus **ten years judicial office / ten years High Court advocacy**, with relevant counting explanations. **Article 124(3)(c) cannot directly appoint a jurist to a High Court**.
- Former **Article 217(2)(c)** jurist route was inserted by **42nd Amendment, 1976**, removed by **44th Amendment, 1978**; reintroducing it requires constitutional change. The source's combined SC/HC practice-question wording is defective. [Constitution with amendment notes](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/08/7af1daa22d65f9d04c00ae9b9aa5a799.pdf).

## Evaluate the reform
- Benefits: specialist knowledge, research quality, varied perspectives, academic–practice links and expertise in complex socioeconomic/technology cases.
- Risks: limited courtroom/procedural experience, practical case management, opaque criteria and institutional resistance; merit assessment, mentoring and procedural training help.
- Source cites USA/Poland/Spain as comparisons; jurisdictions have different ordinary/constitutional court pathways, so they are not identical Indian models.
- Fill vacancies promptly while preserving independence; workload/process reform and support staff matter alongside appointments. Adding academics alone cannot erase the backlog.

## Transfers and in-house accountability
- High Court transfers are governed by **Article 222**, with President acting after constitutionally required consultation, informed by the collegium jurisprudence. Articles 124/217 alone are not the transfer provision.
- Collegium consultation with relevant judges/Chief Justices is distinct from an in-house inquiry into allegations. Transfer is neither conviction nor a substitute for accountability.
- The Supreme Court's March 2025 account distinguished the proposed Yashwant Varma transfer from the inquiry. On **22 March**, a three-member inquiry committee was announced; allegations require a fair opportunity to respond. [Supreme Court release](https://www.sci.gov.in/press-release-dated-march-22-2025/).
- For an HC judge, the in-house committee ordinarily includes two HC Chief Justices and one HC judge. Natural justice, confidentiality where justified, transparent institutional action and constitutional removal procedures are complementary safeguards.

## Removal procedure: July 2025 revision
- **Judges (Inquiry) Act 1968**: notice signed by at least **100 Lok Sabha or 50 Rajya Sabha members**; Speaker/Chairman may admit or refuse after consideration.
- Admitted motion → inquiry committee: **CJI/SC judge, HC Chief Justice, distinguished jurist**. Statutory inquiry differs from the judiciary's in-house administrative inquiry; a finding of guilt is not itself removal.
- Only after the requisite address by both Houses in the **same session**, on proved misbehaviour/incapacity, may the President remove. This mechanism balances independence with accountability.
- **V. Ramaswami (1993):** Lok Sabha motion failed; **Soumitra Sen (2011):** Rajya Sabha passed motion, but resignation preceded Lok Sabha vote. “Impeachment” is common usage; constitutional text speaks of removal.

## July 2025 accountability debate
- Source reports the Law Minister's parliamentary reply restating removal provisions without specifying a reform programme after allegations concerning **Justice Yashwant Varma**. An unanswered reform question is not proof that every possible consultation has been ruled out.
- **HC removal:** Article 217(1)(b), read with 124(4) and 218. The in-house mechanism can seek resignation/retirement, recommend withdrawal of judicial work and communicate serious findings for constitutional action; it cannot itself remove a judge. Thus the source's “no action unless resignation” is too absolute.
- Reform priorities: independent/fair fact-finding, defined timelines, reasoned public institutional communication with necessary confidentiality, complainant protection and judicial independence. The PDF's 250-word/15-mark question is daily practice, not an identified UPSC PYQ.

## September 2025: women in the Supreme Court
- Editorial snapshot: only **Justice B.V. Nagarathna** among sitting judges was a woman; **11 women since 1950**, source denominator **287 appointments (~3.8%)**. Statutory **34 sanctioned posts** is distinct from filled strength. First woman **Justice Fathima Beevi, 1989**; **Indu Malhotra** was the first woman directly elevated from the Bar.
- Source expects Nagarathna to become CJI in **2027** for a short tenure under seniority convention; this is a succession expectation, not an appointment already made.
- Barriers: limited direct elevations, late appointment/short tenure, weak pipeline to seniority and opaque selection. Gender, regional and social diversity can widen experience and institutional trust without assuming all women judges share the same views.
- Proposals: published diversity/selection criteria, reasoned decisions, support for women advocates and HC judges, more consideration of direct elevations. A gender quota is an editorial proposal, not an existing constitutional appointment quota. Existing collegium and President-appointment framework applies.

## November 2025: renewed plea to revive NJAC
- Advocate **Mathews J. Nedumpara** sought revival of NJAC and invalidation of the **2015 NJAC judgment**. CJI **Surya Kant** said the Court would consider the request for listing. An oral remark on listing neither admits every legal contention nor restores the commission.
- Petition alleges opacity/nepotism and excessive judicial displacement of Parliament; these remain **petitioner's arguments**. Constitutional review of amendments is compatible with parliamentary democracy under the basic-structure doctrine; judicial independence is a protected component.
- Former **NJAC**: CJI, two senior SC judges, Union Law Minister, two eminent persons. Eminent-person selection committee: **PM, CJI, Lok Sabha Leader of Opposition (or leader of largest opposition party where no recognised LoP)**. The **99th Constitutional Amendment Act 2014 and NJAC Act 2014 were invalidated in 2015**; an oral listing assurance does not undo that ruling.
- Existing notes give the **1981 executive primacy → 1993 judicial primacy → 1998 expanded consultation** sequence and SC collegium **CJI + four senior-most judges**. “Judges appoint judges” is shorthand: formal appointment remains by the President under the constitutional scheme. Evaluate transparent criteria, diversity/conflict disclosures and timely recommendations alongside independence, without treating allegations as findings.
