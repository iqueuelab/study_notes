# Delimitation, fertility and federal representation

## January 2025 editorial argument
- Southern states fear reduced relative parliamentary representation after population-based delimitation because fertility fell earlier than in many northern states.
- The article discusses Andhra Pradesh reversing a two-child electoral restriction and proposals for larger families. These are reported political responses, not settled national policy.
- **Pro-natal incentives may not reverse low fertility effectively**; China, Japan and South Korea illustrate limits of coercion or incentives and the importance of population composition, ageing and dependency.
- A simple headcount can overlook achievements in education, health and demographic transition; representation must reconcile voter equality and federal fairness.
## Gender and policy dimensions
- Childbearing involves lost earnings, unpaid care and constraints on women's autonomy; childcare, social protection and compensation/support are more substantive than appeals to reproduce.
- Migration can address regional labour/population imbalances sooner than fertility changes; it does not by itself settle constitutional seat allocation.
## Mains distinction
- **Author's proposal:** revisit purely count-based representation and consider broader capability characteristics.
- This is an editorial position, not an enacted delimitation formula. Assess alternatives against constitutional equality, federalism, feasibility and women's reproductive choice.
## Fertility decline: January 2025 discussion
- **TFR**: average children per woman under current age-specific fertility rates; conventional replacement level **about 2.1**, varying with mortality. Below-replacement fertility does not mean population immediately shrinks: **population momentum** matters.
- Source **GBD 2021 estimates**: India **6.18 in 1950**, **1.9 in 2021**; projected **1.04 in 2100**. Projection depends on assumptions, not a forecast guaranteed to occur; do not mix GBD figures with NFHS survey estimates without labels.
- Education, lower desired family size, child survival, contraception, delayed marriage/parenthood, urban living and costs of care drive transition. Source's ranking of infertility/abortion or foreign migration as dominant nationwide causes is not demonstrated.
- Southern States: earlier transition, ageing, changing dependency ratios, healthcare/pension needs and worker migration. Source Kerala migrants **one-sixth by 2030** is an estimate, not a completed census finding; elderly already outnumbering all younger people is an overstatement.
- South Korea source **0.78 (2022), 0.73 (2023)** is rounded/inexact; official 2023 figure **0.72**, illustrating limits of incentives alone.
- Support childcare, affordable housing, secure employment, gender-equitable care and reproductive choice; older-worker opportunities and internal migration complement productivity gains. Smaller youth cohorts do not mechanically mean less innovation.

- Fertility comparison: [Statistics Korea](https://www.kostat.go.kr/boardDownload.es?bid=11748&list_no=437488&seq=1).

## Delimitation trigger
- Articles 82/170 link readjustment to census-based processes; seat freeze concerns the first census **after 2026**, not automatic redistribution in 2026.
- Delimitation Commission includes a serving/former Supreme Court judge, CEC or nominated Election Commissioner and relevant State Election Commissioners. Population, demographic performance and fiscal transfers involve different legal mechanisms.

## SRS 2021 released in May 2025
- **Sample Registration System**, Registrar General of India, sample-based continuous demographic estimates; reference year **2021**, not May 2025 fertility. National **TFR 2.0**, unchanged from **2020**, below conventional **2.1** replacement. Below replacement does not immediately stabilise total population because **momentum/age structure** matter. [Official SRS report](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/45556/download/49753/SRS_STAT_2021.pdf).
- **Bihar 3.0** highest in cited State comparison; **Delhi/West Bengal 1.4** lowest. Embedded clipping also gives **Tamil Nadu 1.5, Andhra Pradesh 1.5, Jammu & Kashmir 1.5, Kerala 1.5, Maharashtra 1.5, Punjab 1.5, Himachal Pradesh 1.6, Telangana 1.6, Karnataka 1.6, Odisha 1.8, Uttarakhand 1.8, Gujarat 2.0, Haryana 2.0, Assam 2.1**. **At** replacement and **below** replacement differ; “≤2.1” is not wholly below.
- **1971→2021 age shares:** 0–14 **41.2%→24.8%**; 15–59 **53.4%→66.2%**; 60+ **6%→9%**. Working-age population is not equivalent to employed workers or guaranteed dividend. The clipping separately gives **65+: 5.3%→5.9%**: do not confuse age thresholds.
- Elderly 60+: **Kerala 14.4%, Tamil Nadu 12.9%, Himachal Pradesh 12.3%**; **Bihar 6.9%, Assam 7%, Delhi 7.1%** are lower. Female mean age at effective marriage **19.3 (1990)→22.5 (2021)**; this is a demographic measure, not the legal marriage age.
- **2024 Interim Budget** proposed a population/demographic-challenges committee; source reports not yet formed at this issue date. Announcement is distinct from functioning institution/findings.
- Demographic transition varies across States: education, nutrition, voluntary family planning and women's autonomy in higher-fertility areas; childcare, secure work, migration, geriatric care/pensions/accessible infrastructure as ageing grows. Invest in skills/jobs to realise the dividend.
- Last completed Census then **2011**; delayed next Census matters for local planning. SRS, NFHS and projections have different methods; a national demographic-transition “stage” does not capture all States.

## June 2025: delimitation framework and assurance
- Delimitation fixes **territorial constituency boundaries**, distinct from allocating total seats among States and from local-body delimitation. Aim: broadly equal population representation and fair boundaries; SC/ST reservation under **Articles 330/332** is related but separate from total State entitlement.
- Initial **1950–51** exercise preceded commissions **1952, 1963, 1973, 2002**, under Acts **1952, 1962, 1972, 2002**. Last general change in State-wise Lok Sabha allocation followed **1971 Census**, completed **1976**. [ECI history](https://www.eci.gov.in/delimitation-2002).
- **42nd Amendment 1976** freeze; **84th Amendment 2001** extended seat-allocation freeze to the first census after 2026 while permitting boundary rationalisation with 1991 data; **87th Amendment 2003** substituted 2001 data. The PDF graphic's “84th Amendment 2002” is incorrect. Boundary readjustment did not itself raise each State's seat total.
- **Articles 82/170** govern census-linked readjustment, subject to constitutional freezes; no automatic redistribution on 1 January 2027. Under **section 3, Delimitation Act 2002**, **Central Government constitutes the Commission**: serving/former Supreme Court judge as chair, CEC or nominated Election Commissioner, relevant State Election Commissioner. The PDF's universal President-appointment formulation is imprecise. Majority decisions and published orders have statutory force; constitutional restrictions on questioning delimitation do not make every related government act immune from judicial review. [Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2004/1/A2002-33.pdf).
- Embedded June report: **Amit Shah** said southern States' rightful share would not be reduced and seats would increase pro rata; **A. Raja/DMK** asked whether this meant existing-seat or population proportions. These are political assurances/questions, **not a final enacted allocation formula**.
- Southern demographic achievements create concerns about relative influence; source **35% GDP/18% population** lacks specified States/year. **Finance Commission tax devolution ≠ Lok Sabha seat allocation**: population, income distance, demographic performance and other criteria differ; using 2011 population does not alone determine all transfers.
