Indian Polity

President’s Rule: constitutional limits and federal safeguards

Editable notes

Trigger and effect

  • Article 356: State government cannot be carried on according to the Constitution; President may act on Governor's report or otherwise. Ordinary law-and-order difficulty or a hung Assembly does not automatically justify it.
  • Article 355: Union duty to protect States and ensure constitutional government. 365: failure to comply with lawful Union directions may support a constitutional-breakdown assessment, not automatic dismissal.
  • President may assume specified State executive functions and declare legislative powers exercisable by Parliament. High Court powers cannot be assumed.

Approval and duration

  • Both Houses must approve within two months, by ordinary majority. Special Lok Sabha dissolution rule: Rajya Sabha approval plus Lok Sabha approval within 30 days of its first sitting after reconstitution.
  • Approved proclamation operates six months, renewable by both Houses in six-month periods, maximum three years.
  • Beyond one year, both conditions: a national emergency operates in India or all/part of the State and ECI certifies election difficulties. Do not substitute either condition alone. President can revoke without parliamentary approval. Parliamentary reference.
  • Article 357: Parliament may confer State legislative power on the President, with permitted delegation; this is different from an Article 123 ordinance.

Safeguards and reform

  • S.R. Bommai, 1994: judicial review, floor-test primacy for majority disputes, limits on dissolution before parliamentary approval; courts may restore unconstitutional dismissal.
  • Rameshwar Prasad, 2006: Bihar Assembly dissolution held unconstitutional. Political apprehensions are not a substitute for relevant constitutional evidence.
  • Sarkaria 1988: last resort, warning/remedial options where feasible. Punchhi 2010: proposed localised responses; district-level Article 356 is not existing constitutional law.
  • Assess federalism, democratic mandate and prompt return to representative government. Source counts of impositions are dated, not a current total.

June 3 clipping: Manipur Assembly and restoration

  • Embedded explainer describes 10 MLAs seeking a viable Manipur government after President's Rule imposed February 2025. Assembly term runs to March 2027, with Assembly in suspended animation, not automatically dissolved.
  • Article 356 history includes inconsistent Governor responses to dissolution advice and political misuse; majority should ordinarily be tested on the floor. Existing notes cover approval, duration, Bommai judicial review and federal safeguards.
  • Restoring a viable majority-supported government need not always await fresh elections. The clipping's usual-election sequence is not a universal constitutional condition for revocation; suspended Assembly permits constitutionally valid alternatives. Bihar 2005 events/Rameshwar Prasad 2006, and Uttarakhand/Arunachal 2016 illustrate judicial intervention; dates of events and judgments differ.

5 February 2026: Manipur government restored

  • The issue reports the end of President's Rule and Yumnam Khemchand Singh's oath as Chief Minister on 4 February 2026, with Nemcha Kipgen and Losii Dikho as Deputy Chief Ministers. PMO confirmation.
  • A suspended Assembly can support restoration of representative government without first holding a fresh election; revocation is not automatic dissolution. Existing notes retain two-month approval, six-month renewals, three-year ceiling, both conditions for extension beyond one year, judicial review and floor-test safeguards.
  • Correct repeated explainer shortcuts: ordinary disorder, election delay or a hung House is not automatically Article 356 breakdown; Article 357 concerns delegated State legislative power, not a generic power to issue ordinances; Article 123 is the separate ordinance provision. Punchhi's localised-emergency recommendation remains a proposal.
