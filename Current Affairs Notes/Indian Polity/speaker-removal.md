# Removal of the Lok Sabha Speaker

## February 2026: notice against the Speaker
- Opposition notice targeting **Om Birla** followed disputes over Rahul Gandhi's attempted reference to a former Army Chief's unpublished memoir and the conduct of proceedings. Treat submission/procedural scrutiny separately from a resolution admitted for debate, voted upon or carried.
- This is **removal under Article 94(c)**, not the **Council of Ministers no-confidence procedure**. Lok Sabha alone decides by **majority of all the then members**: **effective majority excluding vacancies**, not total sanctioned strength or merely those present/voting. At least **14 days' notice**.
- **Rules 200–203:** written notice/full resolution to Secretary-General; specific, clearly worded charges without argument/inference/irony/defamatory statements. At leave stage **at least 50 members must rise** in support; this is not a constitutional minimum number of notice signatories or the House's quorum. Discussion is fixed within **10 days of leave being asked for**; debate stays within the charges. Speeches normally have a **15-minute limit**, extendable by the presiding authority; this is not an absolute limit only on the mover. [Lok Sabha Rules, chapter XVIII](https://sansad.in/uploads/Rules_of_Procedures_E_9d8fd0f4c3.pdf?updated_at=2022-).
- **Article 96:** Speaker **cannot preside** while removal is considered, but may **speak/participate and vote in the first instance**, with **no casting vote** on equality. **2024 PYQ: statement 1 only—(a)**.
- **Article 94(b):** Speaker resigns **to Deputy Speaker**; Deputy Speaker **to Speaker**, not “to the House”. Speaker continues after dissolution until immediately before the next Lok Sabha's first sitting. Previous removal resolutions discussed against **Mavalankar (1954), Hukam Singh (1966), Balram Jakhar (1987)** did not remove them; allegations and constitutional outcomes must remain distinct.
