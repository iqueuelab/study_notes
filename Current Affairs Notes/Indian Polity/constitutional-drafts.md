# Alternative Indian constitutional drafts, 1895–1948

## Competing visions before the 1950 Constitution
| Draft | Revision focus |
|---|---|
| **Constitution of India Bill, 1895** | Early nationalist self-government draft, often associated with Tilak but anonymous authorship; 110 provisions, civil liberties, property and legal equality |
| **M. N. Roy, Constitution of Free India, 1944** | Radical-democratic vision: linguistic federal units, civil/economic rights, citizens’ committees and popular sovereignty, including resistance to tyranny |
| **Hindusthan Free State, 1944** | Mahasabha-associated draft; popular sovereignty, legal equality, religious freedom/no State religion, elected institutions and judicial remedies |
| **Shriman Narayan Agarwal, Gandhian Constitution, 1946** | Village-centred decentralisation, khadi/cottage industry, nonviolence and self-sufficiency; Gandhi wrote the foreword, not the draft |
| **Socialist Party draft, 1948** | Democratic socialism, planned economy, public/cooperative ownership, workers’ welfare and social equality; Jayaprakash Narayan wrote the foreword |
- **Corrections from the original texts:** the 1944 Hindusthan draft explicitly establishes a **federation** and a **bicameral Congress**, contrary to the PDF's “unitary” shorthand. It protects religious/cultural rights; do not reduce it to “one culture” or assert a secession right without the actual provision. [1944 draft](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/historical-constitution/the-constitution-of-the-hindusthan-free-state-act-1944-no-of-1944/).
- The Socialist draft permits a **controlled private sector** alongside state/cooperative sectors; the PDF's “no private ownership of means of production” overstates it. It includes political/civil rights as well as economic aims. [1948 draft](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/historical-constitution/draft-constitution-of-the-republic-of-india-socialist-party-1948/).
- Gandhian draft included a regulated **right to keep/bear arms**, an interesting tension with nonviolence; this was not adopted as a Fundamental Right in 1950. [1946 text](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/historical-constitution/gandhian-constitution-for-free-india-shriman-narayan-agarwal-1946/).
- Compare individual liberty, social/economic equality, centralisation and village participation. Similarities with Fundamental Rights/DPSPs/Panchayati Raj show a wider intellectual context; similarity alone does not prove direct borrowing from every draft.
