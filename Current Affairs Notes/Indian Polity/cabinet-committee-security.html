Cabinet Committee on Security: executive coordination

Editable notes

Composition and constitutional basis

  • CCS is a Cabinet committee for high-level security/defence decisions, chaired by the Prime Minister, with the Defence, Home, Finance and External Affairs Ministers as members. Officials/other invitees can assist; NSA is not thereby a ministerial member or automatically the committee's statutory secretary.
  • Cabinet committees are extra-constitutional in the sense that the Constitution does not individually enumerate them. Their executive-business framework rests on Article 77(3) and the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961; extra-constitutional does not mean unlawful. Composition/remits can be reconstituted by executive decision.
  • CCS handles defence policy, internal/external security and relevant strategic expenditure/inter-ministerial questions. Do not generalise that it makes every security appointment: the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet has its distinct assigned role. Transaction of Business Rules, Rule 6 and First Schedule.
  • The issue lists eight committees: Appointments; Accommodation; Economic Affairs; Parliamentary Affairs; Political Affairs; Security; Investment and Growth; Skill, Employment and Livelihood. Its older “Employment and Skill Development” name is not the 2024 reconstituted title. Parliamentary Affairs and Accommodation are the exceptions to PM chairmanship in that arrangement.
  • An early post-Independence Defence Committee and post-Kargil security reforms form historical context; the compilation does not establish that the present CCS was first legally created in 1947 or only after 1999. Its “3–8 members” is a textbook generalisation, not a statutory ceiling.
