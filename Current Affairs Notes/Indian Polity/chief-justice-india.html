Indian Polity

Chief Justice of India: appointment and functions

Editable notes

Appointment and qualifications

  • Article 124(2): President appoints the CJI. Convention/Memorandum of Procedure favours the senior-most Supreme Court judge considered fit; outgoing CJI recommends → Law Minister → Prime Minister's advice → President. Seniority is primarily continuous SC service; convention supports independence but is not a constitutional election procedure.
  • Article 124(3): Indian citizenship, plus at least 5 years as High Court judge, 10 years as High Court advocate (including successive High Courts), or a distinguished jurist in the President's opinion. Constitution prescribes no minimum age; retirement at 65. Do not imply distinguished-jurist appointments are a regular route.
  • Historical supersessions occurred in 1973 and 1977; 1973 preceded the Emergency, so not all deviations occurred during it.

Functions and appointments

  • Master of roster: constitutes benches and allocates cases; administrative power carries responsibility for fairness, institutional credibility and efficient adjudication.
  • SC appointments: CJI + four senior-most judges form the collegium. HC appointment recommendations involve the CJI + two senior-most SC judges; avoid treating every appointment/transfer consultation as identical.
  • Three Judges Cases: S.P. Gupta (1981) executive primacy; Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association (1993) judicial primacy; Presidential Reference (1998) expanded/clarified collegium consultation. Independence must be balanced with transparent criteria and accountability.
  • Article 127: when quorum is unavailable, CJI may request a qualified HC judge's attendance as an ad hoc SC judge, with prior President's consent and consultation with the relevant HC Chief Justice; not unilateral permanent appointment.
  • Article 130: SC sits in Delhi or places appointed by CJI with President's approval. Article 146: SC staffing/administrative responsibilities. CJI administers the President's oath; Governor's oath is administered by the relevant HC Chief Justice or available senior-most judge (Article 159), not routinely by CJI as the inset implies. Article 143 advisory jurisdiction is distinct from arbitration; inset's Centre–State financial-dispute arbitrator claim should not be learned as a general CJI constitutional power.

Removal and transparency

  • Article 124(4): President removes only after each House's address, in the same session, supported by majority of its total membership and at least two-thirds present and voting, for proved misbehaviour or incapacity. Judges (Inquiry) Act 1968 supplies investigation procedure. “Impeachment” is common shorthand; constitutional terminology is removal.
  • V. Ramaswami, 1993: removal motion failed in Lok Sabha. The source's “only one motion ever introduced” is incorrect; subsequent proceedings included HC judge Soumitra Sen (Rajya Sabha adopted a motion in 2011; resignation followed).
  • 13 November 2019, CPIO, SC v Subhash Chandra Agarwal: CJI office falls within RTI/public-authority framework; disclosure remains subject to statutory privacy, confidentiality, third-party procedure and public-interest balancing, not blanket publication of every collegium record. SC judgment.

15 May 2025 : 52 nd Chief Justice

  • Justice Bhushan Ramkrishna Gavai took oath as 52 nd CJI on 14 May 2025, following Sanjiv Khanna; President Droupadi Murmu administered the oath. The appointment was notified on 30 April, effective 14 May. Separate notification from assumption of office. President’s ceremony.
  • Source describes a roughly six-month tenure; this is a dated transition, not the identity of the current CJI indefinitely. Appointment/removal/roster/RTI principles and source errors are covered above.
