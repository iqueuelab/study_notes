Indian Polity

High Courts: appointments, independence and jurisdiction

Editable notes

Constitutional position

  • Part VI: 214 High Courts for States; 231 permits a common High Court for two or more States or States and a UT, introduced by the 7th Amendment (1956). Article 230 concerns extending/excluding jurisdiction over UTs; statutory reorganisation also matters.
  • Chief Justice and other judges: Constitution fixes no numerical strength. 217 appointment by President after constitutional consultation; appointment collegium: CJI + two senior-most SC judges. Transfer 222 follows the wider transfer collegium process.
  • Qualifications: citizen, ten years' judicial office or ten years as an advocate of a High Court/successive High Courts. No “distinguished jurist” route as for SC, and no constitutional minimum age.
  • Retirement 62 (SC: 65); resign to President; Governor administers oath. Age questions are decided by President after consulting CJI.

Independence and removal

  • Removal for proved misbehaviour or incapacity: each House supports the address by majority of total membership and two-thirds present and voting, followed by Presidential order (217/218 with 124(4)).
  • Judges Inquiry Act: motion signed by at least 100 LS or 50 RS members; admission by presiding officer; statutory committee comprises a SC judge/CJI, HC Chief Justice and distinguished jurist. This is distinct from an in-house inquiry.
  • 223 acting Chief Justice; 224 additional judges for temporary business/arrears (up to two years) and acting judges, subject to age limit; 224A retired judges with required consent and prior Presidential consent.

Jurisdiction

ProvisionRecall
215Court of record; contempt jurisdiction
226Writs for fundamental rights and other legal purposes; wider subject scope than SC Article 32
227Superintendence over subordinate courts/tribunals, excluding armed-forces courts/tribunals under this Article
228Withdrawal of qualifying cases involving substantial constitutional questions
235Control over subordinate judiciary
  • Territorial writ jurisdiction includes where cause of action arises wholly/partly; respondent need not always be located inside the State. The Article 227 military exclusion does not erase all Article 226 judicial review.
  • Original civil jurisdiction varies with statute/charter; election petitions follow election law. First appeals may involve law/fact; second civil appeals require a substantial question of law.
  • District-judge appointments: Governor in consultation with HC (233); other judicial-service appointments follow 234. These are not Presidential High Court appointments.
  • BNS/BNSS came into force in July 2024 with transitional savings; old IPC/CrPC references in the source should not be treated as the only current criminal codes.
