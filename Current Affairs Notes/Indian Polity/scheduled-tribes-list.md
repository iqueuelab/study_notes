# Scheduled Tribes: listing procedure and safeguards

## Listing and the inclusion debate
- **Articles 366(25), 342**: President initially specifies STs for a State/UT, consulting the Governor for a State; **Parliament alone amends inclusion/exclusion by law**. State-specific recognition does not automatically carry to another State. Cabinet approval followed merely by a fresh Presidential notification cannot substitute for Parliament's Article 342(2) role.
- Administrative route: justified **State/UT proposal → RGI and NCST concurrence → Union consideration → legislation**. These modalities differ from constitutional power to specify/amend lists. [Tribal Affairs parliamentary reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2117789&lang=2&reg=48).
- **Lokur Committee 1965** administrative criteria: distinctive culture, geographical isolation, economic backwardness and the historical phrases “primitive traits”/“shyness of contact”. The wording is criticised as dated; the Constitution does not define tribes through this five-point test. Source recalls **Hrusikesh Panda task force, 2014**, and the debate over rigid binary classification versus social change.
- **Fifth Schedule /Article 244(1)**: Scheduled Areas and ST administration outside **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram**; “except Northeast” is too broad. **Sixth Schedule /244(2)**: specified tribal areas in those four States, with autonomous district/regional councils. Governor/TAC safeguards and local autonomy are not State boundary-making powers.
- **2015 PYQ: (a)**—Fifth/Sixth Schedules protect ST interests. **2024 PYQ: (b), statement 2 only**—Governor does not declare a community ST; recognition is State-specific. Criteria reform and competing inclusion demands require evidence, fair consultation and protection of existing communities.
