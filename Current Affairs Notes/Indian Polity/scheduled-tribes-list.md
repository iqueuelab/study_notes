# Scheduled Tribes: listing procedure and safeguards

## Listing and the inclusion debate
- **Articles 366(25), 342**: President initially specifies STs for a State/UT, consulting the Governor for a State; **Parliament alone amends inclusion/exclusion by law**. State-specific recognition does not automatically carry to another State. Cabinet approval followed merely by a fresh Presidential notification cannot substitute for Parliament's Article 342(2) role.
- Administrative route: justified **State/UT proposal → RGI and NCST concurrence → Union consideration → legislation**. These modalities differ from constitutional power to specify/amend lists. [Tribal Affairs parliamentary reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2117789&lang=2&reg=48).
- **Lokur Committee 1965** administrative criteria: distinctive culture, geographical isolation, economic backwardness and the historical phrases “primitive traits”/“shyness of contact”. The wording is criticised as dated; the Constitution does not define tribes through this five-point test. Source recalls **Hrusikesh Panda task force, 2014**, and the debate over rigid binary classification versus social change.
- **Fifth Schedule /Article 244(1)**: Scheduled Areas and ST administration outside **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram**; “except Northeast” is too broad. **Sixth Schedule /244(2)**: specified tribal areas in those four States, with autonomous district/regional councils. Governor/TAC safeguards and local autonomy are not State boundary-making powers.
- **2015 PYQ: (a)**—Fifth/Sixth Schedules protect ST interests. **2024 PYQ: (b), statement 2 only**—Governor does not declare a community ST; recognition is State-specific. Criteria reform and competing inclusion demands require evidence, fair consultation and protection of existing communities.

## Kurmi mobilisation: September 2025
- The issue reports **rail blockades in Jharkhand** demanding **Scheduled Tribe status** and inclusion of **Kurmali in the Eighth Schedule**, despite prohibitory orders. Earlier mobilisation across Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha and Bihar supplies context; demands are not completed legal recognition.
- Kurmis are associated with agrarian livelihoods; Kurmali is an **Indo-Aryan language** spoken particularly in Jharkhand and adjoining eastern regions. Regional Kurmi/Kudmi/Kunbi names and State-specific reservation categories must not be collapsed into one uniform all-India legal identity.
- The source's Kshatriya descent/etymology accounts are community traditions, not established proof of ST eligibility; its sweeping gender-equity and work-ethic characterisations should not stereotype an entire community. General Article 342 procedure and Lokur/Panda debates are already covered in the 16 September notes.

## Motok mobilisation in Assam
- Source reports a torchlight rally by **Motok/Matak community** in Upper Assam seeking **ST recognition** and stronger autonomy through **Sixth Schedule inclusion**. Existing Motok Autonomous Council is a State-law body; a demand does not itself create constitutional council status.
- Historical association with Matak polity/Swargadeo Sarbananda Singha after the Moamoria rebellion supplies identity context. Source identifies Tinsukia, Dibrugarh and Sivasagar; livelihoods vary and should not stereotype all members as tea workers.
- Assam's six frequently cited claimant groups: **Moran, Motok, Chutia, Tai-Ahom, Koch-Rajbongshi, Tea Tribes/Adivasi communities**. Weigh inclusion evidence, existing ST safeguards, representation, land rights and consultation.
- Existing notes already correct Article 342 process: initial Presidential specification differs from **Parliamentary legislation** adding/removing communities. Cabinet followed simply by Presidential notification is not the amendment route. Fifth Schedule exception is four named States, not all Northeast. Lokur 1965/Panda 2014 debates and **2015/2024 PYQs** repeat earlier coverage.
