# Waqf administration and the 2025 reform debate

## Basic distinctions
- Waqf: permanent dedication for religious, pious or charitable purposes recognised by Muslim law; includes public and eligible family-benefit forms. **Waqif creates; mutawalli manages; statutory Board supervises**—the manager is not the personal owner.
- Major national statutory anchors: **1954 Act**, **1995 Act**, **2013 amendments**; Central Waqf Council established **1964**. “1955 Act” in the heading is erroneous. [Historical overview](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2118415&lang=2&reg=48).
- Registration, surveys, accounts, adjudication and legal challenge operate under law; not every disputed ownership claim is finally established waqf property.
- The source incorrectly suggests a two-thirds Board vote permits sale/gift/mortgage: **2013 section 104A prohibits sale, gift, exchange, mortgage or transfer**; leases have separate rules. [2013 Amendment](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/repealedfileopen?rfilename=A2013-27.pdf).
## Legislative timeline and debate
- **1 April 2025 issue** discusses the pending amendment process originating in 2024; draft provisions are not already law on that date. Passage and commencement followed later in April, so this is a historical debate snapshot.
- Proposed reforms concerned verification/registration, administrative accountability, composition and government-property disputes. Women representation was **already required in 2013**; do not say it first appeared in the 2024 proposal.
- Arguments for reform: transparent records, safeguarding beneficiaries/property and accountable management. Concerns: minority participation, excessive executive intervention, due process and religious autonomy.
- Articles **14, 25, 26 and 300A** frame equality, religious freedom/institutional rights and lawful property deprivation; freedoms operate with constitutional limits, not immunity from all regulation.
- Property-count/land-area estimates require registry date/definition; the “third-largest landowner” claim is not a reliable comparable ranking from the source alone.
- Evaluate final statutory text and court orders separately from the old draft. The undated 15-mark essay is practice, not a verified UPSC PYQ.
## 14 April 2025: Murshidabad unrest and constitutional duties
- The issue reports **three deaths and over 150 arrests** during violence associated with protests against the Waqf (Amendment) Act in Murshidabad, West Bengal. These are contemporaneous reported figures, not permanent totals. Reports identify Suti/Samsherganj and Ghospara, attacks on personnel, vehicles and railway property; individual allegations require investigation.
- The source reports Calcutta High Court intervention for central-force deployment and reinforcements including BSF companies. The newspaper clipping describes **17 CAPF companies overall**; BSF subset counts vary with deployment time and should not be confused with the total.
- **The Act was already public:** presidential assent/publication **5 April 2025**, commencement **8 April 2025**. The claim that details were yet to be released is incorrect. [Act Gazette](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/262316.pdf), [commencement notification](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/262340.pdf).
| Provision/institution | Revision distinction |
|---|---|
| Public order and police | Primarily State List responsibilities; governments must protect life, property and peaceful expression |
| Article 355 | Union duty to protect States against external aggression/internal disturbance and ensure constitutional governance; assistance does not itself replace the State government |
| Article 356 | Separate constitutional process for failure of constitutional machinery; central-force deployment does **not** automatically imply President's Rule |
| BSF/CAPFs | Border forces may assist internal security under lawful arrangements; assistance and takeover of State administration are distinct |
| Criminal procedure | BNSS has operated since 1 July 2024, subject to savings/transitional rules; do not cite the old CrPC as the universal current framework |
- Judicial oversight, proportionate policing, impartial investigation, relief and rehabilitation, verified communication and protection of fundamental rights are complementary. Violence by individuals must not be attributed collectively to a religious community.
- Distinguish peaceful constitutional disagreement about waqf administration from criminal acts during unrest. Articles 14, 21, 25 and 26 remain relevant to equal protection, life and religious freedoms.
