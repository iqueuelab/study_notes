# Substantive motions and parliamentary disciplinary powers

## Independent proposals before the House
- **Substantive motion**: self-contained proposal bringing a subject before the House for decision/opinion; a subsidiary motion relates to another proposal. Notice, admissibility and debate/voting follow applicable House rules and Speaker/Chair rulings. The PDF's statement that **every** substantive motion needs a seconder is too broad without the governing rule. [Parliament FAQ](https://sansad.in/rs/faq).
- Source context: **Nishikant Dubey's notice concerning Rahul Gandhi**; notice is not admission, adoption, suspension or expulsion. Distinguish disciplinary proposals, privilege motions, no-confidence motions, adjournment and censure by their purpose and procedure.
- **Article 105** privileges,**Article 118** procedure and **Article 122** protection against review for mere procedural irregularity are relevant. **Article 122 is not total judicial immunity**: constitutional violations/gross illegality remain reviewable. [Supreme Court discussion of Raja Ram Pal](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2012/36970/36970_2012_Judgement_09-May-2018.pdf).
- **2005 cash-for-query** led to expulsion of **10 Lok Sabha MPs plus one Rajya Sabha MP**; *Raja Ram Pal(2007)* upheld the power with limited review. The PDF's unexplained 2008 expulsion example is not retained as a settled precedent. Proportionate procedure protects both House dignity and constituents'representation.
