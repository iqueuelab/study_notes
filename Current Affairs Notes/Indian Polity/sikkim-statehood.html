Indian Polity

Sikkim: statehood and constitutional integration

Editable notes

Fiftieth anniversary in May 2025

  • Sikkim became India's 22nd State on 16 May 1975, through the Constitution (36th Amendment) Act, 1975. The clipping reports a 29 May 2025 virtual golden-jubilee address advocating tourism/sport; a vision of becoming a global hub is not an achieved ranking. Sikkim government account.
  • Namgyal monarchy began in the seventeenth century, headed by the Chogyal. The 1890 Anglo-Chinese Convention recognised British protectorate arrangements; after independence, the 1950 India–Sikkim Treaty formalised India's defence/external affairs/communications role while preserving internal autonomy. Do not treat 1947 alone as the treaty date.
  • 1953 State Council introduced limited representative governance; democratic agitation led by Kazi Lhendup Dorji and others culminated in the 1973 political crisis and tripartite arrangement. Government of Sikkim Act, 1974 reshaped representative government.
  • 35th Amendment Act, title year 1974, received assent 22 February 1975 and commenced 1 March 1975: added Article 2A and the then Tenth Schedule, association terms, one seat each in Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha and related Articles 80/81 changes. The PDF's “associated State status/representation began in 1974” confuses the title year with legal commencement. 35th Amendment text.
  • 1975 referendum supported ending the monarchy and integration; source's approximately 97% refers to the reported vote share, not necessarily 97% of every resident. Preserve democratic/political context rather than treating the transition as an ordinary princely-State accession.
  • 36th Amendment: added Sikkim in the First Schedule, parliamentary representation, Article 371F special provisions; omitted 2A and the association-era Tenth Schedule and removed related association references. This old Tenth Schedule is not today's anti-defection Tenth Schedule, inserted by the 52nd Amendment in 1985. 36th Amendment text.
  • Map: borders Nepal, Bhutan and Tibet/China, with West Bengal to the south. Himalayan connectivity, ecology, tourism and border stability support the strategic/federal relevance.
