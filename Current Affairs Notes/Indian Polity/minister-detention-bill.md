# Constitution (130th Amendment) Bill: ministerial detention

## Proposal in August 2025
- **Constitution (One Hundred and Thirtieth Amendment) Bill, 2025**, a Bill rather than an operative 130th Amendment Act, proposes changes to **Articles 75, 164 and 239AA** concerning Union, State and Delhi ministers. Parallel ordinary Bills cover Puducherry and J&K; do not label those constitutional amendment Acts.
- Trigger: arrest and **30 consecutive days in custody** for an alleged offence punishable with imprisonment that **may extend to five years or more**, not a conviction or a mandatory minimum five-year sentence. Ministers would be removed by the constitutional head on PM/CM advice by day 31; the introduced text makes cessation effective **the day falling after day 31** if advice is absent. PM/CM must resign by day 31 or cease office the day thereafter; reappointment after release is permitted. [Bill as introduced](https://sansad.in/getFile/BillsTexts/LSBillTexts/Asintroduced/130th%20eng820202532515PM.pdf?source=legislation).
- Article 368 special majority means **majority of total membership and at least two-thirds present and voting in each House**; the source's bare two-thirds statement is incomplete.
- **RPA 1951 section 8** concerns legislative disqualification following specified convictions; section 8(3) generally covers a sentence of at least two years and six years after release, while other listed offences have specific rules. Ministerial eligibility and legislative disqualification interact; they are not wholly unrelated.
- Arguments: public integrity/constitutional morality versus presumption of innocence, executive misuse of arrest, federal autonomy and voter representation. Detention/remand is not a guilt finding; judicial safeguards, prompt bail/trial and party candidate selection matter. Source 46% MPs/45% MLAs and winning-probability statistics are attributed ADR-era estimates with different samples, not proof every charged representative is guilty.

## October 2025: arrest, bail and removal debate
- The issue revisits discretionary arrest and potentially prolonged pre-trial custody as objections to the proposed removal trigger. **Joginder Kumar (1994), Arnesh Kumar (2014) and Satender Kumar Antil (2022)** emphasise justified arrest and procedural safeguards. Criminal procedure's **60/90-day default-bail framework** is not a promise every accused is released on day 30.
- Maintain the Bill's **2025** title and exact cessation timing recorded above; the PDF's **2024** title and immediate day-30/day-31 shorthand are incorrect. It remains a proposal in this issue, not an enacted constitutional amendment.
- Avoid the source's blanket assertion that PMLA, NDPS and UAPA all impose identical twin conditions requiring an accused to prove innocence and no reoffending: each statute's bail test differs. Special-law restrictions, remand and bail delay can intensify executive leverage, but do not establish guilt. Weigh public integrity against liberty, federal autonomy and political misuse; suspension is an editorial alternative, not the Bill's enacted rule.
