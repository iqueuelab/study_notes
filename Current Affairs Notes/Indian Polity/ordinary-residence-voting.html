Indian Polity

Ordinary residence and migrant voting rights

Editable notes

Statutory framework

  • RPA 1950 section 19: eligible adult citizens ordinarily resident in a constituency can enter its electoral roll, subject to disqualifications. Section 20: owning a house alone does not establish residence; temporary absence alone does not end it. Residence is determined from the facts, not ownership or a universal permanent-home test. India Code.
  • Special deeming provisions cover service-qualified persons and their eligible wives residing with them, and declared office-holders under the applicable provisions. Service qualification includes armed forces, forces subject to Army Act provisions, State armed police serving outside their State and Government of India staff posted abroad. Avoid treating every government employee or every spouse as automatically eligible under identical rules.
  • Section 20A, inserted 2010: an eligible Indian citizen abroad who has not acquired foreign citizenship can register at the Indian passport address. Overseas elector registration does not itself provide ordinary postal/online voting. ECI overseas-elector explanation.
  • Registration of Electors Rules 1960 regulate roll preparation/revision. Avoid duplicate enrolment; ordinary-residence assessment and deletion require lawful procedure.

Migrant inclusion: July editorial

  • Seasonal workers may retain family/social ties at origin while living temporarily near work. Rigid assessment and costly travel can exclude them; the source estimates roughly 15 crore migrants, not a precisely verified eligible-elector count.
  • Proposed reforms: clarify economic-migration treatment, accessible enrolment/objections, portable participation and tested remote voting. ECI's remote-voting exploration is not proof of nationwide implementation. Aadhaar-related reform proposals do not make Aadhaar mandatory proof of citizenship or justify automatic deletion.

Bihar Special Intensive Revision: July 25 debate

  • ECI's 24 June2025 SIR order and 21 July counter-affidavit were challenged before the Supreme Court. ECI defended electoral eligibility/citizenship verification; critics questioned evidence, burden of documents, treatment of post-2003 voters and exclusion risks. These were competing submissions, not a final judgment of illegality.
  • Article326, RPA1950 sections15,16,19,20 and21, and electoral-registration rules must be read together: rolls are constituency-specific; citizenship, age and residence are distinct conditions; section21 addresses preparation/revision. Source's “section15 alone authorises fresh citizenship proof from all” is incomplete.
  • 2003 Bihar rolls provided a documentary baseline/exemption for listed electors; critics ask why a later existing entry should not receive comparable weight. Aadhaar establishes identity rather than citizenship; neither a ration card nor an EPIC is conclusive citizenship proof. This does not settle which combinations ECI must accept as evidence.
  • The PDF conflates Citizenship Amendment Act2003, citizenship-by-birth rules and the contested CAA2019; these are separate enactments. Birth/parentage criteria depend on birth periods in the Citizenship Act, not an automatic power to conduct a nationwide NRC through electoral revision. NRC and electoral rolls have different purposes/procedures.
  • Issue reports forms from over90% of 7.89crore electors, possible deceased/migrated/multiple registrations, and incomplete documents. Its 53lakh/not-found, ~21.6lakh deceased,31.5lakh migrated,7lakh duplicates categories are overlapping/changing reports, not quantities to add into a reconciled deletion count. Submission, draft inclusion, verification, objection and final deletion are different stages.
  • Safeguards: accessible forms/help for migrants and vulnerable groups, transparent constituency-level data, individual notice/hearing, reasoned correction and appeal, ample document opportunities and non-arbitrary standards. Compare Assam NRC only as a caution on costs/exclusion; electoral revision is not legally identical.
  • ECI source-period progress release,23July2025.

Bihar draft roll: 1 August 2025

  • Draft after SIR enumeration includes over 7.24 crore, roughly 65 lakh below earlier 7.89 crore. Draft omission is not proof every excluded person is foreign or permanently disenfranchised.
  • Claims/objections 1 August–1 September through prescribed forms/EROs; opportunities for inclusion/correction, individual reasons and due process. ECI release records 90,712 booths, 243 constituencies, 38 districts, with distinct BLO, volunteer and party BLA roles. ECI press note 1 August.
  • RPA 1950 section 21(3) permits special revision for recorded reasons; Article 324 covers supervision/direction/control of rolls/elections. Practice wrongly cites RPA 1951.
  • Inclusive documentation, migrant assistance, transparent data, notice, hearing, reasoned decisions and remedies balance clean rolls with enfranchisement. Foreign-infiltration assertions/arbitrary-cutoff criticisms remain competing claims, not final findings.
  • Mohinder Singh Gill was decided 2 December 1977, reported as (1978) 1 SCC 405; distinguish judgment date from report citation. Article 324 supplements statutory law, not overriding it. Supreme Court judgment. Aadhaar/EPIC identity differs from citizenship proof; wider-document proposals do not make every card conclusive citizenship evidence.

14 August 2025: Supreme Court transparency directions

  • Interim order required publication of roughly 65 lakh names omitted from Bihar's1 August draft roll, with reasons, online through DEOs/CEO and physically at Panchayat/BDO offices; names should be searchable by EPIC. Court also directed publicity that aggrieved persons could submit claims with Aadhaar. Deadline19 August was a direction, not proof compliance was already achieved. SupremeCourt order.
  • Omission from the draft is distinct from final rejection, non-citizenship or permanent disenfranchisement. Aadhaar's use to support a claim does not make it conclusive citizenship proof. This interim safeguard did not finally decide every challenge to SIR legality.
  • Wider discussion repeats existing notes on RPA1950 s21(3), Article 324,2003 baseline, migrant/document burdens, party BLAs, notice/hearing and accessible correction. Source's practice key is 2,3 only(C); options referring to statement4 are malformed. Existing note distinguishes the1977 decision date and 1978 citation of MohinderSinghGill.
