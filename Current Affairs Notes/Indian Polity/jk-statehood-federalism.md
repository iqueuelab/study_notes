# Jammu and Kashmir statehood and India’s federal design

## Statehood debate in September 2025
- Source discusses **Zahoor Ahmed Bhat v. UT of Jammu and Kashmir** and delayed statehood restoration after **2019 reorganisation** into **J&K and Ladakh Union Territories**. Concerns: elected accountability, Union–State balance, LG powers and federalism as a basic constitutional feature.
- **Article 1** describes India as a Union of States; **Article 2** permits admission/establishment of States; **Article 3** permits formation, area/boundary/name changes through the prescribed parliamentary procedure. Reorganisation is not ordinary executive discretion alone; State-legislature views are sought where Article 3 requires, without a binding State veto.
- **December 2023 Article 370 judgment** upheld the constitutional steps making Article 370 inoperative; it did **not finally adjudicate the general validity of converting the whole J&K State into UTs**, given the Union's restoration assurance. Do not turn the editorial's categorical “cannot permanently convert” argument into an already settled universal holding. Restoration was to occur at the earliest; the election direction and restoration question are distinct.
- The Assembly election occurred in **September–October 2024**, with results in October; saying all polling occurred in October is inaccurate. A UT with an elected legislature still differs from a full State in distribution of powers.
- **Article 83** makes Rajya Sabha continuing, with periodic retirement; representative federal institutions support Union–State deliberation. Sikkim's **1975** constitutional integration is not interchangeable with Goa's **1961** territorial acquisition; existing Sikkim notes cover the 35th/36th Amendments.
- Mains plan: constitutional procedure → accountability/federal concerns → security and governance considerations → credible restoration roadmap and meaningful elected institutions. Printed **250-word/15-mark** prompt lacks an exam year and remains practice.
