Indian Polity

Criminalisation of politics and electoral disqualification

Editable notes

Conviction, accusation and disqualification

  • RPA 1951 section 8(3): conviction with imprisonment at least two years disqualifies from conviction and for six years after release. Section 8(1) separately covers specified offences; do not impose a universal “moral turpitude” exception. Official Act.
  • Lily Thomas, 2013: struck down section 8(4)'s protection for sitting legislators; filing an appeal alone does not erase disqualification. A judicial stay of conviction differs from bail/stay of sentence.
  • Jan Chaukidar, 2013 incarceration/elector ruling must be read with the 2013 statutory amendment preserving elector status despite section 62(5)'s voting restriction. An undertrial is not automatically disqualified merely by being detained.
  • Section 11: Election Commission can remove/reduce specified statutory disqualification, subject to the Act; source cites the 2019 Prem Singh Tamang decision. This is different from parliamentary immunity.

Evidence and reform

  • Source ADR figures for elected MPs in 2024: 46% declared criminal cases; 31% serious cases; charges/declarations are not convictions. Higher reported win rates show association, not proof criminality causes electoral success.
  • Causes: money/muscle power, slow trials, weak party screening, patronage and voters' expectations of protection/service. Costs: intimidation, corruption, institutional distrust and skewed policy.
  • Law Commission 1999 / 2014 proposals on serious charges and safeguards are recommendations, not an enacted blanket ban. Lifetime-ban petitions were not a February 2025 settled judgment.
  • Responses: candidate disclosures, party accountability, transparent campaign finance, timely fair trials and witness protection. Charge-stage exclusion needs judicial scrutiny and safeguards against politically motivated prosecution.
  • Balance electoral integrity with presumption of innocence, proportionality and voters' choice; reform should target conduct/evidence rather than stereotypes.
