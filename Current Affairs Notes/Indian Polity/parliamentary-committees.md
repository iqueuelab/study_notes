# Parliamentary committees: scrutiny beyond the floor

## Structure and role
- Articles **105 and 118**, House rules and parliamentary practice underpin committee work. Standing committees are continuing institutions; many memberships are reconstituted annually. Ad hoc committees finish a specified task and dissolve.
- **24 department-related standing committees**: 16 serviced by Lok Sabha, eight by Rajya Sabha; **31 members each (21 LS + 10 RS)**. Ministers are not members.
- Examine demands for grants, referred Bills, annual reports and long-term policy. Referral of every Bill is not mandatory; committees do not themselves vote grants or enact law.
| Financial committee | Membership | Main focus |
|---|---|---|
| PAC | 22: 15 LS + 7 RS | Accounts/CAG findings and spending regularity |
| Estimates | 30, all LS | Economy, efficiency and alternative policies |
| Public Undertakings | 22: 15 LS + 7 RS | Public-enterprise reports/accounts and performance |
- Committees can call evidence/records under applicable rules; do not assume every committee can summon every minister without limitation.
- Smaller groups permit detailed, often cross-party examination and expert/public input. Reports are **recommendatory**, not binding court orders.
- Strengthen research support, timely referrals, attendance and action-taken follow-up; balance confidential deliberation with published findings and meaningful consultation.
## May 2025: parliamentary oversight diagnosis
- Parliamentary government gives the executive continuing legislative accountability through questions, motions, debate, financial scrutiny and committees; elections add periodic accountability. Ambedkar's stability/accountability comparison explains the choice, not a claim presidential systems have no accountability.
- **17th Lok Sabha, 2019–24:** Question Hour functioned for **60% of scheduled time in Lok Sabha and 52% in Rajya Sabha**. Disruptions, weak follow-up and isolated questions limit scrutiny of cross-ministry issues. [PRS original analysis](https://prsindia.org/files/parliament/vital_stats/Functioning-17th_Lok_Sabha.pdf).
- Reports seldom receive sustained floor debate; limited research support, narrow consultation and annual membership turnover impair expertise. **Standing committees are continuing institutions**, although memberships are renewed; the PDF's blanket “committees are temporary” is misleading.
## Examples and qualifications
- Source cites Railways committee's **2015** recommendation to waive railway dividends, followed by **2016** budget reform; public-undertakings scrutiny of NHAI land-acquisition/readiness before work; Estimates Committee advocacy of domestic uranium mining; and PAC scrutiny of **2010 Commonwealth Games** spending. Committee advice can inform policy, but it is not itself enactment, project clearance or conviction.
- Transport scrutiny concerned the **Motor Vehicles Amendment Bill, 2016/2017 parliamentary examination**; the relevant **Act was enacted in 2019**, not a “Motor Vehicles Act 2017”. **National Road Safety Board constitution/rules were notified 3 September 2021**. [Government parliamentary reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/179/AU2019.pdf?source=pqals).
- Source's **~180 PAC recommendations/year and ~80% acceptance**, and NHAI **80% land-acquisition** threshold, need report/year/contract context: acceptance does not equal implementation, and a recommendation is not a universal statutory rule.
## Proposed improvements
- **Post-legislative scrutiny:** examine whether Acts meet objectives, implementation burdens and unintended effects through DRSC subcommittees or a dedicated review mechanism. The UK model uses departmental reviews around **3–5 years** after enactment; this is a comparative model, not India's universal legal deadline.
- Select committee reports for floor debate; timely ministerial/action-taken responses; sustained technical/research staff; diverse stakeholder evidence; translated reports and accessible visual explainers.
- AI/data analytics may flag budget anomalies, compare trends and assist better questions. Verify outputs, protect sensitive material and retain MP/committee judgment; technology does not itself confer investigatory authority.
- Aim: transparent, responsive governance and informed parliamentary support while preserving independent scrutiny. Reform recommendations should not be represented as already adopted House rules.
