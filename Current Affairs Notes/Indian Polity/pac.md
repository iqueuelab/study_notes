# Public Accounts Committee and parliamentary financial control

## Composition
- Origin **1921**; parliamentary form after independence. **22 members: 15 Lok Sabha +7 Rajya Sabha**, elected annually by proportional representation through single transferable vote; **ministers cannot be members**.
- Chair appointed by **Lok Sabha Speaker**; Opposition chair is a convention since **1967–68**, not a constitutional command.
## Role and limits
- Examines appropriation/finance accounts and relevant **CAG reports**: spending authorised by Parliament, legal purpose, economy and financial accountability.
- **CAG audits; PAC examines and reports.** PAC does not replace the executive, itself implement programmes or function as a criminal court.
- Excess expenditure and reappropriation receive scrutiny; witnesses/explanations and follow-up action-taken reports connect audit findings to accountability.
- Distinguish **Estimates Committee** (economy/organisation and estimates) and **Committee on Public Undertakings** (public-enterprise oversight).
- Challenges: delayed reports, limited research capacity and weak recommendation follow-up. Improve timely evidence, bipartisan scrutiny and tracked implementation while preserving executive responsibility.
