# Ad hoc High Court judges and Article 224A

## Constitutional mechanism
- **Article 224 A**: High Court Chief Justice, with **President's previous consent**, may request a retired judge of that or another High Court to sit/act; judge's consent required. Entitled to relevant judicial powers while sitting; temporary remedy, not a permanent appointment under Article 217.
- Distinguish **Article 224** additional/acting judges and Supreme Court **Article 127** ad hoc judges; **224 A concerns retired High Court judges**.
## January 30, 2025 order
- **Lok Prahari v Union of India**, modification of **20 April 2021** guidelines to address large criminal-appeal backlog.
- Original safeguard: do not resort to ad hoc appointments unless **80% of sanctioned strength already working or recommended**. January order kept that condition in abeyance; source's description “only when vacancies crossed 20%” reverses this prerequisite.
- **2–5 ad hoc judges per High Court**, **not above 10% of sanctioned strength**; sit with serving judges on **Division Benches for criminal appeals** under the January arrangement. Later orders may clarify bench deployment; do not make the temporary direction timeless.
- Source pendency roughly **60+ lakh**, **20 lakh criminal appeals**, January snapshot; not current totals. [Supreme Court institutional account](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0490f1f4972d133619a60c30f3559e/uploads/2025/05/2025051413.pdf).
## Benefits and safeguards
- Experienced judges can reduce older appeals and delay; appointments need transparent suitability/consent, logistics, Registry support and independence.
- Not a substitute for filling regular vacancies, case management and adequate court infrastructure; criminal appeal disposal is distinct from clearing every pending trial.
- Practice recall **224 A (b)**; source question has no PYQ year and remains coaching practice.
## Judicial administration discussion: February 2025
- Conference emphasis: identify backlog categories, regular judicial officer/prosecutor recruitment, sufficient infrastructure and **permanent IT/data support cadres**.
- Objective transfers, transparent merit-based district-judge elevation, case management and reliable pendency data can improve access to justice under **Article 21**.
- These are reform discussions/proposals, not newly enacted national appointment rules; maintain judicial independence, data protection and accessibility while digitising.

## January 2025 relaxation and limits
- **Article 224 A:** High Court Chief Justice, with President'sprior consent, may request a qualifiedretired High Court judge to sit; judge'sconsent required. This is different from appointing anysenioradvocate underordinaryjudicialeligibility.
- January 30, 2025 Supreme Court order relaxed earlier conditions forad-hocuse; criminal appeal benches witha sitting judge addresspendency.
- criminal appeals generally concern post-trialcases; clearing them is not automatically the same as reducing allundertrial detention.
- Source 62 lakhcases/367 vacanciesof 1, 122 sanctionedposts are dated snapshots. Staff, courtrooms, research support andtransparentappointments matter withjudgenumbers.
- Time-limiteduse complements filling regular vacancies, case management andaccess tojustice; do not treat source proposedproceduralshortcuts as already amendedconstitutionalrules.
