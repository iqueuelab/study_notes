Union Territory assemblies: nomination and democratic accountability

Editable notes

August controversy and statutory architecture

  • Issue reports Home Ministry's position that J&K Lieutenant Governor can nominate up to five MLAs without Council of Ministers consultation; this reported executive interpretation must be distinguished from a final judicial determination of the disputed procedure.
  • J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019: 90 elected seats after delimitation; Section 15 permits up to two women if inadequately represented; 2023 amendment Sections 15A and 15B add up to two Kashmiri migrants, one a woman, and one displaced person from Pakistan-occupied J&K. The two migrant seats are not two unrestricted discretionary nominees. Amending Act.
  • Puducherry: 30 elected MLAs and up to three Central-government nominees under the Government of Union Territories Act, 1963. Delhi: 70 elected seats and no analogous nominated-MLA provision under its 1991 Act. Different UT statutes require separate analysis.
  • Comparators: Rajya Sabha 12 presidential nominees; State Legislative Councils approximately one-sixth governor nominees, ordinarily on ministerial advice. Anglo-Indian nomination to Lok Sabha/State Assemblies ceased in 2020 under the 104th Amendment's effect on Article 334.
  • Source discusses the 2018 Puducherry litigation and elected-government consultation. Nominated voting members can affect majority arithmetic; clear statutory procedure and democratic accountability matter, especially when Union and UT governments differ politically.
  • Correction: the December 2023 Article 370 judgment did not finally decide the validity of converting J&K as a whole into a UT; it recorded the assurance of restoration of statehood and upheld Ladakh's separate UT status. Do not repeat the source's blanket claim that the whole conversion was upheld.
