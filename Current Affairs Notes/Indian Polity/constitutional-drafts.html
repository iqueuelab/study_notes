Alternative Indian constitutional drafts, 1895–1948

Editable notes

Competing visions before the 1950 Constitution

DraftRevision focus
Constitution of India Bill, 1895Early nationalist self-government draft, often associated with Tilak but anonymous authorship; 110 provisions, civil liberties, property and legal equality
M. N. Roy, Constitution of Free India, 1944Radical-democratic vision: linguistic federal units, civil/economic rights, citizens’ committees and popular sovereignty, including resistance to tyranny
Hindusthan Free State, 1944Mahasabha-associated draft; popular sovereignty, legal equality, religious freedom/no State religion, elected institutions and judicial remedies
Shriman Narayan Agarwal, Gandhian Constitution, 1946Village-centred decentralisation, khadi/cottage industry, nonviolence and self-sufficiency; Gandhi wrote the foreword, not the draft
Socialist Party draft, 1948Democratic socialism, planned economy, public/cooperative ownership, workers’ welfare and social equality; Jayaprakash Narayan wrote the foreword
  • Corrections from the original texts: the 1944 Hindusthan draft explicitly establishes a federation and a bicameral Congress, contrary to the PDF's “unitary” shorthand. It protects religious/cultural rights; do not reduce it to “one culture” or assert a secession right without the actual provision. 1944 draft.
  • The Socialist draft permits a controlled private sector alongside state/cooperative sectors; the PDF's “no private ownership of means of production” overstates it. It includes political/civil rights as well as economic aims. 1948 draft.
  • Gandhian draft included a regulated right to keep/bear arms, an interesting tension with nonviolence; this was not adopted as a Fundamental Right in 1950. 1946 text.
  • Compare individual liberty, social/economic equality, centralisation and village participation. Similarities with Fundamental Rights/DPSPs/Panchayati Raj show a wider intellectual context; similarity alone does not prove direct borrowing from every draft.
