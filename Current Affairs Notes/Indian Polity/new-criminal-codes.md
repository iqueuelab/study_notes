# New criminal codes: digital evidence and implementation

## One-year implementation review
| Earlier law | Replacement enacted in 2023 |
|---|---|
| IPC 1860 | Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) |
| CrPC 1973 | Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) |
| Indian Evidence Act 1872 | Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) |
- Main commencement: **1 July 2024**; enactment year is not commencement year. CrPC 1973 was enacted after independence, although it replaced an earlier colonial procedural code.
- **e-Sakshya:** capture crime-scene photos/videos, timestamps/location and witness-related evidence; secure storage and chain of custody matter. Recording does not make tampering logically impossible or evidence automatically admissible.
- **BNSS 176(3): forensic expert visit and videography for offences punishable by seven years or more**, from a date notified by the State within the statutory five-year window; not mandatory attendance at every crime scene. [BPRD compendium](https://bprd.nic.in/uploads/pdf/Compendium_11zon.pdf).
- Reported field constraints: insufficient compatible phones/tablets; cloud evidence still copied to pen drives for courts; forensic capacity/certification gaps; delayed post-mortem/medical reports and resistance to recording discovery proceedings. **MedLEaPR** piloting in Chhattisgarh aims to digitise medico-legal reports.
- BNS **112** deals with petty organised crime; BNS **303(2)** has a specific first-conviction provision for theft below ₹5,000 with restoration/return of property. It does not decriminalise all small thefts. BNSS **184** provides a seven-day period for sending a rape survivor's medical report.
- Priorities: investigators' equipment/training, labs, interoperable police–court systems, evidence authentication, victim safeguards and feedback from police, courts, lawyers and citizens. Lack of IT Act examiner notification does not automatically exclude every electronic record.
