Assisted dying, living wills and dignity

Editable notes

England and Wales: position on June 21, 2025

  • Commons passed the Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill at third reading on 20 June 2025, 314–291. It still required further parliamentary passage; assisted dying was not already legalised. Parliament record.
  • Proposed eligibility: adult 18+, decision-making capacity, terminal illness with six months or less expected life, England/Wales residence for at least 12 months; disability/mental disorder alone does not qualify. Proposed assistance requires a voluntary, informed request and patient self-administration.
  • Medical assessments by two doctors and reflection periods are safeguards. June version used Assisted Dying Review Panels, replacing the original mandatory High Court approval; PDF mixes the original and amended versions. Panels include legal, psychiatry and social-work expertise. Commons Library bill history.
  • Suicide Act 1961 criminalises assistance in England and Wales, with up to 14 years' imprisonment; Northern Ireland has separate legislation. Do not assign that Act identically to the whole UK.

India and ethical distinctions

TermMeaning / June 2025 Indian position
Active euthanasiaDirect intentional lethal intervention; not authorised
Assisted suicideAnother facilitates patient's lethal self-administration; not authorised
Withholding/withdrawing life-sustaining treatmentPermissible under judicial safeguards in appropriate terminal/irreversible circumstances
Advance medical directive / living willAdult records future treatment wishes for incapacity; implementation requires safeguards and medical assessment
  • Aruna Shanbaug (2011) recognised a framework for withdrawal; Common Cause (2018) recognised dying with dignity within Article 21 and advance directives; 2023 order simplified procedures. This is not an unrestricted general right to suicide.
  • Compare autonomy/dignity and relief of suffering with coercion, prognostic uncertainty, disability discrimination, unequal care and the duty to protect vulnerable people. Strengthen palliative care and meaningful informed consent.
  • Netherlands/Belgium/Luxembourg, Switzerland and some US jurisdictions have differing assisted-death rules; no single international model. France's National Assembly passed a proposal at first reading on May 27, 2025; it was not operative assisted-dying law in June. French legislative record.
