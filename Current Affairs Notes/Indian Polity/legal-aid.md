# Legal aid, NALSA and Lok Adalats

## Constitutional and statutory basis
- **Article 39A**, added by **42nd Amendment Act 1976**, is a **Directive Principle**, not itself a Fundamental Right. Articles 14,21 and 22(1) support equality, fair procedure and counsel safeguards.
- **Legal Services Authorities Act 1987**; NALSA established **1995**. Earlier **CILAS 1980** under Justice P.N. Bhagwati helped coordinate legal aid. Distinguish enactment, commencement and institutional creation.
- National: CJI **Patron-in-Chief**, executive chairman a serving/retired SC judge nominated under the Act (conventionally second senior-most sitting judge); State: HC chief justice Patron-in-Chief; High Court legal-services committee; district authority chaired by district judge; taluk committee by senior-most judicial officer as prescribed. Source's second-senior-most phrasing is convention, not the statute's only possible appointee.
## Eligibility and services
- Section 12 categories include women/children, SC/ST, trafficking/begar victims, persons with disability, industrial workmen, disaster/violence victims, persons in custody and persons within prescribed income limits. Category-based eligibility need not depend on low income.
- **Income ceilings vary by State/court**; the PDF's₹1 lakh is not one universal national cap. Supreme Court threshold cited₹5 lakh. Services include advice, counsel, pleadings/translations, court/process expenses and certified documents subject to eligibility/procedure.
## Lok Adalat
- Statutory settlement/conciliation forum for pending or pre-litigation disputes; ordinary Lok Adalat cannot impose its own merits decision where no compromise occurs. No fee; court fee refunded after settlement where applicable.
- Award deemed civil-court decree,**final/binding, no statutory appeal**; excludes non-compoundable criminal offences. Failure to settle permits ordinary litigation; mere dissatisfaction is not a universal right to reopen a valid consent award. **Permanent Lok Adalats** for public-utility disputes have distinct statutory adjudicatory powers. [NALSA](https://nalsa.gov.in/lok-adalats/), [NALSA FAQs](https://nalsa.gov.in/faqs/).
## Capacity constraints:July 2025 editorial
- Source reports over 80% potentially eligible but only **15.5 lakh recipients between April 2023 andMarch 2024**; coverage denominator differs from actual demand. Legal-aid budget below 1% of the wider justice-system budget; some States₹2–₹3 per capita,**para-legal volunteers down 38% since 2019**.
- Embedded clipping: funding about₹601 crore in 2017–18 to₹1,086 crore in 2022–23 across25 States; rising allocation can coexist with underutilisation. Volunteer honoria differ; low pay, thin rural staffing and rigid spending rules limit reach/quality.
- **Legal Aid Defence Counsel Scheme 2022:** salaried criminal-defence/public-defender model; issue reports610 of 670 districts. Adequate funding, training, retention, awareness, mobile advice, flexible local spending, beneficiary feedback and quality audits matter more than case counts alone.
