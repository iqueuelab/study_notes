# Private Members' Bills and parliamentary participation

## Meaning and procedure
- **Private member = MP who is not a minister**, including a ruling-party MP; PMBs propose new laws or amendments. Government Bills are introduced by ministers. Passage confers the same statutory force, subject to normal constitutional requirements. [Lok Sabha FAQ](https://sansad.in/ls/faq).
- Governed by each House's procedure; usually **one month's notice**, with private-members' business allotted Fridays subject to scheduling/ballot rules. Introduction is distinct from consideration/passage; members prepare drafts with procedural/research support.
- **Article 105** parliamentary speech protection operates within constitutional/procedural limits. The source's blanket “freedom from party whip” is incorrect: no universal exemption from party directions/anti-defection rules exists. Defeat of a government Bill is also not automatically a no-confidence vote requiring resignation.
## May 2025 participation diagnosis
- Source reports **17th Lok Sabha: 729 introduced, two discussed; Rajya Sabha in the corresponding period: 705 introduced, 14 discussed**. These describe low deliberation, not 1,434 laws enacted.
- Embedded clipping places **64 introductions in the 2024 Winter Session** and no discussion in the latest Budget Session; the rewritten PDF's “Budget Session 2024” label is inconsistent. Its Rajya Sabha **82 listed/one discussion begun** needs this session context. No PMB had completed both Houses since **1970** in this dated account.
- **Supriya Sule's Right to Disconnect Bill, 2019** addresses after-hours work; **Tiruchi Siva's Rights of Transgender Persons Bill, 2014** passed Rajya Sabha in **2015**, but did not become that Act. The government's **2019** transgender legislation is a separate enactment; “failed in Lok Sabha” is not a substitute for tracing its actual parliamentary status. **Gopal Shetty's free-medical-facilities proposal** illustrates ruling-party private initiative.
- Value: constituency concerns, neglected issues, policy experimentation and executive accountability. Barriers: government/disruption crowding-out, party control, drafting capacity, limited time and weak cross-party support.
## Proposed reforms
- Protect allotted time, research/drafting support, constitutional scrutiny and prioritisation, cross-party consideration and suitable extended sittings. These are recommendations, not implemented rules. A larger future House also requires deliberative capacity; delimitation does not itself guarantee debate time.
- **UK Ten Minute Rule** allows a short speech for introduction and opposition; introduction still does not guarantee passage. Comparative Canada/New Zealand slots and public digital feedback suggest ideas, with safeguards against popularity replacing legislative scrutiny.
