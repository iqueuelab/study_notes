# Lokpal: jurisdiction, composition and safeguards

## Institution
- **Statutory**, not constitutional: **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013**; commencement **16 January 2014**. First Foundation Day celebration **16 January 2025**, Delhi; commencement differs from first appointments.
- **Chairperson + up to eight members**; **50% judicial members**, **not less than 50% of members collectively from SC/ST/OBC/minorities/women**. Not a separate 50% quota for each group.
- Chair: former **CJI**, former **Supreme Court judge**, or eligible eminent person; judicial member: former **SC judge / High Court Chief Justice**. Eminent members require relevant expertise and statutory eligibility, not simply fame.
- **President** appoints on committee recommendation: **PM (chair), Lok Sabha Speaker, Lok Sabha Leader of Opposition, CJI or nominated SC judge, eminent jurist**.
- **Five years or age 70**, whichever earlier; Chair remuneration equivalent to **CJI**, members to **SC judges**. [Act and sections](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2122?col=123456789%2F1362).
## Jurisdiction and exclusions
- Corruption offences under **Prevention of Corruption Act**: **PM, Union ministers, MPs, Central Groups A–D**, covered government-funded/controlled entities; qualifying foreign-contribution recipients above **₹10 lakh annually**.
- MPs' protected parliamentary speech/votes excluded; PM allegations concerning **international relations, external/internal security, public order, atomic energy and space** excluded.
- PM inquiry needs **full bench**, approval of **at least two-thirds**; in-camera proceedings and confidentiality safeguards apply. “PM entirely exempt” is incorrect.
- Complaint rules/form and statutory time limits apply; “any person may complain” does not mean every complaint is admissible regardless of content or age.
## Inquiry and powers
- Preliminary inquiry or investigation by authorised agency including **CBI**; **CVC route** for specified Central servant categories: Group **A/B** reports back to Lokpal; Group **C/D** proceeds under CVC powers with reporting/oversight.
- Lokpal supervises **CBI cases referred by it**, not every CBI case; investigating-officer transfer requires its approval in covered cases.
- Civil-court-type inquiry powers, searches, provisional attachment, record protection and recommendations for transfer/suspension. **Final confiscation is through Special Court**, not unrestricted direct Lokpal confiscation.
- Annual report to **President**, laid before **Parliament**; effectiveness needs staff, independent investigations, timely follow-up and due process.
## Jurisdiction over higher-court judges: February 2025 dispute
- Lokpal **27 January order** asserted High Court judge jurisdiction using the statutory-body wording/history; earlier **3 January order** declined Supreme Court judge jurisdiction. These were contested interpretations, not settled constitutional conclusions.
- **20 February Supreme Court stayed the High Court-related order** and issued notices; source listed a March hearing. Stay ≠final adjudication of every scope question.
- **Articles 124/217/218** protect higher-judge tenure/removal; constitutional independence does **not grant immunity from every corruption investigation**.
- **K. Veeraswami, 1991:** higher judges can fall within anti-corruption public-servant law, with special safeguards including CJI consultation/permission before criminal proceedings; source's claim the higher judiciary simply never qualifies is misleading.
- Removal following parliamentary address differs from criminal investigation/prosecution. **NJAC judgment 2015** concerns judicial appointments/independence, not automatic resolution of Lokpal jurisdiction.
- Mains: independent investigations, due process, judicial autonomy and public accountability require a constitutionally valid procedure; do not assume either blanket impunity or unrestricted ordinary executive control.

- [Supreme Court20 February2025 order](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/9527/9527_2025_2_301_59727_Order_20-Feb-2025.pdf): stay of the disputed Lokpal order; preserve distinction between interim relief and final ruling.

## High Court judges: jurisdiction dispute
- Lokpal's January 2025 order claimed jurisdiction over High Court judges through section 14(1)(f); the Supreme Court stayed that order in February. **A stayed interpretation is not settled law**.
- The argument that older High Courts originated in colonial statutes was contested; constitutional High Court status cannot be reduced to their historical origins.
- Being a public servant under anti-corruption law does not automatically establish jurisdiction under every investigatory statute. Judicial independence does not give immunity from corruption investigation.
- *K. Veeraswami* (1991) recognised anti-corruption accountability with safeguards involving consultation with the CJI; it should not be simplified to unrestricted executive investigation.
- Removal is a separate constitutional process: Presidential order after each House's special-majority address on proved misbehaviour/incapacity; Article 124(4), with Article 217/218 for High Courts. An inquiry or transfer is not removal.
- Lokpal consists of a chairperson and **up to eight** members, at least half judicial; statutory social-representation requirements apply. PM-related exclusions and full-bench two-thirds approval safeguards remain important.
- Its CBI supervision relates to referred cases, not blanket command over all CBI work; attachment/confiscation and prosecution involve statutory procedures, not arbitrary seizure.
