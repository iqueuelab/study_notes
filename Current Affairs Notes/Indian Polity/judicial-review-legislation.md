# Judicial review, legislative power and contempt

## Nandini Sundar and Chhattisgarh's 2011 Act
- **5 July 2011 judgment:** arming/deployment of inadequately trained SPOs for anti-Maoist activity violated **Articles 14/21**; directions included stopping such use, recalling firearms, ending Union funding for that purpose and preventing/disbanding vigilante activity such as Salwa Judum/Koya Commandos.
- Chhattisgarh enacted the **Auxiliary Armed Police Force Act, 2011**; its screening, training, service, role and control provisions were cited in later proceedings. Enactment does not establish that all abuses ended or all provisions are immune from review.
- **15 May 2025 order:** mere enactment of a law is not contempt; constitutional challenge tests **legislative competence and constitutional validity**. Legislatures can remove a judgment's legal basis through valid legislation but cannot simply declare judicial findings wrong without curing the defect.
- Separation of powers includes checks and balances: **courts can review and strike down invalid legislation**. Dismissal of contempt is not a blanket merits endorsement of the Act. Interim stay, final invalidation and punishment for wilful disobedience are distinct.
- State/Concurrent List competence remains subject to constitutional distribution, repugnancy and fundamental rights; equality and life/dignity protect both recruits and affected communities.
- [Supreme Court order, 15 May 2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/173622808/).
