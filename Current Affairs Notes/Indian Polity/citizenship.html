Indian Polity

Citizenship, visas and deportation

Editable notes

Constitutional and statutory distinction

  • Part II, Articles 5-11 addresses citizenship at commencement and Parliament's power to legislate; it does not itself prescribe all present-day acquisition procedures.
  • Citizenship Act, 1955 governs birth, descent, registration, naturalisation and incorporation of territory; loss through renunciation, termination or deprivation.
  • Citizenship is a Union subject; CAA 2019 provides a special route for specified Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi and Christian migrants from Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Pakistan entering by 31 December 2014, subject to statutory conditions/exemptions. It is not automatic citizenship for every such entrant.

MHA annual report 2023-24: source figures

  • FRRO deportations 2,331: Nigeria 1,470; Bangladesh 411; Uganda 78; reasons include visa violations/overstay.
  • 1,112 long-term visas to Pakistani minority-community members; LTV is not citizenship.
  • Citizenship certificates 1,699: registration 1,278 + naturalisation 421; this source table excludes CAA figures.
  • Foreign visitors 98 lakh: Bangladesh 21.1 lakh, US 17.4 lakh, UK 9.9 lakh.
  • Source lists Amendment Act years 1986, 1992, 2003, 2005, 2015, 2019; avoid turning this source list into an assertion that no other changes exist.

NRI, PIO and OCI: diaspora distinction

TermCitizenship / meaning
NRIIndian citizen residing abroad; residence definitions depend on the law/context
PIOPerson of Indian origin; historical/demographic description can continue
OCI cardholderEligible foreign national, registered under Citizenship Act, 1955 section 7A; not an Indian citizen
  • PIO card scheme merged into OCI in 2015; the demographic category 'person of Indian origin' was not abolished.
  • Eligibility connects to qualifying Indian citizenship/territory and descendants, subject to statutory conditions; Pakistan/Bangladesh citizenship/ancestry exclusions apply as prescribed. Do not infer eligibility from Indian ethnicity alone.
  • OCI is not dual citizenship. No Indian voting rights, election eligibility or ordinary entitlement to constitutional/public posts; visa/residence privileges and permitted activities remain subject to rules. MHA explanation, OCI FAQ.

US birthright citizenship: January 2025 clipping

  • 20 January executive order sought to restrict recognition for specified US-born children of non-citizen parents; State litigation immediately followed, invoking 14th Amendment protection.
  • An executive announcement is not proof constitutional birthright citizenship was finally abolished. Birth citizenship ≠ H-1B employment permission ≠ green-card status; Indian citizenship rules must be assessed separately.
  • EO targeted specified children whose mother was unlawfully present or lawfully temporary and father neither US citizen nor lawful permanent resident, prospectively after a 30-day period; parental tests are more specific than “all foreign parents”. 20 January order.

US deportations: February 2025

  • 5 February 2025: US military flight brought 104 Indians to Amritsar, correcting the PDF's preliminary “around 200”. Nationality verification and acceptance of own citizens differ from endorsing detention/transport conditions. Contemporaneous report.
  • Source 18,000 refers to a reported removal/enforcement population; not a census of all undocumented Indians in the US. Legal students/workers are not automatically undocumented migrants.
  • Balance sovereign immigration enforcement with due process, dignity, humane treatment, consular assistance and protection against trafficking; reintegration needs counselling, skills/jobs and action against fraudulent agents.
  • Trump, not Biden, was US President for this February 2025 sequence; Modi's impending visit was a diplomatic context, not proof of a settled deportation agreement.
