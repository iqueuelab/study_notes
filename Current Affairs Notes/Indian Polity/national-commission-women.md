# National Commission for Women

## Institution and composition
- **Statutory**, not constitutional: constituted in **January 1992** under the **National Commission for Women Act, 1990**. The 1974 Committee on the Status of Women helped build the case for a national watchdog; administrative ministry is Women and Child Development.
- Central Government nominates **Chairperson + five members + member-secretary**. Chairperson must be committed to women's cause; members have relevant legal, labour, voluntary-sector, health, education or other experience. At least **one SC and one ST member** among the five. Member-secretary is an appropriate expert or civil servant.
- Section 4: term specified by Centre, **not exceeding three years**; resignation provisions differ for a serving government member-secretary.
- Removal grounds: insolvency; conviction/sentence for moral turpitude; unsoundness of mind declared by competent court; refusal/incapacity to act; absence without leave from **three consecutive meetings**; abuse of position prejudicial to public interest. Opportunity of hearing applies. Mere other paid employment is not an enumerated ground.
[Act, Sections 3–4](https://cdn.ncw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/ncwact_3.pdf).
## Functions and powers
- **Section 10**: investigate safeguards; report annually/otherwise to Centre; review laws; take complaints and suo motu notice; research discrimination; advise development planning; inspect jails/remand homes and other custodial institutions holding women; support litigation involving large groups of women.
- Centre consults NCW on major policy matters affecting women (**Section 16**).
- For specified investigations NCW has civil-court powers: summon/examine witnesses on oath, require documents, receive affidavits, requisition public records and issue examination commissions. Its recommendations are advisory; it is not a criminal court imposing punishment.
- April 2025 context: NCW formed a panel to investigate reported violence affecting women in Murshidabad.
## Effectiveness and reform
- Main constraints: follow-up on recommendations, staff/resources, rural/tribal access, awareness and preventive action. Statutory SC/ST representation already exists; assess effectiveness rather than claiming the law provides none.
- Improve legal literacy, regional outreach, legal-aid referrals, trauma-sensitive staff training, transparent complaint tracking and coordination with State commissions/police. Extra enforcement powers would require legal change, with safeguards.
- Constitutional links: **Articles 14, 15(3), 39(a)/(d)** and India's **CEDAW** commitments.
- **Law Commission Report 257 (2015)** concerns guardianship/custody reforms, not an NCW punitive-powers recommendation. [Official report](https://lawcommissionofindia.nic.in/report-no-257-custody-laws/).
