# BNS Section 152: sovereignty, dissent and safeguards

## Press freedom and national security
- Embedded issue report: press bodies criticised **Assam FIRs/summons against journalists Siddharth Varadarajan and Karan Thapar**, arguing Section 152 was being used to target journalism; accusations and criticism are not convictions or final findings of illegality.
- **IPC Section 124A** concerned hatred/contempt/disaffection toward government established by law; **BNS Section 152**, effective with the new code from **1 July 2024**, concerns acts endangering **sovereignty, unity and integrity**. It is not identically worded statutory sedition, although critics see substantial continuity.
- Section 152 uses **purposely or knowingly** and covers words, signs, visible representation, electronic communication, financial means or otherwise that excite/attempt secession, **armed rebellion**, subversive activities, separatist activities, or endanger sovereignty/unity/integrity. Penalty: **life imprisonment or imprisonment up to seven years, plus fine**. Its explanation protects lawful efforts to alter government measures without exciting prohibited activities. [BNS text](https://bprd.nic.in/uploads/pdf/Final_BNS.pdf).
- Concerns: undefined breadth, arrest/detention burdens, electronic forwarding treated without sufficient context, and chilling effects. Purpose/knowledge and statutory ingredients must be proved; forwarding or criticism is **not automatically an offence**. Cognisable/non-bailable classification does not mean every arrest is lawful or bail impossible.
- **Kedar Nath Singh (1962)** read IPC sedition narrowly around violence/public disorder; **Balwant Singh (1995)** distinguished isolated slogans without incitement; **Javed Ahmad Hazam (2024)** stressed a reasonable-person standard; **D. K. Basu (1997)** concerns arrest safeguards. Their principles inform constitutional analysis, but old Section 124A holdings should not be described as a final ruling on every aspect of Section 152. SC's **2022 abeyance directions** were not repeal by judgment.
- Reform framework: precise incitement/harm thresholds, recorded reasons, proportional arrest/bail, independent judicial scrutiny and remedies for misuse. Source's 12 convictions/548 arrests mixes different denominators/cohorts; it is not a sound case-conviction-rate calculation. Undated 250-word/15-mark mains prompt retained as practice only.
