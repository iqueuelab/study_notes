# Parliamentary speech, privileges and expunction

## Speech, records and democratic accountability
- **Article 105(1)** protects parliamentary speech subject to the Constitution and House procedure; **105(2)** gives immunity from court proceedings for anything said or any vote given in Parliament/committees and authorised publication. These protections support scrutiny, not unlimited immunity for every act of an MP.
- **Lok Sabha Rule 380:** Speaker may expunge defamatory, indecent, unparliamentary or undignified words. **Rajya Sabha Rule 261** gives the corresponding authority to its Chairman. Expunction removes material from the official record; it differs from suspension, expulsion or statutory disqualification. [Parliament record quoting Rule 380](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/853139/1/04_VIII_08-08-1969_p130_p154_PII.pdf).
- **Article 121** bars discussion of judicial conduct in discharge of duties except on a motion for presenting an address seeking a judge's removal. Rules on sub judice matters, allegations and notice further regulate debate.
- Editorial argues indiscriminate deletion makes speeches incoherent and weakens Opposition scrutiny; proportionate, reasoned rulings should preserve meaningful criticism and dignity. This is an institutional argument, not a court judgment that particular expunctions violated Article 105.
- The source's claim Parliament has **no expulsion power** is too broad: *Raja Ram Pal (2007)* upheld the power subject to constitutional review. Articles 102/191 disqualification and House privilege-based expulsion are distinct, as explained in the substantive-motions notes. The unyearred 15-mark exercise is practice, not an authenticated CSE PYQ.
