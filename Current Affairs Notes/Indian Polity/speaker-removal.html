Indian Polity

Removal of the Lok Sabha Speaker

Editable notes

February 2026: notice against the Speaker

  • Opposition notice targeting Om Birla followed disputes over Rahul Gandhi's attempted reference to a former Army Chief's unpublished memoir and the conduct of proceedings. Treat submission/procedural scrutiny separately from a resolution admitted for debate, voted upon or carried.
  • This is removal under Article 94(c), not the Council of Ministers no-confidence procedure. Lok Sabha alone decides by majority of all the then members: effective majority excluding vacancies, not total sanctioned strength or merely those present/voting. At least 14 days' notice.
  • Rules 200–203: written notice/full resolution to Secretary-General; specific, clearly worded charges without argument/inference/irony/defamatory statements. At leave stage at least 50 members must rise in support; this is not a constitutional minimum number of notice signatories or the House's quorum. Discussion is fixed within 10 days of leave being asked for; debate stays within the charges. Speeches normally have a 15-minute limit, extendable by the presiding authority; this is not an absolute limit only on the mover. Lok Sabha Rules, chapter XVIII.
  • Article 96: Speaker cannot preside while removal is considered, but may speak/participate and vote in the first instance, with no casting vote on equality. 2024 PYQ: statement 1 only—(a).
  • Article 94(b): Speaker resigns to Deputy Speaker; Deputy Speaker to Speaker, not “to the House”. Speaker continues after dissolution until immediately before the next Lok Sabha's first sitting. Previous removal resolutions discussed against Mavalankar (1954), Hukam Singh (1966), Balram Jakhar (1987) did not remove them; allegations and constitutional outcomes must remain distinct.

11 March 2026: resolution rejected

  • Lok Sabha rejected the Article 94(c) resolution against Speaker Om Birla by voice vote. This was a removal resolution, distinct from a no-confidence motion against the Council of Ministers; rejection left the Speaker in office.
  • Correct the compilation's present-and-voting majority: passage requires a majority of all the then members, excluding vacancies (effective majority), with 14 days' notice. Article 96 prevents the Speaker presiding over consideration of his own removal. A voice vote does not amend the constitutional threshold. Constitution, Articles 94–96.
  • Opposition alleged restrictions on debate, microphone access and reference to the former Army Chief's unpublished memoir; government defended allocations of speaking/question time. Its 56% Zero Hour time and debates in 14 regional languages are attributed arguments with a stated parliamentary context, not independent measures proving impartiality.
  • Parliamentary legitimacy requires fair presiding decisions, substantive scrutiny, reasoned procedural rulings and constructive participation by both government and Opposition. Productivity/time statistics alone cannot settle whether minority viewpoints were adequately heard. The 2024 notice concerning Rajya Sabha Chairman/VP removal followed a different constitutional route; it must not be conflated with Article 94 or treated as the cause of his later resignation.
