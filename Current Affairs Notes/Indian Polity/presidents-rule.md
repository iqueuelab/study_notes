# President’s Rule: constitutional limits and federal safeguards

## Trigger and effect
- **Article 356:** State government cannot be carried on according to the Constitution; President may act on Governor's report **or otherwise**. Ordinary law-and-order difficulty or a hung Assembly does not automatically justify it.
- **Article 355:** Union duty to protect States and ensure constitutional government. **365:** failure to comply with lawful Union directions may support a constitutional-breakdown assessment, not automatic dismissal.
- President may assume specified State executive functions and declare legislative powers exercisable by Parliament. **High Court powers cannot be assumed**.
## Approval and duration
- Both Houses must approve within **two months**, by ordinary majority. Special Lok Sabha dissolution rule: Rajya Sabha approval plus Lok Sabha approval within **30 days of its first sitting after reconstitution**.
- Approved proclamation operates **six months**, renewable by both Houses in six-month periods, **maximum three years**.
- Beyond **one year**, both conditions: a national emergency operates in India or all/part of the State **and** ECI certifies election difficulties. Do not substitute either condition alone. President can revoke without parliamentary approval. [Parliamentary reference](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/761237/1/Presidents_Rule_in_St_UT_Eng_9th_ed_2016.pdf).
- **Article 357:** Parliament may confer State legislative power on the President, with permitted delegation; this is different from an **Article 123 ordinance**.
## Safeguards and reform
- **S.R. Bommai, 1994:** judicial review, floor-test primacy for majority disputes, limits on dissolution before parliamentary approval; courts may restore unconstitutional dismissal.
- **Rameshwar Prasad, 2006:** Bihar Assembly dissolution held unconstitutional. Political apprehensions are not a substitute for relevant constitutional evidence.
- **Sarkaria 1988:** last resort, warning/remedial options where feasible. **Punchhi 2010:** proposed localised responses; district-level Article 356 is **not existing constitutional law**.
- Assess federalism, democratic mandate and prompt return to representative government. Source counts of impositions are dated, not a current total.
