# Uttarakhand UCC: live-in registration and privacy

## January 2025 framework
- Uttarakhand **UCC Act 2024 / Rules 2025**, implemented **27 January 2025**; regulates marriage, divorce, succession and qualifying live-in relationships, with statutory scope/exemptions. **Article 44** is a DPSP, not an independently enforceable fundamental right.
- Live-in category is an adult man and woman living in a shared household in a relationship in the nature of marriage. Existing marriage/live-in partnership, prohibited relationship (subject to lawful custom) and coercion/fraud can bar registration.
- Statement required within **one month**; registrar enquiry and termination reporting. Children recognised as legitimate; abandoned woman can seek maintenance under relevant provisions.
- **Under 21: parents/guardians notified; notification is not a blanket statutory requirement of parental consent for adults**. Source conflates these.
## Registration and penalties
- Rules involve identity/age/residence and shared accommodation documentation, online/offline processing, provisional arrangements and landlord-related obligations; protect documents from needless disclosure.
- Source maximums: non-submission **3 months / ₹10,000 / both**, false statement **3 months / ₹25,000 / both**; failure after registrar's notice has a separate stronger penalty. Check applicable statutory section rather than treating all omissions identically.
- ST/constitutionally protected customary categories exempted; territorial/resident applicability matters, not every person nationwide covered. [State rules](https://www.ucc.uk.gov.in/server/file/uploads/shared-files/ucc-rules-2025-en.pdf).
## Rights analysis
- Potential benefits: evidence for maintenance, protection against abandonment, recognised children and legal clarity.
- Concerns: compulsory intimate-data disclosure, police/parent notification, housing exclusion, data leaks, disproportionate effect on interfaith/inter-caste couples.
- **Article 21**, *Puttaswamy* privacy **2017**: legality, legitimate aim, necessity/proportionality and safeguards; criticism does not mean a court has struck down the law.
- Safeguards: data minimisation, restricted access, secure retention, accessible grievance/appeal process and proportionate enforcement. Source questions are practice with no PYQ year.
