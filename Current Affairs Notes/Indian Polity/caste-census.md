# Caste census, SECC and evidence-based welfare

## 30 April 2025 decision
- The **Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs** approved caste enumeration in the forthcoming Census on **30 April 2025**, not 2024 as the source says. This was approval of a future exercise, not completed enumeration or publication of results. [Cabinet announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2125526&lang=1&reg=1).
- Census is a **Union List, Entry 69** subject; administered by the Registrar General and Census Commissioner under **MHA** and the **Census Act, 1948**. States' caste surveys differ from the statutory national Census. First synchronous census: **1881**; the last published comprehensive caste count: **1931**. Wartime constraints affected 1941 tabulation; avoid treating its unpublished material as a comparable dataset.
- Since 1951, regular censuses enumerated SC/ST caste/tribe data rather than all castes. The 2021 census was postponed during COVID-19; as of this issue, new dates had not been notified.
- Two phases: **Houselisting and Housing Census → Population Enumeration**. Census statistics support planning, resource allocation and representation; individual census returns receive statutory confidentiality.
- **SECC 2011** was separate from the regular Census: household deprivation, assets, housing, education, work and self-reported caste, for welfare targeting. Rural work involved Rural Development, urban work Housing/Urban Poverty Alleviation, and caste enumeration the RGI—not Rural Development alone. Socioeconomic findings were published, while caste data remained unpublished amid classification/quality problems. SECC beneficiary identification differs from confidential Census returns; this does not authorise unrestricted publication of every personal detail.
- Rationale: measure educational, occupational and representation gaps; target welfare; inform OBC subcategorisation. A caste count itself neither changes reservation percentages nor proves every group has identical disadvantage.
- Challenges: inconsistent names, subcastes, self-identification, comparability, privacy, enumeration training and political misuse. Use transparent coding, quality checks, safeguards and socioeconomic indicators alongside caste totals.
- Delimitation is linked to the **first Census taken after 2026** under the constitutional freeze, rather than automatic redrawing in 2026. Women's legislative reservation under the **106th Amendment** requires the prescribed subsequent Census/delimitation process; this decision alone did not activate reserved seats.
## 5 June 2025: Census 2027 announced
- **MHA, 4 June:** two-phase **Population Census 2027 including caste enumeration**; reference date **00:00, 1 March 2027**. For **Ladakh and specified snow-bound non-synchronous areas of J&K, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand: 00:00, 1 October 2026**. Reference date is not proof publication will conclude by that day. [MHA announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2133845&lang=2&reg=48).
- Source body’s “no official dates announced” is stale relative to its own current clipping. Its “2024 reversal” also remains incorrect: caste decision was **30 April 2025**, recorded above.
- Census returns are confidential under the Census Act; self-reported enumeration still involves training, consistency/quality checks, so “no verification of any kind” is too broad. SECC’s welfare-use model is not unlimited public disclosure of personal data.
- The 1931 caste-publication baseline, wartime 1941 limitations, 2011 SECC and regular SC/ST enumeration are covered above. Data can inform subcategorisation/welfare but does not itself amend quotas.
- Delimitation/women’s reservation follow constitutional Census-linked requirements, not automatic constituency redraw on 1 March 2027. Census postponement creates planning gaps; methodology, privacy and transparent coding matter.
