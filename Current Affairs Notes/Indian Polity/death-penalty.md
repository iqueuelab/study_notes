# Death penalty and the rarest-of-rare doctrine

## Doctrine and case distinctions
- **Jagmohan Singh**, decided **1972** (reported 1973), upheld capital punishment; **Bachan Singh 1980**: life imprisonment is the rule, death an exception when the alternative is unquestionably foreclosed.
- Consider **both crime and offender**, aggravating/mitigating circumstances and genuine reform prospects; brutality or public outrage alone does not automatically justify death.
- **Machhi Singh 1983:** manner, motive, socially abhorrent nature, magnitude and victim circumstances; illustrative guidance, not five automatic statutory switches.
- **Mithu 1983:** struck down mandatory IPC 303 death sentence for murder by a life convict, under **Articles 14/21**.
- **Law Commission 262, 2015:** recommended abolition except **terrorism-related offences and waging war**; recommendation, not enacted general abolition. [Commission report catalogue](https://lawcommissionofindia.nic.in/cat_Indian_Penal_Code/).
## February source examples
- RG Kar rape/murder: trial court gave **Sanjay Roy life imprisonment**; Sharon Raj poisoning: trial court gave **Greeshma death**. Different case facts/sentencing evidence and appellate stages matter; trial outcomes are not final national precedent.
- **2022 sentencing-framework reference** sought proper collection/consideration of mitigating evidence; source does not establish a completed final Constitution Bench ruling.
- Court procedures, High Court confirmation, appeals/review and constitutional mercy powers are distinct from initial trial sentencing.
## Ethics and reform
- Debate: deterrence/retribution and victim justice versus irreversible error, unequal legal assistance, inconsistent sentencing and rehabilitation.
- “140+ countries abolished” may combine law/practice categories; no undated number substitutes for definitions. Article **19 concerns specified freedoms**, not a provision titled “freedom of life”.
- Improve competent defence, mitigation reports, reasoned proportional sentencing, timely review and survivor support. Special fixed/non-remittable life sentences require judicial/legal authority, not a universal ban on parole.

## Annual Statistics 2025: appellate scrutiny
- **The Square Circle Clinic, NALSAR University of Law**, reported **574 persons on death row at 31 December 2025 (550 men, 24 women)**; the Supreme Court confirmed **no death sentences in 2023–25**, while acquitting **10 people in 2025**. Non-confirmation is not general abolition. [Research institution's findings](https://thesquarecircleclinic.org/our-work/death-penalty).
- The issue reports **128 trial-court death sentences in 2025**, and a ten-year cohort of **1,310 sentences**, of which **842 received High Court outcomes: 70 confirmations, 285 acquittals, 411 commutations**, with other procedural outcomes accounting for the remainder. **70/842 ≈ 8.31%**: do not describe it as 8.31% of all 1,310, or confuse persons/sentences with criminal cases.
- Its Supreme Court cohort **37 cases, 15 acquittals and 14 commutations** does not establish that no death sentence was confirmed over the whole decade; the **zero-confirmation finding concerns 2023–25**. Cohort, court, reference period and denominator must stay attached to each statistic.
- Reported widespread omission of psychological, probation and prison-conduct material (about **95% of 2025 trial sentences**) raises fair-sentencing concerns. Separate, meaningful mitigation hearings, competent defence and reform prospects matter alongside offence gravity.
- Long death-row periods before reversal compound dignity/mental-health harms. High acquittal/commutation rates demonstrate the importance of appellate safeguards; **commutation is not acquittal**. Fixed/non-remittable life terms are a judicial sentencing alternative, not a blanket removal of every lawful release/mercy mechanism.
