Indian Polity

Religious conversion: autonomy, safeguards and State laws

Editable notes

August 2025 legal debate

  • Embedded news describes a proposed Uttarakhand amendment Bill, including severe punishment up to life for specified offences. The issue says it would be tabled; do not record proposed provisions as already commenced law.
  • State freedom-of-religion laws prohibit specified forced/fraudulent/coerced/allurement-based conversions; definitions, notice requirements, enhanced penalties and burden provisions vary. Voluntary change of faith and freedom of conscience remain protected; interfaith marriage alone does not establish unlawful conversion.
  • Historical source examples: princely Raigarh1936, Patna1942, Sarguja1945, Udaipur1946 laws; unsuccessful central Bills 1954,1960,1978; Odisha's 1967 and MP's 1968 enactments. TamilNadu's later law was repealed. Do not treat the issue's State list as a verified operative-law inventory.
  • Correct “religion is a State subject”: legislative competence depends on the measure and relevant entries, including State public order; there is no single blanket State-list religion entry. Failed central proposals alone do not establish Parliament can never legislate.

Constitution and case-law distinctions

  • Article 25: conscience/profession/practice/propagation, subject to public order, morality, health and other PartIII provisions; regulation of secular activities/social reform permitted. Article 26 protects denominational religious management subject to its limits. 27 concerns taxes specifically appropriated to promote/maintain a particular religion.
  • Article 28(1) concerns institutions wholly maintained by State funds, with clause2 exception and clause3 consent safeguards; not a blanket ban across every aided institution. Article 29(1) protects any section of citizens with a distinct language/script/culture, not only minorities; 30 concerns religious/linguistic minority educational institutions. Constitution.
  • Rev.Stainislaus(1977) upheld MP/Odisha anti-conversion laws and distinguished propagation from a right to convert another; freedom of conscience is equal for all. Sarla Mudgal(1995), Lily Thomas(2000): conversion does not automatically dissolve an existing Hindu marriage or legitimise a second marriage; “all conversion to Islam is invalid” is incorrect.
  • Puttaswamy(2017) links belief/faith/personal choice with privacy, dignity and autonomy. Notice rules, vague allurement definitions, reverse burdens and misuse allegations require scrutiny for necessity, proportionality and fair process. Source HP2012/Gujarat2021 references concern particular provisions/interim relief, not invalidation of every State law.
  • Source M.Chandra(2010) concerns evidence of conversion/community status in its context; do not turn it into one mandatory ritual for every faith. The GrahamStaines discussion is not authority to legitimise violence or override conscience.
  • Way forward: precise offences, protect voluntary adult choice, fair investigation/hearing, accessible remedies and interfaith dialogue. Practice intended two statements(b); privacy PYQs point to Article 21/PartIII.
