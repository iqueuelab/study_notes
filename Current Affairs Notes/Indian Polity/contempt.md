# Contempt of court and legitimate criticism

## Law and limits
- **Articles 129 (SC) and 215 (HC)**: courts of record with contempt powers. **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971** defines and regulates contempt.
- **Civil**: wilful disobedience of a court order or wilful breach of an undertaking. **Criminal**: specified scandalising/lowering authority, prejudicing proceedings or obstructing justice.
- Fair and accurate reporting and fair criticism of a judicial act have statutory protection. **Truth may be a defence**, when public interest and bona fides satisfy section 13, amended in **2006**.
- Criticism is not contempt merely because a judge dislikes it; protect justice administration while permitting accountability and expression. Context, interference and statutory safeguards matter.
- Parliament can legislate on contempt within constitutional competence; statutory definitions do not mean SC/HC constitutional powers originate only in the Act.
- PYQ 2022: statements **1, 2 and 4** are correct. Statement 3 is false: the **Act**, not the Constitution, defines civil and criminal contempt.
