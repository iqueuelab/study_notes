# Contempt of court and legitimate criticism

## Law and limits
- **Articles 129 (SC) and 215 (HC)**: courts of record with contempt powers. **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971** defines and regulates contempt.
- **Civil**: wilful disobedience of a court order or wilful breach of an undertaking. **Criminal**: specified scandalising/lowering authority, prejudicing proceedings or obstructing justice.
- Fair and accurate reporting and fair criticism of a judicial act have statutory protection. **Truth may be a defence**, when public interest and bona fides satisfy section 13, amended in **2006**.
- Criticism is not contempt merely because a judge dislikes it; protect justice administration while permitting accountability and expression. Context, interference and statutory safeguards matter.
- Parliament can legislate on contempt within constitutional competence; statutory definitions do not mean SC/HC constitutional powers originate only in the Act.
- PYQ 2022: statements **1, 2 and 4** are correct. Statement 3 is false: the **Act**, not the Constitution, defines civil and criminal contempt.

## November 2025: social-media criticism and procedure
- The issue revisits derogatory comments about the CJI/SC and the boundary between criticism and obstruction. **Article 19(2)** permits reasonable speech restrictions for contempt, but offence is not automatic whenever a judge is insulted: apply statutory elements, context, safeguards and the **section 13 substantial-interference threshold**.
- Clarify **section 15** for criminal contempt outside the court's presence: courts may act **suo motu**; authorised law officers may move; private motions ordinarily need the specified officer's **written consent**. For SC, the statutory expression includes **Attorney-General or Solicitor-General**; for HC, the State Advocate-General, with a special Delhi provision. This is not a universal consent requirement for every civil contempt application. [Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1514/1/A1971-70.pdf).
- Reform arguments concern the vagueness/chilling effect of “scandalising”, proportionality and transparency; protecting administration of justice differs from insulating individual judges against accountability. England and Wales abolished scandalising-the-court as a form of contempt in **2013**; do not turn this into a blanket claim that every UK contempt power vanished.
- **2022 PYQ recap: 1, 2 and 4 correct**, because the Act supplies definitions and Parliament has legislative competence while SC/HC powers have constitutional foundations. The PDF omits the question's complete option set; the full question is already preserved in the common Indian Polity bank, so no duplicate or invented reconstruction is added.

## February 2026: NCERT textbook proceedings
- **26 February Supreme Court order** imposed a complete ban on further publication, reprinting and digital dissemination of the concerned **Class VIII Social Science Part II textbook**, ordered seizure/sealing of copies and removal of online access. Its discussion of judicial corruption prompted **suo motu proceedings and show-cause notices** to the School Education Secretary and NCERT Director. These were prima facie observations and procedural orders, not a final conviction proving a conspiracy. [Supreme Court order](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2026/12453/12453_2026_1_55_68880_Order_26-Feb-2026.pdf).
- NCERT had halted distribution, proposed rewriting the chapter and apologised; the Court reserved consideration of whether that apology purged the alleged contempt. NCERT is an autonomous organisation under the Education Ministry. The debate joins age-appropriate, balanced education with institutional accountability and judicial independence.
- Existing Articles **19(2),129,215**, civil/criminal distinction and sections **5/13/15** remain applicable. **Fair criticism and truth subject to statutory conditions are protected**; the source's shorthand that disrespect or any negative depiction automatically constitutes contempt is too broad. Law-officer consent is not required for every civil contempt proceeding.
- Legislative competence: **Union List Entry 77** includes Supreme Court contempt; **Concurrent List Entry 14** covers contempt other than Supreme Court contempt. The PDF's Union Entries 78/95 should not replace the specific contempt entries. **Article 142(2)** concerns the Supreme Court's specified powers subject to parliamentary law.
- The complete **2022 prelims question already exists** in the common bank: **1,2,4 correct (b)**. Constitution supplies powers; the 1971 Act supplies definitions. Reform discussion should assess substantial interference, proportionality, transparency and room for reasoned scrutiny rather than equating judicial criticism with obstruction.
