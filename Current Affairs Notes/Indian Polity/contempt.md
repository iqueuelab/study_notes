# Contempt of court and legitimate criticism

## Law and limits
- **Articles 129 (SC) and 215 (HC)**: courts of record with contempt powers. **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971** defines and regulates contempt.
- **Civil**: wilful disobedience of a court order or wilful breach of an undertaking. **Criminal**: specified scandalising/lowering authority, prejudicing proceedings or obstructing justice.
- Fair and accurate reporting and fair criticism of a judicial act have statutory protection. **Truth may be a defence**, when public interest and bona fides satisfy section 13, amended in **2006**.
- Criticism is not contempt merely because a judge dislikes it; protect justice administration while permitting accountability and expression. Context, interference and statutory safeguards matter.
- Parliament can legislate on contempt within constitutional competence; statutory definitions do not mean SC/HC constitutional powers originate only in the Act.
- PYQ 2022: statements **1, 2 and 4** are correct. Statement 3 is false: the **Act**, not the Constitution, defines civil and criminal contempt.

## November 2025: social-media criticism and procedure
- The issue revisits derogatory comments about the CJI/SC and the boundary between criticism and obstruction. **Article 19(2)** permits reasonable speech restrictions for contempt, but offence is not automatic whenever a judge is insulted: apply statutory elements, context, safeguards and the **section 13 substantial-interference threshold**.
- Clarify **section 15** for criminal contempt outside the court's presence: courts may act **suo motu**; authorised law officers may move; private motions ordinarily need the specified officer's **written consent**. For SC, the statutory expression includes **Attorney-General or Solicitor-General**; for HC, the State Advocate-General, with a special Delhi provision. This is not a universal consent requirement for every civil contempt application. [Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1514/1/A1971-70.pdf).
- Reform arguments concern the vagueness/chilling effect of “scandalising”, proportionality and transparency; protecting administration of justice differs from insulating individual judges against accountability. England and Wales abolished scandalising-the-court as a form of contempt in **2013**; do not turn this into a blanket claim that every UK contempt power vanished.
- **2022 PYQ recap: 1, 2 and 4 correct**, because the Act supplies definitions and Parliament has legislative competence while SC/HC powers have constitutional foundations.
