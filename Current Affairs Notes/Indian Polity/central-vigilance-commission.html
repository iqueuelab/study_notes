Central Vigilance Commission: powers and accountability

Editable notes

October 2025: Vigilance Awareness Week

  • Observed 27 October–2 November 2025, theme Vigilance: Our Shared Responsibility. Preventive vigilance links complaint/case disposal, capacity, asset management and digital initiatives with administrative integrity. Official campaign.
  • Established by executive resolution 1964 following Santhanam Committee recommendations; statutory status under CVC Act 2003, not a constitutional body. Composition: Central Vigilance Commissioner plus up to two Vigilance Commissioners. President appoints on recommendation of PM, Home Minister and Lok Sabha Leader of Opposition committee; tenure four years or age 65, whichever earlier.
  • Supervises vigilance administration and specified CBI/DSPE Prevention of Corruption Act investigations, reviews investigation/sanction progress, advises authorities and can cause inquiries within statutory jurisdiction. It is not a general police force/prosecuting court and cannot dictate the disposal of a particular CBI case. Annual report goes to President for laying before Parliament. CVC Act.
  • Removal for proved misbehaviour/incapacity involves President and Supreme Court inquiry; specified statutory grounds have a separate route. Advisories, civil-court inquiry powers and reporting should not be confused with power to impose criminal sentences or enforce all recommendations.
  • Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014 was not brought into force in the government's February 2025 reply. CVC receives public-interest disclosures under PIDPI Resolution 2004 arrangements; do not attribute an operating nationwide statutory protection regime to the uncommenced Act. Government clarification.
  • The text's blanket 'government permission for Joint Secretary and above' is outdated: distinguish judicial invalidation of the former rank-based DSPE protection, prior approval for specified official decisions under amended PCA section 17A, and prosecution sanction. Jurisdiction/conditions matter. Supreme Court quarterly record, 2014.
  • Transparent appointments, reasoned follow-up on advice, whistleblower confidentiality, sound procurement controls, coordination with CBI/Lokpal and staffing support accountability. Making every recommendation binding or adding independent police powers are reform proposals.
