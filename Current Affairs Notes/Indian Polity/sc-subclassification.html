Indian Polity

Scheduled Caste sub-classification

Editable notes

Telangana: 14 April 2025

  • Source reports commencement of the Telangana Scheduled Castes (Rationalisation of Reservations) Act, 2025, on Ambedkar Jayanti. The 59 listed SC communities are allocated across Group I: 15 communities, 1%; Group II: 18, 9%; Group III: 26, 5%: total SC reservation remains 15%.
  • Applies prospectively under the notified implementation provisions; source says previously notified recruitment vacancies are excluded. Do not generalise that saving provision to every admission/recruitment without checking the notification.
  • Describe Telangana's claim as an early/first implementation after the 2024 judgment, not India's first historical attempt: Andhra Pradesh's earlier classification was struck down in E.V. Chinnaiah (2004). Source population percentages, particularly “26 communities = 3.43% of SC population,” lack a secure denominator and should not be memorised.

Constitutional rule

  • State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh, 1 August 2024: seven-judge Constitution Bench, 6:1, permitted evidence-based SC sub-classification for distributing reservation benefits and overruled E.V. Chinnaiah. Supreme Court judgment
  • Article 341 fixes the notified SC list; States cannot add/delete communities through sub-classification. Classification distributes benefits within the list and requires a rational empirical foundation; it is subject to judicial review.
  • Links: Articles 14, 15(4), 16(4) and Article 46. Formal common membership does not establish identical disadvantage or representation.
  • Creamy-layer comments in separate opinions must be distinguished from a universally enacted new SC/ST exclusion rule. This SC litigation should not be mechanically presented as an identical operational ST policy in every State.
  • Mains: targeted equity versus fragmentation; credible data on representation/deprivation, transparent criteria, periodic review and access to education/opportunity beyond quotas are necessary.
