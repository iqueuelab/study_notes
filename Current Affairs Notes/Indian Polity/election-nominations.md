# Election nominations: eligibility, scrutiny and reform

## November 2025: procedure and access to the ballot
- **RPA 1951 sections 33–36**, **Conduct of Elections Rules 1961**, and ECI schedules govern filing/scrutiny. Candidate or proposer presents papers in person to the notified RO/authorised ARO, **11 a.m.–3 p.m.**, excluding public holidays, within the notified period. Digital preparation does not itself replace required physical presentation.
- Proposers must be constituency electors: **one** for a candidate set up by a recognised party; **ten** for independent/unrecognised-party candidates. Candidate eligibility is different: a Lok Sabha candidate need not be an elector of that very constituency; an Assembly candidate ordinarily needs registration in an Assembly constituency of the State, subject to statutory seat qualifications. Correct the source's blanket local-elector restriction. [ECI](https://www.eci.gov.in/faq/1/7).
- Up to **four nomination papers in the same constituency**, but **one security deposit**, not four. No requirement to “withdraw three papers” merely because all are valid: withdrawal concerns candidature. General deposit **₹25,000 Lok Sabha/₹10,000 Assembly**, halved for SC/ST candidates, including when contesting an unreserved seat. [ECI candidates' handbook](https://www.eci.gov.in/eci-backend/public/uploads/monthly_2018_09/1443786440_HandbookforCandidates_pdf.ec83260aa415a72d889d4b3f052ba9bc).
- Documents include the prescribed nomination, **Form 26 affidavit** (criminal cases/assets/liabilities/education), relevant party authorisation, deposit and electoral-roll proof when needed. Constitutional oath/affirmation before scrutiny and eligibility under **Articles 84/173** differ from merely filing a form.
- **Section 36(4) already forbids rejection for defects not of substantial character**. Section 36 permits summary inquiry, opportunity to rebut objections within its timetable, and requires written brief reasons for rejection. Missing mandatory affidavit information/signature or an invalid oath can be substantial; not every blank field or clerical mismatch is automatically fatal. [Supreme Court discussion of section 36](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2024/54085/54085_2024_6_1501_63309_Judgement_14-Aug-2025.pdf).
- **ADR (2002)** disclosure and **Resurgence India (2013)** affidavit completeness promote voters' right to know. Correct “false information allowed”: false declarations may attract prosecution/disqualification/election remedies under applicable law; disclosure completeness and truth are both important.
- **Article 329(b)** generally channels challenges calling an election in question into election petitions after the process; avoid treating it as eliminating every conceivable court order that facilitates rather than interrupts elections. Right to contest is governed by statute/constitutional qualifications, not a free-standing fundamental right under Article 19 to contest any seat.
- The editorial uses Surat 2024, Tej Bahadur Yadav/Varanasi 2019 and Debasish Dhar/Birbhum as case studies of exclusion/certification disputes. Its unverified “Bihar 2023” example and sweeping claim of no official rejection data are not established facts. Individual facts/reasons differ; not every rejected nomination proves partisan illegality.
- Proposed reforms: clearer substantial/curable-defect guidance; prompt notice and correction support; reasoned public decisions/rejection data; training ROs as impartial facilitators; accessible digital checks, affidavits and tracking. A uniform **48-hour correction window** and digital-default filing are proposals, not current universal entitlements. Overseas comparisons require jurisdiction-specific verification.
