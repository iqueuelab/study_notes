# Women’s reservation: constitutional design and implementation

## Constitutional design and the implementation debate
- **Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023**, assent **28 September 2023**: amends **Article 239AA** and inserts **330A, 332A, 334A** for approximately one-third reservation in Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and Delhi Assembly, including one-third within SC/ST reserved seats as applicable. It does not cover Rajya Sabha/Legislative Councils and creates no separate OBC sub-quota; OBC women are not barred from contesting women's seats.
- **Article 334A:** relevant figures of the **first census after the Act's commencement** must be published, followed by delimitation for this purpose. The source's “census after 2026” conflates this with the separate seat-freeze provisions in Articles 82/170. Assent, notified commencement and implementation are distinct.
- Rotation follows **each subsequent delimitation as Parliament determines by law**, not automatically every general election. Existing Houses' representation continues until dissolution. Article 334A(1) provides a **15-year** period from the stated commencement, while clause (2) allows continuation as Parliament determines by law. [Assented Act text held by Parliament](https://sansad.in/getFile/BillsTexts/LSBillTexts/PassedBothHouses/const%20128th%20amend104202340415PM.pdf?source=legislation).
- The editorial's **2034 election** timeline and **800-plus Lok Sabha seats** are scenarios, not constitutional deadlines or approved strength. Census processing, delimitation legislation/capacity and federal consensus affect timing; southern concerns concern relative representation as well as absolute seats.
- Legislative milestones: first bill **1996**, earlier bill passed Rajya Sabha **2010**, amendment **2023**. Suggested removal of the census link, temporary reservation or seat expansion are reform proposals requiring legal change. The common delimitation notes retain commission history and the distinction between seat allocation and boundary adjustment.
