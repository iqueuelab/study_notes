Indian Polity

Sabarimala: faith, equality and constitutional rights

Editable notes

February 2026: review debate

  • Indian Young Lawyers Association v State of Kerala (2018): 4:1 majority invalidated Sabarimala's exclusion of women of menstruating age and Rule 3(b); the majority did not recognise Ayyappa devotees as a separate religious denomination. Separate opinions differ in reasoning; do not attribute every Article-based argument identically to all four judges.
  • Justice Indu Malhotra dissented, stressing religious autonomy, pluralism and limits on judicial intervention in belief. Dissent is distinct from the governing majority decision; the February review debate is not a fresh final reversal.
  • Articles 14/15: equality/non-discrimination; 21: dignity; 25: individual conscience/religion, subject to public order, morality, health and other Part III rights; 26: denominational rights with its stated limitations. Article 25(2) also permits social-reform/public-temple access measures.
  • Essential religious practices test: courts assess whether claimed practices warrant constitutional protection, but “essential” does not mean immunity from every constitutional limitation. Criticism: judges may become arbiters of theology.
  • Anti-exclusion approach, discussed in Chandrachud's reasoning, asks whether a practice excludes persons in a way that harms dignity and equal participation. It is an analytical alternative, not proof all religious practices have been replaced by one mechanically applied rule.
  • Broader questions include religious autonomy, women entering places of worship, Parsi rights and Dawoodi Bohra excommunication. Balance reasoned rights review, institutional competence, denominational identity and social reform; avoid predicting the review outcome.
