Indian Polity

Ladakh: domicile, reservation and local governance

Editable notes

4 June 2025: new legal framework

  • Following the J&K Reorganisation Act 2019, Ladakh is a UT without a legislature; J&K is a UT with a legislature. Leh Apex Body and Kargil Democratic Alliance have sought Sixth Schedule protection, employment/land safeguards and greater local autonomy; Sonam Wangchuk’s protests highlight tribal identity and fragile ecology.
  • 2 June recruitment/reservation regulations; 3 June domicile procedure rules: domicile routes include 15 years’ residence, or seven years’ study and appearance in Class 10/12 examination in Ladakh; specified children of eligible long-serving Central-government personnel and family/spouse categories are covered. Exact categories/conditions follow the rules rather than an unlimited “any child/spouse” entitlement. Tehsildar issues certificates; Deputy Commissioner hears appeals; physical/electronic application. Ladakh administration chronology.
  • Reservation cap raised 50% → 85%, with 10% EWS separate, covering the prescribed recruitment/admission categories including professional institutions. Ceiling, category shares and implementation notifications are distinct; do not say 85% includes EWS or guarantees every applicant a post.
  • Official Languages Regulation: English, Hindi, Urdu, Bhoti and Purgi; promotes Shina, Brokskat, Balti and Ladakhi. Official use is subject to the Administrator’s notified date; English continues for existing purposes. Recognition creates a legal framework, contrary to the source’s “without legal backing” phrase. Gazette.
  • LAHDC amendment: one-third seats in Leh/Kargil councils reserved for women by rotation. This does not convert councils into Sixth Schedule autonomous district councils.

What remains unresolved

  • Article 240 presidential regulations have force and effect of parliamentary law, not merely informal executive guidelines; they remain amendable and subject to constitutional review. They do not themselves entrench Sixth Schedule status or create a UT legislature.
  • The package does not establish the comprehensive land/autonomy safeguards campaigners demand. Blanket assertions that every non-domicile may buy every parcel ignore existing land categories/restrictions; assess applicable law before claiming unrestricted access.
  • Priorities: transparent recruitment/certificate delivery, funded multilingual administration, women’s participation, tribal/community consultation and ecological carrying capacity. Local job protection, land rights and constitutional autonomy are separate questions.

24 September 2025: Leh protests turn violent

  • The clipping reports arson/vandalism, security-force injuries, police firing, deaths and restrictions during protests for statehood, Sixth Schedule safeguards, land/jobs protection, a separate Public Service Commission and stronger parliamentary representation. Sonam Wangchuk's hunger strike and the Leh Apex Body/Kargil Democratic Alliance campaign provide the context; government allegations about provocative speeches are allegations, not a judicial finding of individual responsibility. Public broadcaster report.
  • Border sensitivities involving China/Pakistan heighten governance concerns; they do not make demands for constitutional safeguards inherently separatist. Dialogue, lawful proportionate policing, accountability and credible representation must accompany employment/ecological safeguards.
  • June domicile, reservation, language and women's-council measures are retained above. The clipping juxtaposes 84% ST share and an 85% reservation ceiling excluding EWS: category share and overall ceiling differ. Do not infer a new September constitutional status from administrative concessions.
  • Sixth Schedule applies to specified areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram—not Nagaland. Ladakh's LAHDCs are not Sixth Schedule councils; extension remains a demand. Standard council composition is up to 30 (26 elected, four nominated) subject to specific constitutional exceptions; blanket 30-member claims should not erase special arrangements. District/regional councils have specified land, non-reserved-forest, custom and revenue powers, subject to constitutional procedures/Governor assent—not unlimited sovereignty.
  • 2015 PYQ (a): Fifth/Sixth Schedules protect ST interests. Already banked under scheduled-tribes-list; no duplicate.

October 2025: constitutional safeguards in dialogue

  • The issue reports renewed MHA dialogue with the Leh Apex Body and Kargil Democratic Alliance, with an offer of Article 371-type safeguards, while local representatives sought statehood and Sixth Schedule inclusion, detainees' release and compensation. Discussion/offering is not a completed constitutional amendment or grant of statehood.
  • Articles 371–371J, Part XXI contain different State-specific arrangements; there is no uniform package automatically barring all outsider land ownership or reserving all jobs. Article 371 itself concerns Maharashtra/Gujarat; 371A Nagaland /371G Mizoram illustrate distinct customary-law/land safeguards. Extending a comparable constitutional arrangement to Ladakh would require appropriate legal change.
  • Existing Sixth Schedule notes explain specified autonomous-council powers and the four northeastern States. Its protections do not automatically attach to Ladakh's LAHDCs, and the source's “Article 371 limited /Sixth Schedule high” scale oversimplifies varied legal designs.
  • Ladakh became a UT without legislature in 2019; statehood would create a new legislative arrangement, not “restore” a former Ladakh Assembly. The source's over 97% tribal claim needs a defined population denominator and must not replace the earlier 84% ST-share context as a verified Census figure.
  • Land, cultural identity, employment, ecological carrying capacity and representative government intersect with sensitive borders. Address these through credible consultation, accountable policing and legal clarity; demands for constitutional protection are not inherently separatist.
