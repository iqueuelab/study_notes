Indian Polity

Deputy Speaker of the Lok Sabha

Editable notes

Constitutional office and May 2025 vacancy

  • The issue notes no Deputy Speaker throughout the 17th Lok Sabha (2019–24) and continued vacancy in the 18th Lok Sabha as of May 2025. Article 93 obliges the House to choose a Speaker and Deputy Speaker as soon as may be; there is no specified numerical deadline, but prolonged vacancy raises constitutional accountability concerns.
  • Election: Lok Sabha members choose one of themselves by the ordinary voting majority; Rule 8 lets the Speaker fix the date. No separate oath beyond the MP's oath is prescribed. Deputy Speaker is an independent constitutional officer, not the Speaker's subordinate.
  • Article 94: ceases on losing membership; resigns to the Speaker (Speaker resigns to Deputy Speaker); removal requires a majority of all the then members, an effective majority excluding vacant seats, with at least 14 days' notice. Correct the PDF's “absolute majority of total sanctioned membership”.
  • Article 95(1): during Speaker-office vacancy, Deputy Speaker performs duties; if both offices are vacant, the President appoints a Lok Sabha member for that purpose. 95(2) addresses absence from a sitting, with provision for other authorised/House-chosen members. Thus vacancy does not create the PDF's claimed absolute inability to preside.
  • Article 96: presiding officers cannot preside over consideration of their own removal. Articles 178–180 are State Legislative Assembly counterparts; 180(1) does not govern Lok Sabha. Constitution, Part V, Lok Sabha FAQ.
  • When presiding, the Deputy Speaker has the same procedural powers as Speaker; constitutional voting provisions and removal proceedings still require their own distinctions. Speaker's survival in office until immediately before the first sitting after dissolution should not be automatically attributed to Deputy Speaker.
  • Committee roles depend on rules: Deputy Speaker heads a committee when appointed to it under the relevant chairmanship rule; Rules Committee is chaired by the Speaker, so “Deputy Speaker automatically chairs the Rules Committee” is wrong. Lok Sabha Rules.
  • Opposition occupancy is a convention promoting bipartisan trust, not a constitutional eligibility rule, and historical practice has exceptions. Electing the office supports continuity, balanced procedure and impartiality; allegations of a precise motive for delay are editorial inference, not a judicial finding.
  • Historical recall: Sachchidananda Sinha, first Central Legislative Assembly Deputy President (1921); M. Ananthasayanam Ayyangar, first elected Lok Sabha Deputy Speaker (1952). After Mavalankar's death (1956), Ayyangar performed Speaker duties and was elected Speaker during the first Lok Sabha, then continued in the second—not first elected only in the second.
