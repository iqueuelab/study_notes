# NOTA and uncontested elections

## Background and 16 May debate
- **PUCL v Union of India,2013 :** secret expression of rejection through **None of the Above**. First used in 2013 Assembly polls in Chhattisgarh, Mizoram, Rajasthan, Delhi, MP, then 2014 Lok Sabha.
- In parliamentary/Assembly elections, NOTA is **not a binding right to reject/restart**: candidate with highest votes wins even if NOTA exceeds individual candidates. **RPA 1951 section 53** governs uncontested declaration; absence of a poll differs from excluding an option on a contested ballot.
- Source reports **Vidhi Centre PIL** seeking NOTA even when only one candidate remains; EC opposed the change as unnecessary for rare uncontested elections and requiring law/rule amendments. This is a litigation position, not a completed reform/judgment.
- Source historical counts **nine unopposed since 1952/six since 1971**, and NOTA shares~1% nationally, Bihar 2015 ~2.48%, Gujarat 2017 ~1.8%, are dated claims requiring election-series context, not fixed present totals.
- Arguments: voter dissent, secrecy and pressure for candidate quality versus election cost, representation/vacancy and uncertain practical effect. Threshold victory, binding NOTA and reruns with fresh candidates are **proposals**, not current national rules. Local-body frameworks may differ.
## May 2025 practice: indirect elections
- **2018 Supreme Court ruling** disallowed NOTA in **Rajya Sabha** elections; direct popular voting and indirect proportional-representation elections differ.
- Source practice answer **(b), statements 3 and 4**, captures its intended freedom-of-expression/direct-election comparison; statement 4’s **“only Lok Sabha and Assembly”** is overbroad if it excludes all local-body polls.
