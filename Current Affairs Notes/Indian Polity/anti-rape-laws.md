# Anti-rape laws: consent, custodial power and survivor dignity

## November 2025: revisiting the Mathura case
- Source reports the CJI's criticism of the **Mathura custodial-rape acquittal, Tukaram v State of Maharashtra**, commonly cited **(1979) 2 SCC 143**. The **1972** incident and subsequent open-letter campaign exposed the danger of equating non-resistance, prior sexual history or submission under police authority with free consent. Treat the reported criticism as institutional reflection, not a fresh reversal of the old acquittal.
- **1979 open letter:** Upendra Baxi, Lotika Sarkar, Vasudha Dhagamwar and Raghunath Kelkar challenged the consent reasoning and disregard of custodial coercion and vulnerability. The case's reporting year should not be confused with the incident year; refrain from deriving a precise judgment date from the PDF's inconsistent timeline.
- **Criminal Law (Amendment) Act 1983:** stronger custodial-rape provisions, victim-identity protection/in-camera safeguards and **Evidence Act section 114A** presumption concerning absence of consent in specified aggravated prosecutions when statutory conditions are met. This does not reverse every ingredient's burden in every rape case. Later restrictions on sexual-history evidence developed through subsequent amendments; the **Family Courts Act 1984** was separate legislation, not a provision enacted by the 1983 rape-law amendment. [1983 Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/5429/1/criminal_law_%28amendment%29_act%2C_1983.pdf).
- **Vishaka (1997)** followed Bhanwari Devi's experience and addressed workplace sexual harassment; **POSH Act 2013** is separate from rape offences. **December 2012 Delhi gangrape → 2013 criminal-law reform**: broader definition covering specified penetrative/oral acts, consent age **18**, stalking, voyeurism, acid-attack/disrobing offences, reporting duties and survivor care. It did not make every rape punishable by death; enhanced capital provisions concern specified aggravated circumstances.
- **2018 reform:** enhanced punishment for rape of girls under **16**, and life/death possibilities for specified offences involving girls under **12**; investigation/trial/appeal time targets are distinct stages, not a universal combined six-month guarantee.
- **BNS Act title 2023; operative criminal code from 1 July 2024.** Correct the PDF: rape under **section 63 remains gender-specific**, framed as a man against a woman; it is **not universally gender-neutral**. Consent means unequivocal voluntary agreement; lack of physical resistance alone does not establish consent. **Section 70(2)** provides life for the remainder of natural life or death for gangrape of a woman under 18. **Section 69** concerns intercourse by specified deceitful means where it does not amount to rape; distinguish the offences. [Official BNS](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/20062/1/a2023-45.pdf).
- Revision: survivor dignity, police accountability, sound investigation, legal/medical support and timely fair hearings must accompany statutory changes; severe punishment alone cannot ensure implementation or eliminate patriarchal bias.
