Indian Polity

Death penalty and the rarest-of-rare doctrine

Editable notes

Doctrine and case distinctions

  • Jagmohan Singh, decided 1972 (reported 1973), upheld capital punishment; Bachan Singh 1980: life imprisonment is the rule, death an exception when the alternative is unquestionably foreclosed.
  • Consider both crime and offender, aggravating/mitigating circumstances and genuine reform prospects; brutality or public outrage alone does not automatically justify death.
  • Machhi Singh 1983: manner, motive, socially abhorrent nature, magnitude and victim circumstances; illustrative guidance, not five automatic statutory switches.
  • Mithu 1983: struck down mandatory IPC 303 death sentence for murder by a life convict, under Articles 14/21.
  • Law Commission 262, 2015: recommended abolition except terrorism-related offences and waging war; recommendation, not enacted general abolition. Commission report catalogue.

February source examples

  • RG Kar rape/murder: trial court gave Sanjay Roy life imprisonment; Sharon Raj poisoning: trial court gave Greeshma death. Different case facts/sentencing evidence and appellate stages matter; trial outcomes are not final national precedent.
  • 2022 sentencing-framework reference sought proper collection/consideration of mitigating evidence; source does not establish a completed final Constitution Bench ruling.
  • Court procedures, High Court confirmation, appeals/review and constitutional mercy powers are distinct from initial trial sentencing.

Ethics and reform

  • Debate: deterrence/retribution and victim justice versus irreversible error, unequal legal assistance, inconsistent sentencing and rehabilitation.
  • “140+ countries abolished” may combine law/practice categories; no undated number substitutes for definitions. Article 19 concerns specified freedoms, not a provision titled “freedom of life”.
  • Improve competent defence, mitigation reports, reasoned proportional sentencing, timely review and survivor support. Special fixed/non-remittable life sentences require judicial/legal authority, not a universal ban on parole.
