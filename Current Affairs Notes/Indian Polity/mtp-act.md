# Medical Termination of Pregnancy: law and access

## Framework as discussed in June 2025
- **MTP Act 1971**, amended **2021**: legal termination on statutory grounds, including risk to life/grave injury to physical or mental health and serious fetal abnormality. Contraceptive-failure presumption extends to any woman and her partner; it is not confined to marriage.
- **Up to 20 weeks:** one RMP's good-faith opinion; **20–24 weeks:** two RMP opinions for prescribed categories, including sexual-assault survivors, minors, specified disabilities/illnesses, marital-status changes and fetal malformations. Supreme Court interpretation protects unmarried women's equal access; autonomy and statutory conditions must both be represented.
- For substantial fetal abnormalities diagnosed by a State/UT Medical Board, the ordinary gestational limit does not apply. **Section 5 separately permits immediately necessary life-saving termination**; therefore 'beyond 24 weeks only fetal abnormality' is incomplete.
- Ordinary authorised places include government hospitals **and approved private places**; the PDF's blanket exclusion of private hospitals is misleading. Confidentiality restricts identity disclosure except to legally authorised persons. [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/15312?locale=en), [section 5](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/6832/1/mtp-act-1971.pdf).
- Access barriers: delayed boards/referrals, rural service gaps, stigma, unclear operational protocols and clinician uncertainty. Timely care, informed consent, privacy and trained providers matter. Source's simplified foreign-country comparison is not a complete account of those jurisdictions.
