# Reservation ceiling and substantive equality

## September 2025 debate on the 50% ceiling
- **Articles 15/16** combine equality/non-discrimination with enabling affirmative-action provisions; formal identical treatment differs from addressing historic disadvantage. **M.R. Balaji (1963)**, not the source's 1962 label, discussed reasonable limits; **N.M. Thomas (1976 judgment/reporting, delivered 1975)** developed substantive equality; **Indra Sawhney (1992)** upheld OBC reservation/creamy-layer exclusion and ordinarily a 50% ceiling with narrow extraordinary circumstances.
- **103rd Amendment Act 2019**, Articles **15(6)/16(6)**, enables up to **10% EWS** beyond existing reservations. **Janhit Abhiyan (2022)** upheld it; this does not automatically authorise any State to raise every backward-class quota without constitutional scrutiny. [Supreme Court discussion of Balaji](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2002/8431/8431_2002_31_1501_21807_Judgement_22-Apr-2020.pdf).
- Source Central illustration: **SC 15%, ST 7.5%, OBC 27%, EWS 10% = 59.5%**, for the relevant recruitment framework; do not transplant all percentages to every State/course. Article **16(4)** concerns backward classes inadequately represented in services.
- Supporters of expansion cite exclusion and population share; concerns include open competition, constitutional limits and unequal distribution within categories. Source Rohini Commission benefit concentration/backlog percentages need a defined dataset/period; do not assume every reserved post has a 40–50% vacancy rate.
- **Davinder Singh (2024)** permits SC sub-classification subject to evidential/constitutional conditions; judicial remarks on creamy layer are distinct from an implemented uniform SC/ST exclusion policy. Proposals: better caste/social data, targeted sub-classification, quality schooling and skills, transparent recruitment and backlog reduction.
