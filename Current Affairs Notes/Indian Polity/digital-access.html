Digital access as a constitutional right

Editable notes

30 April 2025: accessible digital KYC

  • In Pragya Prasun v. Union of India, with Amar Jain's connected petition, Justices J. B. Pardiwala and R. Mahadevan treated inclusive, meaningful digital access as part of Article 21. Supreme Court judgment, 2025 INSC 599.
  • Acid-attack survivors with facial/eye disfigurement could not satisfy blink-based live-photo KYC; blind users faced selfie, signature, printed-form and short-window OTP barriers. A formally identical process can create unequal access.
  • The Court required accessible KYC alternatives and compliance with disability-accessibility obligations, with 20 directions. Treat the judgment's broad digital-inclusion reasoning separately from the precise regulator/agency directions; it does not impose an identical new process on every website.
  • Constitutional setting: Article 14 equality, 21 dignity and effective service access, and 38 reducing inequality; the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 supplies statutory accessibility duties. Disability is not itself a separately enumerated Article 15 ground; avoid overstating that provision.
  • Universal design, screen-reader compatibility, alternative identity/liveness verification, accessible forms, assisted/offline routes and trained staff can preserve security without exclusion. Multilingual interfaces, connectivity and digital literacy also matter for older people, rural users and poorer households.
  • Mains chain: inaccessible design → exclusion from banking/welfare → loss of dignity/substantive equality → accessible alternatives, accountability and remedies.
