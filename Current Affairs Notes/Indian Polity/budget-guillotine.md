# Demands for Grants and the parliamentary guillotine

## March 2026: completion of expenditure voting
- Lok Sabha passed outstanding **2026–27 Demands for Grants** using the **guillotine**; the report describes the House approving demands associated with the **over ₹53 lakh crore** expenditure plan. This is a legislative voting stage, not proof that the entire amount has already been spent.
- At the appointed time on the last allotted day, the Speaker puts the **remaining demands to vote without further discussion**. Guillotine ends debate; it does not abolish voting, make the Budget an executive order or authorise Rajya Sabha to vote grants.
- **Article 113:** Lok Sabha votes demands; it can assent, refuse or reduce them. Charged expenditure is discussable but **not submitted to vote**. **Article 114 Appropriation Bill** authorises withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund; Finance Bill deals with tax proposals. Do not treat grant approval alone as completion of all Budget legislation.
- Limited floor time makes ministry-by-ministry debate selective. Department-related standing-committee scrutiny and subsequent **CAG/PAC** examination complement debate, but cannot fully replace informed ex-ante legislative control. Balance timely supply with meaningful spending scrutiny.
