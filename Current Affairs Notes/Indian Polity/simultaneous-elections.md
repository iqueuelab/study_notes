# Simultaneous elections and federalism

## Proposal and constitutional questions
- Synchronise Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections; distinguish this from any separate plan for local-body elections. The January 2025 discussion is a **proposal**, not proof of enacted implementation.
- Relevant term provisions: **Article 83** (Parliament) and **Article 172** (State legislatures); synchronisation and premature dissolution raise tenure and accountability questions.
## Arguments to balance
- Benefits claimed: lower repeated election costs, less recurring deployment/model-code disruption and more sustained governance.
- Concerns: curtailed state mandates, national campaigns crowding out local issues, regional-party/federal autonomy, major logistical demand and short remaining terms after mid-cycle elections.
- The source's critical editorial stance is an argument, not a judicial finding that simultaneous elections are unconstitutional.
## Linked safeguards
- **Article 356**: President's Rule; **S.R. Bommai (1994)** limits arbitrary dismissal and recognises federalism. Source's 'first President's Rule in Kerala 1959' is incorrect; Punjab had an earlier imposition in 1951. [Parliamentary reference](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/761237/1/Presidents_Rule_in_St_UT_Eng_9th_ed_2016.pdf).
- **Tenth Schedule, 52nd Amendment (1985)**: anti-defection; timely adjudication, Speaker neutrality and lawful merger provisions need attention. Do not confuse the surviving merger exception with the removed one-third split exemption.
## Mains conclusion
- Evaluate efficiency alongside federalism, meaningful electoral choice, constitutional procedure and government stability; recommend consensus, independent administration and credible mid-term safeguards.
## Historical cycle
- Lok Sabha/assembly elections broadly coincided in **1952, 1957, 1962, 1967**. Premature assembly dissolutions in **1968-69** and Lok Sabha dissolution in **1970**, followed by the **1971** election, disrupted synchronisation.

## June 27 constitutional debate
- Contents quotes a former CJI's view that **staggered polls cannot be assumed an immutable constitutional feature**. This is a reported opinion, not a binding judgment approving a particular amendment.
- Repeated arguments add administrative/security deployment pressures and the risk of campaign polarisation/populist timing. Model Code restrictions concern announcements/use of official machinery, **not an automatic halt to all ongoing development or essential services**.
- Article **85/174** (sessions/dissolution) interact with **83/172** (terms); Article **356** cannot simply be used to force synchronisation. The source's blanket “five Articles must change, 50% State ratification required” is not a universal constitutional rule: the actual Bill's provisions determine procedure and whether Article 368's ratification proviso is triggered.
- Cost and voter-behaviour estimates (2014 election **₹3,870 crore**, Bihar 2015 **₹300 crore**, projected synchronisation **₹4,500 crore**, same-party **77%/61%**) are **dated source claims with differing scope/methodology**, not current budget savings or proof any voter will choose identically. Assess federal mandates, accountability, mid-term dissolution safeguards and logistics.
