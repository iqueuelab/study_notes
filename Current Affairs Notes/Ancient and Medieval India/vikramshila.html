Ancient and Medieval India

Vikramshila and the Pala Buddhist learning network

Editable notes

Historical anchors

  • Vikramshila Mahavihara, founded by Pala king Dharmapala, late eighth/early ninth century; remains at Antichak, Bhagalpur, Bihar.
  • Important centre of Buddhist learning, especially Vajrayana, alongside grammar, logic and philosophical debate; linked to scholarly exchanges and Sanskrit–Tibetan translation.
  • Atisha (c. 980/982–1054) is associated with the institution and transmission of Buddhist teaching to Tibet. Approximate birth-year traditions differ.
  • Monastic complex with a central shrine and cells; institutional patronage, disciplined study and cross-regional networks are key themes.
  • Destruction/decline around 1200/early thirteenth century, commonly associated with Bakhtiyar Khalji; do not convert an uncertain date into an exact late-twelfth-century event.
  • Pala network: Nalanda, Vikramshila, Odantapuri, Jagaddala and Somapura. Somapura at Paharpur, Bangladesh became a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1985.
  • A modern university proposal near the site is not proof that the ancient monastery has been reconstructed or a new institution already operates.
