Ancient and Medieval India

Tamil-Brahmi in Egypt: inscriptions and Indo-Roman connections

Editable notes

Visitor inscriptions and Indian Ocean mobility

  • February report describes nearly 30 South Asian inscriptions in six Valley of the Kings tombs, Theban Necropolis, Egypt, assigned broadly to first–third centuries CE. Researchers Charlotte Schmid (EFEO) /Ingo Strauch identified Tamil-Brahmi and other Indic material during 2024–25 studies. These are visitor graffiti/names, evidence of inland mobility beyond Red Sea ports; they do not by themselves prove every visitor's occupation or royal identity. Researcher's institutional lecture notice,12 February.
  • Tamil-Brahmi is an adaptation of Brahmi used to write early Tamil; script and language are different concepts. Broad textbook period third century BCE–third century CE is approximate, not a date established for every graffito.
  • Source reading Cikai Korran reportedly recurs eight times across five tombs. Parallels include Korran at Berenike (sherd found 1995), Pittankorran in Sangam texts and Pugalur inscriptions; Kopan, Catan, Kiran are also noted. Etymologies connecting Cikai with śikhā or Korran with victory/royalty/Korravai are scholarly interpretations, not proof of a particular Chera king's personal visit.
  • Indo-Roman commerce linked Tamilakam, Roman Egypt and Mediterranean markets through ports including Muziris, Arikamedu, Korkai and Barygaza/Bharuch; pepper/spices, ivory, textiles and gems, together with Roman coin finds, provide complementary evidence. Tamilakam covered a broader southern region than the Malabar coast alone; Pugalur should not be memorised simply as “the ancient Chera capital”.
  • Periplus of the Erythraean Sea: anonymous first-century CE Greek maritime trade account of Red Sea/Arabian Sea/Indian Ocean routes; Wilfred H. Schoff's 1912 work is a modern translation. It was not authored by Ctesias, Pliny, Ptolemy or Strabo. The BPSC 64 th question is excluded from CSE banks.
