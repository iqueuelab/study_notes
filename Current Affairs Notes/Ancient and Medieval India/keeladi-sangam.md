# Keeladi archaeology and Sangam literature

## Site and dating
- **Keeladi/Keezhadi**, **Sivaganga district, Tamil Nadu**, near Madurai on the **Vaigai**: excavated by ASI and subsequently the Tamil Nadu archaeology department. Brick buildings, wells, pottery, beads, metalworking evidence and inscribed sherds illuminate settlement, crafts and literacy.
- The State department reports carbon samples dated to about **580 BCE (sixth century BCE)**, earlier than the PDF's blanket fifth-century-BCE start. A dated organic sample establishes its archaeological context; it does not directly date every inscription, structure or the entire site to one year. Link stratigraphy, sample association and independent tests. [Tamil Nadu archaeology account](https://www.tnarch.gov.in/node/1257).
- Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions and graffiti are different evidentiary categories; graffiti should not automatically be read as deciphered writing. Urbanisation, craft production and exchange are supported by material finds, but literacy rate, every literary event and specific Roman links require their own evidence.
- The map also distinguishes **Porunai/Tamiraparani** sites **Sivagalai and Adichanallur**, near Tirunelveli, from Vaigai-basin Keeladi. Its Sivagalai offering-pot paddy/husk result is reported as about **3,200 years old**, approximately **1200 BCE**, not the same date as Keeladi. ASI excavated Adichanallur earlier; State work in these southern sites began in **2020** according to the graphic.
## Read the literature critically
- Early historic/Sangam Tamilakam is conventionally studied around **third century BCE–third century CE**, with debated composition/compilation dates and earlier archaeology; this is an approximate literary-period framework, not a uniform lifespan of every site/text.
- Traditions describe **three Sangams** under Pandyan patronage: first **Madurai**, second **Kapadapuram**, third **Madurai**. Treat these as later literary tradition, not three independently verified historical academies with securely established dates.
- **Tolkappiyam**, traditionally attributed to **Tolkappiyar**, is an early surviving Tamil grammatical/poetic work: **Ezhuttu (letters), Col (words), Porul (subject matter/poetics)**. Its date/layers are debated; the tradition linking it to the second Sangam is not settled archaeological fact.
- **Ettutogai (Eight Anthologies): Ainkurunuru, Natrinai, Akananuru, Purananuru, Kuruntokai, Kalittokai, Paripatal, Patirruppattu**. **Akam** treats interior/love themes; **puram** public life, warfare, generosity and fame.
- **Pattuppattu (Ten Idylls)** includes **Tirumurukarruppatai, Perumpanarruppatai and Maduraikkanchi**. **Patinenkilkanakku** comprises eighteen mainly didactic works, generally associated with the post-Sangam tradition; **Tirukkural**, Thiruvalluvar: **aram, porul, inbam** (virtue, wealth/polity, love).
- **Silappatikaram — Ilango Adigal; Manimekalai — Sattanar**: later Tamil epic traditions, not securely contemporaneous eyewitness accounts of all Sangam events. Religions, ethics, social ideals and economy can be studied through them with critical dating.
- **Chera, Chola, Pandya**, smaller chiefs, cultivation, crafts and trade appear in literature; women poets and varied social roles are significant, but do not infer universal gender equality or a hierarchy-free society. Much poetry concerns everyday life, yet “entirely secular” is too sweeping given religious themes.
- Combine textual genres and archaeological evidence; the literary geography of Tamilakam should not be reduced to every territory south of Krishna/Tungabhadra or projected as today's State boundaries.
