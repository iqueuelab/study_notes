# Harappan civilisation: chronology and urban features

## Revision anchors
- Broad Harappan sequence approximately **3300–1300 BCE**; distinguish **Mature urban phase c. 2600–1900 BCE** from early/late phases. Spread covered present Pakistan and northwestern/western India beyond only the main Indus banks.
- **Harappa—Punjab, Pakistan; Mohenjo-daro—Sindh**; **Dholavira/Lothal—Gujarat; Kalibangan—Rajasthan; Rakhigarhi—Haryana**. Harappa's excavation supplied the conventional name; Mohenjo-daro's Great Bath illustrates public works.
- Planned settlements, drainage, bricks, craft production, standardised weights, bronze tools and beads; agriculture, exchange networks and maritime links supported complex urban economies. Lothal's interpreted dock/water structure and trade role should not imply every inference is uncontested.
- Script remains undeciphered; archaeological evidence does not establish a complete political hierarchy or prove no religion existed merely because clearly identified palaces/temples are absent.
- Environmental/climatic changes, river shifts and regional settlement/economic reorganisation feature in decline explanations. **Invasion is an older hypothesis, not an established general cause**; urban decline did not mean every population disappeared. [NCERT historical overview](https://www.ncert.nic.in/dgs/pdf/KGBV-HistoryENGLISH.pdf).
- Floodplains/resource access facilitated settlement; contemporary Mesopotamia/Egypt illustrate separate early civilisations. Study planning, technology and exchange through evidence rather than projecting modern institutions onto ancient towns.
