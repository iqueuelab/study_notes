# Thirumalapuram: Iron Age burial archaeology

## October 2025: preliminary excavation findings
- Tamil Nadu archaeology excavations at Thirumalapuram, Tenkasi district near the Western Ghats, report a burial landscape between seasonal streams near Kulasegarapereri tank. The source describes a roughly 35-acre burial area and an unusual rectangular chamber made with 35 stone slabs, associated with urn burials/cobblestone filling.
- Finds include white-painted black-and-red ware, red/red-slipped/black-polished/coarse red pottery; urn motifs include human, mountain, deer and tortoise forms. The assemblage includes iron weapons/tools, a bronze tweezer, small gold rings and bone objects, with 78 antiquities reported. These help investigate funerary practices, metalworking and material connections; motifs alone do not establish deciphered writing or a specific language.
- Comparisons with Adichanallur, Sivagalai, Thulukkarpatti, Korkai and T. Kallupatti concern pottery/burial traditions. Similarity does not prove simultaneous occupation or an identical polity. The source's early-to-mid-third-millennium BCE attribution is explicitly preliminary, pending scientific dating; do not present 3000–2500 BCE as a confirmed excavation result.
- The source's comparison table assigns firm BCE dates and “oldest urban settlement” labels without adequate evidence; these are held for review. Its embedded iron-dating graphic concerns **Sivagalai**, so its dates cannot be transferred to Adichanallur or this site. Burial complexity alone does not establish an urban civilisation or independently settle North–South developmental chronology.
