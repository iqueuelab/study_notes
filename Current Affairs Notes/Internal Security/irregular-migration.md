# Irregular migration and the donkey route

## Migration, smuggling and exploitation
- “Donkey/dunki route” describes circuitous, often irregular cross-border journeys arranged by agents, including routes through Latin America towards the US. The **Darién Gap lies across the Colombia–Panama border**. The map's Delhi/Punjab/Gujarat/Mumbai–Istanbul–Guatemala/US paths are illustrative, not one fixed itinerary.
- Source reports agent demands of **₹40–60 lakh** and dangerous forest/desert/sea crossings, document fraud, extortion, injury, trafficking and deportation; costs vary by route/case. Its old graphic's Guatemala/US price ranges use different endpoints. Do not reproduce its unverified visa-free claims as current travel rules.
- **Migrant smuggling** facilitates irregular entry; **trafficking** involves exploitation and can occur without border crossing. They can overlap but are not synonyms. Seeking asylum is not automatically fraudulent and does not itself guarantee permission to stay.
## July 9 enforcement report and response
- ED searches at **11 locations in Punjab/Haryana** were linked to a money-laundering investigation based on **17 police FIRs** against agents/middlemen; the clipping cites alleged charges around **₹45–50 lakh per person**. Allegations are not convictions, and PMLA proceedings require the applicable scheduled-offence/proceeds-of-crime nexus.
- Responses: prosecute fraudulent/exploitative networks, regulate recruitment agents, improve safe legal migration information and livelihoods, cooperate across borders, and provide victim/consular support. Distinguish criminal organisers from migrants and protect privacy and due process.
- The source's proposed 250-word mains question is a practice prompt, not an authenticated PYQ.

## October 2025: investigation follow-up
- Embedded report says **NIA chargesheeted two people in an India–US dunki-route case**, alleging a network involving irregular crossings, forged documents and exploitation. A chargesheet records allegations, not conviction.
- The source repeats the July ED searches and route/cost/risk explanations already covered. Its visa-free-country assertions and folk etymology of “donkey route” are not verified travel rules or established linguistic history.
