# Mahad Satyagraha and Ambedkar’s anti-caste movement

## Mahad: equal citizenship and public resources
- **20 March 1927, Mahad, Maharashtra:** B.R. Ambedkar and supporters asserted access to the **Chavdar/Chawdar Tank**, challenging untouchability. **20 March 2026 is the 99th anniversary; the centenary falls in 2027**. Water access symbolised human dignity and equal civic rights, extending freedom's meaning beyond colonial rule.
- **S.K. Bole's 1923 Bombay Legislative Council resolution** backed access to public facilities, but entrenched exclusion obstructed implementation. Violence and ritual “purification” after the march exposed the gap between formal permission and social equality.
- **25 December 1927:** Manusmriti was burnt at the Mahad conference to reject caste hierarchy; Gangadhar Sahasrabuddhe participated in the act. The tank litigation ended in a Bombay High Court rejection of exclusionary claims on **17 March 1937**. [Government-published writings and judgment](https://meacms.mea.gov.in/Images/attach/amb/Volume_17_01.pdf).
- Link the struggle to **Article 15(2)** on access to public facilities, including wells/tanks, and **Article 17** abolishing untouchability. These are constitutional connections, rather than a claim that one protest alone produced the provisions.
## Ambedkar: revision anchors
- Born **14 April 1891, Mhow**; experiences of exclusion, including “No Peon, No Water”, informed his struggle. Sayajirao Gaikwad of Baroda and Shahu Maharaj of Kolhapur supported his education/social advancement. He chaired the Constitution's Drafting Committee.
- **Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924)** supported the Depressed Classes; **Samaj Samata Sangh (1927)** promoted social equality; **Independent Labour Party (1936)** and **All India Scheduled Castes Federation (1942)** organised political representation. **Kalaram Temple movement at Nashik began in 1930**, extending the challenge to religious exclusion.
- Writings: **The Problem of the Rupee**, **Annihilation of Caste**, **Thoughts on Pakistan**. Periodicals: **Mooknayak (1920), Bahishkrit Bharat (1927), Samata (1928), Janata (1930), Prabuddha Bharat (1956)**. The source's unqualified “Sanskrit as national language” claim confuses Constituent Assembly official-language debate with a constitutional national-language designation; India has no constitutionally designated national language.
- **2012 Prelims revision: (b), 2 and 3 only**—Ambedkar established the Scheduled Castes Federation and Independent Labour Party, not the Peasants and Workers Party of India.
