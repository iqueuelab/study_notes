# Indigo, Champaran and blue as a symbol of equality

## From indigo cultivation to political symbolism
- **Bengal Indigo Revolt, 1859–60**: peasants resisted coercive indigo cultivation, low returns, advances/debt and planter violence. Source graphic names **Digambar Biswas and Bishnucharan Biswas** and the **1860 Indigo Commission**. **Dinabandhu Mitra's Nil Darpan, 1860**, and press support brought planter oppression into public debate. Do not infer every zamindar uniformly supported planters or every peasant action was identical.
- **Champaran, Bihar, 1917**: Gandhi investigated indigo cultivators' grievances, organised non-violent resistance and challenged coercive agrarian arrangements. **Tinkathia = indigo on 3/20 of a holding**; it reduced cultivators' control over land and returns. Inquiry and subsequent agrarian reform ended the obligation; partial refunds formed part of the settlement. This is a major early Indian satyagraha linking peasant grievances to national politics, not Gandhi's first satyagraha anywhere in the world.
- **2018 CSE PYQ answer (c): joining peasant unrest to India's National Movement**. This does not mean peasants, Dalits or tribal communities had no political resistance before Gandhi, nor that Champaran immediately ended commercial cropping throughout India.
- The editorial traces blue from indigo exploitation to **Ambedkarite/Dalit assertion**—blue suits/statues, dignity, reason, equality and the Constitution. It interprets colour traditions in ancient texts as hierarchies that political symbolism can challenge; this is **interpretive cultural analysis**, not a universal unchanging meaning of blue or proof Ambedkar always wore one colour.
- The national flag's **navy-blue Ashoka Chakra** connects the article's discussion to law and ethical action; **Articles 14 and 19** support equality and relevant freedoms. The author's “constitutional morality” reading is not an additional legal clause fixing one mandatory meaning of the colour. Colours can carry historical memory and resistance without one exclusive continuous lineage from 1859 to all modern uses.
- The supplied **“UPSC PYQ 2013” Tinkathia MCQ is attributed in exam compilations to UPPSC Prelims 2013**, so it is excluded from CSE banks; its **3/20** explanation remains useful revision.
