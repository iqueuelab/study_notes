# Savitribai Phule: education, gender and anti-caste reform

## Life and educational work
- **Savitribai Phule (1831–1897)**, born in present-day Satara district, Maharashtra; birth anniversary **3 January**. A pioneering modern Indian woman teacher and headmistress, she worked with Jyotirao Phule to open the **1848 girls' school at Bhide Wada, Pune**. Describe this as a pioneering Indian-run school rather than claiming no girls' schooling existed earlier anywhere in India.
- Expanded education among girls and marginalised communities, including **Mahars and Mangs**; persevered despite social hostility, boycott and abuse. Education functioned as a means of emancipation from caste and gender exclusion.
## Social and literary contributions
- **Mahila Seva Mandal**, widow welfare and remarriage, and **Balhatya Pratibandhak Griha** offered social support, including shelter for vulnerable pregnant widows and prevention of infanticide.
- Supported **Satyashodhak marriages** with simple rituals and opposition to dowry/priestly domination. **Satyashodhak Samaj**, founded by **Jyotirao Phule in 1873**, means Truth-Seeking Society; associated chiefly with an **anti-caste movement in Maharashtra** and equality for Shudras, Ati-Shudras and women.
- Marathi works include **Kavya Phule** and **Bavan Kashi Subodh Ratnakar**, using poetry to advocate learning, equality and rationalism.
- Ethics examples: courage of conviction under opposition, compassion, equality, social justice and rational thought. Contemporary applications include inclusive schooling, women's agency, anti-dowry reform and dignity beyond caste.
