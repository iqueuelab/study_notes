# India–Australia defence policy cooperation

## Ninth Defence Policy Talks
- Held in New Delhi on **17 March 2025**; covered maritime awareness, information-sharing, industry/science cooperation and interoperability. [Defence Ministry account](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2111839&lang=2&reg=48).
- The 2020 Mutual Logistics Support Arrangement enables agreed support/access; it does not grant unlimited ownership of each other's bases.
- Malabar/Quad participation differs from **AUKUS**, of which India is not a member.
- ECTA became operational in 2022; broader CECA negotiations are a separate process. Procurement ideas and cooperation proposals are not completed hardware contracts.
- Balance maritime security and technology opportunities with strategic autonomy, domestic capacity and regulatory safeguards.
## 4 May 2025: Australian election
- **3 May federal election:** Anthony Albanese’s **Labor Party** won reelection; he claimed a second term. The clipping reports opposition Liberal leader **Peter Dutton** conceded and lost his seat. Early majority projections are distinct from final certified seat totals. [Prime Minister’s election-night speech](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/election-night).
- Campaign priorities included renewable energy, housing and healthcare; Dutton advocated lower migration and nuclear energy. These were political platforms, not completed policy outcomes. Australia’s parliamentary election returns MPs; voters do not directly elect the Prime Minister.

## October 2025: Renewable Energy Partnership
- **India–Australia Renewable Energy Partnership**, launched by the Prime Ministers in **November 2024**, covers eight areas: **solar PV, solar supply chains, green hydrogen, energy storage, circular economy for renewables, two-way investment, capacity building and other mutually agreed areas**. A **Track 1.5 dialogue** brings government, industry and researchers together. [Partnership text](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/establishment-india-australia-renewable-energy-partnership.pdf).
- The 15 October issue anticipates **Chris Bowen's meetings with Pralhad Joshi** and an Australia India Institute oration. The Australian minister's **11 October** travel announcement confirms planned ministerial energy engagements; later outcomes should not be treated as already achieved in this issue. [Visit announcement](https://minister.dcceew.gov.au/bowen/media-releases/ministerial-travel-china-and-india).
- Complementarity: Australia's minerals/research and India's market/manufacturing/workforce can diversify concentrated solar, battery and critical-mineral chains. Joint pilots, technology commercialisation, investment, recycling and training are pathways; a proposed green corridor is not completed infrastructure. Refining, not merely mining, is a critical supply-chain bottleneck; Australia has refining activity, so “no refining capacity” is too absolute.
- **India 500 GW non-fossil electricity capacity by 2030**; achieving roughly half installed capacity from non-fossil sources in 2025 does not mean half electricity generated. Australia's source-era **2035 target: 62–70% below 2005 emissions; net zero 2050**. PLI, storage/grid/port investment, finance, certification and standards coordination support implementation.
- Source China-share, pandemic production-loss and **2050 displacement/80%-population** figures have differing or unclear baselines; use them as concentration/climate-risk context rather than a reconciled quantitative forecast. Climate ambition, regional resilience and Quad cooperation do not automatically resolve commercial, technology or geopolitical constraints.
