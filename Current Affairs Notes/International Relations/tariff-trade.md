# US tariff escalation: implications for India

## February 2025 sequence
- **1 February** announcement: additional tariffs **25% Canada/Mexico**, **10% China**; Canadian energy **10%** exception. [US announcement](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/02/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-imposes-tariffs-on-imports-from-canada-mexico-and-china/).
- **3 February** Canada/Mexico measures paused for about a month; source discussion precedes this development. [Mexico pause order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/02/progress-on-the-situation-at-our-southern-border/). Rates/coverage are dated policy snapshots, not present-day universal tariffs.
## Channels affecting India
| Opportunity | Constraint/risk |
| --- | --- |
| US buyers diversify from China | Capacity, quality, prices and rules of origin |
| Textiles/electronics/engineering exports | Competitors may also gain; gains not assured |
| China-plus-one investment | Logistics, policy certainty and skilled labour |
| Canada/Mexico alternative markets | Their demand and trade rules still matter |
- US tariff is collected on US imports; **does not mechanically increase the price of Chinese goods imported into India**. Diversion might instead depress prices and intensify competition; supply disruption can raise some costs.
- Standard goods tariffs do not directly apply to IT services in the same manner. Oil prices, dollar strength, portfolio flows and uncertainty create indirect inflation/currency channels.
- Chinese commercial competition, minerals/supply dependence and Indo-Pacific diplomacy require a differentiated response; economic change does not establish military retaliation.
## Policy response
- Strengthen manufacturing, standards, ports and diversified sourcing; evaluate PLI outcomes, not subsidy announcements alone.
- Bilateral dialogue and lawful WTO-consistent trade remedies; anti-dumping requires evidence/investigation, not merely rising imports.
- India's US **GSP benefits removed 2019**; restoration was a negotiating suggestion in source, not an accomplished February reform.
## Tariff mechanics and February source traps
- Example: imported car **$100**, domestic **$120**; **50% tariff** can raise import's landed price to **$150** if fully passed through. The importer pays customs; incidence is shared among exporters, importers and consumers according to market conditions.
- Motives: protection, fiscal revenue, bargaining and encouraging local investment. Costs: dearer inputs/consumption, inefficient protection, retaliation, disrupted supply chains and uncertainty. Revenue can fall if imports collapse.
- Cutting export price is **not automatically dumping**; dumping tests compare export price with normal value and require the applicable investigation. Simple repackaging does **not** lawfully change origin; circumvention/rules-of-origin enforcement matter.
- Tariff-jumping FDI and exchange-rate adjustment are possible responses, not guaranteed compensation. Trade balances also reflect saving/investment and exchange rates, not tariffs alone.
- **25% EU / 100% BRICS** in this PDF are threats/proposals, not the same implemented measures as the dated Canada/Mexico/China announcements. “Limited impact on India” is a forecast requiring sector exposure and indirect-channel analysis, not a settled result.

## Shrimp-export window: 15 April 2025 report
- Industry expected **35,000–40,000 tonnes** of shrimp shipments following the temporary suspension of India's **26% country-specific additional reciprocal tariff**. This was expected shipment volume, not independently verified completed exports.
- **9 April 2025 order**, effective **10 April**: suspended most country-specific rates for **90 days**, while retaining a **10% additional tariff**, subject to exceptions; China was treated separately. This is dated April policy, not today's tariff rate. [White House order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/04/modifying-reciprocal-tariff-rates-to-reflect-trading-partner-retaliation-and-alignment/)
- Authority principally invoked **IEEPA**, not a Section 301 Trade Act investigation. Existing ordinary duties and applicable **anti-dumping/countervailing duties** could remain: “only 10% total duty” is misleading. Similar reciprocal rates do not guarantee identical total costs across competitors.
## India's seafood exposure
- Frozen shrimp is India's major seafood export; US demand is especially important for farmed **vannamei/whiteleg shrimp**. Exporter associations communicate concerns, but do not unilaterally negotiate sovereign tariff exemptions.
- Official **FY2023–24** data: frozen shrimp **716,004 tonnes**, **US$4.881 billion**, **66.12% of seafood dollar earnings**, not an undated blanket “over 70%”; its **40.19% share is of India's seafood export quantity**, not global shrimp supply. [Commerce Ministry export release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2026456&lang=2&noshow=1&reg=48)
- Temporary relief can release inventories and improve orders/farm incomes, but uncertainty, freight, food-safety compliance and market concentration persist.
- Priorities: diversify destinations, strengthen traceability/testing and cold chains, develop value-added products and maintain predictable trade diplomacy. A unilateral pause does not establish a permanent bilateral trade settlement.

## May 2025: proposed steel/aluminium retaliation
- Embedded May 14 clipping reports India's WTO communication dated **9 May 2025** reserving suspension of concessions on selected US goods after US **25% steel/aluminium tariffs effective 12 March 2025**. This is a **proposed response**, not proof retaliatory tariffs were already imposed.
- Reported exposure: **US$7.6bn Indian exports affected /US$1.91bn US duty collection**; proposed suspension sought equivalent duty impact. Do not reinterpret $7.6bn as already imposed Indian duties or US goods already taxed.
- India characterises the measures as safeguards; the US invokes national security and disputes that characterisation. **Agreement on Safeguards Articles 8.2/12.3** procedures and GATT rules matter; disputed legal classification is not a final adjudication. [WTO's contemporary committee account](https://www.wto.org/english/news_e/news25_e/safe_28apr25_e.htm).
- Source's **2018 25% steel/10% aluminium** rates are historical, not the March 2025 common 25% rate. Its older retaliation summary also omits withdrawal of relevant additional Indian duties in **2023**; distinguish episodes rather than combining them.
- Negotiation, diversification, efficient inputs and lawful remedies can manage exposure; a notification does not guarantee implementation or resolution.

## 1 June 2025: metals-tariff forecast
- Embedded clipping reports US announcement to **double 25% steel/aluminium Section 232 tariffs to 50%**, scheduled **4 June**. At this issue date announcement is distinct from effective collection; later **3 June proclamation** confirms June 4 implementation with specific UK treatment. [Official proclamation, later confirmation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/06/adjusting-imports-of-aluminum-and-steel-into-the-united-states/).
- **EEPC India** forecasts roughly **US$1 billion export impact**, not realised loss. Source FY 2025 US-bound iron/steel/aluminium total **US$4.56 bn**, including **US$587.5 m iron/steel, US$3.1 bn articles thereof, US$860 m aluminium/related articles**; rounding/category scope explain totals. Its broader~US$5 bn exposure is not another additive segment.
- Source Indian steel price **~US$984/t**, US **~US$690**, China **~US$392**, projected US price **~US$1,180/t** after higher tariff: dated scenario quotes, not guaranteed uniform transaction prices. Auto parts, castings/fasteners/holdings and engineering users face margin/competitiveness pressures.
- Exporters seek diversification, bilateral engagement and possible relief analogous to UK arrangements; UK treatment is specific rather than automatic Indian exemption. Proposed Indian WTO retaliation and~US$2 bn US import context are not proof enacted duties.
- Rewritten **2018 rates 25% steel/10% aluminium** and **US$7.6 bn retaliation** repeat the older episode incorrectly; common May 14 notes already distinguish March 2025 tariffs/proposed countermeasures. Steel/aluminium production/background also repeats earlier corrections; no new general aluminium PLI or blanket power subsidy established here.

## June23 growth and trade policy argument
- The editorial connects tariff uncertainty, transport/input costs and disrupted supply chains to exporter investment risk. US exposure is concentrated in sectors such as apparel, gems, pharmaceuticals, electronics, auto components and MSMEs; its roughly**20% export share** is a dated approximation.
- Policy proposals: negotiate balanced US arrangements including services/non-tariff barriers; diversify through UK/EU/Australia agreements; monitor diverted imports and use evidence-based trade remedies; sustain public capital expenditure and supportive monetary conditions where inflation allows.
- Attract supply-chain relocation through reliable land/infrastructure, skills, predictable rules and sector-specific electronics/EV/textile capacity. Extending PLI to wearables/IoT/batteries is an **editorial proposal**, not proof a new scheme was notified.
- Cheap diverted imports are not automatically **dumping**: the legal test compares export price with normal value and requires injury/causal analysis before anti-dumping duties.

## Proposed automobile-tariff rebalancing
- **3 July2025 WTO communication**, reported July5: India proposed suspension of concessions/rebalancing of about **US$724million** in response to US **25% automobile/certain auto-parts tariffs** announced **26March2025**. This is a proposal, not evidence new Indian duties had already taken effect or that a bilateral mini-deal was concluded.
- India characterised the measures as safeguards and invoked **GATT1994, Agreement on Safeguards Articles12.3 and8**; absence of consultation/notification forms India's legal position, not a final WTO adjudication. The US characterises its tariffs differently; applicability of safeguard rebalancing is contested.
- **Ad valorem** means a percentage of value. US$724million is the proposed equivalent suspension/concession value tied to the burden, not simply US$724million of import trade lost.
- Product/rate lists were not supplied in this report. Coordination of litigation, negotiations and domestic adjustment matters; escalation can affect automobiles, equipment and consumer-goods supply chains despite broader strategic cooperation. Article8 contains conditions/timing; lack of consultation alone is not a universal automatic retaliation licence.
