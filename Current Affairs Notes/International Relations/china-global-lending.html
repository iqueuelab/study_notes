China’s global lending: AidData and economic statecraft

Editable notes

November 2025: Chasing China report

  • AidData (William & Mary), Chasing China, covers 2000–2023. Its global dataset examines 217 countries/territories, 200 receiving at least one grant/loan, with about US$2.2 trillion in aid and credit. The source's 179 is a narrower loan-recipient count; do not equate loans with the entire grants-plus-loans universe.
  • US over US$200 billion / nearly 2,500 projects and activities, EU-27 US$161 billion and high-income-country credit US$943 billion demonstrate that financing reaches rich economies. These are accumulated financing commitments/activities, not a sovereign-debt bill owed by the US government or disbursements made only in 2023.
  • Study identifies commercial acquisition/working-capital and infrastructure finance, often routed through offshore vehicles/bank syndicates, with strategic exposure in technology, minerals and infrastructure. Its 80% acquisition-approval success is a portfolio finding, not evidence every loan is coercive or every regulator ineffective.
  • Crucial correction: infrastructure project lending's portfolio share fell from around 75% to below 25%; this is not simply the fraction of all lending to BRI member countries. Source cites ODA US$1.9 billion in 2023, versus a typical US$5.7 billion annually; ODA is much narrower than commercial official credit. AidData release and methodology.
  • The PDF additionally reports Russia US$172 billion, Australia US$130 billion, India US$11.1 billion, the latter linked especially to energy/finance. Treat these as 2000–2023 study totals, not present external-debt stocks; its US US$320 million (2000) → US$19 billion (2023) comparison is an annual-flow series, distinct from the accumulated US total.
  • India's policy questions: transparent beneficial ownership, investment screening calibrated to sector risk, reliable financing and supply-chain diversification; credible development finance and cooperation with Global South partners. Debt distress, BRI restructuring and strategic-commercial lending can coexist. Data alone do not establish a universal intentional “debt trap” or that every Chinese loan is cheaper/faster.
