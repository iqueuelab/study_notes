# Multipolarity: US–China rivalry and strategic autonomy

## December 2025: multipolar structure with bipolar tendencies
- Editorial interpretation: multiple centres—US, China, Russia, EU/India/Japan/Brazil—coexist with increasingly dominant **US–China competition** in technology, trade and security. 'Multipolar with bipolar characteristics' is an analytical frame, not a legally declared global order.
- Post 1991 US unipolar moment weakened with China's rise and Russia's assertion; US capabilities remain substantial. Article interprets Caribbean/Venezuela pressure and greater European defence burdens as strategic reprioritisation, not total US withdrawal.
- Russia's nuclear forces, energy, geography and use of force—**Georgia 2008, Crimea 2014, Ukraine 2022**—provide leverage despite smaller economic scale. Its closeness to China and tensions with West create a contingent 'swing power' role, not proven freedom to choose any alignment without cost.
- Fluid alliances/economic interdependence distinguish today's competition from rigid Cold War blocs. India can combine domestic capability, multiple partnerships, issue-based cooperation and strategic autonomy; assess dependence and risks rather than assuming neutrality automatically yields benefits.
