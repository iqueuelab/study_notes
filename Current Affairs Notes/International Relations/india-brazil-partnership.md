# India–Brazil partnership: minerals, trade and technology

## February 2026: minerals, trade and digital cooperation
- Lula's **18–22 February State visit**, with bilateral talks on **21 February**, advanced India–Brazil cooperation in critical minerals/rare earths, steel/mining, digital technology, health, agriculture and energy. Mineral cooperation can diversify exploration, processing, refining and recycling; an MoU is not proof commercial supply or a new mine is operating.
- **Digital Partnership for the Future** links DPI and AI cooperation; agricultural research, animal genetics, sustainable farming and biofuels connect technology with food/energy security. Defence industrial cooperation and affordable pharmaceuticals provide further South–South opportunities.
- **Trade target distinction:** the joint statement retains **US$20 billion by 2030**; Lula's **US$30 billion** was the higher ambition reported in talks. Do not silently replace the formal figure or present the source's undated US$12–15 billion range as a fresh measurement.
- India–MERCOSUR PTA: **signed 2004, effective 2009**; expansion remains under negotiation, rather than an already completed new FTA. Reciprocal tourist/business multiple-entry visa validity was extended **five→ten years**. [Official joint statement](https://eoibrasilia.gov.in/pdf/India-Brazil%20Joint%20Statement%20-%20State%20Visit%20of%20President%20of%20Brazil%20to%20India%20-%2021%20February%202026-1%20%281%29.pdf).
- Existing geography notes cover major commodities and groupings. Rare earths are **17 chemical elements**, not 17 minerals; reserves do not equal economically recoverable output. Avoid the unsupported claim that 70% of Brazil's rare-earth reserves are unexplored.
