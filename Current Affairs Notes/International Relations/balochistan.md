# Balochistan and the CPEC corridor

## Region and conflict
- The Baloch cultural region spans Pakistan, Iran and Afghanistan. Pakistan's largest province by area has copper, gold, coal and gas, but sparse population and uneven development.
- Kalat's disputed accession in 1948 and the centralising One Unit arrangement (1955–70) are historical background; the entire present province was not one uniform independent kingdom.
- Resource distribution, political representation, disappearances and alleged abuses fuel grievances. Distinguish peaceful civil protests from armed separatist violence, including the March 2025 Jaffar Express hijacking.
- **CPEC connects Kashgar in Xinjiang to Gwadar** on the Arabian Sea. Port concessions are commercial arrangements, not a transfer of Pakistani sovereignty to China.
- Infrastructure can improve connectivity; fishing livelihoods, local participation, security and accountability determine whether benefits reach residents.
## 22 May 2025: rights protests and resource governance
- Source highlights women-led demands against **enforced disappearances**, alleged extrajudicial killings and marginalisation; distinguish allegations requiring investigation and peaceful civic activism from **BLA armed violence**. The clipping's claim about the “most prominent” separatist group is descriptive, not grounds to equate all Baloch activism with terrorism.
- Map: **Quetta**, provincial capital; western Pakistan borders **Iran/Afghanistan**, with Arabian Sea coast/Gwadar. CPEC's Kashgar–Gwadar road/rail/energy links pass through **Gilgit-Baltistan/Pakistan-administered territory claimed by India**, giving the corridor a sovereignty dimension; map colours do not settle disputed boundaries.
- Fisheries concerns, jobs, education/healthcare and unequal returns from copper/gold/gas resources underpin opposition. Source **5% Pakistan GDP** has no year/series and should not be memorised. Claims of Chinese trawler exploitation need specific evidence rather than attributing all fisheries pressure to one nationality.
- A reported **40-year Gwadar operating concession** is not transfer of sovereignty or proof of an established Chinese military base. Assess actual infrastructure/agreements, local participation, environmental impacts and security arrangements; feared militarisation is a risk claim.
