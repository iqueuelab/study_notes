# Quad and the Indo-Pacific

## Members and chronology
- **India, US, Japan, Australia**; an informal diplomatic grouping without a mutual-defence treaty.
- **2004** Indian Ocean tsunami: coordinated humanitarian relief; **2007** first Quad dialogue; stalled in **2008**; revived in **2017**.
- Foreign ministers marked **20 years of cooperation on 31 December 2024**. [Joint statement](https://www.foreignminister.gov.au/minister/penny-wong/media-release/joint-statement-quad-foreign-ministers-commemorating-20th-anniversary-quad-cooperation).

- Free, open and inclusive Indo-Pacific; navigation, rules, ASEAN centrality, climate/disaster resilience, health and technology.
- **Malabar** is a naval exercise involving the four countries, with its own history; it is not the Quad's founding treaty.
## India: significance and limits
- Protect sea lanes, balance coercion, strengthen disaster response, build resilient supply chains and access technology/research cooperation.
- Challenges: different national priorities, uncertain resources, informal coordination, China-related tensions and overlap with other arrangements, including AUKUS.
- Better delivery: practical projects, predictable funding, ASEAN consultation and coordination. Membership expansion is an editorial suggestion, not an agreed decision.
## Prelims traps
- Do not call Quad a formal military alliance or a trade bloc merely because a Mains question uses that premise.
- The **Supply Chain Resilience Initiative** involves India, Japan and Australia; do not equate it automatically with a four-member Quad programme.
- The PDF conflates the 2007 dialogue with Abe's later 'security diamond' formulation; memorise the verified Quad chronology above.

## Malabar 2025 at Guam
- **INS Sahyadri**, an indigenously built **guided-missile stealth frigate**, reached **Guam, northern Pacific**, for **Malabar 2025**. Harbour phase: operational planning, communication protocols, ship familiarisation and sports; sea phase: joint fleet drills, **ASW, gunnery and flying operations**. [Defence release, 9 November 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2188052&lang=1&reg=3).
- Exercise chronology: **1992 India–US bilateral origin; Japan permanent participant from 2015; Australia returned in 2020**. Correct the sheet: Australia participated in **2007**, so 2020 was not its first-ever involvement; invited navies in 2007 were participants, not merely observers. [MEA 2007 account](https://www.mea.gov.in/Uploads/PublicationDocs/178_foreign-relations-2007.pdf). Malabar has its own history and is not a formal Quad treaty/alliance exercise.
- Benefits: common procedures, maritime-domain awareness, surface/subsurface/air interoperability, preparedness and defence diplomacy; HADR cooperation is a wider maritime benefit. The sheet's tables of assets “usually participating” are not a verified 2025 full order of battle.
- Indo-Pacific relevance: sea-lane security, freedom of navigation, operational trust and regional partnerships. China-related strategic interpretation is distinct from a declared collective-defence obligation; hosting in different oceans is a pattern, not an inflexible annual alternation rule.

## February 2026: naval cooperation and Indo-Pacific security
- At Visakhapatnam's International Fleet Review/MILAN discussion, the US Pacific Fleet commander emphasised India–US naval roles in secure sea lanes, stability, navigation and disaster response. **MILAN** is an India-hosted multilateral exercise; **RIMPAC** is US-led; these are distinct from Malabar and Quad diplomacy.
- Maritime awareness, anti-submarine cooperation, common procedures and HADR improve readiness. India operates **P-8I** aircraft; the US operates **P-8A**, so the sheet's claim that both use P-8I is incorrect. MH-60R helicopters support naval surveillance/ASW.
- **Hormuz, Malacca and Bab-el-Mandeb** are strategic chokepoints. Freedom of navigation has UNCLOS-defined rights and jurisdictional limits, not unrestricted access to every water body. The issue's 80%-of-global-oil-trade figure should not be treated as a verified universal IOR share.
- Partnerships can support India's strategic autonomy and maritime public goods without creating a Quad mutual-defence obligation. Distinguish Chinese commercial port investment, an overseas military base and a proven military deployment.
