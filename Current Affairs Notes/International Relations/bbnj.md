# BBNJ agreement and high-seas biodiversity

## Position as of June 2025
- **Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction (BBNJ)** agreement under **UNCLOS**: marine genetic resources/benefit sharing; area-based management tools including MPAs; environmental impact assessments; capacity building/marine technology transfer.
- India signed **September 2024**; June issue reports ratification still pending domestic legal preparations, including biodiversity legislation. A predicted parliamentary timeline is not a completed ratification or a verified session calendar.
- Source reports **49 ratifications as of 10 June**. Entry into force requires **120 days after deposit of the 60th instrument**, not immediate operation on the 60th ratification. This note records the June status, not later treaty developments. [UN agreement overview](https://www.un.org/Depts/los/bbnj.htm).
- Equity concerns: access to marine genetic resources, sharing benefits, scientific/financial capacity and protection of common ocean ecosystems. Areas beyond national jurisdiction include high-seas water and relevant seabed areas; water-column and continental-shelf rights are not identical.
## UNCLOS diagram/PYQ recall
- **Territorial sea: up to 12 nautical miles from baselines (Article 3)**; all States' ships enjoy **innocent passage (Article 17)** subject to applicable rules. **Contiguous zone: up to 24 nm**; **EEZ: up to 200 nm from baselines (Article 57)**, sovereign rights over resources rather than full territorial sovereignty. Source's “from coastline” is simplified.
- **2022 PYQ: all three statements correct → (d)**. Continental shelf can legally extend beyond 200 nm under qualifying criteria; do not treat the diagram as a universal shelf limit.
## UN Ocean Conference, Nice: Indian initiatives
- **Samudrayaan:** human-occupied submersible target **6,000 m**, expected deployment/trial in **2026** as described in June, not achieved descent. **SAHAV** digital ocean-data portal; source explicitly invents its acronym expansion, so retain the name only.
- India highlighted marine conservation, selected single-use-plastic restrictions, port development and fisheries. **US$80bn port projects** is not a single unrestricted blue-economy fund. [Earth Sciences Ministry, 10 June](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2135434).
