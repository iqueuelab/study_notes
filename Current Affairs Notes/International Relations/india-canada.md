# India–Canada: trade, minerals and strategic cooperation

## November 2025: renewed trade and investment dialogue
- **13 November joint statement**, seventh **Ministerial Dialogue on Trade and Investment**, during Canadian Trade Minister **Maninder Sidhu's 11–14 November India visit**, with **Piyush Goyal**. It follows the PMs' G7 Kananaskis meeting and **13 October foreign-minister roadmap** after diplomatic strain. [Joint statement](https://www.canada.ca/en/global-affairs/news/2025/11/india-canada-joint-statement-2025-ministerial-dialogue-on-trade-and-investment.html).
- Agreed to encourage long-term **critical-mineral supply partnerships and clean-energy cooperation**, and identify aerospace/dual-use trade and investment opportunities; domain-level stakeholder engagement remains necessary. These are cooperation commitments, not proof of concluded mineral supply contracts, technology transfer or unrestricted defence trade.
- Canada offers mining expertise and resources including lithium, nickel, cobalt, graphite and copper; minerals support chemistry-specific batteries, electricity networks and advanced manufacturing. Dual-use technologies have civilian and military applications; security/export controls still matter.
- Indian interests include supply diversification, energy transition, aviation/industrial investment and Canadian pension-capital links; Canadian interests include India's market and Indo-Pacific engagement. Diaspora, students and people-to-people ties support the partnership; the source's approximate **1.6 million diaspora** is a dated, definition-dependent count.
- Renewable energy, hydrogen, storage and low-carbon technology are prospective collaboration areas; India's **2070** and Canada's **2050** net-zero goals do not themselves establish implemented joint projects. Assess resilient supplies, commercial viability and diplomatic/security follow-through.
