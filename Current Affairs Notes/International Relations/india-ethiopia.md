# India–Ethiopia: education, investment and strategic cooperation

## December 2025: opportunities in a changing Ethiopia
- Editorial argues for renewed engagement after the **Modi–Abiy Ahmed G20 meeting in Johannesburg**. Ethiopia's large market, Horn of Africa location, hydropower potential, AU diplomatic role and **BRICS membership** make it significant despite being landlocked. Its source “109 million (2024)” population is not retained as an established 2024 estimate.
- **Education** is a major foundation: Indian teachers/professors, university links, scholarships and Ethiopian postgraduate students; the issue recalls Ethiopia's **2007 Pan-African e-Network pilot** and IIT Delhi links. These longstanding links support skills and goodwill, rather than proving an unsourced exact student ranking.
- Source reports **over US$4 billion private Indian investment**, businesses present since the **1950s**, and expanded concessional credit from **2006**. Agriculture, pharma, agro-processing, light manufacturing and mining offer potential; foreign-exchange shortages, taxation, logistics and regulatory uncertainty have discouraged investors.
- **Gold, rare earths and other critical minerals** offer possible supply diversification, subject to commercial geology, infrastructure, responsible mining and predictable approvals. Embassy surveys identify opportunities, not automatically viable mines.
- Defence links include assistance from **1956**, **Harar Military Academy** and training teams since **2009**, with scope for training/equipment cooperation and an institutional joint committee. New defence credit is an editorial option requiring financing and applicable debt constraints, not an announced loan.
- Proposed agenda: update investment/tax arrangements; improve foreign-exchange availability and approval consistency; use **AfCFTA** market access and India's **Duty-Free Tariff Preference** framework where eligible. Ethiopian firms cannot assume automatic duty-free access for every product regardless of origin rules.
- Post-conflict recovery, access-to-sea efforts via Djibouti/Somaliland/Eritrea and uncertain external trade preferences shape opportunities and risks. Proposed reforms and projected manufacturing-hub status remain recommendations.
