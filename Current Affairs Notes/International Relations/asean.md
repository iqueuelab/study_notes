# ASEAN and India’s Act East partnership

## Organisation and June 2025 membership
- **ASEAN: Bangkok Declaration, 8 August 1967**; founders Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand. June 2025 ten members add Brunei, Vietnam, Laos, Myanmar, Cambodia. Secretariat **Jakarta**; Summit, Coordinating Council and Secretariat coordinate cooperation; consultation/consensus and non-interference shape decisions.
- **ARF began 1994; India joined 1996**. Vietnam's ASEAN Future Forum is a dialogue initiative, not a treaty body with India as a founding member.
- India: sectoral dialogue 1992 → full dialogue 1995 → summit partnership 2002 → strategic partnership 2012 → comprehensive strategic partnership 2022.
- **2024 Vientiane 21st Summit:** ten years of Act East, theme *Enhancing Connectivity and Resilience*, ten-point agenda, digital public infrastructure, fintech/cybersecurity and ASEAN–India Fund for Digital Future. **2026–2030 Plan of Action** was to be developed, not already operative. [Summit statement](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Final-Chairmans-Statement-of-the-21st-ASEAN-India-Summit.pdf).
## Trade and connectivity
- **FY2023–24 trade ~US$120.9 billion**, exports ~41.2 billion/imports ~79.7 billion; deficit ~38.5 billion versus 43.57 billion in 2022–23 and 9.66 billion in 2016–17 (source figures). Imports include electronics, machinery, palm oil; exports minerals/pharmaceuticals. Source FDI estimates: ASEAN→India US$156 billion (2000–2023), India→ASEAN US$56 billion; Singapore leads inward flows.
- Review **AITIGA**, preference utilisation, non-tariff barriers and imbalances; negotiating friction does not prove talks permanently ended.
- **Kaladan:** Kolkata–Sittwe (sea)–Paletwa (river)–Zorinpui/Mizoram (road); alternative Northeast access bypassing Siliguri. **IMT Highway:** Moreh–Myanmar–Mae Sot, ~1,360 km; proposed extensions to Cambodia, Laos and Vietnam. Forecast completion is not operational completion; Myanmar conflict, funding and missing roads constrain progress.
- **RCEP:** negotiations launched 2012; India stepped back 2019 over import competition, safeguards, agriculture/MSMEs and services. Signed 2020: ten ASEAN countries plus China, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand. **India is not a member**.
- Trade/investment, Northeast development, Malacca maritime security, tourism and education matter. China's BRI/economic presence constrains India's influence, but ASEAN states retain agency.
- **2016 PYQ:** RCEP is ASEAN-led. **2018 PYQ:** Australia, China, India, Japan are FTA partners among the listed countries; Canada/US are not. ASEAN+1 agreements differ from RCEP membership.
- Embedded article reports nine review meetings without adequate progress and flags rules-of-origin/value-addition concerns over goods processed through ASEAN. These are negotiating concerns, not proof every ASEAN import circumvents rules.

## 26 October 2025: 22nd ASEAN–India Summit
- PM Modi participated virtually in the Kuala Lumpur summit, co-chaired with Malaysia's **Anwar Ibrahim**. **2026** was declared **ASEAN–India Year of Maritime Cooperation**; maritime security, blue economy and connectivity link Act East with the Indo-Pacific. [Official remarks](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2182637&lang=2&reg=48).
- **Timor-Leste became the eleventh ASEAN member** at the October summit cycle. The issue's ten-member table is the preceding membership baseline, not the full new total. Preserve existing **1995 full dialogue partnership** date; its 1996 date refers to a different relationship milestone.
- Priorities include early completion of the **AITIGA review**, the **2026–2030 Plan of Action**, digital inclusion, resilient supply chains, food security, education, green energy, health, counterterrorism/cybersecurity and HADR. Review is not a completed revised trade treaty.
- Recent engagement cited: Prabowo Subianto as January Republic Day chief guest, Ferdinand Marcos Jr.'s August visit and Lawrence Wong's September visit. ASEAN retains centrality and member-state agency; trade deficits, connectivity delays/Myanmar conflict and divergent South China Sea interests require sustained diplomacy.
