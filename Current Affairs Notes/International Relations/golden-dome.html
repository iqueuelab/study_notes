International Relations

Golden Dome and layered missile defence

Editable notes

May 2025 proposal

  • President Donald Trump outlined Golden Dome on 20 May 2025, following 27 January 2025 Executive Order 14186. A planned US homeland missile-defence system, not an operational shield or a geographical copy of Israel's Iron Dome. Space Force General Michael Guetlein was selected to lead the effort.
  • Planned architecture integrates space/ground sensors, tracking, interceptors and command-and-control against ballistic, cruise and hypersonic missiles, including conventionally or nuclear-armed threats. Space-based detection and proposed interceptors could permit earlier engagement; satellite numbers, autonomous operation and universal interception capability remain design claims, not demonstrated facts. US DoD, 20 May.
  • A distributed real-time network must detect → classify → track → assign interception → assess results. Coverage, discrimination of decoys, saturation, survivability, cost and integration are challenges. Source's “nuclear drones” wording should not be confused with a confirmed category of nuclear-powered drones being intercepted.

Strategic and treaty debate

  • Russian/Chinese objections concern weaponisation of space, arms racing and strategic stability; sensor satellites and weapons stationed in space are distinct issues.
  • Outer Space Treaty 1967, Article IV prohibits nuclear weapons/other WMD in orbit or stationed in space; celestial bodies must be used exclusively for peaceful purposes, without military bases, weapons testing or manoeuvres. It does not expressly prohibit every conventional Earth-orbit interceptor. Treaty legality cannot be inferred merely from the word space; UN Charter obligations and due regard remain relevant. UN treaty text.
