# Yemen, Houthis and Red Sea shipping

## Conflict and geography
- Houthis/Ansar Allah emerged from northern Yemen's Zaidi Shia context; took Sana'a in 2014. Saudi-led intervention began in 2015. Yemen is not under one uncontested national authority.
- **Bab el-Mandeb links the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden**, between Yemen and the Djibouti/Eritrea side of the African coast.
- US strikes on 15 March 2025 and Houthi retaliation were reported; early casualty figures and claims of hits require attribution and later verification.
- Iranian support does not establish identical control over every aligned group. Hamas is Sunni; the loosely described “Axis of Resistance” is not uniformly Shia.
- Shipping reroutes via the Cape of Good Hope raise journey time, insurance and freight costs, affecting Indian trade and energy supplies.
- Self-defence claims under UN Charter Article 51 require analysis of necessity and proportionality; invoking the Article is not a final ruling that every strike is lawful. Civilian protection remains essential.
## Movement and regional context
- **Ansar Allah** means “Partisans of God”; the Houthi movement developed in the **1990s** in northern Yemen's Zaidi Shia setting, with armed conflict against the government beginning in **2004**. Zaidism differs from Twelver Shiism; Yemen's war involves political, tribal and regional interests as well as sectarian identity.
- Arab Spring upheaval in **2011** weakened the existing political order. Houthi control of Sana'a in **2014**, Saudi-led intervention and Iranian support internationalised the conflict. Anti-US/anti-Israel rhetoric is part of the movement's political messaging; territorial shares vary and do not measure population control.
- **Land neighbours:** Saudi Arabia to the north and Oman to the east. Across surrounding waters lie **Djibouti, Eritrea and Somalia**. Red Sea → Bab el-Mandeb → Gulf of Aden → Arabian Sea is the maritime sequence.
- India’s **Operation Rahat (2015)** evacuated Indians and foreign nationals from Yemen. Protecting seafarers, diaspora and trade corridors remains relevant alongside humanitarian relief and diplomacy. [MEA Operation Rahat](https://www.mea.gov.in/Images/amb1/lu1509_aneexr.pdf).
