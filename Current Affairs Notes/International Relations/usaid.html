International Relations

USAID, foreign assistance and the 2025 funding freeze

Editable notes

Institution and February context

  • 1961: Foreign Assistance Act and Kennedy's executive order established USAID; development/humanitarian assistance also serves US foreign-policy and soft-power objectives. Official historical account.
  • 20 January 2025: Trump ordered a 90-day foreign-assistance review/pause; subsequent implementation disrupted programmes and staffing. Emergency waivers and litigation meant “every programme permanently abolished” was an inaccurate February conclusion. Contemporaneous account.
  • Work spans health, food security, disaster response, education, livelihoods, governance and climate; Feed the Future and support to PEPFAR programmes illustrate inter-agency links, not exclusive USAID ownership of all US aid.

India and global consequences

  • US development assistance to India began 1951, before USAID's creation. Source reports roughly US$1.5 billion over a decade / US$79.3 million in 2024; dated estimates require consistent disbursement/commitment definitions.
  • Disrupted HIV treatment, food supply, disease surveillance and partner organisations can create immediate humanitarian losses and longer-term instability.
  • Source 42% concerns a particular tracked humanitarian-aid denominator, not 42% of all world development finance; staffing and projected six-million HIV-death figures are dated estimates/scenarios, not observed outcomes.
  • Accountability debate: effective aid, transparency and recipient ownership versus abrupt withdrawal, dependency and geopolitical influence. Reform can improve evaluation without abandoning life-saving continuity.
  • India: assess actual project exposure, protect essential services, diversify finance and strengthen domestic/public-health institutions; opportunities for other powers to fill gaps are possibilities, not automatic outcomes.
