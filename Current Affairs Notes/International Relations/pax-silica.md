# Pax Silica: technology supply chains and economic security

## 12–13 December 2025: inaugural grouping
- US-led **Pax Silica** links **critical minerals/energy → processing/manufacturing → semiconductors/AI infrastructure → logistics**, seeking resilient trusted supply chains and fewer coercive dependencies. It is broader than a rare-earth-only coalition.
- Source inaugural participants include **Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Netherlands, UK, Israel, UAE and Australia**, alongside the US. Meeting participation, signing a declaration and later membership/activities should be distinguished. **Singapore confirms participation in Washington on 12 December 2025**. [Singapore MDDI](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/mddi-statement-on-singapore-s-participation-at-the-pax-silica-summit/).
- **India was absent from the source's initial group**; this is a December 2025 snapshot, not permanent exclusion or a prediction it can never join. Quad/IPEF participation does not automatically create participation in every technology initiative.
- The article attributes absence to trade frictions, limited chip-fabrication/mineral-processing capacity and US selection of established technology ecosystems. These are **reported analytical explanations**, not an official published exclusion rule or proof a bilateral FTA is mandatory for entry.
- Policy response: processing/refining and component capability, reliable energy/logistics, semiconductor/AI investment, recycling/substitution, diversified overseas supply, predictable rules and diplomacy. China's dominance differs by mineral/stage; it does not mine/refine every mineral exclusively. Assess delivered investment and actual supply resilience, not strategic labels alone.

## January 2026: prospective Indian invitation
- **Image-only update supersedes the repeated December exclusion narrative:** new US Ambassador **Sergio Gor** said on **12 January** that India would be formally invited to Pax Silica **the following month**. This is a reported prospective invitation, **not Indian accession already completed by 13 January**. [Contemporaneous report of the envoy's statement](https://apnews.com/article/a71870e8eb2f62cb698bda074f4ecd49).
- The original December participant list, minerals-to-AI scope and processing-capability recommendations repeat existing coverage. India’s initial absence was not a permanent exclusion rule; bilateral trade talks, invitation, signing and implementation are distinct. The source's suggested trade-deal/capability explanations remain analysis, not published mandatory admission criteria.

## January 2026: India's choices in technology supply chains
- Beyond the previously covered planned invitation, the editorial weighs India's **digital public infrastructure, AI market, engineering talent and semiconductor investment** against weak upstream manufacturing/mineral-processing capacity. Participation can support trusted supply access and collaboration; assess delivered capability rather than assuming invitation itself secures chips or minerals.
- India must negotiate room for **domestic industrial subsidies, procurement policy and strategic autonomy**. Suggested future China-led/Pax-Silica-led technology blocs and new export-control regimes are analytical scenarios, not established treaty obligations. **ASML's dominance in EUV lithography** does not mean the Netherlands monopolises every type of chipmaking equipment.
- Source lists participants/observers inconsistently (including Qatar in prose while its inset retains the earlier list). Do not infer accession from that list or describe all participants as formal US allies; membership, summit participation and invitation remain distinct.

## 20 February 2026: India signs the declaration
- **India formally joined Pax Silica at the India AI Impact Summit in New Delhi on 20 February**, moving beyond January's prospective invitation. Ashwini Vaishnaw participated in the signing with US representatives; the initiative connects minerals, chip fabrication, AI and deployment infrastructure. [MeitY confirmation](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2230648&lang=1&reg=1).
- The issue reprints an earlier editorial saying India's invitation is still expected; that passage is superseded by the accession report. Its Canada/EU member list conflates participation categories; do not treat the inconsistent lists as a complete official signatory register.
- Existing notes cover diversification, domestic manufacturing capability, industrial-policy space and strategic autonomy. Joining does not itself guarantee mineral supply, technology transfer or a military alliance. The complete **2012 rare-earth PYQ already exists** in the Science and Technology bank: **1 and 3 only**; deposits also occur outside the four countries in statement 2.
