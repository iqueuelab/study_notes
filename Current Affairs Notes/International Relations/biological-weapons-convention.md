# Biological Weapons Convention and biosecurity

## December 2025: BWC at 50 and Global South preparedness
- New Delhi hosted **‘50 Years of BWC: Strengthening Biosecurity for the Global South’, 1–2 December 2025**. Jaishankar warned that rapid biotechnology advances, dual-use research and non-State misuse outpace preparedness. **Bioterrorism** is intentional use of biological agents/toxins for terror; not every outbreak is deliberate and not every biological agent spreads between people. [MEA conference](https://www.mea.gov.in/bwc-conference.htm).
- **Biological Weapons Convention**, opened for signature **1972**, in force **1975**: prohibits development, production, acquisition, stockpiling and retention of biological/toxin weapons. Its central weakness is absence of a comprehensive, legally binding verification regime comparable to some other arms-control systems.
- Correct the PDF's absolute ‘no professional staff/body’: a **BWC Implementation Support Unit exists**, supporting meetings, national implementation and confidence-building measures. It is not an IAEA-style inspectorate. Nor does absence of a dedicated verification organisation mean the UN has no mechanism at all for investigating alleged biological-weapons use. [UN BWC confidence-building portal](https://bwc-cbm.un.org/en).
- The source describes a proposed national implementation framework: **high-risk-agent controls → dual-use research oversight → domestic reporting → incident response**. Treat this as a reform proposal, not an enacted new Indian law on these pages. Biosafety prevents accidental harm; biosecurity additionally addresses theft/misuse and deliberate threats.
- Global South priorities: laboratory/public-health capacity, early detection, equitable vaccines/therapeutics, manufacturing access and international cooperation. COVID-19 exposed unequal access; the clipping's ‘150+ countries’ vaccine-supply count lacks a consistent metric and is not adopted as a verified Vaccine Maitri total.
