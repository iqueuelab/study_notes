# India–Pakistan: nuclear-site lists and consular cooperation

## 1 January 2026: annual exchanges continue
- Nuclear installations/facilities lists exchanged under the **Agreement on Prohibition of Attacks against Nuclear Installations and Facilities**, signed **31 December 1988**, in force **27 January 1991**; first exchange **1 January 1992**, making 2026 the **35th consecutive exchange**. Lists are exchanged each **1 January** through diplomatic channels; the treaty bars attacking or assisting attacks on covered installations. [Pakistan Foreign Ministry](https://mofa.gov.pk/press-releases/annual-exchange-of-lists-of-nuclear-installations-and-facilities-between-pakistan-and-india-4).
- **Prisoner/fishermen lists are a separate arrangement**, under the **2008 Agreement on Consular Access**, exchanged **1 January and 1 July**, not just once annually. Source Pakistan list: **257 Indian/believed-Indian people—58 civil prisoners and 199 fishermen**. Nationality-belief, sentence completion, consular access and repatriation are distinct legal/administrative stages.
- Narrow confidence-building and humanitarian channels can persist despite wider tension; they reduce risks without resolving border disputes or guaranteeing restraint in every crisis. Fishermen face navigation/weather constraints and disputed maritime boundaries near **Sir Creek**, reinforcing consular-access and release needs.
- Embedded nuclear-policy/stockpile infographics are dated secondary estimates, not disclosures of every current weapon or deployment. India's 2003 doctrine includes a caveat concerning major chemical/biological attacks; do not convert the simplified “never first use” graphic or speculative claims of Pakistan's constant high alert into complete verified doctrine.
