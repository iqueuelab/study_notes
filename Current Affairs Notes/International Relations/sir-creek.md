# Sir Creek: boundary dispute and maritime security

## October 2025: security warning and boundary recall
- On **2 October 2025 at Bhuj**, Defence Minister Rajnath Singh warned against aggression in Sir Creek and referred to Pakistan's reported military build-up. His account of Operation Sindoor and upgraded L-70 guns intercepting drones is an official operational claim. The warning does not constitute a boundary settlement. [Official broadcast](https://newsonair.gov.in/union-defence-minister-warns-pakistan-of-decisive-response-to-any-sir-creek-misadventure/).
- **96-km tidal creek/estuary**, Rann of Kutch marshes between Gujarat and Sindh, opening towards the Arabian Sea. Boundary position affects the starting point of maritime delimitation, fishing, EEZ/resource rights and coastal security; it does not itself establish proved oil/gas reserves.
- **Correct the PDF's reversed claims:** India argues for the **mid-channel/thalweg**; Pakistan claims the **eastern bank**, placing the whole creek on its side. Interpretation of the **1914 resolution/map** and navigability underlies the disagreement. Thalweg concerns the principal navigable channel, not a mechanically fixed geometric middle in every waterway. [MP-IDSA study](https://idsa.in/system/files/Monograph22.pdf).
- Fisher detentions and uncertain maritime limits link livelihoods to security. Joint surveys and bilateral dialogue can support delimitation; continued Army/BSF vigilance and counterterrorism concerns do not remove the need for negotiated resolution.
