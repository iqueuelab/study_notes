# Chimerica: US–China interdependence and India’s choices

## October 2025: strategic interdependence under strain
- **Chimerica**, associated with **Niall Ferguson and Moritz Schularick**, describes intertwined Chinese manufacturing/savings and US consumption/finance. Economic interdependence can coexist with trade disputes, technology controls, military rivalry and selective bargaining; full separation should not be assumed.
- Historical anchors: wartime US–Nationalist China ties, PRC establishment **1949**, US–PRC rapprochement/Nixon visit **1972**, diplomatic normalisation **1979**, China's **WTO accession 2001**, and intensified competition in the 2010s. The 1972 visit began rapprochement; it did not itself complete normalisation.
- **C. Raja Mohan's editorial** argues India should shape regional outcomes proactively through stronger domestic technology/manufacturing, maritime capacity and diversified diplomacy. Assess US–China deals as well as confrontation; claims about sacrificing Taiwan/other partners are analytical concerns, not an established treaty outcome.
- India's options: deepen **Quad** cooperation, manage China border/trade risks, diversify supply chains and retain strategic autonomy. **India is not an AUKUS member**, and Quad is not a mutual-defence treaty. Border security, economic exposure and partnership credibility need separate assessment.
- Practice prompt asks US–China dynamics' implications for India's strategic choices (**250 words/15 marks**). Use interdependence → rivalry/bargaining → India's exposure → domestic capability/partnerships/autonomy; it has no known CSE year and stays outside PYQ banks.
