# India–China relations and border normalisation

## Distinguish disengagement and normalisation
- Galwan violence in 2020 damaged relations. October 2024 arrangements concerning Depsang and Demchok preceded the Modi–Xi meeting at **Kazan on 23 October 2024**, not 2023.
- Disengagement at specified points is not complete de-escalation, border settlement or restoration of trust. “Stable” and “normal” reflect different assessments.
- Flights, visas, pilgrimages and journalist access were diplomatic agenda items; announcements do not prove every channel was already restored.
- Long cultural/commercial contact coexists with the **1962 war** and later clashes; “no history of conflict” is incorrect.
- India's April 2020 Press Note 3 imposes approval requirements for relevant land-border-country investment; it is not a universal ban on all Chinese FDI.
- Trade asymmetry, CPEC through Pakistan-occupied territory and Indian Ocean competition accompany cooperation in multilateral forums. Quad cooperation is not automatically a mutual-defence alliance.
## Recall correction
- The LAC is a disputed line of actual control, **not an agreed, fully demarcated international boundary**. India fought China in 1962.
- “Largest trading partner” depends on year and whether goods or goods-plus-services are measured; a dated ranking must not become a timeless statement.
