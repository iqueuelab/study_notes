# India–Africa economic ties: trade, investment and diversification

## December 2025: five-pillar trade agenda
- Editorial proposes deeper trade beyond diplomatic solidarity; **AU’s permanent G20 membership in 2023** and recent visits to Ghana, Namibia and Ethiopia are context, not simultaneous new December accessions/visits.
- Source reports **India’s FY2023–24 goods exports to Africa US$38.17 billion**, products including petroleum, engineering goods, medicines, rice and textiles. Its **~US$100 billion two-way trade**, China **>US$200 billion**, and import shares **India ~6% / China ~21%** have different or unspecified periods: do not present these as a single verified FY2024–25 table.
- August 2025 official public-broadcaster report quotes ministerial exports/imports **US$42.7/40 billion** and ambition to **double bilateral trade by 2030**; a dated ambition, not realised growth. It also corrects the revision box’s reversal: **India is described as a major trading partner of Africa**, not necessarily Africa India’s fourth-largest partner on the same statistical basis. [Akashvani report](https://newsonair.gov.in/india-aims-to-double-bilateral-trade-with-africa-by-2030-piyush-goyal/).
- Five proposed pillars: **(1)** preferential/comprehensive agreements with countries/regional blocs and reduced non-tariff barriers; **(2)** joint manufacturing/local value addition linked with AfCFTA; **(3)** MSME trade finance, lines of credit/local-currency settlement and risk insurance; **(4)** modern ports, hinterland links and maritime corridors; **(5)** IT, healthcare, education/skills and professional services.
- Opportunities: agro-processing, renewable energy, infrastructure and critical minerals; public-sector/private partnerships can support exploration and projects. Constraints include freight/credit costs, administrative barriers, political risk and misleading investment routing via financial centres such as Mauritius.
- Source’s **~40% FY24 exports to US/EU** is a rounded concentration claim, not the share of all external earnings including services/remittances. Its **~33% of Chinese exports under HS84/85** is an article statistic with an unspecified reference period; those headings encompass broad machinery/electrical categories, not only semiconductors.
- Local production does not automatically obtain US preferential access: eligibility, rules of origin and current trade arrangements must be checked. Africa is a diverse set of economies; AfCFTA is not automatic tariff-free treatment for every Indian export. The 250-word/15-mark question is explicitly potential practice, with no PYQ year.
