India’s Arctic policy and strategic outlook

Editable notes

Science, shipping and strategic competition: May 2025

  • Arctic warming changes ecosystems, livelihoods and access to resources/shipping; Russia's military/logistics infrastructure, China's ambitions and renewed US interest in Greenland intensify competition. Trump's 2019 purchase proposal is an earlier episode, not a completed transfer of sovereignty.
  • Northern Sea Route: along Russia's Arctic coast; can shorten some Europe–East Asia voyages. The PDF's “nearly operational year-round due to melting” overstates reliability: substantial ice hazards persist, icebreaking/ice-class ships may be needed, and no reach is guaranteed ice-free at a given date. Weather, insurance, search/rescue, infrastructure and sanctions affect commercial viability. NSIDC.
  • Arctic diversion could affect selected trade routes; displacement of Indian Ocean relevance or failure of SAGAR/Indo-Pacific Oceans Initiative (IPOI) is a scenario, not an observed inevitable outcome. Much Indian Ocean trade links regions not efficiently served by Arctic shortcuts.
  • Military competition strains cooperation; the Arctic Council concerns sustainable development/environment, excludes military security from its mandate, and is not a defence alliance. Science and indigenous/community participation remain valuable even amid geopolitical tension.

India's existing framework

  • Arctic Council observer since 2013; Himadri research station, Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard (Norway); polar expeditions support climate/ecosystem studies. Observer status is not membership, voting power or Arctic territorial entitlement.
  • 2022 Arctic Policy, “India and the Arctic: building a partnership for sustainable development”, has six pillars: science/research; climate/environment; economic/human development; transport/connectivity; governance/international cooperation; national capacity building. Calling it exclusively climate-focused overlooks its transport/governance provisions. MoES policy launch.
  • Arctic–Himalayan “Third Pole” climate links and effects on South Asia remain scientific priorities; geophysical links do not make India an Arctic littoral state.

Source's proposed three-part strategy

1. Institutionalise coordination: dedicated MEA/Defence Arctic desks, inter-agency consultations and specialist research. These are editorial recommendations, not proof new offices were notified.

2. Cooperate on dual-use capabilities: polar logistics, maritime awareness and satellite/space monitoring with Arctic partners, ensuring scientific value, lawful access and transparent purposes.

3. Participate in governance: shipping/blue-economy, digital and infrastructure standards through legitimate forums; respect coastal-state rights, Indigenous interests and environmental limits.

  • Strategic autonomy requires practical partnerships and domestic polar capacity; avoid assuming science loses relevance merely because competition increases. Evaluate opportunity against ecological risk, finance, logistics and diplomatic commitments.
