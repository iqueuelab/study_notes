Shanghai Cooperation Organisation: membership and India

Editable notes

Origins and institutions

  • Shanghai Five (1996): China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan; SCO founded 15 June 2001 in Shanghai, adding Uzbekistan. Origins include border confidence-building and regional security after the Soviet breakup.
  • June 2025 membership: ten—the six founders plus India and Pakistan (2017), Iran (2023), Belarus (2024). Afghanistan and Mongolia were observers. Do not revise from the source's pre-Belarus nine-member snapshot. SCO 2024 accession statement.
  • Council of Heads of State is the highest decision-making body; Council of Heads of Government handles cooperation including economic priorities. Permanent bodies: Secretariat, Beijing, and Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure (RATS), Tashkent; exercises, intelligence and cooperation against terrorism, extremism and trafficking.

India's interests and constraints

  • Access to Central Asian partners and dialogue on security, Afghanistan, connectivity, trade and energy; participation can support strategic autonomy in a multipolar setting.
  • China–Pakistan tensions, sovereignty concerns over BRI, uneven connectivity and balancing Russia/China constrain benefits. India did not endorse BRI in the 2024 declaration. Astana declaration.
  • Source's roughly 40% world population and US$23-trillion GDP are approximate dated estimates; membership and nominal/PPP/year definitions affect such totals. SCO is not a collective-defence alliance.

Membership PYQ

  • 2022 PYQ: AIIB, MTCR and SCO—all three (d). India: AIIB founding membership 2016, MTCR 2016, SCO 2017. Background repeats June 20 notes; Belarus was already the tenth member in June 2025.

June 27 development

  • Source reports India declined to sign the SCO Defence Ministers' joint statement because the Pahalgam attack was omitted; failure to reach consensus prevented adoption. This demonstrates the limits of regional counter-terrorism cooperation when members differ on terrorism narratives; it does not mean India left SCO.
  • Existing ten-member list, RATS/Secretariat and India interests apply. 2022 membership PYQ: (d), all three—India belongs to AIIB, MTCR and SCO. The complete question was already captured from the June24 issue, so it is not duplicated in the bank.

Tianjin summit preview and practice correction

  • Embedded report previews the 31 August–1 September 2025 Tianjin summit and China's call for regional solidarity. At the issue date this was an upcoming meeting, not completed summit outcomes. Existing notes cover ten members, institutions and India's strategic-autonomy/BRI constraints.
  • The practice's statement 1 wrongly puts Uzbekistan in the 1996 Shanghai Five, omitting Tajikistan. Therefore the printed all four (d) key is wrong: with statements 2, 3 and 4 as printed, 2, 3 and 4 only (b) is correct. India/Pakistan joined in 2017; RATS is in Tashkent; Belarus became the tenth member in 2024, not the explanation's 2023.
  • 2022 CSE PYQ: AIIB, MTCR and SCO—all three (d); the full repeated question is available for deduplication. SCO is a regional cooperation organisation, not an automatic collective-defence alliance.

Tianjin summit outcomes, 1 September 2025

  • Tianjin Declaration condemned attacks including Pahalgam, Jaffer Express and Khuzdar, and called against cross-border terrorist movement. Contrast with June's failure to agree a defence-ministers' statement; inclusion of Pahalgam does not mean the declaration formally named Pakistan as perpetrator.
  • Source highlights opposition to unilateral coercive economic measures, concern over Gaza civilian deaths, condemnation of attacks on Iran's nuclear facilities and an inclusive Afghan government. These are collective diplomatic positions, not creation of a collective-defence guarantee.
  • Ten full members remain unchanged; summit approved a decision to introduce SCO Partner status and abolish observer/dialogue-partner categories, initiating legal amendments. Existing statuses continue pending entry into force; Laos was granted dialogue-partner status by decision. Do not describe an instantaneous completed merger or call the reported 27-country SCO family 27 full members. SCO status explanation; contemporaneous Laos decision.
  • China promoted the Global Governance Initiative, a development bank proposal, and announced source ¥2 billion grants/¥10 billion loans over three years; pledges/proposals differ from disbursed funds or an operating bank.
  • India remained outside BRI endorsement owing to sovereignty concerns, including CPEC through Pakistan-occupied Kashmir. Engage Central Asia/security partners while preserving strategic autonomy and balancing differences with China/Pakistan; “anti-Western alliance” overstates SCO's legal character. 250-word/15-mark question is undated practice.
