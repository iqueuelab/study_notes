# India–Russia relations and strategic autonomy

## Historical anchors
- Diplomatic relations began **13 April 1947**, before Indian independence. **1971** Treaty of Peace, Friendship and Cooperation; **2000** strategic partnership; **2010** special and privileged strategic partnership.
- Defence: BrahMos joint venture, Su-30MKI, S-400 and INDRA exercises. SIPRI's **36% share** concerns the volume of Indian arms imports in **2020–24**, not all defence expenditure or installed equipment.
- Civil nuclear cooperation: Kudankulam; different units have different construction/operational status. Space: Aryabhata (1975), Bhaskara-I (1979) and Gaganyaan training links.
## Economy and diplomacy
- FY 2023–24 bilateral trade about **US$65.7 billion**; **US$100 billion by 2030** was a target, not achieved trade. Oil imports dominate; diversify exports, connectivity and investment.
- National-currency settlement can ease payment issues but does not automatically remove sanctions/legal risks. INSTC and maritime routes require viable logistics and compliance.
- BRICS, SCO and UN cooperation coexist with India's US/European partnerships, China-related concerns and advocacy of dialogue on Ukraine.
- Strategic autonomy means issue-based independent decisions; diversify supplies while retaining useful cooperation, protecting sovereignty and managing technology/spares dependence.
## 5–6 May 2025: counter-terrorism diplomacy
- Embedded clipping: **Putin–Modi telephone conversation, 5 May**, condemned Pahalgam terrorism and reaffirmed cooperation against terrorism. Diplomatic support does not establish a new military alliance or automatic intervention. [MEA bilateral account](https://www.mea.gov.in/Portal/ForeignRelation/IndiaRussia25new.pdf).
- The issue incorrectly says ties began after independence: **13 April 1947** preceded independence. Existing historical anchors—1971 treaty, 2000 strategic partnership and 2010 upgrade—remain unchanged.
## Complementary cooperation and constraints
- **Vostok 2022** illustrates multilateral exercises alongside bilateral INDRA; defence diversification and domestic production aim to reduce single-supplier/spares dependence.
- Trade baskets: Russian crude, coal and fertilisers; Indian pharmaceuticals, machinery and agricultural goods. **US$65.7 billion FY2023–24** and **US$100 billion by 2030 target** are different stages; national-currency settlement does not automatically bypass sanctions law.
- Kudankulam's **six-unit programme** must not be described as six newly operating plants. Sakhalin-1 is an Indian overseas-energy investment; the issue's blanket “highly profitable” assessment lacks a period/accounting measure.
- Source cites STI cooperation in nanotechnology, quantum computing and materials, and **Sputnik V** emergency-use collaboration. Agreements/roadmaps are not proof all technologies were delivered.
- Russia supports India's UNSC permanent-seat aspiration; UN/G20/BRICS/SCO engagement coexists with divergence over China, Quad/Indo-Pacific and Ukraine. Maintain dialogue, diversify exports and connectivity, improve cultural/educational ties, and assess sanctions/payment risks without equating strategic autonomy with unconditional alignment.

## December 2025: Putin visit and the 23rd annual summit
- Issue reports **Putin's arrival in India**, Modi's reception at Palam and private dinner, ahead of the **23rd India–Russia Annual Summit** at Hyderabad House. First India visit after the 2022 full-scale Ukraine-war escalation; ceremonial gestures indicate diplomatic emphasis, not a new alliance obligation.
- **Rajnath Singh–Andrei Belousov** defence talks focused on co-production, technology transfer, niche technologies and Aatmanirbhar Bharat. **Denis Manturov–Nirmala Sitharaman** engagement covered banking, credit, payments and investment protection. Existing notes explain Russian-origin aircraft, submarines, S-400, spares and dependence/diversification.
- Source presents energy, civil nuclear cooperation, defence and investment as **likely summit focus areas**, not an agreed outcome list. Distinguish arrival-stage expectations from signed agreements.
- Embedded Sberbank account describes direct settlement technology and a **First-India fund linked to Nifty50** for Russian investment. A payment channel without Western intermediaries does not establish universal SWIFT replacement, unlimited transaction availability or immunity from sanctions/compliance requirements.
- Strategic-autonomy analysis: preserve independent choices and dialogue while managing Ukraine-war pressures, energy interests, technology dependence and multipolar institutions; “time-tested” ties are a diplomatic characterisation.

## December 2025: caution on new defence offers
- The issue reports a **tepid Indian response** to Russian offers covering **Su-57, Geran/Shahed-type long-range attack drones, submarines and S-500** around Putin's visit. Offers/discussions are not signed purchase orders; no major deal for those platforms was announced in the account.
- Indian priorities include **Tejas Mk2, AMCA, domestic UAV/loitering-munition capability, conventional AIP submarines and nuclear-submarine programmes**. Each has a different development/procurement stage; indigenous ambition does not mean all systems are operational or imports have ended.
- Earlier FGFA difficulties, costs, technology access, sanctions-related production/delivery concerns and lifecycle dependence influence evaluation. Describing a drone as less sophisticated is the quoted source's procurement assessment, not proof it lacks battlefield utility.
- Continuing military-technical dialogue, spares, maintenance, training and co-production illustrate a continuing relationship alongside diversification. This does not establish either complete dependence on Russia or complete freedom from it. Existing defence-production notes already retain **FY2024–25 ₹1,50,590 crore production** and dated export figures; do not count them again as new December production.
- PYQ recall: **INS Astradharini 2016—torpedo launch/recovery vessel; Dhruv 2008—advanced light helicopter; Rafale/MiG-29/Tejas Mk1 2024—none fifth-generation; Dornier/IL-76/C-17 2025—two correct pairs**. All four are already in the common Science and Technology bank, so no duplicate entries.
