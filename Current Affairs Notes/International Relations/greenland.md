# Greenland and Arctic geopolitics

## Geography and political status
- World's largest island; between Arctic and North Atlantic, roughly **80% ice-covered**, population about **57,000**, predominantly Inuit. Arctic climate and ice-sheet loss make it important for global sea levels, ecosystems and infrastructure. Kaffeklubben is associated with the northernmost **permanent** land; small shifting/disputed discoveries make an unqualified “northernmost land” label risky.
- Danish colonisation began in the eighteenth century; **Home Rule 1979**, expanded **Self-Government 2009** within the Kingdom of Denmark. Greenland governs many domestic fields and mineral resources; Denmark retains central responsibility for defence/foreign affairs and currency, with Greenland's participation in relevant external affairs. The source overstates immigration as an already devolved domestic power: the Act permits future transfers in specified fields, which must be distinguished from powers actually assumed. [Danish Foreign Ministry](https://um.dk/en/foreign-policy/the-arctic/greenland-and-the-faroe-islands/).
- Greenland left the EEC in **1985**; Danish-national Greenlanders have EU citizenship despite the territory being outside the EU. Indigenous self-determination and the historic involuntary contraceptive/IUD scandal inform independence debates.
## Strategic resources and US interest
- Critical minerals, including rare earths/nickel/cobalt, matter for batteries, renewables, electronics and defence; deposits are not the same as economical operating mines. Harsh conditions, infrastructure, investment and environmental/social consent constrain extraction.
- The source's blanket ban on new mining is wrong: **2021 uranium restrictions** affect qualifying deposits; Greenland also halted future oil exploration. Easier physical access from warming does not override these policy limits. [Greenland oil decision](https://naalakkersuisut.gl/Nyheder/2021/07/1507_oliestop?sc_lang=da), [US State Department mineral-policy account](https://2021-2025.state.gov/reports/2022-investment-climate-statements/denmark/).
- Historical US wartime presence and **1946 $100 million purchase offer** frame later interest; Trump's **2019** purchase proposal was rejected. **Pituffik Space Base**, renamed from Thule on **6 April 2023**, supports missile warning and space surveillance; retain the current name. [US Space Force](https://www.spaceforce.mil/news/article/3355840/thule-air-base-gets-new-name/).
- April issue highlights Greenland's rejection of being treated as property amid renewed US interest. Strategic competition involves US/NATO, Russia's Arctic military posture and China's **2018 Arctic policy / “near-Arctic state”** designation and investment ambitions.
## Governance and risks
- Melting **sea ice** can open Arctic routes; **Northern Sea Route lies along Russia**, not Greenland. Shorter seasonal voyages do not remove ice hazards, costs or geopolitical risks. Greenland's land-ice melt has distinct sea-level implications.
- A purchase proposal alone is not automatically a UN Charter violation; coercion/use of force and denial of self-determination raise different legal issues. Greenland is not a global common, and neither are all adjacent waters: territorial sovereignty, EEZ/continental-shelf resource rights and high-seas freedoms differ under **UNCLOS**. [UN overview](https://www.un.org/Depts/los/convention_agreements/convention_overview_convention.htm).
- Arctic Council: forum of eight Arctic states including Denmark (with Greenland/Faroes), focused on environment/sustainable development; not a military-security alliance. Priorities: Inuit participation, sovereignty/self-determination, cooperative environmental governance, careful resource development and allied diplomacy. Annexation pressure can strain NATO cooperation; ecological damage and great-power rivalry are risks, not inevitable outcomes.

## January 2026: renewed US interest and critical-mineral mapping
- Issue revisits US purchase/annexation rhetoric, Denmark/Greenland sovereignty, Pituffik defence significance and Arctic competition; existing notes retain the constitutional, EU-citizenship, self-determination and sea-route distinctions. Territorial acquisition by coercion/use of force differs from a consensual proposal; the Arctic is not uniformly a global common.
- Mineral map highlights **rare earths, platinum-group metals, graphite, titanium, vanadium, molybdenum and feldspar**, especially accessible coastal/southern occurrences. Deposits assessed or explored are not all operating mines. **GEUS's critical-resource review dates to 2023**; the sheet's “2025 survey” and exact **25 of 34** tally should not be treated as a newly verified assessment without matching mineral-list definitions. [GEUS review](https://pub.geus.dk/en/publications/critical-raw-material-resources-in-greenland/).
- Applications: selected REEs in permanent magnets for EV motors/wind turbines; graphite commonly in lithium-ion **anodes**; PGMs in catalysts/fuel cells; titanium in aerospace/implants; vanadium in alloys/flow batteries; molybdenum in alloys and feldspar in ceramics/glass. They are not interchangeable battery ingredients.
- Source **28,000 km² ice loss** lacks its historical period and must not be read as January loss or as sea-ice-route area. Warming can change access but infrastructure, ecology, consent and policy remain constraints; Greenland's 2021 restrictions were not a universal ban on all mineral mining.
- PYQ recall already banked: **2025 Botswana–diamond /Chile–lithium /Indonesia–nickel: all three (c)**; **battery cathodes: cobalt, lithium, nickel; graphite is usually anode → only three (c)**. Chemistry varies: not every EV battery requires cobalt/nickel.
