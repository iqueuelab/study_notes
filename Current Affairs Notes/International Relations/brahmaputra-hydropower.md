# Brahmaputra hydropower and India–China river cooperation

## July 2025 project
- China announced construction commencement **19 July 2025** for the lower **Yarlung Zangbo/Tsangpo** project in **Nyingchi, Tibet**; Mainling was the groundbreaking location. It comprises **five cascade stations**, investment about **1.2 trillion yuan (source US$167.8 bn)**; source projected annual generation **300 billion kWh** is a future estimate, not operating output. [Chinese government announcement](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202507/19/content_WS687b6a8ac6d0868f4e8f4486.html).
- The river bends around the eastern Himalaya before entering Arunachal Pradesh as Siang and forming the Brahmaputra in Assam; it continues to Bangladesh. Upstream/downstream location matters for India and Bangladesh.
## Risk assessment and cooperation
- Potential concerns: altered seasonal releases, sediment trapping and floodplain fertility, aquatic/wetland ecology, livelihoods and water security; seismicity/landslides create infrastructure risk. A possible sudden-release/failure hazard is not proof deliberate water weaponisation has occurred.
- Assess actual design/storage, rainfall and tributary contributions, cumulative impacts and transparent operating rules; the source's unsupported label **world's largest infrastructure project** is broader than a hydropower comparison.
- **Expert Level Mechanism (2006)** supports dialogue; India–China have hydrological-data arrangements, **not a comprehensive water-sharing treaty**. Source wrongly implies regular data continued: later official reply confirms Brahmaputra data suspended from **June 2023**, Sutlej data not received since **2022**. [Parliamentary clarification](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/268/AU3287_qYFiZe.pdf?source=pqars).
- India's priorities: resume data/early warnings, obtain design and environmental information, diplomatic engagement and basin cooperation. Dibang/Upper Siang projects feature in debate, but **Dibang is on a tributary** and the Upper Siang proposal should not be described as an already completed counter-dam. Domestic hydropower also requires ecological/community safeguards.
