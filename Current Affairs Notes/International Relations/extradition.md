# Extradition and international criminal cooperation

## Framework and institutions
- **Extradition** is formal surrender of a fugitive for prosecution or sentence enforcement under applicable law/treaty; India's principal law is the **Extradition Act, 1962**. Bilateral treaties, notified arrangements and relevant convention provisions support cooperation; arrangements are not necessarily informal ad hoc understandings.
- **MEA is India's Central Authority; its CPV Division is the nodal point**. Investigating agencies/state police assemble the request and evidence; foreign authorities apply their domestic judicial/executive procedure. Incoming cases generally involve magistrate inquiry and Central Government decision under the Act. MHA's role in criminal **mutual legal assistance** should not be substituted for MEA's extradition authority. [MEA process](https://www.mea.gov.in/from-india.htm)
## Principles and distinctions
- **Dual criminality:** conduct must satisfy criminality requirements in both jurisdictions. **Specialty:** prosecution is ordinarily confined to offences for which surrender was authorised, subject to treaty exceptions/consent.
- Political-offence exclusions, limitation, double jeopardy, nationality and evidentiary requirements vary by treaty. Terrorism need not qualify as a protected political offence.
- Fair trial, torture and capital-punishment risks may require judicial assessment and binding assurances. Death penalty is not a universal absolute bar; Abu Salem's surrender from Portugal illustrates assurance-based restrictions.
- **INTERPOL Red Notice ≠ international arrest warrant**; domestic law determines arrest. **Deportation** concerns immigration removal; **MLAT** assists evidence/service/investigation; neither is identical to extradition.
## Dated case context
- **10 April 2025:** NIA secured Tahawwur Rana's extradition from the US in the 2008 Mumbai attacks case; surrender does not itself establish conviction in the Indian proceedings. [NIA release](https://nia.gov.in/nia-secures-successful-extradition-2611-mumbai-terror-attack-mastermind-tahawwur-rana-us)
- Mallya/Nirav Modi cases show prolonged judicial/diplomatic procedures. The source's Choksi-in-Antigua account was outdated: April 2025 reporting placed him under arrest in Belgium; do not treat arrest as completed extradition.
- Priorities: complete admissible evidence, treaty-specific requests, reliable human-rights assurances, translation/case tracking and diplomatic cooperation. Avoid unsupported treaty-network counts or claims that a proposed statutory revision is already law.
