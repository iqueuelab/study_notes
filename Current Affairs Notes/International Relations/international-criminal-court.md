# International Criminal Court: jurisdiction and independence

## US sanctions and institutional independence
- **20 August 2025:** US announced additional sanctions against **judges Kimberly Prost and Nicolas Guillou**, and **deputy prosecutors Nazhat Shameem Khan and Mame Mandiaye Niang**; ICC objected to the impact on judicial independence. The news concerns sanctions, not abolition of the Court or a change to its treaty jurisdiction. [ICC statement](https://www.icc-cpi.int/news/icc-strongly-rejects-new-us-sanctions-against-judges-and-deputy-prosecutors).
- **Rome Statute adopted 17 July 1998; effective 1 July 2002**. Permanent court at **The Hague**, prosecuting **individuals** for genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and, under special conditions, aggression. **ICJ** adjudicates inter-State disputes and gives advisory opinions; it is not the same body.
- **Complementarity:** national systems have primary responsibility; ICC admissibility examines genuine national investigation/prosecution, unwillingness/inability and gravity. It is not an automatic appellate court for every acquittal.
- Ordinary jurisdiction can follow a State Party's territory or accused's nationality; non-party acceptance and **UN Security Council Chapter VII referrals** provide additional routes. Jurisdiction is generally prospective from **1 July 2002**, subject to each State's relevant acceptance/entry dates; aggression has additional activation/jurisdiction restrictions.
- **18 judges**, normally **non-renewable nine-year terms**; Presidency, Pre-Trial/Trial/Appeals Divisions, Prosecutor and Registry perform distinct functions. ICC is **not a UN organ**, but cooperates with the UN; it relies heavily on State cooperation rather than its own police. Funding includes assessed State Party contributions and authorised voluntary contributions.
- Issue-period membership **125 States Parties**; India, US, China, Russia and Israel were not parties. Party status differs from signing a treaty or being subject to territorial/referral jurisdiction. **Hungary's 2025 withdrawal notice was not an immediately effective exit**.
- Practice **C (1 and 3)**, with the caveat that aggression is not subject to identical conditions as the other three crimes. UNSC referral does not bypass all Rome Statute safeguards.
