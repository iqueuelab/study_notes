# Kimberley Process: conflict diamonds and India’s chairmanship

## 25 December 2025 announcement: India to chair in 2026
- Kimberley Process plenary selected India by consensus as **Chair from 1 January 2026**, its **third chairmanship**; previous terms **2008 and 2019**. Vice-chair responsibility began **25 December 2025**. At the December 26 issue, the 2026 chairmanship is upcoming. [Commerce Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2208375&lang=2&reg=48).
- **Kimberley Process Certification Scheme (KPCS)** began **1 January 2003**: international controls/certification for **rough diamonds** to exclude conflict diamonds. Definition centres on rough diamonds used by **rebel movements or allies to finance conflict undermining legitimate governments**, in line with UN resolutions; it is not a certification of every ethical attribute of all polished jewellery.
- **Tripartite structure:** participating governments, diamond industry and civil society. India implementation's nodal body is **Gem and Jewellery Export Promotion Council (GJEPC)**. India's major cutting/polishing industry, especially Surat, makes traceable legitimate rough-diamond trade economically significant; the source's 90% share needs its processing-volume denominator.
- Limits: narrow conflict definition, member implementation/enforcement, labour exploitation and abuses outside rebel-financing cases. Reform proposals seek transparency, better monitoring and wider ethical credibility; proposed reform is not an already amended definition.
- Practice MCQ **kimberlite pipes—diamonds(c)** is undated practice, not a CSE PYQ. Common diamond notes retain geology, mine locations and India's distinction between importing rough stones and domestic mining.
