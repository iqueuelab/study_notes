International Relations

India–Afghanistan engagement

Editable notes

January 2025 diplomatic step

  • 8 January 2025, Dubai: Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri met Taliban administration's acting Foreign Minister Amir Khan Muttaqi; an upgrade to Foreign Secretary-level contact after the 2021 Taliban takeover, not the first India–Afghanistan meeting ever.
  • Humanitarian assistance, Afghan needs, trade via Chabahar, Iran, connectivity and regional security. Engagement did not constitute formal Indian recognition of the Taliban government in this January context.
  • 1950 Treaty of Friendship; development links include Salma/Afghan–India Friendship Dam, Herat; 218-km Zaranj–Delaram road; Afghan Parliament; Indira Gandhi Institute of Child Health and Habibia School, Kabul.
  • Source graphic also cites Pul-e-Khumri–Kabul 220-kV transmission line and Chimtala substation, plus historical aid commitment ~$3.1 billion since November 2001; not an annual aid figure.

Interests → constraints → response

  • Central Asian access and trade; Chabahar bypasses Pakistan-dependent overland access. Afghanistan is landlocked, not an Arabian Sea port state.
  • Prevent terrorist safe havens; humanitarian goodwill and people-to-people links; avoid abandoning Afghan citizens amid political change.
  • Constraints: Taliban governance/rights and security concerns, Pakistan transit/security factor and growing Chinese engagement.
  • Practical response: conditional project delivery, aid with accountability, diplomatic channels and connectivity, without conflating contact with endorsement of domestic practices.

Map PYQ revision

  • 2022 Afghanistan borders: (c), Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan among the five listed countries. Afghanistan also borders Pakistan, Iran and China; Azerbaijan and Kyrgyzstan do not border Afghanistan. River segments include the Amu Darya system; do not assume every boundary segment is one river.

October 2025: embassy upgrade announcement

  • During acting Afghan Foreign Minister Amir Khan Muttaqi's India visit, EAM S. Jaishankar announced upgrading the Kabul technical mission (re-established June 2022) to an embassy. This was a new diplomatic-presence step; India had not thereby formally recognised the Taliban government at this snapshot. January 2025 Misri–Muttaqi engagement already occurred, so the issue's “first high-level engagement since 2021” is too broad.
  • India offered six new health projects and 20 ambulances, alongside humanitarian, education, visa and development cooperation. Security concerns include preventing Afghan territory's use by anti-India terrorist groups. The source describes April 2025 Afghan visa-module changes; these are distinct from embassy upgrade and diplomatic recognition. Public broadcaster's account of EAM remarks.
  • Regional considerations: Afghan sovereignty, humanitarian access, terrorism, Pakistan transit/security constraints, Chinese/Russian engagement and Chabahar connectivity. Continued assistance protects people-to-people ties; diplomatic contact alone does not settle concerns over women's rights or create a security guarantee.

October 2025: Bagram debate and mineral-resource diplomacy

  • Bagram, about 50 km north of Kabul, was a major US military/logistics base during the 2001–2021 war. The editorial discusses a possible US return/access arrangement for counterterrorism and regional surveillance; no completed return or agreed Taliban basing concession is established by this issue.
  • “New Great Game” extends the historical British–Russian Central Asian rivalry analogy to contemporary US, Chinese, Russian, Iranian, Pakistani and Indian interests. Surveillance, terrorism, connectivity and minerals interact. Claims about each state's motives are analysis; geographic proximity does not prove a specific surveillance capability or inevitable diplomatic alignment.
  • For India, development ties/Chabahar access and counterterrorism matter alongside mineral opportunities. The prior Hajigak consortium interest should not be described as a functioning Indian mine. Existing project/connectivity notes remain relevant; engagement is distinct from recognition or an Indian military commitment. The four-mineral question is unyearred practice.
