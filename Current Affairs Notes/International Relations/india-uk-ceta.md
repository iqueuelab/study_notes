# India–UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA)

## July 2025 agreement: commitments versus entry into force
- **Signed 24 July 2025**, following negotiations begun January 2022 and conclusion announced May 2025; goods, services and wider economic rules. It was **not yet in force at this issue date**: domestic legal/ratification processes and agreed commencement remained; a projected 6–12 months was not a guaranteed deadline.
- UK commits to tariff elimination on **99% of Indian tariff lines**; India liberalises **90% of tariff lines**, covering roughly**92% of UK goods exports**. Newly duty-free exports differ from exports already at zero duty: source identifies textiles, leather/footwear, carpets, seafood, fruit and engineering/auto products as potential beneficiaries.
- Whisky/gin tariffs **150%→75% on entry,40% by year 10**; cars can reach**10% within quotas**. Product/quota schedules matter; this is not a 10% tariff on unlimited imports. Source notes delayed treatment of EV/hybrid/hydrogen vehicles.
- Services commitments include IT/consultancy and selected financial, telecom, environmental/professional services, with reservations and regulation. Recognition frameworks **do not automatically license every UK lawyer/accountant to practise in India**. UK access to specified Indian central-government procurement is subject to coverage/thresholds; it does not open every public contract.
- **Double Contributions Convention:** July side-letter commitment to reciprocal exemption for qualifying temporarily detached workers, initially up to**three years**, while continuing home-system contributions. The source's **75,000** is an estimate, not an immigration quota or universal exemption for all Indian hires; DCC is distinct from income-tax relief and from visa rights. [UK July explainer, with later changes separately dated](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-india-trade-deal-double-contributions-convention-explainer).
- Opportunities: labour-intensive exports, investment, service mobility and supply-chain links. Evaluate preference utilisation, rules of origin, standards, sector adjustment and procurement safeguards; projected trade gains are not realised outcomes.

## Digital sovereignty: law and editorial
- Digital sovereignty concerns control over data, infrastructure and regulation. CETA was signed, but **not yet operative at this issue date**.
- **Article 12.15** source-code protection preserves **regulatory, judicial, inspection, enforcement/conformity-assessment exceptions**, with confidentiality safeguards; not a total loss of regulator access.
- **Article 12.12** concerns **non-proprietary government information made public**, not compulsory release of all sensitive data.
- **Article 12.20** specified later third-country commitments can trigger **consultations on equivalent data-flow/localisation disciplines**, not automatic present blanket free-flow concessions. [Treaty Chapter 12](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-india-ceta-chapter-12-digital-trade/chapter-12-digital-trade-web-version).
- Editorial concerns about dependence should be evaluated with these safeguards; response: technology/cybersecurity expertise in negotiations, public scrutiny, clear data policy, indigenous capabilities and implementation monitoring.

## October 2025: Starmer visit and implementation
- Keir Starmer's first official India visit on 8–9 October followed July's signed CETA and adoption of Vision 2035; the official delegation contained 125 business, university and cultural leaders, correcting the issue's approximate 100. Vision 2035 spans trade, technology, defence, climate, health, education and people-to-people cooperation. [Joint statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/india-uk-joint-statement-9-october-2025).
- The £25.5-billion annual bilateral-trade increase is a UK projection, not trade already achieved. CETA does not supply general visa liberalisation or automatic access to EU markets: Britain and the EU have separate trade regimes. Rolls-Royce's proposed Indian capability expansion and films planned in Britain from 2026 were announced intentions.
- FTA export gains depend jointly on preference margins relative to MFN tariffs, origin compliance, sanitary/technical standards, domestic ports/logistics and partner demand. The source's CDS 2024 question is outside the CSE bank; all five listed factors matter. Lower SPS protection is not a necessary policy goal: equivalence, transparent procedures and compliance can facilitate trade while protecting health.
