# Strait of Hormuz: geography and energy security

## Location and shipping
**Persian Gulf → Strait of Hormuz → Gulf of Oman → Arabian Sea**.
- Strait between **Iran and Oman's Musandam Peninsula**; UAE lies near the southern approaches. About **33 km** wide at narrowest point; traffic separation lanes are narrow and heavily used.
- Critical outlet for Persian Gulf oil producers and Qatar's LNG. Conflict/blockade can disrupt supply, freight/insurance and energy prices, affecting importing countries including India.
## Correct denominator: June 2025
- EIA's **16 June 2025** account: **2024 flow ~20 million barrels/day**, equal to about **20% of global petroleum-liquids consumption**; the PDF incorrectly labels this simply 20% of seaborne oil trade. Use a common year and denominator when comparing flows.
- Saudi and UAE pipelines offer limited bypass capacity; EIA estimated **~2.6 million b/d available additional capacity**, far smaller than total strait flows. This limits complete substitution. Qatar is a leading LNG exporter; the source's claim it is unqualified world number one is not reliable for 2024. [EIA June 16 analysis](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=65504).

## March 2026: oil and LNG exposure
- The issue's **2024** infographic distinguishes **about 30% of seaborne oil trade** from **about one-fifth of global petroleum-liquids consumption**; these denominators are not interchangeable. It reports **20.3 million barrels/day oil and products** and around **20% of global LNG trade**. Existing EIA notes already cover the rounded oil-flow/consumption measure. [EIA analysis](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=65504).
- Qatar's LNG dependence and Asian importers' exposure make gas supply important alongside crude. The graphic's **~80% of transiting LNG destined for Asia / ~20% Europe** are dated trade-flow estimates, not shares of all Asian or European consumption.
- Actual transit disruption, a threat to close and a sustained complete blockade are different conditions. Higher freight/insurance and precautionary vessel delays can interrupt supplies even without a physical barrier across the strait. Existing geography remains **Persian Gulf → Hormuz → Gulf of Oman → Arabian Sea**.

## 4 March 2026: disruption and India's fuel exposure
- The issue reports sharply reduced oil transit and delayed cargoes following the strikes. It estimates **~2.5–2.7 million barrels/day, roughly half India's crude imports**, exposed to Hormuz; route exposure varies with supplier mix. **More than 88% crude-import dependence** concerns total domestic crude requirement, a different denominator.
- Distinguish **~15 million barrels/day crude** from the earlier **~20 million barrels/day crude plus petroleum products**; they need not contradict. Claims that pipelines leave **9% of world demand structurally at risk** and oil could exceed **$100/barrel** are model/scenario estimates, not observed outcomes or guaranteed prices.
- **LPG (mainly propane/butane)** and **LNG (mainly methane)** need dedicated infrastructure. The source's **80–85% of India's LPG needs imported** confuses import dependence with Gulf/route concentration and should not be memorised. LPG import share of consumption, Gulf share of imports and Hormuz transit share are three separate ratios. Its **~60% LNG-import route exposure** is likewise a source estimate. Commercial gas stocks differ from a dedicated government strategic reserve.
- Price rises can widen the import bill/CAD, raise inflation and pressure the rupee, depending on volumes, exchange rates and policy. The printed **$1/barrel → $1.8–2 billion annual bill** is an illustrative constant-volume calculation, not a universal coefficient.
- Responses: monitor actual stocks/transit, diversify compatible crude/gas cargoes and routes, use authorised reserves, coordinate shipping/insurance/diplomacy and manage demand. Longer term: storage, reliable contracts, efficiency, domestic production and clean-energy substitution. The source's 250-word/15-mark prompt is authored practice, not an authentic CSE PYQ.
- Map recall: **Bab-el-Mandeb connects the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden**. On the practice page this is **option (d)**; the printed answer letter **(c)** is erroneous.

## 14 March 2026: Indian LPG carriers and maritime safety
- The issue reports SCI's Indian-flagged **Shivalik**, LPG capacity **over 54,000 tonnes**, departing **Ras Laffan, Qatar, 7 March**, and transiting Hormuz. The same-day official briefing confirmed **Shivalik and Nanda Devi** crossed safely with **92,700 tonnes LPG combined**, expected at **Mundra/Kandla on 16/17 March**; expected arrival is not completed delivery. This contradicts the compilation's generic US-destination assertion. [Government briefing via Akashvani](https://newsonair.gov.in/two-indian-vessels-carrying-lpg-cross-strait-of-hormuz-safely-govt-says-adequate-crude-oil-inventories-in-country/).
- Distinguish **Sahyadri**, another SCI LPG carrier mentioned as joining in 2025, from these confirmed two vessels. **Jag Prakash** is a Great Eastern Shipping products tanker (~48,000-tonne capacity), while **Shenlong** is a crude tanker reportedly carrying ~1 million barrels and transiting on 8 March; vessel type, flag and ownership are different attributes.
- **AIS, Automatic Identification System**, broadcasts vessel identification/position information. “Dark transit” describes disabling transmission; masters may switch AIS off when its operation threatens safety/security under applicable guidance, not as an unrestricted routine practice. [IMO guidance](https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/security/pages/faq.aspx). Missing public tracking data alone cannot prove a vessel's route, hostile intent or safe passage. **GNSS/GPS spoofing** supplies false navigation data, whereas **jamming** disrupts reception; either can threaten navigation without physical damage.
- Source-era estimates **~600 ships stranded, 23 Indian ships in Persian Gulf/3 Gulf of Oman, ~23,000 Indian maritime workers and four Indian seafarer deaths** are dated reports, not permanent totals. Safety advisories, communications and coordinated assistance matter; an individual transit does not establish normal traffic or a guaranteed safe corridor. Existing strait geography/energy ratios repeat.
