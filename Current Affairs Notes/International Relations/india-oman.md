# India–Oman: CEPA and strategic partnership

## 11 December 2025: CEPA signing anticipated
- The issue reports India–Oman **CEPA text/market-access negotiations finalised** and a possible visit the following week, with final dates/approval pending. Treat this as **pre-signature reporting**, not a pact already signed, ratified or in force on 11 December.
- Negotiations began **November 2023**; source describes five rounds and reopening offers after a March 2024 Cabinet deferral. Reported concerns include sensitive-sector safeguards, better market access and investment/labour terms. A comprehensive economic partnership may cover goods, services and wider regulatory cooperation; not every tariff or regulation disappears.
- Oman lies beside **Strait of Hormuz/Gulf of Oman** approaches; **Duqm access** supports Indian maritime logistics/cooperation, not Indian sovereignty over the port. Energy, minerals, diaspora links and West Asia/GCC access complement commerce.
- Source **roughly US$12bn annual trade and 7–8 lakh diaspora** are dated approximate figures requiring reference periods, not permanent totals. Potential gains: pharmaceuticals, engineering, textiles, food, petrochemicals and services, subject to actual schedules/rules of origin and implementation.
- The source's ‘still negotiating UK FTA’ shorthand is outdated: **UK CETA signed July 2025**, while entry into force is a separate step. EU/New Zealand negotiation-progress reports are not concluded/operative agreements at this issue date; an Oman pact does not itself create an India–GCC FTA.

## December 2025: additional strategic and economic background
- Ahead of the **17–18 December Oman leg** of the three-country tour, the issue marks **70 years of diplomatic relations (1955–2025)**; **strategic partnership 2008**, **defence-cooperation MoU 2005**. Oman was an invited guest during India's **2023 G20 Presidency**.
- Existing notes explain Duqm logistics access; its arrangement dates to **2018**. Overflight/transit support and exercises involving all three Indian services add to the defence relationship; these do not establish a sovereign permanent Indian base.
- Source **FY2024–25 bilateral trade US$10.613 billion**, cumulative Omani FDI into India **US$605.57 million (2000–2025)**; different measures/periods. **Oman–India Joint Investment Fund**, SBI–Oman Investment Authority venture: source reports **US$600 million across tranches**, including a **US$300 million third tranche announced in 2023**—fund size/commitments differ from disbursed investment.
- **2022 NPCI–Central Bank of Oman MoU** concerns digital-payment cooperation; an MoU alone does not establish universal RuPay/UPI merchant acceptance. [MEA historical bilateral account](https://www.mea.gov.in/Portal/ForeignRelation/Oman-Dec-23.pdf).
- Beyond the already-covered anticipated CEPA, the editorial proposes green hydrogen/renewables, strategic petroleum storage, critical minerals, defence production and offshore education campuses. These are possibilities at the issue date, not concluded operational outcomes.
- **IMEC** remains a broader connectivity discussion; Oman is **not an original signatory** to the September 2023 memorandum ([official MoU](https://www.mea.gov.in/Images/CPV/Project-Gateway-Multilateral-MOU.pdf)). The source's implication of settled formal corridor membership is misleading.

## 18 December 2025: CEPA signed
- India–Oman **CEPA signed at Muscat**, a new stage after the prior issue's anticipated signing. Comprehensive agreements can cover goods/services, investment, professional mobility and regulatory cooperation; **FTA is not inherently restricted to goods**.
- Oman offers **zero duty on 98.08% tariff lines, covering 99.38% of Indian export value**; India liberalises **77.79% lines/94.81% import value**. Tariff-line share differs from trade-value share. Labour-intensive exports—textiles, leather, footwear, gems/jewellery, engineering, pharma, medical devices, agriculture, plastics/furniture—gain access; sensitive exclusions/quotas protect specified Indian sectors.
- **Mode 4** commitments: intra-corporate transferee quota **20% → 50%**; contractual service suppliers' stay **90 days → two years**, possible additional two years. **Mode 4 is temporary natural-person mobility**, not permanent immigration. [Commerce Ministry signing release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2205889&lang=1&reg=1).
- **Signing ≠ entry into force**; the source's “will now enter duty free” should be read as agreement commitments subject to implementation, origin rules and schedules. Oman is India's second GCC bilateral trade partner after UAE CEPA; Oman–US **2006** agreement is historical.
- Source **FY2024–25 exports US$4.06 billion/imports about US$6.5 billion**, total about **US$10.61 billion**, already consistent with earlier bilateral notes. Gateway/logistics opportunities toward the Gulf/Africa depend on real routes/rules; signing does not give automatic preferential access to all GCC, Central Asian or Eastern European markets.
- Embedded awards graphic notes PM Modi's **Order of Oman, 18 December 2025**, and Ethiopia's **Great Honour Nishan of Ethiopia, 16 December**. These are foreign civilian honours, not Indian gallantry decorations; the collage's running count is an issue-era graphic, not a permanent total. Other listed 2025 honours (Sri Lanka, Mauritius, Cyprus, Ghana, Trinidad and Tobago, Brazil, Namibia) are background rather than new December events.
