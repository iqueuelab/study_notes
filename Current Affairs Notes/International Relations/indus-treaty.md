# Indus Waters Treaty and dispute settlement

## Allocation: January 2025 treaty context
- Signed **19 September 1960, Karachi**: **Jawaharlal Nehru / Ayub Khan**, with World Bank facilitation after extended negotiations.
| Eastern rivers: India | Western rivers: Pakistan, subject to permitted Indian uses |
| --- | --- |
| **Ravi, Beas, Sutlej** | **Indus, Jhelum, Chenab** |
- Allocation concerns water-use rights, not transfer of territorial sovereignty over entire rivers. India may undertake specified domestic/non-consumptive/agricultural uses and hydropower/storage under annexure conditions.
- Source approximations **80% / 19.48%** relate to aggregate flow, not an invariant volumetric quota; **3.6 million acre-feet** permitted Indian storage is subject to categories/conditions.
- Run-of-river projects are permitted within design constraints; advance project information/notices matter. **Kishenganga: Jhelum tributary; Ratle: Chenab**.
## Article IX: questions, differences, disputes
- **Permanent Indus Commission**, one commissioner each, first examines questions.
- Specified technical **differences → Neutral Expert** under Annexure F; qualifying **disputes → negotiation/arbitration** under Annexure G. Not every case mechanically passes through three appellate courts; jurisdiction depends on subject and treaty procedure.
- World Bank has specified appointment responsibilities, **not general authority to decide every water dispute**. [Treaty text](https://www.mea.gov.in/bilateral-documents.htm?dtl%2F6439%2FIndus=&outputType=chromeless).
## Dated procedure
- Pakistan requested Neutral Expert **2015**, sought arbitration **2016**; India sought Expert. World Bank paused parallel appointments **2016**, resumed both processes **2022**. [World Bank account](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2022/04/06/world-bank-resumes-processes-under-indus-waters-treaty).
- Source's **90-day modification notice / 62-year wording** refers to **January 2023**, not a newly issued January 2025 notice. **Article XII(3)** modification requires a **duly ratified treaty agreed by both governments**; notice alone does not amend it.
- January 2025 news: Neutral Expert decision **7 January**, public release **20 January**, on **competence** over technical issues; jurisdiction decision differs from final merits ruling. [PCA release](https://pca-cpa.org/en/news/pca-press-release-pca-case-no-2023-14-neutral-expert-issues-decision-on-certain-issues-pertaining-to-the-competence-of-the-neutral-expert/).
- Keep this January treaty account separate from subsequent diplomatic/legal developments.
## Pahalgam and bilateral escalation: 23–24 April 2025
- **22 April:** Pahalgam terrorist attack killed **26 people**; the clipping attributes the attackers to Pakistan-linked Lashkar-e-Taiba militants. Attribution is an allegation in this dated report, not a completed judicial finding.
- **23 April:** India announced that the **Indus Waters Treaty would be held in abeyance**, linking restoration to Pakistan credibly and irrevocably abandoning support for cross-border terrorism. The clipping's generic “maximising use” account misses this announced change; abeyance does not mean engineering works instantly stop all western-river flows. [Government account](https://newindiasamachar.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/flipbook/2025/Aug/2nd/English/files/basic-html/page10.html).
- **24 April, Pakistan's announced response:** closure of its airspace to Indian-owned/operated airlines; suspension of trade, including third-country transit; Wagah border closure with a limited return window; cancellation of SAARC Visa Exemption Scheme privileges for Indians, excluding Sikh religious pilgrims; expulsion of Indian defence/naval/air advisers by **30 April**; mission strength reduced to **30** from **30 April** (source previous strength 55).
- Pakistan asserted a right to hold bilateral agreements, including **Simla Agreement**, in abeyance and labelled stopping/diverting treaty waters an “act of war”. These are Pakistan's stated positions, not an adjudication of treaty law. [Pakistan statement transmitted to the UN](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n25/106/44/pdf/n2510644.pdf).
- Source reports the **Kartarpur pilgrimage corridor remained open** at this snapshot. Ordinary border transit, SAARC visa privileges and a dedicated religious corridor are separate arrangements. Cross-LoC trade had already been suspended by India in **2019**; avoid describing every trade restriction as newly introduced in April.
- **Simla Agreement, 1972**, followed the 1971 war and commits to peaceful bilateral settlement; **Attari–Wagah** is a major land gateway. Avoid treating its closure as proof no other historic transit route ever existed.
- Effects: longer aviation routes and costs; disrupted family/pilgrim contacts; fewer diplomatic channels; water-security rhetoric and escalation risks; further strain on SAARC/regional integration. The clipping's approximate **$2-billion trade** figure lacks a year and trade definition and should not be used as an established annual baseline.
- Mains approach: counter-terrorism and national security → proportionate diplomatic/economic measures → transboundary-water law and humanitarian interests → crisis communication and verifiable de-escalation. Treaty allocation and permitted Indian uses remain distinct from the parties' April legal positions.

## 2 May 2025: abeyance and international-law debate
- India's **23 April** announcement used “held in abeyance”; distinguish the announced position from a mutually agreed treaty termination. **Article XII(3)** modification and **XII(4)** termination envisage a duly ratified treaty agreed by both governments; political terminology alone does not settle legality.
- **1969 Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (VCLT): Article 60** material breach; **Article 62** fundamental change of circumstances. India is not a party; Pakistan signed but did not ratify. Relevant customary-law principles may apply independently of membership; the Convention cannot simply be assumed directly binding in its entirety.
- Article 62 requires an unforeseen change affecting an essential basis of consent and radically transforming outstanding obligations, with exceptions. The **1997 Gabčíkovo–Nagymaros (Hungary/Slovakia)** judgment rejected the claimed changes as grounds for treaty termination; treaty stability demands a high threshold. It was about Danube dam obligations, not a ruling on the 2025 India–Pakistan dispute. [ICJ case](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/92).
- A terrorism allegation does not automatically prove a material breach of this water treaty. Suspension temporarily interrupts obligations; termination ends them. Legal arguments over India's measure remain contested, without a final judgment established by this source.
- Western rivers are allocated to Pakistan **subject to permitted Indian uses**, including specified domestic/agricultural, non-consumptive, hydroelectric and storage uses. They are not Pakistan's exclusive territorial property.
- Infrastructure limits prevent instant diversion of all western-river water. Timing uncertainty, altered operations and abrupt releases could affect irrigation/hydropower/flood risk; source **80% agriculture/one-third electricity** figures are approximate dependency claims with differing denominators, not measured losses already caused.
- Treaty-specific commission/expert/arbitration processes are distinct from an ICJ case; **ICJ contentious jurisdiction requires a valid consent basis**, so Pakistan cannot automatically obtain it merely by filing. Evaluate security, treaty obligations, engineering capability and downstream humanitarian effects together.
