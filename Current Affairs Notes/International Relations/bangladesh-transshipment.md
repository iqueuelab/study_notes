# Bangladesh cargo transshipment through India

## 8 April 2025 withdrawal
- **CBIC Circular 13/2025-Customs**, dated **8 April 2025**, rescinded **Circular 29/2020-Customs of 29 June 2020**, which enabled Bangladesh export cargo destined for third countries to move through Indian land customs stations to ports/airports.
- Cargo already entered India under the facility could complete its exit under the earlier procedure. Distinguish this logistics permission from tariff preferences or a general ban on Bangladesh exports. [CBIC circular](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/view-pdf/1003276/ENG/Circulars).
- **Important scope correction:** withdrawal of this port/airport transshipment facility did **not** end Bangladesh exports to **Nepal or Bhutan transiting India**. The issue's claim that those countries necessarily lose their transit access conflates separate arrangements; MEA clarified this on 9 April. [MEA clarification reported on 9 April](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/diplomacy/news/3rd-country-export-india-revokes-transshipment-bangladesh-3867491).

## Economic explanation and implications
- Government explanation concerned congestion at Indian ports/airports, delays, rising costs and backlogs affecting Indian exporters. The issue also cites apparel exporters' complaints, including an attributed **20–30 Bangladesh trucks daily at Delhi air cargo complex**; retain it as an industry claim, not an audited official traffic count.
- Possible Indian gains: more cargo capacity, less congestion and improved export competitiveness. These are expected effects, not established measured outcomes.
- Bangladesh exporters using this facility may need costlier or slower alternative routes; assess actual destination, mode, capacity and contracts before estimating losses.
- India–Bangladesh relations extend beyond this measure: tariff preferences, bilateral trade, border management and regional connectivity. The issue reports **$12.9 billion FY2023–24 bilateral trade**; do not mistake trade value for the value of cargo covered by the cancelled facility.

## Strategic evaluation
- The source links the decision to strains in bilateral relations and Bangladesh–China contacts near the **Siliguri Corridor**. These are attributed geopolitical interpretations; the circular does not establish them as its legal or official rationale.
- Balance domestic logistics needs with predictable neighbourhood connectivity: consult stakeholders, improve capacity and explore clearly regulated bilateral/BBIN arrangements. **BBIN = Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal**; naming the grouping does not establish that all contemplated transport arrangements are operational.
