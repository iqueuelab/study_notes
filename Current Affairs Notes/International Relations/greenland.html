International Relations

Greenland and Arctic geopolitics

Editable notes

Geography and political status

  • World's largest island; between Arctic and North Atlantic, roughly 80% ice-covered, population about 57,000, predominantly Inuit. Arctic climate and ice-sheet loss make it important for global sea levels, ecosystems and infrastructure. Kaffeklubben is associated with the northernmost permanent land; small shifting/disputed discoveries make an unqualified “northernmost land” label risky.
  • Danish colonisation began in the eighteenth century; Home Rule 1979, expanded Self-Government 2009 within the Kingdom of Denmark. Greenland governs many domestic fields and mineral resources; Denmark retains central responsibility for defence/foreign affairs and currency, with Greenland's participation in relevant external affairs. The source overstates immigration as an already devolved domestic power: the Act permits future transfers in specified fields, which must be distinguished from powers actually assumed. Danish Foreign Ministry.
  • Greenland left the EEC in 1985; Danish-national Greenlanders have EU citizenship despite the territory being outside the EU. Indigenous self-determination and the historic involuntary contraceptive/IUD scandal inform independence debates.

Strategic resources and US interest

  • Critical minerals, including rare earths/nickel/cobalt, matter for batteries, renewables, electronics and defence; deposits are not the same as economical operating mines. Harsh conditions, infrastructure, investment and environmental/social consent constrain extraction.
  • The source's blanket ban on new mining is wrong: 2021 uranium restrictions affect qualifying deposits; Greenland also halted future oil exploration. Easier physical access from warming does not override these policy limits. Greenland oil decision, US State Department mineral-policy account.
  • Historical US wartime presence and 1946 $100 million purchase offer frame later interest; Trump's 2019 purchase proposal was rejected. Pituffik Space Base, renamed from Thule on 6 April 2023, supports missile warning and space surveillance; retain the current name. US Space Force.
  • April issue highlights Greenland's rejection of being treated as property amid renewed US interest. Strategic competition involves US/NATO, Russia's Arctic military posture and China's 2018 Arctic policy / “near-Arctic state” designation and investment ambitions.

Governance and risks

  • Melting sea ice can open Arctic routes; Northern Sea Route lies along Russia, not Greenland. Shorter seasonal voyages do not remove ice hazards, costs or geopolitical risks. Greenland's land-ice melt has distinct sea-level implications.
  • A purchase proposal alone is not automatically a UN Charter violation; coercion/use of force and denial of self-determination raise different legal issues. Greenland is not a global common, and neither are all adjacent waters: territorial sovereignty, EEZ/continental-shelf resource rights and high-seas freedoms differ under UNCLOS. UN overview.
  • Arctic Council: forum of eight Arctic states including Denmark (with Greenland/Faroes), focused on environment/sustainable development; not a military-security alliance. Priorities: Inuit participation, sovereignty/self-determination, cooperative environmental governance, careful resource development and allied diplomacy. Annexation pressure can strain NATO cooperation; ecological damage and great-power rivalry are risks, not inevitable outcomes.
