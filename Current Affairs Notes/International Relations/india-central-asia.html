International Relations

India and the Central Asian republics

Editable notes

Region and June 2025 dialogue

  • Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan: landlocked region linking Russia, China and Afghanistan; historical/cultural links, security and energy interests support India's engagement.
  • 4th India–Central Asia Dialogue, New Delhi, 6 June 2025, chaired by India's External Affairs Minister; accompanying Business Council discussions sought wider commercial cooperation. Connectivity, digital/fintech, financial links and partnerships were policy priorities, not all completed projects. MEA joint statement.
  • SCO/RATS supports regional security dialogue; membership and cooperation differ across the five republics. Avoid implying every CAR is an identical SCO full member or that SCO is an Indian-controlled security alliance.

Energy, trade and connectivity

  • Kazakhstan uranium ties, oil/gas resources and possible long-term energy agreements diversify supply. TAPI—Turkmenistan–Afghanistan–Pakistan–India is a pipeline project, not gas already flowing to India; security, finance and transit conditions matter.
  • INSTC /Chabahar seek alternative access around direct overland constraints; individual railway/port links have distinct operational and construction stages. Lack of direct geographic access, border friction, NTBs and transport costs inhibit pharmaceuticals/IT and other trade.
  • Source ~US$2 billion annual India trade and China–CAR US$89.4 billion in 2023 need comparable periods/trade coverage; the disparity illustrates competition, not proof every CAR rejects Indian investment.

Security and diplomacy

  • Afghanistan instability, terrorism/extremism and 2022 Kazakhstan unrest shape risk. Partnership should respond to CAR priorities and sovereignty rather than reduce all relations to countering China/Russia.
  • Dustlik—Uzbekistan; Khanjar—Kyrgyzstan, training and intelligence cooperation; cooperation does not automatically authorise cross-border joint combat operations.
  • PDF's unqualified “India's military base in Tajikistan” overstates public evidence: India historically helped renovate Ayni and maintained training cooperation; historical assistance is not proof of a continuously operating sovereign Indian overseas airbase in June 2025. MEA historical clarification.
  • Education, scholarships, cultural centres and people-to-people links complement logistics/energy/defence. Proposals: practical corridor completion, investment facilitation, resilient supply chains, sustained training/counter-terror engagement and cultural exchange; prioritise feasible milestones and diverse partners.
