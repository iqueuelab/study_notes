International Relations

Extradition and international criminal cooperation

Editable notes

Framework and institutions

  • Extradition is formal surrender of a fugitive for prosecution or sentence enforcement under applicable law/treaty; India's principal law is the Extradition Act, 1962. Bilateral treaties, notified arrangements and relevant convention provisions support cooperation; arrangements are not necessarily informal ad hoc understandings.
  • MEA is India's Central Authority; its CPV Division is the nodal point. Investigating agencies/state police assemble the request and evidence; foreign authorities apply their domestic judicial/executive procedure. Incoming cases generally involve magistrate inquiry and Central Government decision under the Act. MHA's role in criminal mutual legal assistance should not be substituted for MEA's extradition authority. MEA process

Principles and distinctions

  • Dual criminality: conduct must satisfy criminality requirements in both jurisdictions. Specialty: prosecution is ordinarily confined to offences for which surrender was authorised, subject to treaty exceptions/consent.
  • Political-offence exclusions, limitation, double jeopardy, nationality and evidentiary requirements vary by treaty. Terrorism need not qualify as a protected political offence.
  • Fair trial, torture and capital-punishment risks may require judicial assessment and binding assurances. Death penalty is not a universal absolute bar; Abu Salem's surrender from Portugal illustrates assurance-based restrictions.
  • INTERPOL Red Notice ≠ international arrest warrant; domestic law determines arrest. Deportation concerns immigration removal; MLAT assists evidence/service/investigation; neither is identical to extradition.

Dated case context

  • 10 April 2025: NIA secured Tahawwur Rana's extradition from the US in the 2008 Mumbai attacks case; surrender does not itself establish conviction in the Indian proceedings. NIA release
  • Mallya/Nirav Modi cases show prolonged judicial/diplomatic procedures. The source's Choksi-in-Antigua account was outdated: April 2025 reporting placed him under arrest in Belgium; do not treat arrest as completed extradition.
  • Priorities: complete admissible evidence, treaty-specific requests, reliable human-rights assurances, translation/case tracking and diplomatic cooperation. Avoid unsupported treaty-network counts or claims that a proposed statutory revision is already law.

October 2025: prison standards and stronger case preparation

  • At the CBI's 16 October conference, “Extradition of Fugitives: Challenges and Strategies”, Amit Shah urged States to establish special prisons meeting international standards and expert special cells for extradition case preparation. These are directions/proposals for capacity and assurances, not proof every State has already built a facility. MHA speech.
  • Better CBI–IB–MEA–State police/immigration/passport coordination, financial-footprint tracing, a fugitive database and international police cooperation can improve location and admissible evidence. The issue describes a proposed joint CBI–IB task force under MAC and a campaign moving suitable Blue Notice cases towards Red Notices; legal prerequisites must be satisfied rather than notices converting automatically.
  • Blue Notices seek additional identity/location/activity information; Red Notices request location and provisional arrest pending extradition/similar legal action, and remain not international arrest warrants. Passport cancellation needs competent action under applicable law; issuance of a notice does not itself automatically cancel a passport.
  • Humane detention, sanitation, medical care and fair trial are substantive obligations, not merely “excuses” to dismiss. Nelson Mandela Rules provide minimum prisoner-treatment standards; credible assurances support foreign judicial review but do not guarantee surrender. Source's 47 treaties/10 arrangements is a dated, unverified network count, and its Choksi-in-Antigua reference is outdated against the existing Belgium correction.
  • BHARATPOL links Indian agencies with INTERPOL channels; its newspaper explainer is background, not a new October launch. Extradition, deportation, evidence assistance and asset confiscation under the Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018 remain distinct legal processes.
