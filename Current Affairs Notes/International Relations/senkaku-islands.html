Senkaku Islands: East China Sea territorial dispute

Editable notes

November 2025: coast-guard tensions and map recall

  • Source clipping reports Chinese Coast Guard vessels near Japan-administered Senkaku Islands, illustrating recurring maritime friction. East China Sea, northeast of Taiwan and near Japan's Yaeyama/Ryukyu chain; distinguish from the South China Sea.
  • Names: Senkaku (Japan), Diaoyu (China), Diaoyutai (Taiwan), Pinnacle Islands. Japan administers the group within Ishigaki, Okinawa Prefecture; China and Taiwan contest sovereignty. A description of administrative control does not settle territorial title.
  • Group: Uotsuri (largest), Kuba, Taisho, Kitakojima, Minamikojima, plus rocks Tobise, Okinokitaiwa, Okinominamiiwa; total land area approximately 6 sq km, with source Uotsuri area around 3.6 sq km. Source's parenthetical equivalence of Taisho with Kumeakashima is unreliable and omitted. Japanese MOFA island list.
  • Rich fishing grounds, shipping proximity, potential hydrocarbons and strategic position near Taiwan explain significance; potential oil/gas is not a proven commercially recoverable reserve. Source geology includes sedimentary/volcanic material and uplifted coral; exact formation chronology is not established by that summary.
  • Japan's position: incorporation in 1895, later US administration, return of administrative control in 1972, and no unresolved sovereignty issue. China/Taiwan's positions: earlier historical use/title and wartime/post-war restitution arguments. Present these as rival claims, not neutral findings by a court.
  • United States distinguishes sovereignty neutrality from recognition of Japanese administration; it affirms treaty defence coverage under the US–Japan Security Treaty. Coast-guard encounters create escalation risks without proving automatic war.
  • 2022 Prelims: (b)—China and Japan engage in maritime disputes over these islands in the East China Sea; artificial-island, permanent-US-base and ICJ-no-man's-land alternatives are incorrect.
