# Moldova, Transnistria and energy security

## Map and conflict
- **Moldova**: landlocked Eastern Europe, **Romania west; Ukraine north/east/south**.
- **Transnistria**: breakaway territory mainly along the eastern/left bank of the **Dniester/Nistru**, bordering Ukraine; de facto administration does not equal internationally recognised independence.
- Proclaimed separatist republic in **1990**, not the PDF's 1991 claim; armed conflict and ceasefire in **1992**. Russia supports the region, but support is not recognition of statehood. [OSCE conflict history](https://moldova.osce.org/sites/default/files/f/documents/4/3/42308.pdf).
## January 2025 energy shock
- Russian gas supplies to Transnistria stopped on **1 January 2025**, as Ukraine transit arrangements expired; heating and gas-fired electricity were disrupted.
- The Moldova-wide effect included loss of electricity previously supplied by the Transnistrian power plant, but **Moldova proper had diversified supplies**. On 1–2 January, government reported electricity needs met through domestic production and Romanian imports. [Moldovan government](https://gov.md/en/press-releases/russia-scales-regional-security-risks-halting-gas-supplies-transnistrian-region).
- Do not reduce the crisis to 'all Moldova still buys all gas from Russia'; separate gas, electricity, the two banks and the reporting date.
## Diplomacy and Mains links
- India–Moldova diplomatic relations began in **1992**; the PDF cites a newly opened Moldovan embassy in New Delhi as diversification of external ties.
- Multiple passports among residents do not establish internationally recognised 'Transnistrian nationality'.
- Analyse energy dependence, geopolitical coercion, sovereignty, de facto control and winter humanitarian risk; diversification, interconnections and contingency planning increase resilience.
