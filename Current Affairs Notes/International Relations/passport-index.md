# Henley Passport Index and mobility

## Methodology
- **Henley & Partners**, began **2006** as Visa Restrictions Index; based on **IATA Timatic** data and additional research.
- Source scope: **199 passports, 227 destinations**; score measures destinations accessible **without a prior visa**, not passport holders' wealth, democracy or military power.
- Distinguish ordinary visa-free access, visas on arrival and relevant electronic authorisations from **prior-approved visas/e-visas**; immigration checks still apply. A 'destination' can be a territory, not necessarily a sovereign country. [Methodology explanation](https://www.henleyglobal.com/publications/global-mobility-report/2025-january/henley-passport-index-visa-free-travel-defined).
## January 2025 snapshot
- Source **India 85th, 57 destinations**, from a cited earlier **80th**; multiple updates during a year mean different 2024/2025 releases can show different ranks.
- **Singapore 1st, 195; Japan 2nd, 193**. Source **UAE 10th** and China's longer-term **94th → 60th** are dated comparisons, not current figures. [Publisher's January release](https://www.henleyglobal.com/newsroom/press-releases/henley-global-mobility-report-january-2025%C2%A0).
- Visa rules change, and ranking depends on other passports too: a rank change does not by itself establish a proportionate change in diplomacy or geopolitical influence.
- Pandemic restrictions affected mobility after **2020**; source's 'COVID impact from 2015' is chronologically wrong.
- Mains use: mobility inequality, diaspora/business travel, bilateral visa facilitation and regional cooperation; travel ease is one indicator, not a complete measure of national power.

## 22 July 2025 Henley release
- **India 77th, 59 destinations**, up from **85th/57 in January 2025**, an eight-place rise despite only two additional destinations. The PDF's “85th in 2024” misdates the baseline. **Singapore 1st/193; Japan and South Korea joint 2nd/190; seven EU passports joint 3rd/189**. [Publisher's July release](https://www.henleyglobal.com/newsroom/press-releases/henley-global-mobility-report-july-2025).
- Source names Philippines/Sri Lanka as recent access additions and Malaysia, Indonesia, Maldives, Thailand as examples; entry conditions, visit purpose and prior-authorisation rules still require destination-specific checks. Rankings measure destinations accessible without a prior visa, not unconditional entry rights.
