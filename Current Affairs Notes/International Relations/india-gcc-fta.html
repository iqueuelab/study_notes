India–GCC free trade negotiations

Editable notes

February 2026: negotiating framework

  • 5 February 2026, New Delhi: India and the GCC signed Terms of Reference, defining the scope and modalities of proposed FTA negotiations. This is a negotiating milestone; tariff concessions and an operative FTA had not been established by the ToR. The issue's “revived in 2025” shorthand is incorrect for this signing. Commerce Ministry.
  • GCC members: Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and UAE; Iran is not a member (2016 PYQ). Earlier rounds took place in 2006 and 2008, followed by a prolonged pause.

Economic logic

  • FY2024–25 trade about US$179 billion and nearly 10 million Indians resident in the bloc are dated relationship snapshots. Food, engineering, machinery, chemicals, textiles and services complement Gulf energy, petrochemicals, capital and infrastructure demand.
  • Potential gains: predictable market access, investment, skilled-service opportunities, food/energy cooperation and supply diversification. Actual benefits depend on final concessions, origin rules, services commitments, safeguards and implementation; an FTA does not guarantee fixed oil prices or permanent immigration rights.
  • Existing bilateral agreements differ from a bloc-wide pact: UAE CEPA effective May 2022; Oman CEPA signed December 2025, with entry into force a separate stage. These do not automatically extend identical preferences across all GCC members.
  • The source's ToR practice question tests a useful distinction: defining negotiations does not mean tariff negotiations are complete. It is practice material, not a UPSC PYQ.
