International Relations

UN peacekeeping: principles, mandates and India

Editable notes

Mandates and core principles

  • Military, police and civilian personnel support peace processes; Security Council establishes mandates, with UN organs/member States providing administration, finance and personnel. Peacekeeping is not expressly named in the Charter; practice draws on Charter powers, including Chapters VI/VII as relevant. Capstone Doctrine articulates operational principles.
  • Consent of main parties; impartiality; non-use of force except self-defence/defence of mandate. Impartiality means even-handed mandate enforcement, not neutrality/inactivity towards violations; robust authorised action differs from unrestricted warfighting. UN principles.
  • Tasks can include ceasefire monitoring, DDR, civilian protection, police/rule-of-law support, human rights, electoral assistance and confidence building; exact authority/resources vary by mandate.
  • UNTSO, 1948, first operation, Middle East. Source's 11 active operations in May 2025 is consistent with the Berlin ministerial account. Examples: UNMISS—South Sudan; MINUSCA—CAR; MONUSCO—DR Congo; UNIFIL—Lebanon; UNMIK—Kosovo. MINUSMA—Mali closed 31 December 2023, so it was not an active May 2025 mission. Mission closure.

Berlin ministerial: 13–14 May 2025

  • Ministerial involved over 130 member States/international partners; 74 States made pledges, including capabilities and support. UN account: over 61,000 military/police plus over 7,000 civilians across 11 operations. Pledges are not proof every unit already deployed. UN outcome.
  • Embedded clipping reports India's proposed quick-reaction company, male CRPF formed-police unit, women-led formed-police unit, counter-explosive/IED-disposal unit, dog unit and SWAT police unit, plus training partnerships/courses for observers, commanders and uniformed personnel. These are offered capacities pending implementation/confirmation; India–US training of African peacekeepers is a partnership example.
  • Clipping reports 5,375 Indians /fourth-largest contributor, whereas accompanying prose says 5,424 across nine missions. Rankings/headcounts require an exact monthly snapshot and personnel scope; do not merge these into one audited figure. 0.16% assessed-budget share is inadequately dated in the PDF, distinct from troop contribution and not a secure current rate.

India, achievements and limitations

  • Indian contribution began with a medical unit in Korea, 1950; distinguish medical support, later neutral-repatriation responsibilities and combat formations. Women serve in UNISFA, Abyei; source reports 150 Indian UNMISS personnel receiving service medals, not necessarily individual gallantry “medals of honour”.
  • Major Radhika Sen, MONUSCO in DRC (March 2023–April 2024), received 2023 Military Gender Advocate of the Year award in 2024. Award year and presentation year differ. UN award account.
  • UN peacekeepers received Nobel Peace Prize 1988; operations supported transitions in Namibia, Cambodia, Mozambique, El Salvador and Tajikistan.
  • Failures: Rwanda genocide 1994 killed around 800,000, mainly Tutsi and also moderate Hutu; Srebrenica 1995, over 8,000 Bosniak men/boys. Limited mandates, resources, political will and civilian-protection failures matter; not all Rwanda victims were Tutsi.
  • Gaza/Ukraine deployment proposals face mandate, Council politics, consent and feasibility barriers; no existing peacekeeping force there can be assumed merely from the UN's general peace role.
  • Mains: achievable mandates, political settlements, trained/equipped contingents, women’s participation, civilian protection, accountability and clear exit strategies; military deployment alone cannot solve political conflict.

October 2025: troop-contributing countries and reformed multilateralism

  • India hosted the UNTCC Chiefs’ Conclave, 14–16 October, Manekshaw Centre, New Delhi. At its opening Rajnath Singh advocated Consultation, Cooperation, Coordination and Capacity Building—the 4Cs—for safer and more effective missions. Greater troop-contributor input, specialised training, logistics and adequate resources should accompany realistic mandates. MoD opening address.
  • Secure communications, surveillance and unmanned platforms can improve protection against asymmetric threats and misinformation; technology needs trained personnel and accountable use. Reformed multilateralism seeks institutions representative of contemporary realities and developing-country voices, while upholding international rules.
  • The source's over 2.9 lakh cumulative Indian personnel across 50+ missions is a historical contribution measure, distinct from current deployed strength. Its 179 fatalities is inadequately dated and should not be taken as the definitive October total. India's all-women formed police unit served in Liberia from 2007.
  • G4 reform advocacy links India, Brazil, Germany and Japan; a demand for permanent membership does not itself change Security Council composition. The PDF's inference naming particular states as the targets of the minister's criticism is commentary, not an official identification.
