International Relations

India–US partnership: February 2025 agenda

Editable notes

Relationship and dated developments

  • Trump became 47th US President on 20 January 2025; Modi's Washington visit 13 February 2025. India's “natural ally” rhetoric is not a mutual-defence treaty.
  • COMPACT: Catalyzing Opportunities for Military Partnership, Accelerated Commerce & Technology; TRUST technology initiative; intent to expand bilateral trade to US$500 billion by 2030, and negotiate a first trade-agreement tranche. Targets/negotiations, not achieved outcomes. Joint statement.
  • Defence cooperation includes proposed procurement/production, autonomous systems and logistics; US statements about offering advanced fighters do not establish an Indian F-35 order.
  • STA-1 eligibility, 2018: facilitates specified licensed-exception exports; not blanket access to every controlled technology. Quad revived 2017 /INDOPACOM renamed 2018 are separate institutional developments.

Trade, technology and tensions

  • Defence, digital/critical technology, space, energy, education and diaspora deepen ties; tariff reciprocity, market access, immigration and strategic differences can create friction.
  • Trade-partner and FDI figures must distinguish fiscal/calendar years, goods/services and investment stock versus annual inflow; source's undifferentiated US$51.6-billion figure should not be used as one year's FDI.
  • Tariff threats and an “External Revenue Service” proposal are not proof a new statutory agency or all levies had taken effect in February.
  • India's 2025 Budget was already presented on 1 February; the PDF's “upcoming Budget” is stale. Market/currency predictions remain forecasts.
  • Mains approach: expand reciprocal, transparent cooperation while preserving strategic autonomy, data/technology safeguards and diversified partnerships.

Defence technology and autonomy

  • Engine delays and export/licensing constraints illustrate dependence on critical foreign subsystems; assess co-production, IP access, spares, supply-chain diversification and lifecycle support.
  • Partnership does not require abandoning independent decisions on other partners. SIPRI import-volume shares cannot be interpreted as shares of India's entire defence budget.

February 2025 agenda: additional details

  • The meeting discussed in the April issue was an official working visit on 13 February 2025, rather than a fresh April state visit.
  • TRUST = Transforming the Relationship Utilizing Strategic Technology: defence, AI, semiconductors, quantum, biotechnology and space; resilient supply chains include critical minerals and active pharmaceutical ingredients. Strategic Mineral Recovery seeks recovery from heavy-industry streams such as aluminium, coal and oil/gas.
  • February defence intentions included a new 10-year framework, pursuing additional P-8I aircraft, Javelin/Stryker procurement and co-production, and review of arms-transfer rules. These were prospective commitments in this issue; the F-35 discussion established no Indian purchase contract.
  • ASIA = Autonomous Systems Industry Alliance: Anduril–Mahindra maritime/counter-drone cooperation and L3Harris–BEL towed-array sonar exemplify industry partnerships. Expanded Tiger Triumph supports tri-service interoperability.
  • First BTA tranche was targeted for fall 2025; market access, tariff/non-tariff barriers and supply chains were negotiation objectives. Civil-nuclear law changes were proposed, not laws already amended. Invitation to a 2025 Quad summit did not establish a completed visit.
  • Source's approximately US$100-billion US trade deficit claim confuses trade totals and balances; retain period-specific goods/services data rather than this figure. Quad is a four-country partnership, not a treaty military alliance.
  • Revision linkage: defence interoperability → technology access → co-production and resilient supply chains; manage export licensing, data rules, Russia/CAATSA differences and humane migration procedures while preserving strategic autonomy.

February joint statement.

June 2025: tariffs and cooperation recall

  • US steel/aluminium Section 232 tariffs rose from 25% to 50%, effective 4 June 2025 (with specified exceptions). This is a sectoral tariff development during BTA negotiations, not proof negotiations had produced an agreement. US proclamation.
  • Source calendar-2024 goods figures US$129.2bn total, $87.4bn Indian exports, $41.8bn imports imply about $45.6bn surplus using rounded numbers; its $45.7bn is inconsistent at this precision. Source's $65.19bn US FDI during 2000–24 is cumulative, not one year's flow; ranking depends on period/definition.
  • Defence recall: GSOMIA 2002 classified-information security; LEMOA 2016 logistics; COMCASA 2018 secure communications; BECA 2020 geospatial cooperation. 2005 civil-nuclear announcement differs from later agreements/implementation.
  • Vajra Prahar special-forces/Army, SALVEX naval salvage/diving, Cope India air, and Malabar multilateral naval exercises. I2U2 = India–Israel–UAE–US; IPEF and ASEAN-centred forums offer cooperation without making India/the US ASEAN members.
  • Diaspora, research/education, trade and supply chains support ties; IP/data rules, visas, defence licensing and market access require continued management. Source's 3.5-million diaspora count depends on definition/reference date.

September 2025: alignment pressure and tariff friction

  • The issue reports Trump's “lost India to China” remarks and US pressure over India's Russian oil purchases and BRICS/SCO engagement. Trump was the serving US President, not the “former President” described in the coaching text.
  • Source's 50% tariff headline describes the combined announced US pressure on covered Indian goods; it must not imply every Indian product faced an identical tariff without exceptions. US criticism and a social-media photograph do not establish an Indian alliance with China.
  • India's strategic autonomy entails interest-based engagement with the US/Quad, Russia and China-centred forums while managing border rivalry, defence/energy needs and diversified technology/trade ties. BRICS/SCO membership does not require abandonment of the US partnership.
  • Source reports MEA rejection of Peter Navarro's oil-trade remarks and Jaishankar's engagement with Ukraine; distinguish attributable official statements from anonymous commentary. Claims about Trump skipping a future Quad summit were reports/speculation at this issue date.
  • Mains: tariff exposure and political rhetoric can strain trust and Quad cooperation; maintain diplomatic channels and diversified export/energy options without assuming binary geopolitical blocs.

September 2025: possible renewal of trade talks

  • The embedded report describes positive Trump–Modi messages and prospects for resuming trade negotiations after tariff/Russian-oil tensions; a direct conversation in coming weeks and timing of renewed talks were prospective, not a concluded trade pact.
  • India's outreach included Ambassador Vinay Kwatra, US lawmakers and administration contacts; Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal stressed reciprocity. Diplomacy can keep negotiations open while disagreements over energy choices and market access persist.
  • The bilateral aim remained a first trade-agreement tranche by fall 2025. “Fall” should not be redefined as the end of the calendar year; an aim is not a guaranteed deadline. A possible Quad visit was also unconfirmed in this issue.
  • Existing defence/space cooperation provides reasons to stabilise ties, but the issue's “unaffected” label is too categorical. 50% referred to covered goods under combined US measures, with exceptions; the source's August oil imports near 2 million barrels/day are a dated estimate, not a legal requirement or present flow.
