# Indian Ocean blue economy: resilience and shared growth

## December 2025: cooperation through ocean stewardship
- Editorial advocates Indian Ocean leadership combining **commons stewardship, climate resilience and inclusive growth**. Green shipping, responsible aquaculture, offshore renewables and marine biotechnology need healthy ecosystems and equitable local benefits, rather than growth measured only by extraction or vessel numbers.
- **UNCLOS** differentiates sovereign resource rights and **the Area's common-heritage regime**; it does not classify every ocean resource or all high seas as one ownership category. **SAGAR, 2015—Security and Growth for All in the Region**, links security/development; environmental cooperation complements maritime awareness and disaster response.
- Coral loss, warming/acidification, sea-level rise, cyclones and IUU fishing threaten livelihoods and resilience. Observation, early warning, restoration, sustainable fisheries and technology access for African coastal/island States can reduce vulnerability. The ‘one-third of world population’ regional count needs a defined Indian Ocean membership/geographical scope.
- Proposed **Regional Resilience and Ocean Innovation Hub** and **Indian Ocean Blue Fund**, seeded by India with development-bank/private/philanthropic finance, are **editorial recommendations**, not proof newly established Indian institutions/funds exist.
- Source cites Monaco **2025 Blue Economy and Finance Forum: €25bn pipeline/€8.7bn commitments**, development-bank ocean coalition **US$7.5bn/year**, and **One Ocean Partnership US$20bn by 2030**. These refer to different pledges/pipelines/targets, not money already received by an Indian fund; avoid adding them as realised finance.
- **Nice UN Ocean Conference 2025, COP30 Belém and BBNJ** provide governance context. The article's early Indian BBNJ ratification request is a proposal at the issue date; a reference to **2026** is prospective. Finance should translate into transparent regional projects, community benefits, safeguards and measurable resilience; ocean-based carbon-removal proposals require scientific/environmental scrutiny.
