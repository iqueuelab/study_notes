# India–Africa digital cooperation and public infrastructure

## Africa Day and development partnership
- **Africa Day, 25 May:** OAU founded **1963**; African Union’s **Digital Transformation Strategy 2020–2030** advances inclusive digital services/governance and capacity. [AU strategy](https://www.au.int/en/documents/20200518/digital-transformation-strategy-africa-2020-2030).
- Editorial proposes a new **India–Africa digital compact**, complementing lines of credit, infrastructure, scholarships and technical training with co-development/social enterprises. It is a recommendation, not a new continent-wide agreement already concluded.
- Indian examples: **Aadhaar** identity, **UPI** payments, **CoWIN** vaccination and **DIKSHA** education. DPI includes technology, institutions, standards and safeguards; it is incorrect to assume every component of every named Indian system is open-source merely because some reusable platforms are.

## Conditions for inclusion and sovereignty
- Expensive devices/data, rural and gender access gaps, unreliable power and limited skills impede uptake. Reliable energy/connectivity, local-language access, training and sustainable operations accompany software.
- Open standards and adaptable platforms can reduce vendor lock-in and enable local control; they do not automatically eliminate surveillance, privacy or exclusion risks. Consent/legal safeguards, cybersecurity, accessible offline alternatives and public accountability matter.
- Co-design with African institutions, interoperability and durable local capacity can strengthen healthcare, education, payments and governance. Avoid portraying all African countries as one market or all competing models as necessarily surveillance-heavy.
