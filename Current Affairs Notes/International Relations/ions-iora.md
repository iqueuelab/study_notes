# IONS and IORA: Indian Ocean cooperation

## Naval cooperation and regional institutions
- **IONS** is a voluntary naval cooperation initiative among Indian Ocean littoral states. Inaugural symposium: **2008, New Delhi**, under Indian Navy leadership; not 2015. It encourages trust, professional exchanges, information sharing, maritime safety/security and humanitarian assistance/disaster relief; it is not a collective-defence alliance.
- Rotating chairmanship, Chiefs' conclaves and working groups support cooperation. The **October 2025 Indian Navy announcement** described **25 members and 9 observers** and anticipated India's next chairmanship; in this January issue, keep the planned handover prospective. The PDF's **34/36 “littoral members”** conflates members, observers and regional geography. [Navy announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2184765&lang=2&reg=3).
- Regional divisions used for participation: South Asian; West Asian; East African; South-East Asian/Australian littorals. France participates through its Indian Ocean territories. **Observer status is distinct from full membership**, and “all observers are non-littoral” is incorrect—Madagascar is an Indian Ocean island state.
- **IORA**, formerly IOR-ARC, is an **intergovernmental association founded in 1997**, with Secretariat at **Ebène, Mauritius** and Council of Ministers as apex body. Its agenda includes trade/investment, fisheries, disaster risk reduction, tourism, academic/scientific exchange and blue economy, extending beyond naval security. India assumed its **2025–27 chair on 12 November 2025**. [Australian government overview](https://www.dfat.gov.au/international-relations/regional-architecture/indian-ocean/indian-ocean-rim-association).
- **2017 PYQ: IONS statement 2 only (b)**; **2015 PYQ: IOR-ARC neither statement (d)**. Both are already in the common bank: IORA was not newly created in response to piracy/oil spills, nor is it a security-only alliance.
### Geography and India's interests
- Indian Ocean: third-largest ocean; connects the Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal, Andaman Sea, Red Sea and Persian Gulf with major commercial routes. **Hormuz, Bab-el-Mandeb, Malacca and Suez** are critical passages; fisheries, offshore hydrocarbons and mineral resources support coastal economies.
- India's interests combine trade, energy routes, diaspora safety and disaster response. **SAGAR /MAHASAGAR**, UNCLOS, maritime awareness, exercises (**MILAN, SIMBEX, AIKEYME**), coordinated patrols and first-response capacity connect security with development. These platforms have separate membership/mandates; leadership does not create a single Indian-controlled regional institution.
- Source's approximate shares of oil/cargo/container flows use unclear denominators; retain the strategic dependence, not an interchangeable table of percentages. Its **7,500km coastline** is the legacy measurement, not a newly verified 2026 coastal survey figure.
