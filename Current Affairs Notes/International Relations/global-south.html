International Relations

Global South and India’s development diplomacy

Editable notes

Meaning and common concerns

  • Global South is a broad political/development category, mainly countries in Africa, Asia and Latin America, rather than a literal southern-hemisphere division or a homogeneous bloc. Shared concerns include colonial legacies, poverty/inequality, commodity dependence, access to technology and unequal influence in global institutions.
  • Global North broadly refers to richer industrialised economies, including Australia and New Zealand despite their latitude. The source associates Third World with Alfred Sauvy (1952) and the modern Global South formulation with Carl Oglesby (1969); the Cold War term initially related to bloc alignment.
  • Growing influence of India, China and Brazil supports multipolarity, but national interests differ. Comparisons of BRICS/G7 economic size require a PPP versus nominal GDP distinction; a prediction about the four largest economies in 2030 is not an observed fact.
  • Priorities: climate finance and fair transition, food/fuel security after the Ukraine war, debt sustainability, fairer trade, development finance and institutional reform. BRI infrastructure can offer connectivity while creating project-specific debt/governance risks; a universal debt-trap claim is not established.

India’s initiatives

  • At the January 2023 Voice of Global South Summit, India announced a Centre of Excellence, Science and Technology Initiative, Aarogya Maitri disaster/health support, Young Diplomats Forum and Global South scholarships.
  • These combine knowledge sharing, capacity building, health solidarity and educational/diplomatic networks. Climate-fund inadequacy is an equity concern; use the correct named fund rather than the source's generic “Green Energy Fund”.

September 2025: UNGA-side coordination

  • India hosted a high-level meeting of 20 like-minded Global South countries in New York on the sidelines of the 80th UNGA. The source groups participants as nine Asian, six African and five American countries. Jaishankar called for collective action as multilateral institutions faced funding, effectiveness and representation pressures. MEA speech listing.
  • Practical agenda: UN/UNSC reform, resilient food/fertiliser/energy supply chains, South–South trade, development finance and fair climate cooperation. Shared exposure to pandemic/war/climate shocks does not erase different national interests.
  • Existing notes cover North/South geography, PPP-versus-nominal GDP and 2023 initiatives. The issue's assertion of summits in 2023, 2024 and 2025 does not establish a new full Voice of Global South Summit in 2025: the first two were January and November 2023, the third August 2024. This UNGA-side meeting is a distinct format.
  • Printed UPPSC PCS 2024 question is a State PCS question, excluded from the UPSC CSE PYQ banks; its answer is only statement 1 in its historical context, not proof the second summit just occurred in September 2025.

South–South and triangular cooperation: September 2025

  • South–South cooperation (SSC): developing countries exchange knowledge, technology, policy and skills. Triangular cooperation: a partner/multilateral body supplies finance, technical or institutional support to a Southern partnership; not every project must have both a Northern state and UN agency. Complements North–South assistance.
  • Buenos Aires Plan of Action, 1978, anchors technical cooperation; UN Day, 12 September, commemorates its adoption. 2025 theme: New Opportunities and Innovation through South-South and Triangular Cooperation. Principles include solidarity, equality, mutual respect/benefit, national ownership and shared learning. UN 2025 concept note.
  • Practical areas: agriculture/food security, health, climate resilience, digital infrastructure, decent work/social protection and policy capacity. UNOSSC coordinates; FAO supports agrifood exchange, UNDP technology/institutional learning (eVIN vaccine logistics from India to Indonesia), ILO labour-policy/capacity exchange.
  • India's ITEC builds capacity in source-reported 160+ countries; India–UN Development Partnership Fund, 2017, supports developing partners, particularly LDCs/SIDS. Source 75+ projects/56 countries is a dated reported portfolio, not a permanent count. MEA Development Partnership Administration and African Union's G20 membership complement summit diplomacy already covered.
  • India–WFP collaboration: Annapurti grain ATMs, PDS logistics optimisation, fortified rice and women-led take-home rations provide possible adaptation models; local nutrition, infrastructure and governance needs still determine suitability. Digital public infrastructure likewise needs consent, privacy and institutional capacity, not mechanical copying.
  • Source reports WFP US$10.9 million mobilised in 2024, and a UN SSC fund supported by 47 governments reaching 70 countries/155 indirectly. Preserve these as article-specific dated claims; its recap changes countries to projects, so do not combine those units.
  • Mains argument: locally adaptable solutions and additional finance can help SDGs, including food security and climate action. Strong institutions, sustained finance, measurement, accountability and private/civil-society participation are needed; cost-effectiveness and self-reliance are aims, not automatic results. Source's 250-word/15-mark SSTC question is explicitly UPSC-style practice, not a dated PYQ.
