BBNJ agreement and high-seas biodiversity

Editable notes

Position as of June 2025

  • Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction (BBNJ) agreement under UNCLOS: marine genetic resources/benefit sharing; area-based management tools including MPAs; environmental impact assessments; capacity building/marine technology transfer.
  • India signed September 2024; June issue reports ratification still pending domestic legal preparations, including biodiversity legislation. A predicted parliamentary timeline is not a completed ratification or a verified session calendar.
  • Source reports 49 ratifications as of 10 June. Entry into force requires 120 days after deposit of the 60th instrument, not immediate operation on the 60th ratification. This note records the June status, not later treaty developments. UN agreement overview.
  • Equity concerns: access to marine genetic resources, sharing benefits, scientific/financial capacity and protection of common ocean ecosystems. Areas beyond national jurisdiction include high-seas water and relevant seabed areas; water-column and continental-shelf rights are not identical.

UNCLOS diagram/PYQ recall

  • Territorial sea: up to 12 nautical miles from baselines (Article 3); all States' ships enjoy innocent passage (Article 17) subject to applicable rules. Contiguous zone: up to 24 nm; EEZ: up to 200 nm from baselines (Article 57), sovereign rights over resources rather than full territorial sovereignty. Source's “from coastline” is simplified.
  • 2022 PYQ: all three statements correct → (d). Continental shelf can legally extend beyond 200 nm under qualifying criteria; do not treat the diagram as a universal shelf limit.

UN Ocean Conference, Nice: Indian initiatives

  • Samudrayaan: human-occupied submersible target 6,000 m, expected deployment/trial in 2026 as described in June, not achieved descent. SAHAV digital ocean-data portal; source explicitly invents its acronym expansion, so retain the name only.
  • India highlighted marine conservation, selected single-use-plastic restrictions, port development and fisheries. US$80bn port projects is not a single unrestricted blue-economy fund. Earth Sciences Ministry, 10 June.

November 2025: entry-into-force threshold and implementation

  • Correct source September 2024: the 60th ratification threshold was reached 19 September 2025; as of this issue the Agreement was set to enter into force 17 January 2026, 120 days later. Adoption was 19 June 2023, distinct from ratification and entry into force. UN dated October 2025 record.
  • Existing notes retain four pillars: MGRs/benefit sharing, ABMTs/MPAs, EIAs, capacity building/technology transfer. Implementation concerns: equitable access to marine research, monitoring/enforcement finance, data sharing, developing-country capabilities and coordination with ISA and RFMOs rather than undermining their mandates.
  • Common heritage of humankind and freedoms of marine scientific research/high seas are both recognised principles. High-seas freedoms are subject to international obligations, not permission for unregulated exploitation. UNCLOS Part XI common heritage for the Area/seabed resources is not a universal identical ownership regime for Antarctica, all outer space and all biological material.
  • Reject the source's claim that the treaty specifies no benefit-sharing institution: Article 15 establishes an access and benefit-sharing committee; detailed operational arrangements require COP decisions. “30×30” is a wider biodiversity target, not an automatic designation of 30% of oceans by this treaty. Official Agreement text.
  • High-seas water and the seabed/continental shelf have distinct legal rules; beyond 200 nm from the coastline is an oversimplification of ABNJ. Do not turn the source's US/China/Russia non-ratification snapshot into an undated present statement.
