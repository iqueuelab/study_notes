# Arms transfers: diversification and supplier dependence

## Changing South Asian procurement
- India has diversified historically substantial USSR/Russian procurement towards France, Israel/US and domestic manufacture; Pakistan increasingly depends on China, with Turkish UAVs alongside Chinese air systems. Source cites Pechora/OSA-AK, SkyStriker and PL-15 in recent operations; procurement origin is distinct from independent verification of battlefield performance.
- Embedded charts concern particular **decades/categories**; never mix shares of air-power transfers, all arms imports, recipient imports and exporter destinations. Source's Russian 75%, Pakistan–China 95%, US global 65%, China<2% lack stable common periods and should not replace verified 2020–24 figures.
- **SIPRI 2020–24:** Russia **36%** of India's major-arms imports (55% 2015–19,72% 2010–14); China **81%** of Pakistan's imports. Global export shares **US 43%, Russia 7.8%, China 5.9%**. SIPRI major-arms transfer-volume measures are not total defence spending or invoice values. [SIPRI 2024 transfer report](https://www.sipri.org/publications/2025/sipri-fact-sheets/trends-international-arms-transfers-2024).
- France/Rafale, US/GE-F414 and Israeli drone/surveillance cooperation illustrate diversification; agreements and technology-transfer ambitions are not completed domestic production. Legacy Russian equipment still needs spares/maintenance; new import shares do not equal installed-inventory shares.
- Strategic implications: supply resilience, interoperability, quality, lifecycle costs, sanctions/export controls and strategic autonomy. Ukraine-related Russian constraints are one factor, not the sole cause of all historical shifts. Indigenous capability/defence diplomacy complements diversified supply.
- Undated practice answer supplied **B(2 only)**; statement 3's unbounded air-power claim and supporting percentages need category/year clarification, so retain as practice with caveat, not official PYQ.
