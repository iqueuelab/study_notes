# Chabahar: connectivity, sanctions and strategic autonomy

## Map and India role
- **Chabahar, Iran's Gulf of Oman coast**, outside the Strait of Hormuz bottleneck; **Shahid Beheshti** terminal is India's operational focus. **Gwadar, Pakistan** is a distinct port linked with CPEC.
- India engagement from **2003**; **Zaranj–Delaram road, 2009**, links onward Afghan access. Sea–road/rail connectivity bypasses Pakistan and supports Afghanistan/Central Asia trade and humanitarian supplies.
- **May 2024 ten-year port-operation contract** is a relevant background milestone; potential INSTC linkage needs completed onward infrastructure and commercial interoperability, not just a line on a map.
## February 2025 sanctions context
- **US NSPM-2, 4 February 2025**, directed modification/rescission of waivers including Chabahar-related relief under renewed Iran pressure. It was a policy direction and risk, **not evidence all port activity became illegal worldwide on that day**. [Original memorandum](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/02/national-security-presidential-memorandum-nspm-2/).
- **2018 exemption** linked to Afghanistan's development/humanitarian interests; waivers are limited and can change, not permanent immunity from all sanctions.
- Modi's upcoming US visit framed dialogue; Biden-era policy is background, not the February administration.
## Mains balance
- Interests: resilient routes, Afghan assistance, Central Asian access and autonomous diplomacy; constraints: US sanctions exposure, banking/insurance, regional conflict, security, financing and incomplete links.
- Seek clear transaction-specific compliance/waiver arrangements, phased commercial viability, diversified corridors and regional consultation. Describe Chabahar–Gwadar competition without reducing every economic partnership to an anti-China alliance.

## September 2025: withdrawal of sanctions exception
- **16 September 2025:** US State Department announced revocation of the **2018 IFCA exception** for Afghanistan reconstruction/economic development, **effective 29 September 2025**. The announcement and future effective date must remain distinct. [Indian parliamentary confirmation](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/270/AU723_MQYlMP.pdf?source=pqars).
- Raises sanctions, banking, insurance and investment risks for India's Shahid Beheshti terminal operations and Afghanistan/Central Asian connectivity. It does not establish an immediate physical closure or a worldwide legal prohibition on every port transaction.
- The issue reports **₹200 crore invested of ₹400 crore planned** and a **43% vessel-traffic rise in 2023–24**: dated measures, not the complete lifetime financial commitment or present throughput. The ten-year 2024 operating agreement is already covered; it should not be mislabelled sovereignty over the port.
- Responses discussed: negotiate clear relief, protect humanitarian connectivity, phase investment and diversify corridors. China–Iran cooperation and India–US tensions are context; the source's $400-billion China–Iran claim is a prospective framework figure, not verified disbursed investment.
- PYQ recall: **2017 Chabahar—(c), access to Afghanistan/Central Asia without Pakistan; 2016 BRI—(d), China**. Silk Road Economic Belt is the land component; Maritime Silk Road the sea component.

## October 2025: six-month sanctions exemption
- The embedded newspaper headline reports India secured a **six-month US sanctions exemption** for Chabahar, announced by MEA on **30 October 2025**. This new development qualifies the typed notes, which repeat September's withdrawal as though no subsequent relief occurred. [Contemporaneous MEA announcement reporting](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/india-six-month-us-sanctions-waiver-chabahar-port-trump-10335924/).
- Temporary relief supports continuity of Shahid Beheshti terminal operations and Afghan/Central Asian connectivity while leaving renewal, transaction-specific compliance, banking and insurance uncertainty. It does not create permanent immunity or establish that all September risks have disappeared.
- Geography, 2024 ten-year agreement, ₹200/₹400 crore source investment and 43% historical vessel-traffic figure repeat earlier coverage. **2017 Chabahar (c)** and **2016 BRI (d)** are already in the common Prelim bank; retain their existing full options.

## December 2025: first Coast Guard port call
- **ICGS Sarthak**, an **offshore patrol vessel**, visited **Chabahar, Iran, 16–19 December 2025**, the **first Indian Coast Guard ship visit to this port**. This is distinct from India's earlier commercial terminal operations and Navy visits elsewhere. [Defence Ministry, 17 December](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2205444&lang=1&reg=3).
- Planned professional exchanges with Iranian maritime agencies address **search and rescue, maritime law enforcement, marine pollution, MRCC coordination and VBSS**; port calls support maritime cooperation and Indian connectivity interests toward Afghanistan/Central Asia. The visit does not itself alter sanctions exemptions or complete onward transport infrastructure.

## January 2026: conditional waiver and continued talks
- **16 January MEA briefing**, reproduced in the embedded clipping, said the US Treasury's **28 October 2025** guidance provided a conditional sanctions waiver **valid until 26 April 2026**, and that India remained engaged with the US on arrangements and Iran on Chabahar. This is a time-limited position at the issue date, not a permanent exemption or confirmation India had abandoned the port. [Recorded spokesperson statement](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QhcAZu5eRf0).
- The typed September-2025 revocation narrative omits this subsequent relief; read it alongside the October waiver and January clarification. The source's reports about possible alternative Indian operational arrangements were not an official announcement of a new structure.
- Existing map, Shahid Beheshti terminal, 2024 ten-year operating contract and Pakistan-bypassing Afghan/Central Asian access remain relevant. Banking, insurance, renewal risk and onward connectivity constrain commercial continuity; strategic importance alone does not remove transaction-specific sanctions exposure. Both printed PYQs are already banked.

## February 2026: equipment commitment fulfilled
- The issue reports the government's **6 February parliamentary reply** that India had fulfilled its **US$120 million equipment-procurement commitment**, under the **13 May 2024 ten-year IPGL–Iran Ports and Maritime Organisation contract** for Shahid Beheshti terminal. Distinguish this contribution from a loan, full lifetime project cost or sovereignty over the port.
- Meeting a financial commitment does not establish uninterrupted operation: banking, insurance, sanctions renewal and onward infrastructure remain material constraints. Absence of a new annual budget provision alone does not prove an official withdrawal announcement.
- Read the repeated September 2025 sanctions-withdrawal material alongside the later conditional waiver **valid until 26 April 2026**, already recorded in January notes. It is misleading to present September's change as if no subsequent relief occurred.
- Chabahar provides a Pakistan-bypassing route toward Afghanistan/Central Asia; it is not literally India's only possible route to the region. The 2017 Chabahar and 2016 BRI questions repeat the common bank.
