International Relations

US travel restrictions and vetting proposals

Editable notes

March 2025 proposal

  • Reports described a draft covering 41 countries: possible full restrictions, partial restrictions and conditional compliance reviews. A reported draft is not an operative travel ban.
  • Afghanistan, Iran, Syria, Cuba and North Korea appeared among proposed full-restriction examples; Myanmar and Haiti among partial examples; Pakistan among conditional-review examples.
  • The January 2025 vetting direction, 2017 executive orders and the 2018 Trump v. Hawaii judgment concern different instruments and periods.
  • Assess security screening alongside family separation, refugee protection, non-discrimination and diplomatic effects. Predictions of retaliation or economic loss are not already observed outcomes.

June 4 proclamation: draft versus adopted measure

  • PDF juxtaposes a March 2025 reported 41-country draft with the new 4 June 2025 proclamation. Its “2024 proposal” table label is wrong; the March memo was not the June final list.
  • June measure, effective 9 June 2025, 12:01 a.m. EDT: full entry restrictions for Afghanistan, Myanmar/Burma, Chad, Republic of the Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Haiti, Iran, Libya, Somalia, Sudan, Yemen; partial restrictions for Burundi, Cuba, Laos, Sierra Leone, Togo, Turkmenistan, Venezuela.
  • Scope generally covers designated nationals outside the US and without a valid visa at the effective date, with exceptions/waivers including permanent residents, qualifying dual nationals, official categories and specified family/sporting/SIV cases. Partial restrictions target immigrant and specified visitor/student/exchange categories, rather than an identical ban on every visa. White House proclamation.
  • Older draft breakdown 10 full /5 partial /26 conditional, including proposed 60-day vetting reviews, is not a completed June rule for all 41. Pakistan/Syria/North Korea examples in that draft must not be inserted into the June 19-country list.
  • Claimed justification: identity/vetting gaps, information sharing, security and overstays; these are administration rationales, not a conclusion that every affected citizen is dangerous.
  • 2017 seven-country orders, subsequent revised proclamation and 2018 Trump v. Hawaii judgment have separate dates/scope; earlier judicial approval is not automatic adjudication of the June 2025 instrument.
  • Mains: security screening versus proportionality, families/refugees/students/business mobility and diplomatic reciprocity; possible challenges or retaliation are scenarios, not already established results.
