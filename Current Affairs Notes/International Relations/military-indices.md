## Spending and risks: dated facts
- **2023 world spending US$2,443 bn**, **+6.8% real**; India **US$83.6 bn, fourth spender, +4.2%**. [SIPRI 2023 release](https://www.sipri.org/media/press-release/2024/global-military-spending-surges-amid-war-rising-tensions-and-insecurity).
- Source **over 60 conflicts** and **civilian deaths +30%** lacks a consistent underlying dataset/year definition; distinguish number of conflicts, state-based conflicts and civilian fatalities. “Highest in history” is not established by the generic claim.
- Global Risks Report treats armed conflict as a major near-term concern; perceived risk differs from a military-capability rank.
- Mains: spending, equipment readiness, training/jointness, logistics, geography, technology and partnerships all matter; numerical manpower or spending alone cannot settle strategic outcomes.
## SIPRI 2024 expenditure: released April 2025
- India **US$86.1 billion**, **fifth**, **+1.6% real** from 2023; Pakistan **$10.2 billion**, making India's total roughly **8.4 times**, rather than precisely nine times, as large.
- Top five: **USA, China, Russia, Germany, India**, together **60%** of world spending. USA **$997 billion** (source's $916 billion is an older figure); China **$314 billion**, **+7%**, not continuous double-digit growth.
- World total **$2,718 billion**, **+9.4% real**. Russia **+38%**, **7.1% GDP**; Ukraine military burden **34% GDP**, the highest, and expenditure about **43%** of Russia's. [SIPRI release](https://www.sipri.org/media/press-release/2025/unprecedented-rise-global-military-expenditure-european-and-middle-east-spending-surges), [fact sheet](https://www.sipri.org/publications/2025/sipri-fact-sheets/trends-world-military-expenditure-2024).
- SIPRI: independent Stockholm research institute established **1966**, studying conflict, armaments, arms control and disarmament.
- India's China/Pakistan border challenges support preparedness, infrastructure, naval/cyber capability and modernisation. Spending totals do not measure readiness or guarantee deterrence.
- Evaluate personnel/modernisation balance, import dependence, indigenous R&D and manufacturing, UP/Tamil Nadu defence corridors, procurement efficiency and health/education opportunity costs. The source's Defence Production Policy 2020 reference should be read as the **draft DPEPP 2020**, not proof of an operative final policy.
