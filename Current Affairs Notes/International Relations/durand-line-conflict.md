# Durand Line: Afghanistan–Pakistan conflict

## Boundary and escalation
- The **1893 Durand agreement** between British India's representative **Mortimer Durand** and Afghan Amir **Abdur Rahman Khan** delineated a boundary inherited by Pakistan in 1947. The roughly **2,640-km** frontier cuts across Pashtun communities; Afghan authorities have long contested its legitimacy while Pakistan treats it as the international boundary.
- The issue reports Pakistani strikes in Kabul/Kandahar and border provinces, Afghan attacks on Pakistani posts and Pakistan's **Operation Ghazab lil-Haq**, a marked escalation beyond routine skirmishes. Pakistan's “open war” statement is political language; do not infer a formal war declaration or independently verified battlefield totals from it.
- Pakistan accuses **Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP)** of using Afghan safe havens; Kabul contests responsibility. TTP and the Afghan Taliban are distinct organisations, despite links. **Islamic State–Khorasan** is another, hostile militant actor; it should not be conflated with either.
- **UNAMA's 3 March statement** recorded at least **42 civilians killed and 104 injured inside Afghanistan, late 26 February–2 March**; these are verified period/location-specific civilian figures, not total military casualties on both sides. It urged a halt, civilian protection and humanitarian access. [UNAMA statement](https://unama.unmissions.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/3_march_2026_-_unama_urges_halt_in_afghanistan-pakistan_clashes_english.pdf).
- **Torkham** is a major eastern Afghan transit crossing; border closures, refugee returns/deportations and reliance on Pakistani ports worsen economic and social strain. Military asymmetry favours Pakistan but does not guarantee a quick political settlement. Mediation, counterterrorism assurances and civilian/trade access matter for India and regional stability; outside-power intervention is a risk scenario, not an established event.
