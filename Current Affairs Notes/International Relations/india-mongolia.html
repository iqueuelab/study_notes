India–Mongolia relations and military exercises

Editable notes

Nomadic Elephant 17: underway inJune 2025

  • India–Mongolia annual bilateral Army exercise, alternating hosts; 17 th edition 31 May–13 June 2025,Ulaanbaatar SpecialForcesTrainingCentre; 16 th at Umroi, Meghalaya, July 2024. Source's “scheduled” misses the accompanying clipping showing it already underway. The claimed 2006 origin is source background requiring historical verification.
  • 45 Indian personnel, primarily Arunachal Scouts, with Mongolian 150 Special Forces unit (a unit designation, not necessarily 150 participants); joint semi-conventional operations in semi-urban/mountain terrain, interoperability, operational tactics, cultural understanding. Defence Ministry 29 May.
  • UN Chapter VII concerns Security Council action for threats/breaches of peace/aggression; exercise under a UN-mandate scenario is not itself an authorised UN deployment. Khaan Quest is a separate multinational peacekeeping exercise hosted in Mongolia; do not equate bilateral training with all UN peacekeeping law.

Mongolia map and economic links

  • Landlocked between Russia (north) and China (south/east/west); capital Ulaanbaatar, plateau/high-altitude continental setting. Altai, Khangai, Khentii mountain systems; Orkhon River / UNESCO Orkhon Valley cultural landscape; Lake Khuvsgul major freshwater lake. “Younger sister of Baikal” is a nickname, not hydrological classification.
  • Coal, copper, gold, fluorspar are major resource sectors; lithium prospects do not by themselves establish Mongolia as a leading commercial lithium producer.
  • Source reports trial coking-coal sourcing by India to diversify steelmaking inputs beyond suppliers such as Australia. Plans/trials are not proof of major established deliveries or eliminated dependence; landlocked transport via neighbours affects cost/feasibility. Coking coal makes coke for conventional blast-furnace steelmaking, not every steel-production route.

Khaan Quest 2025: scheduled 14–28 June

  • Indian contingent arrived Ulaanbaatar on 11 June for the 22nd multinational peacekeeping exercise, scheduled 14–28 June 2025; 40 personnel, mainly Kumaon Regiment, including one woman officer and two women soldiers.
  • Origin US–Mongolia bilateral 2003, multinational since 2006. Trains multinational interoperability/readiness for UN peace support, including checkpoints, cordon/search, patrols, civilian evacuation, counter-IED and combat first aid. This is separate from Nomadic Elephant. Defence Ministry.
  • Peacekeeping training is not itself deployment authorisation. Mongolia's defence engagement supports India’s wider Asian partnerships; Mongolia is normally located in East Asia, despite broader Central Asian strategic linkages.

October 2025: border-force training and Buddhist diplomacy

  • During President Khurelsukh Ukhnaa's India visit, PM Modi announced a forthcoming capacity-building programme for Mongolia's border security forces; India had appointed a defence attaché at its embassy. These complement military training and Nomadic Elephant, rather than establish an alliance or a completed deployment.
  • 1955 diplomatic ties; 2015 Strategic Partnership: the October visit marks 70 and 10 years respectively. Free e-visas for Mongolian citizens and dispatch of Sariputra and Maudgalyayana relics in 2026 were announced; ten signed MoUs covered immigration, humanitarian aid, geology/minerals, yoga and governance. The PM's statement confirms the training, attaché, visas and relic plans. PM statement, 14 October.
  • “Third neighbour” means diversification beyond Russia and China. Buddhist heritage, mineral/development cooperation and people-to-people links support India's Asian diplomacy. Reject the PDF's reversed claim that Mongolia was India's first non-socialist diplomatic partner, its unsupported 3rd-century-BCE Buddhism-via-Tibet chronology and its 2024 label for this presidential visit.

Mongolia: additional map and livelihood recall

  • Southern Gobi is a cold desert; temperate steppe and extreme continental climate support pastoralism. Dzud combines severe winter conditions with grazing/feed stress, threatening livestock; desertification and mineral-price exposure add livelihood risks. Mongolia is the second-largest landlocked country after Kazakhstan.
  • Ulaanbaatar on the Tuul River faces winter coal-heating pollution. Mongolian language uses Cyrillic alongside revived traditional script; ger/yurt is the portable pastoral dwelling. Tibetan Buddhism and shamanic traditions shape culture. Oyu Tolgoi is a major copper-gold mine; livestock, wool/cashmere and mining matter economically.
  • Historical recall: Genghis Khan's 13th-century empire; Kublai Khan's Yuan rule in China; later Qing rule; independence declared in 1911, revolution in 1921, democratic transition 1990 and Constitution 1992. These stages should not be collapsed into one independence date.
  • 2013 Prelims: (b), Central Asian Steppe—continental extremes, scanty rainfall and historic nomadic herding. Chernozem is associated with temperate grasslands but is not every Mongolian soil. The tundra distractor is not disproved by claiming herding never occurs there; reindeer pastoralism exists.
