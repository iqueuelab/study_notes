International Relations

FIDE and international chess

Editable notes

Organisation

  • Fédération Internationale des Échecs (FIDE), international chess governing body; founded 1924, Paris, headquarters Lausanne, Switzerland; motto Gens Una Sumus (We are one family).
  • Standardises rules, rankings/titles and organises the World Championship cycle, Olympiad, World Cup/Candidates; recognised by IOC in 1999. Source 199 national federations is a dated membership count, not a permanent total.

July 2025 Indian hosting announcement

  • FIDE announced India as 2025 World Cup host on 21 July; then scheduled 30 October–27 November, with 206 players, knockout format and qualification places for 2026 Candidates. Host-city selection was still pending in this source; do not import later Goa confirmation into the July announcement. FIDE July 21 release.
  • AICF is India's national federation; players including Anand, Praggnanandhaa and Gukesh illustrate India's competitive growth.
  • FIDE institutional profile supports the organisation, headquarters, motto and source-era membership details.

Divya Deshmukh: 28 July 2025 milestone

  • 19-year-old Divya Deshmukh, Nagpur, defeated Koneru Humpy in rapid tiebreaks at Batumi, Georgia, becoming the first Indian winner of the FIDE Women's World Cup, India's 88th Grandmaster and fourth Indian woman GM after Humpy, D. Harika and R. Vaishali. Both finalists qualified for the Women's Candidates. FIDE final report.
  • Her World Junior Girls title was 2024, not the PDF's 2023; its table also incorrectly dates the Women's World Cup win to 2024. She contributed to India's 2024 Budapest Olympiad women's team gold. FIDE profile.

Chess revision

  • Chaturanga developed in India around the sixth century: infantry, cavalry, elephants and chariots; transmission through Persia/Shatranj preceded European modern rules in the fifteenth–sixteenth centuries.
  • Checkmate: king attacked with no legal escape; stalemate: no legal move while king is not in check, hence draw. Elo measures competitive performance; GM and IM are FIDE titles, distinct from World Champion.
  • Anand: first Indian GM (1988), five-time World Champion; World Cup winner in 2000/2002 (older tournament format). Praggnanandhaa: 2023 World Cup finalist. Gukesh: youngest undisputed world champion, defeating Ding Liren in Singapore in 2024; he did not take the title directly from Carlsen.
  • India hosted the 44th Olympiad, Chennai 2022 and won both open and women's team gold in 2024. PDF's 'open bronze in 2020' is wrong: India shared online Olympiad gold in 2020; open bronze came in 2014/2022. Keep online and over-the-board events distinct.
