# Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam and the Nile dispute

## Inauguration: 9 September 2025
- **Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD)** on the **Blue Nile in Ethiopia**, near Sudan; construction began **2011**. Inaugurated **9 September 2025**, with about **5,150 MW** installed capacity and **74 billion cubic metres** reservoir storage; Africa's largest hydropower project. [Builder's dated account](https://www.webuildgroup.com/en/media/press-releases/grand-ethopian-renaissance-dam-gerd-inaugurated/).
- Source describes a dam around **170 m high /2 km long**, costing roughly **US$4 billion**. Dimensions vary with which dam structure is measured; do not combine them with other project estimates as exact engineering specifications.
## Upstream development versus downstream security
- Ethiopia seeks electricity access, industrialisation, fewer blackouts and regional power exports. The dam is also presented as a national-unity and African self-reliance symbol; potential electricity sales are not proof every planned cross-border line is operating.
- **Egypt**, heavily dependent on Nile water, fears filling/drought operation could threaten drinking water and irrigated agriculture. Its objection that unilateral action violates law is Egypt's position, not an adjudicated conclusion in these notes.
- **Sudan** may gain regulated flows, lower flood risk and electricity, while facing dam-safety and coordination concerns. Its interests should not be flattened into identical opposition to Egypt's.
- Hydropower is largely non-consumptive, but reservoir **filling, evaporation, drought releases and operating rules** still influence downstream timing/availability. Ethiopia's assurance of no significant harm does not eliminate these questions.
- The **Blue Nile** is a major seasonal contributor; the issue's unqualified “85% of Nile water” conflates the Blue Nile with wider Ethiopian-highland contributions and is not retained as a verified standalone share.
- Negotiations/mediation have involved the African Union and other actors without a comprehensive settlement at this issue date. Revision: equitable use, avoiding significant harm, transparent flow data, dam safety, binding drought/filling coordination and sustained basin diplomacy.
