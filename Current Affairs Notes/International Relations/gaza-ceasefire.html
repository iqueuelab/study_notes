Gaza ceasefire: January 2025 agreement

Editable notes

Background and casualty correction

  • 7 October 2023 Hamas-led attack, Israeli military response, hostage captivity and grave civilian humanitarian losses. Distinguish armed actors from entire civilian populations.
  • Source Israel over 1,200 deaths and about 100 remaining hostages, dated approximations.
  • Coaching's over 64,000 Gaza deaths is inconsistent with the January snapshot: OCHA cites Gaza MoH 46,645 reported fatalities as of 14 January 2025, with casualty verification qualifications; roughly 1.9 million displaced, 90% of Gaza. OCHA dated snapshot.

Three phases: agreement is not guaranteed completion

PhaseIntended steps
1: six weeksCeasefire; 33 hostages exchanged for Palestinian detainees/prisoners; Israeli pullback from populated areas; return of displaced people; aid target 600 trucks/day
2Remaining living hostages; further prisoner exchange; full withdrawal/permanent cessation negotiated
3Return of deceased hostages' remains and reconstruction
  • January 17 source describes pending Israeli Cabinet approval. Ceasefire began 19 January 2025; phase one lasted to 1 March. This does not establish all later phases implemented. UN response review.
  • Obstacles: sequencing, verification, prisoner/hostage categories, security conditions, domestic coalition pressures, access and reconstruction finance. Source's claim that war strengthened one politician is commentary, not a settled causal fact.
  • Mains: protect civilians, lawful humanitarian access, credible monitoring, return/reconstruction and negotiated durable political settlement.

Gaza geography and historical anchors

  • Coastal enclave of roughly 365 sq km: Mediterranean to the west, Israel to north/east and Egypt to south-west; densely populated and dependent on external supplies.
  • Egyptian administration followed the 1948 war; Israel captured Gaza in 1967. Oslo arrangements (1993–95) envisaged Palestinian self-government; Israel withdrew settlements in 2005; Hamas took control in 2007.
  • Withdrawal of settlements does not itself settle Gaza's international-law occupation status. Border control and humanitarian access vary during conflict; Rafah has Egyptian and Gaza sides, not one permanently unchanged controller.
  • Erez and Kerem Shalom are Israel-side crossings; Rafah is the Egypt-border crossing. Blockade, warfare and damaged water/health infrastructure affect civilians and livelihoods.
  • India supports a negotiated two-state solution, maintains ties with Israel and Palestine, and assists Palestinian civilians, including through UNRWA.

PYQ map recall

  • Yom Kippur War, 1973: Israel versus Arab forces led by Egypt and Syria.
  • Lebanon and Syria border the Mediterranean; Jordan and Iraq do not. Golan Heights belong to the Middle East context.
  • The supplied “two-state solution” question is from 2018, not the printed 2008 label.

Gaza revision: April 2025 complementary points

  • Gaza's Mediterranean climate, scarce freshwater, import/aid dependence and restricted fishing access compound the damage to health, power, sanitation and livelihoods. The source's unemployment “over 40%”, aid-dependence “over half” and hours-of-electricity figures are undated background, not reliable April wartime measurements.
  • Armed-group rocket attacks, tunnels and Israeli security concerns coexist with obligations to protect civilians and permit humanitarian relief. Earlier conflict episodes include 2008–09, 2012, 2014 and 2021; they do not replace the continuing post-October-2023 war context.
  • Responses vary: UN and humanitarian agencies emphasise aid access/civilian protection; Arab League/OIC support Palestinian rights; Western support for Israeli security coexists with calls for humanitarian access. Avoid implying every state within a group takes an identical position.
  • Sustainable settlement requires ceasefire monitoring, hostage release, humanitarian access, reconstruction, functioning health/education services and political negotiations; opening trade and employment can reduce dependence, but these are policy aims rather than achieved outcomes.
  • Map/PYQ recall: Jordan is landlocked and Iraq has a Persian Gulf coast; neither borders the Mediterranean. Lebanon and Syria do. Golan Heights is a Middle East location; Yom Kippur War (1973) involved Israel and Arab forces led by Egypt/Syria; the two-state solution concerns Israel–Palestine. The supplied two-state PYQ is 2018, correcting the printed 2008 label already flagged in these notes.
