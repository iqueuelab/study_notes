International Relations

India–Germany relations and strategic cooperation

Editable notes

Partnership timeline and priorities

  • Strategic partnership since 2000 →25th anniversary in 2025, not 2024. 1974 S&T intergovernmental agreement →50 years in 2024; keep the two anniversaries separate. 7th IGC 25 October 2024, New Delhi, co-chaired by Modi/Scholz; source May 2025 editorial reflects continued cooperation amid German leadership transition. Joint statement.
  • Innovation and Technology Partnership Roadmap covers renewable energy, startups, semiconductors, AI/quantum, climate risk/resource management/agroecology, with space cooperation potential. IGSTC funds collaborative research; WISER—Women in Science and Engineering Research supports participation. Source 49 priority projects is a dated programme count, not universal current total.
  • Green and Sustainable Development Partnership 2022: German €10 billion commitment through 2030, not all already spent; solar/renewables, green hydrogen, agroecology and urban mobility cooperation. ISA/CDRI engagement supports climate/disaster resilience.

Economic, people and security links

  • Germany is a major European trade/investment partner; source US$33.33 billion 2023 trade, US$14.5 billion FDI April 2000–December 2023/9th rank are historical snapshots, not May 2025 current shares. Verify goods/services and equity/total FDI definitions before comparisons.
  • Skills/student/migration links and India’s market support cooperation; Germany's China exposure motivates diversification, but “China+1” is not complete economic disengagement. Clipping notes roughly 2,000 German companies/750,000 jobs and 50,000 Indian students without common latest cutoff; treat as reported context.
  • Defence cooperation agreement 2006; naval/air exercises MILAN, PASSEX, TARANG SHAKTI and industrial dialogue. Project-75I interest concerns potential collaboration for six conventional submarines, not a concluded delivered deal. Cyber/counterterrorism and classified information/MLAT cooperation deepen institutional ties.

Challenges and options

  • EU CBAM exposure, tax/regulatory predictability, supply-chain risks, China ties and Ukraine-policy differences affect cooperation; source's human rights concerns require specific attributed evidence rather than a blanket indictment.
  • June 2024 Chinese EV tariff 38.1% was a provisional-era source figure, not a timeless final tariff. India–EU FTA was under negotiation at this issue; do not treat recommendation as completed agreement.
  • Priorities: conclude balanced trade arrangements, facilitate mobility/research, expand sustainable technology and Indo-Pacific cooperation, diversify semiconductor/pharma/EV supply chains while preserving strategic autonomy.
