# Global South and India’s development diplomacy

## Meaning and common concerns
- **Global South** is a broad political/development category, mainly countries in Africa, Asia and Latin America, rather than a literal southern-hemisphere division or a homogeneous bloc. Shared concerns include colonial legacies, poverty/inequality, commodity dependence, access to technology and unequal influence in global institutions.
- Global North broadly refers to richer industrialised economies, including Australia and New Zealand despite their latitude. The source associates **Third World** with Alfred Sauvy (1952) and the modern Global South formulation with Carl Oglesby (1969); the Cold War term initially related to bloc alignment.
- Growing influence of India, China and Brazil supports multipolarity, but national interests differ. Comparisons of BRICS/G7 economic size require a **PPP versus nominal GDP** distinction; a prediction about the four largest economies in 2030 is not an observed fact.
- Priorities: climate finance and fair transition, food/fuel security after the Ukraine war, debt sustainability, fairer trade, development finance and institutional reform. BRI infrastructure can offer connectivity while creating project-specific debt/governance risks; a universal debt-trap claim is not established.
## India’s initiatives
- At the **January 2023 Voice of Global South Summit**, India announced a Centre of Excellence, Science and Technology Initiative, **Aarogya Maitri** disaster/health support, Young Diplomats Forum and Global South scholarships.
- These combine knowledge sharing, capacity building, health solidarity and educational/diplomatic networks. Climate-fund inadequacy is an equity concern; use the correct named fund rather than the source's generic “Green Energy Fund”.
