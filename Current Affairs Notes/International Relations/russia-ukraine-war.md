# Russia–Ukraine war: territorial claims and escalation

## 8–9 June 2025: reported Dnipropetrovsk advance
- Source reports Russian Defence Ministry claim that tank forces reached the western Donetsk boundary and advanced towards/into **Dnipropetrovsk**. Treat as an attributed battlefield claim, not independently established final territorial control. June 2025 is the **fourth year since the 24 February 2022 full-scale invasion**, correcting PDF's “third year”.
- **Dnipro**, regional capital on Dnipro River; Dnipropetrovsk is central-eastern Ukraine, west of Donetsk, with mining/industrial/logistics importance. Source **~1 million city / ~3 million region pre-war** are approximate historical populations, not wartime census results.
- Industrial/economic pressure, supply-route disruption and bargaining effects are analytical possibilities; no proof every claimed military motive or future sanction/NATO response had materialised.
## Territory and international law
- Russia purported to annex **Crimea 2014; Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson, Zaporizhzhia 2022**; Dnipropetrovsk was outside those declared claims. Actual occupation, annexation claims and internationally recognised boundaries are distinct.
- **All these areas remain internationally recognised Ukrainian territory**. The UN Charter's prohibition on force/territorial acquisition does not apply only to areas Russia has not already claimed. **UNGA ES-11/4 (12 October 2022)** condemned attempted annexation of the four regions. [UN resolution account](https://press.un.org/en/2022/ga 12458.doc.htm).
- India calls for dialogue/diplomacy and respect for sovereignty/territorial integrity while maintaining Russia/Western relationships. Strategic autonomy does not validate occupation or mean every issue has the same voting/trade position.
- Mains: territorial integrity, civilian protection, industrial damage,energy/mineral supply and credible negotiations; distinguish conflict reporting from a final recognised change in borders.
