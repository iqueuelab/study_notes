# Iran, the NPT and nuclear verification

## Treaty framework and Iran
- **NPT** opened for signature **1 July1968**, entered into force **5 March1970**; pillars: non-proliferation, peaceful nuclear use and disarmament. Recognised nuclear-weapon states are the US, Russia, UK, France and China under its pre-1967 test definition. India, Pakistan and Israel never joined; North Korea announced withdrawal. [UN treaty account](https://legal.un.org/avl/ha/tnpt/tnpt.html).
- Iran signed in1968 under **Mohammad Reza Pahlavi**, remained a party after the **1979 revolution**; undeclared-site disclosures in **2002** and safeguards disputes led to inspections, negotiations and sanctions. Safeguards non-compliance must not be equated automatically with proof that Iran possessed a bomb.
- **JCPOA2015:** Iran and P5+1 (five permanent UNSC members plus Germany), alongside EU coordination, agreed enrichment/verification restrictions for sanctions relief. US withdrawal in **2018** and renewed sanctions preceded Iran exceeding agreement limits.
- June2025 attacks on Iranian nuclear facilities intensified debate over inspections, regional security and proliferation risks. Iran's assertion of peaceful intent and Israeli/US threat assessments are competing claims; enrichment capability and weaponisation are different.
- **Article X** permits withdrawal when extraordinary treaty-related events jeopardise supreme national interests, with **three months' notice** to all parties and UNSC explaining those events. Withdrawal does not retroactively erase breaches or automatically make weapon acquisition lawful under all other obligations. The PDF's 'leave and legally build a bomb' is unsound.
- Risks: weakened verification, regional arms competition and escalation; diplomatic verification and security assurances remain relevant alongside non-proliferation commitments.
