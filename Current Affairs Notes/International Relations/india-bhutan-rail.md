# India–Bhutan rail connectivity

## September 2025 railway agreement
- **29 September 2025, New Delhi:** Bhutan and India signed the railway-links MoU for **Kokrajhar (Assam)–Gelephu** and **Banarhat (West Bengal)–Samtse**. The issue's “2024” heading recalls the March 2024 precursor; it is not the date of this signing or proof trains already run. [Bhutan foreign ministry](https://www.mfa.gov.bt/press-release-709/).
- Proposed first cross-border rail links: source combined length **~89 km**, cost **₹4,033 crore**; Indian embassy rounds length to **~88 km**, so avoid false kilometre precision. Source project targets: four years for Kokrajhar–Gelephu, three for Banarhat–Samtse; latter **₹577 crore**. These are planned completion periods, not completed outcomes. [Indian embassy background](https://indembthimphu.gov.in/pages/NDEy).
- Source engineering details: larger line six stations, two viaducts, 29 major/65 minor bridges, two goods sheds, one flyover and 39 underpasses; smaller line two stations and 37 underpasses. Its “24 minor flyovers” likely confuses bridges with flyovers; retain for project-document review. Vande Bharat readiness is a design claim, not an announced operating service.
- Landlocked Bhutan gains connections to Indian ports/markets; lower freight costs, tourism, border livelihoods and industrial growth support India's neighbourhood policy. This is a connectivity/development rationale; it does not prove a new Chinese railway project exists.
- Wider cooperation: **₹10,000 crore** Indian support for Bhutan's **13th Five-Year Plan, 2024–29**; hydropower links include Chukha, Tala, Kurichhu, Mangdechhu and Punatsangchhu-II. Project completion, commissioning and power sale agreements require separate dates.
- **Jogighopa inland-waterway terminal inaugurated 18 February 2025**, on Brahmaputra/NW-2, correcting the issue's February 2024. Supports India–Bhutan–Bangladesh cargo connectivity. [Official inauguration](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2104393&lang=2&reg=48).
