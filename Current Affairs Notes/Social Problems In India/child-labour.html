Social Problems In India

Child labour and the Velpur model

Editable notes

June 12: burden and statistical correction

  • World Day Against Child Labour: 12 June, ILO; SDG 8.7 targeted ending child labour by 2025. ILO–UNICEF 2024 estimates, released 11 June 2025: about 138 million children (7.8% of ages 5–17), including 54 million in hazardous work; source's 160 million/one in ten belongs to 2020, not the latest estimate. ILO release.
  • India Census 2011 43.53 lakh refers to main workers ages 5–14, while roughly 10.1 million includes main and marginal workers. The PDF's infographic and prose use different definitions, not interchangeable counts. Labour Ministry clarification.
  • Poverty, parental debt, insecure informal livelihoods, inaccessible schooling and trafficking/bondage reinforce work in agriculture, beedi-making, carpets and fireworks.

Framework and governance

  • 1986 Act, amended 2016: broadly prohibits work below 14; limited family-enterprise/child-artist exceptions have conditions and education safeguards; adolescents 14–18 prohibited from hazardous occupations/processes. The PDF's unconditional “complete prohibition in every circumstance” omits exceptions. 2017 rules.
  • RTE 2009: ages 6–14; National Policy on Child Labour 1987 and historical NCLP (1988) used withdrawal/bridge schooling/rehabilitation. Do not assume NCLP remains a separate current scheme merely because the case study describes its earlier schools.
  • India ratified ILO C138 (Minimum Age, 1973) and C182 (Worst Forms, 1999) in 2017. Worst forms include slavery/trafficking, forced recruitment for conflict, sexual exploitation, illicit activities and hazardous work. 2018 PYQ answer: (a) child labour.

Velpur: community ownership

  • Velpur Mandal, Nizamabad, Telangana (then Andhra Pradesh): campaign began June 2001, declared child-labour-free 2 October 2001. Source records a 20-year commemoration 8 October 2021, presentation to parliamentary committee 24 November 2022 and continued retention reported in 2025; an exact 100% claim needs local monitoring data.
  • Employer forgiveness of about ₹35 lakh parental hand loans, village awareness/social commitments, sarpanch MoUs with Collector/DEO, teachers/infrastructure/mid-day meals and bridge schooling addressed demand, debt and access together. Village signboards reinforced the commitment.
  • Recognition by ILO, VVGNLI (Labour Ministry, Noida), NHRC and appreciation from A.P.J. Abdul Kalam supported wider learning. Lesson: combine debt relief/livelihood support, education quality, community mobilisation and labour–education–local government coordination; rescue alone cannot prevent relapse.
