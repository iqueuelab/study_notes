# Medical oxygen access and health-system resilience

## Essential medicine and measured access gap
- **Medical oxygen**, WHO essential medicine, treats hypoxaemia in pneumonia/acute respiratory disease, neonatal distress, severe systemic illness and surgery/anaesthesia. Reliable access requires a **service ecosystem**, not only possession of cylinders or a PSA plant.
- **Lancet Global Health Commission 2025** estimates over **5 billion people** lack safe, affordable, quality oxygen-service access. Acute medical-patient coverage gaps: **South Asia 78%**, **East Asia/Pacific 74%**; these are not proportions of all residents or all surgical/chronic patients. **Sub-Saharan Africa 91%** is higher, so the source's “highest gaps worldwide” claim for the Asian regions is wrong. [Commission presentation](https://stoppneumonia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Lancet-Commission-Official-Deck-9.pdf).
- In **LMIC general hospitals**, pulse oximeters available in about **54%**, oxygen therapy in **58%**; corresponding tertiary-hospital values **83% /86%**. Source omits facility types and incorrectly implies the figures describe every hospital uniformly. [Commission paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/11865010/).
- Additional financing to close LMIC coverage gaps: about **US$6.8 billion annually**, including **US$2.6 billion for South Asia**; commission's **US$34 billion 2025–30** estimate is a programme-cost calculation, not an already funded global commitment. Source's unqualified “$6.8 billion globally” loses geography and time unit. [Commission policy brief](https://stoppneumonia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Policy-Brief-English-1.pdf).
## Equipment → sustained safe service
- Challenges: missing pulse oximetry/delivery devices, poor distribution, rural inequity, unstable electricity, scarce biomedical engineers/technicians and unfunded maintenance; installed equipment can be nonfunctional.
- **PSA** separates oxygen from air through pressure-dependent adsorption; concentrators support smaller-scale supply. Cryogenic production/liquid oxygen is a different supply route. Cylinders, booster pumps, community supply hubs and transport networks support delivery; purity, fire safety and quality standards are essential.
- Solar power with adequate storage/backup may improve reliability in remote settings; Ethiopian/Nigerian examples illustrate adaptation, not guaranteed uninterrupted operation or universal savings. Use clinical demand, local energy conditions and maintenance costs to choose systems.
## Governance and equity
- **WHA resolution 76.3 (2023)** urges increased access; **WHO Director-General** reports implementation progress to WHA in **2026, 2028 and 2030**, rather than a blanket direct reporting deadline imposed on every individual facility. [Resolution](https://apps.who.int/gb/ebwha/pdf_files/WHA76/A76_R3-en.pdf).
- National scale-up plans should fund lifecycle operation, trained staff, local manufacture where feasible, procurement/production/storage/transport standards and emergency reserves within **UHC**. A proposed access scorecard supports monitoring; distinguish planning/reporting tools from proven local implementation.
- WHO's **2022 South-East Asia lessons report** stresses sustained investment beyond COVID surges. Source's Nepal–Bhutan technician training/PSA collaboration offers a regional-cooperation example, subject to project-specific verification.
- Digital demand forecasts, supply inventories and real-time equipment monitoring; research on affordable context-suitable systems; public/private/civil-society coordination. **SDGs 3 and 10** link oxygen security to health and equitable access. Donation counts alone do not establish patient access or clinical quality.
