# Maternal mortality: indicators, trends and prevention

## Definition and denominator
- **Maternal death:** during pregnancy or within **42 days** of termination, irrespective of pregnancy duration/site, from a cause related to/aggravated by pregnancy or its management; excludes accidental/incidental causes.
- **MMR = maternal deaths ÷ live births × 100,000** in the same period. It differs from maternal mortality rate, lifetime risk and absolute deaths.
## UN estimates 2000–2023: published 7 April 2025
- Global deaths **443,000 → 260,000**; MMR **328 → 197**, about **40% reduction**. Annual reduction slowed from about **2.2% (2000–2023)** to **1.6% (2016–2023)**.
- Sub-Saharan Africa: about **70%** of deaths and MMR **454**; Central/Southern Asia **112**, Oceania excluding Australia/New Zealand **173**, Australia/New Zealand around **3**. Different regional boundaries matter. [UN inter-agency report](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240108462).
- India 2023 estimate: MMR about **80** and **19,000 deaths (~7.2% world total)**, among the largest absolute contributors; DRC ~7.2%, Pakistan ~4.1%. Population size and MMR measure different burdens.
- Source gives India **384 (2000), 103 (2020), 80 (2023)**: this series implies roughly **79% reduction since 2000**. Separate official comparison: **86% India vs 48% global reduction during 1990–2023**, not during 2000–2023. Modelled UN estimates must not be interchanged with India's multi-year **SRS** estimates. [Official clarification, published 10 May](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2128024&lang=2&reg=48).
## SDG and implementation
- **SDG 3.1:** global MMR **below 70 by 2030**; required 2024–30 annual reduction around **14.8%**, much faster than recent progress. A target is not an achieved result.
- India programmes: **JSY** institutional-delivery incentives; **PMSMA** antenatal assessment; **LaQshya** labour-room/maternity-care quality; **Poshan Abhiyaan** nutrition; skilled midwifery services.
- Address unequal rural/tribal access, higher-burden States, anaemia/nutrition, adolescent reproductive health, antenatal/postnatal continuity, timely referral, emergency obstetric care, respectful treatment and trained personnel. Institutional delivery alone does not establish safe high-quality care.

## June issue: programme and indicator distinctions
- Source revisits UNFPA's recognition of India at **50 years of partnership**; do not treat the commemorative occasion as necessarily newly held in June 2025.
- **SUMAN** seeks free, dignified maternal/newborn care and zero denial at public facilities; complements PMSMA antenatal services and skilled midwifery. The source's 90,000-midwives/90%-services wording describes an ambition, not a verified completed workforce.
- Source historical indicators: institutional deliveries about **79% (NFHS-4, 2015–16) → 89% (NFHS-5, 2019–21)**; IMR **39 (2014) → 28 (2020)** per 1,000 live births. Keep reference periods distinct.
- **MMR 97** belongs to **SRS 2018–20**, not simply calendar 2020 or a UN estimate of 70% decline ending at 97. MMR means maternal mortality **ratio**, with live births as denominator; retain the separate UN/SRS series already explained above.

## SRS 2019–21 and the three delays
- **SRS MMR 93** maternal deaths per 100,000 live births. Selected State ratios: **Madhya Pradesh 175, Assam 167, West Bengal 109, Haryana 106; Karnataka 63, Gujarat 53, Jharkhand 51, Tamil Nadu 49, Maharashtra 38, Kerala 20**. Keep this series distinct from UN modelled estimates. Assam is often grouped with EAG States for analysis but is not itself one of the eight EAG States.
- **Three delays:** deciding to seek care → reaching a suitable facility → receiving timely appropriate care. Poverty, gender inequality, transport/referral failure and shortages of trained staff/blood/operating facilities can act at different stages.
- Major preventable pathways: postpartum haemorrhage (often uterine atony), obstructed labour, hypertensive disorders/eclampsia, sepsis and unsafe abortion; anaemia, TB and malaria can contribute indirectly. Unsafe abortion and illegal abortion are not synonyms.
- Strengthen antenatal registration/nutrition, emergency obstetric first-referral units and specialist teams, maternal-death review and postpartum mental-health care. The article proposes **four FRUs per two million population**; its **66% specialist vacancy** figure lacks a current reference period and is not a July 2025 census.
- **Kerala Confidential Review of Maternal Deaths** illustrates learning from each death. Uterine artery clamps/suction techniques are clinical tools requiring trained teams. **JSSK** provides free public-facility maternity/newborn services; **PMMVY**'s second-child benefit is conditional on the child being a girl, not unconditional payment for every second birth.

## September 2025: Kerala SRS rise
- The embedded Kerala clipping reports **MMR 18 (2020–22) → 30 (2021–23)** per 100,000 live births; Kerala and Andhra Pradesh share the lowest ratio in the newer bulletin. National **SRS 2021–23 MMR = 88**, unchanged from 2020–22. These multi-year SRS estimates differ from the **UN-modelled India 2023 estimate of 80** already covered. [Registrar General's bulletin](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/46177/download/50425/SRS_MMR_Bulletin_2021_2023.pdf).
- The clipping attributes concern to **97 COVID-related maternal deaths in Kerala in 2021** and falling live births. These are the article's reported explanations; a smaller live-birth denominator can raise a ratio even without a proportional rise in deaths. Overlapping three-year windows are not independent annual observations.
- The second embedded article quotes an older **SRS 2019–21 ratio of 93**, not the September 2025 latest figure; its older State values should not overwrite the newer series. The supplied global trends, schemes and 2024 TFR explanation repeat existing notes.
