# Cardiovascular risk among young adults

## Distinctions and burden
- **Myocardial infarction (heart attack):** interruption of blood supply injures heart muscle, commonly after coronary plaque rupture and clot formation. Cardiac arrest is loss of effective pumping; heart failure is impaired pumping/filling. These diagnoses are not interchangeable.
- Warning features include chest discomfort, breathlessness, sweating, pain radiating to arm/jaw/back, dizziness and sometimes nausea; prompt emergency assessment matters.
- Source’s **17.9 million CVD deaths in 2019**, roughly **85% heart attack/stroke**, concerns all ages and broad cardiovascular disease; it does not quantify a new Indian youth heart-attack trend. [WHO cardiovascular overview](https://www.who.int/health-topics/cardiovascular-diseases/).
- Indian **28.1% of deaths in 2016 versus 15.2% in 1990** refers to CVD burden. The source’s **25.43% registered deaths in 2020** cannot be treated as a national heart-attack-only share without its medical-certification denominator and disease classification; the quoted **272/100,000 versus 235 globally** also needs year/standardisation for comparison.
## Risk and prevention
- Tobacco, harmful alcohol use, inactivity, unhealthy diet, obesity, raised blood pressure/glucose/lipids and air pollution increase cardiovascular risk; stress, poor sleep, stimulant drugs and family history also matter.
- COVID can affect the cardiovascular system, but finding viral RNA does not by itself establish the cause of every young person’s heart attack. ICMR’s 47-hospital case-control study of unexplained sudden deaths found no increased risk from COVID vaccination; sudden death is not synonymous with confirmed MI. [ICMR 2023–24 report](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/Documents/1744706555_icmr_annualreport_2023-24_78804328_1.pdf).
- Promote healthy food, tobacco control, walkable/cyclable spaces, workplace wellness and appropriate primary-care risk assessment with reliable treatment/referral; integrate mental-health support and cardiac rehabilitation.
- Premature disease raises treatment costs, lost earnings and household stress, threatening human capital. Financial protection should accompany prevention and continuity of care; symptoms and personal risk require clinical assessment.
