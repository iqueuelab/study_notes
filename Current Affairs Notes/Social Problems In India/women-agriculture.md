# Women in agriculture: recognition, work and remuneration

## 2026 recognition and invisible work
- **UN General Assembly declared 2026 the International Year of the Woman Farmer**, with FAO leading implementation alongside partners; FAO alone did not declare it. It concerns women across agrifood value chains, including crop production, livestock, fisheries, processing and trade. [FAO](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1743183/).
- Seasonal, unpaid family and home/farm work overlaps care tasks and can be under-reported. Rising measured participation need not mean more secure paid employment; distinguish labour-force participation from **worker-population ratio (WPR)** and household ownership from individual work counts.
- Editorial's dated rural women **15+ WPR** comparison: about **35% in 2011–12 →46.5% in 2023–24**; self-employed share among rural women workers **60%→73%**. Its **117.6 million women agricultural workers** comprises **95.1 million self-employed,21.7 million hired/casual,0.8 million regular**, compared with **127.5 million men**. These are source estimates/definitions, not today's employment count or proof of uniformly improved earnings.
## Village evidence: contribution exceeds remuneration
| Source village evidence | Finding reported in the editorial |
|---|---|
| Palakurichi and Venmani, Tamil Nadu | Women's share of total family+hired crop labour **61%/57%** |
| Mahatwar, eastern Uttar Pradesh | Women's labour share **41%** |
| Women's farm daily wages | Tamil Nadu **₹290**, below half male pay in those study villages; UP **₹242–276** |
| Imputed livestock earnings | About **₹100/day** in some study villages |
| Annual crop returns | Below **₹16,000 in eastern UP/₹24,000 in Tamil Nadu** in study villages |
- FAS/PARI village samples illustrate mechanisms, not nationally representative wage/income levels. The issue also cites **Labour Bureau November 2025 female agricultural wage ₹384/day, Kerala ₹646**; occupation/state/sample comparability matters, and real wage stagnation requires inflation adjustment.
- Women feed animals, clean sheds, milk cattle and manage poultry, often without wages. The cited **40 million rural households with milch animals (2018–19 debt/investment survey)** cannot establish exactly 40 million female workers; nor is “two hours per animal” a universal time-use coefficient.
- Constraints: unequal land titles/access to credit and extension, low bargaining power, gender wage gaps, unpaid care, invisibility in surveys and mechanisation that displaces some manual tasks. The worksheet's **10% rural women owning land** needs its survey/ownership denominator, so do not read it as a share of all agricultural land.
- Responses: recognise cultivators independent of title, improve activity/time-use measurement, joint/individual titles, accessible credit/extension and women-suited equipment, childcare, equal pay/labour protection and collective marketing. A higher self-employment share alone is not economic empowerment.
