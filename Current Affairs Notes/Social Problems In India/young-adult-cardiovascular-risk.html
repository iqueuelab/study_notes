Social Problems In India

Cardiovascular risk among young adults

Editable notes

Distinctions and burden

  • Myocardial infarction (heart attack): interruption of blood supply injures heart muscle, commonly after coronary plaque rupture and clot formation. Cardiac arrest is loss of effective pumping; heart failure is impaired pumping/filling. These diagnoses are not interchangeable.
  • Warning features include chest discomfort, breathlessness, sweating, pain radiating to arm/jaw/back, dizziness and sometimes nausea; prompt emergency assessment matters.
  • Source’s 17.9 million CVD deaths in 2019, roughly 85% heart attack/stroke, concerns all ages and broad cardiovascular disease; it does not quantify a new Indian youth heart-attack trend. WHO cardiovascular overview.
  • Indian 28.1% of deaths in 2016 versus 15.2% in 1990 refers to CVD burden. The source’s 25.43% registered deaths in 2020 cannot be treated as a national heart-attack-only share without its medical-certification denominator and disease classification; the quoted 272/100,000 versus 235 globally also needs year/standardisation for comparison.

Risk and prevention

  • Tobacco, harmful alcohol use, inactivity, unhealthy diet, obesity, raised blood pressure/glucose/lipids and air pollution increase cardiovascular risk; stress, poor sleep, stimulant drugs and family history also matter.
  • COVID can affect the cardiovascular system, but finding viral RNA does not by itself establish the cause of every young person’s heart attack. ICMR’s 47-hospital case-control study of unexplained sudden deaths found no increased risk from COVID vaccination; sudden death is not synonymous with confirmed MI. ICMR 2023–24 report.
  • Promote healthy food, tobacco control, walkable/cyclable spaces, workplace wellness and appropriate primary-care risk assessment with reliable treatment/referral; integrate mental-health support and cardiac rehabilitation.
  • Premature disease raises treatment costs, lost earnings and household stress, threatening human capital. Financial protection should accompany prevention and continuity of care; symptoms and personal risk require clinical assessment.

September 2025: preventive-health priorities

  • Source discussion stresses premature cardiovascular disease's loss of earnings/productive years, household care costs and pressure on services. Existing notes cover tobacco, inactivity, poor diet, stress/sleep, metabolic risk and family history; their interaction is not a fixed universal lifestyle/genetic percentage.
  • Printed “50% of heart attacks under 40”, “70% combined risk”, and “25% recognise symptoms” lack identifiable population/sample/denominator and should not be memorised as national findings. Editorial clinical observations are not surveillance estimates.
  • Strengthen primary-care risk assessment, tobacco control, healthier workplaces/food environments, hypertension/diabetes treatment, emergency referral and financial protection. NPCDCS is the historical programme name; expanded NP-NCD terminology should be used for contemporary noncommunicable-disease control. CT/calcium scoring requires clinical indication, not blanket population imaging.
  • The daily question on socioeconomic effects and prevention (15 marks/250 words) is coaching practice, excluded from PYQ banks.
