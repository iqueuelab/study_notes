# Demographic dividend, skills and the future of work

## August 2025 education–employment debate
- A rising working-age share can boost output through employment, productivity, savings and investment, provided education, health, women's participation and jobs improve. Age structure creates an opportunity, not an automatic dividend.
- Rote learning, slowly updated curricula, weak practical exposure/career counselling and poor industry links can leave graduates without marketable skills; AI changes tasks as well as occupations. Employment status, employability test scores and automation exposure are different measures.
- Source figures **43% job-ready graduates**, **61% curriculum mismatch**, **93% awareness of seven careers**, and **70% jobs at risk** are survey/model claims with differing samples and denominators; do not generalise them into national unemployment or a certain 2030 job-loss forecast. WEF's 170 million created/92 million displaced is a **global projection**, not India's observed employment outcome.
- Update curricula for digital/green/technical and transferable skills; strengthen apprenticeships, school career guidance, industry–academia partnerships and lifelong reskilling. Coordinate PMKVY, PMKK, SANKALP and internship programmes with measured placement/retention outcomes.
- Policy must create productive labour demand alongside training; address regional/gender gaps, informal work and access barriers. Youth frustration can cause social instability, but that outcome is not inevitable. Source's future-work Mains prompt and motivational quotes are coaching material, not PYQs or authenticated quotations.

## January 2026: make skill certification valuable in the labour market
- Editorial diagnosis: training, assessment, certification and placement are fragmented, leaving **Sector Skill Councils (SSCs)** without clear accountability for sustained employment. Certificates can have weak signalling value when employers rely on their own training, experience or referrals. This is an institutional critique, not proof every SSC or public qualification fails.
- Integrate practical skills within school/degree pathways, allow education–work mobility, expand workplace apprenticeships under **NAPS**, and give employers responsibility for curricula and competency-based assessment. Track placement quality, retention, wages and progression rather than enrolments/certificates alone.
- SSCs' industry-standard/certification role should link to measurable employability; the issue cites **PM-SETU and ITI modernisation through industry partnership** as enabling initiatives. Labour demand, informal work and regional/gender barriers still matter alongside training.
- Source **~1.40 crore PMKVY candidates (2015–25)**, **4.1% formally vocationally trained**, **28% higher-education GER**, **44% OECD vocational enrolment /70% selected countries**, and **30–40% industry attrition** have different populations and reference periods; do not compare them as one survey. **NEP 2020's 50% higher-education GER by 2035** is a target, not achieved coverage.

## March 2026: a slower-growing, ageing population
- The editorial discusses **IIMAD–Population Foundation of India projections for 2021–2051**, not a completed 2021 Census count. Its scenario projects population **1.35bn→1.59bn**, roughly **0.5% average annual growth**; projections depend on fertility, mortality and migration assumptions.
- Source estimates: people aged **60+ about 130m (9.6%)→325m (20.5%)**; median age **28→40 years**. The elderly **number** is roughly 2.5 times the baseline, while the **share** roughly doubles. These are projected values, not observed 2051 outcomes.
- Working-age **15–59 share** is projected to peak around **2041**, falling from **65.2% in 2021 to 62.8% by 2051** after the intervening peak. This is an age category, not measured labour-force participation/employment; a national peak is not a universal deadline for every State.
- Fewer young children may reduce school demand and improve pupil–teacher ratios, but rural access, distance and quality should guide consolidation. Source government/private school counts **11.07→10.18 lakh /2.88→3.31 lakh** lack clear endpoints in the summary and should not be treated as a clean like-for-like causal series.
- Priorities: skills and productive jobs now, women's paid-work participation, reproductive choice/health, geriatric and long-term care, pension/social-security coverage and age-friendly infrastructure. **Silver economy** opportunities do not automatically produce a second demographic dividend; savings, health, productivity and institutions matter.
- [IIMAD–PFI projection project](https://cpanel.iimad.org/iimad-pfi-population-projections/) identifies the 2021–2051 study; projections should be distinguished from the Registrar General’s separate official projection series.
