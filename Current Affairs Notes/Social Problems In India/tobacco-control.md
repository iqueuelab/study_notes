# Tobacco affordability, taxation and cancer prevention

## Dated burden and affordability
- **GATS-2, India 2016–17, adults 15+: 42.4% men, 14.2% women, 28.6% overall** currently used tobacco. These are survey-period rates, not measurements made in May 2025. Smoking and smokeless products have different patterns; the source's global **70% smokeless-user share** needs a defined dataset/year. [WHO factsheet](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/ncds/ncd-surveillance/data-reporting/india/gats/gats-india-2016-17-factsheet.pdf?download=true&sfvrsn=59fbdbff_2).
- WHO-supported study estimates **₹177,341 crore** economic costs from tobacco-attributable disease/deaths among people **aged 35+ in 2017–18**, about **1.04% of GDP**: roughly **74% smoking/26% smokeless**. Direct care, morbidity/productivity and premature mortality costs are included. This is neither a measured 2025 annual loss nor the cost of all cancer alone. [WHO account](https://www.who.int/india/news-room/detail/09-02-2021-india-loses-1-of-its-gdp-to-diseases-and-early-deaths-from-tobacco-use-finds-who-study).
- Low unit prices, single-stick/sachet sales, rising purchasing power, product substitution and tax undershifting can sustain affordability. Prices by pack, stick and sachet are not interchangeable; source **₹1–₹5**, **₹5–₹95**, **87% vendors**, **450-million middle class** and **88-country ban** claims lack a consistent national sample/year.
- Tobacco causes oral, lung and other cancers and cardiovascular/respiratory disease; second-hand smoke harms non-users. **India is not first worldwide for all male cancer incidence/mortality**—the source conflates site-specific oral-cancer burden with all cancers.
## Tax and wider policy
- WHO benchmark **at least 75% of retail price in total taxes** is a tax-*share* indicator. A proposed **35% GST slab** is an ad-valorem rate with a different denominator and ignores other taxes/cess; the PDF's direct 35-versus-75 comparison is invalid. Proposal reporting does not establish enactment as of this issue. [WHO tax benchmark](https://www.who.int/europe/health-topics/tobacco/promoting-taxation-on-tobacco-products).
- Taxes should raise real prices faster than income growth, cover product substitutes, and limit evasion; assess burden alongside reduced disease/household spending and accessible cessation. Manufacturer price responses mean tax-rate change does not ensure equal retail-price change.
- Single-stick restrictions, plain packaging, stronger zoning and earmarking taxes are **recommendations here**, not all newly enacted universal rules. COTPA already restricts sales to minors and within **100 yards of educational institutions**; “near every tea stall” is not the existing nationwide statutory rule.
