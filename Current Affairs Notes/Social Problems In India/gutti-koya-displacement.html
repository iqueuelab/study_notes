Social Problems In India

Gutti Koya displacement and rehabilitation

Editable notes

Displacement and present difficulties

  • The issue calls for durable rehabilitation of Gutti Koya/Gond communities displaced from Chhattisgarh during counterinsurgency from 2005, many now living in Andhra Pradesh/Telangana. Source estimates nearly 50,000 affected; use as a reported estimate, not a settled census.
  • Forced grouping into roadside camps sought to separate insurgents from civilian support, disrupting forest livelihoods and community ties. Historical village grouping in Mizoram during the 1960s offers a comparison; it should not be presented as the sole cause of the later peace accord.
  • They are internally displaced persons (IDPs): crossing a State boundary within India does not make them international refugees. Housing insecurity, disputed occupation, missing documents, education/welfare access and intergenerational exclusion persist.
  • Reports allege evictions, house demolition and planting on cultivated plots; safe rehabilitation must address these allegations and rights. Some displaced people assist anti-Maoist operations through terrain/language knowledge; basic rights do not depend on security service.

Forest rights and constitutional precision

  • FRA Section 3(1)(m) recognises in-situ rehabilitation, including alternative land, for eligible forest-dwelling STs/other traditional forest dwellers illegally evicted or displaced before 13 December 2005 without legal rehabilitation. It is not an automatic blanket grant to every later migrant settlement.
  • Claims depend on statutory eligibility/evidence and prescribed procedures. Section 4(5) bars eviction/removal until recognition and verification are complete; Gram Sabha participation is central. PESA applies in notified Fifth Schedule areas, not automatically to every settlement.
  • Article 342 ST lists are State/UT specific. Migration does not erase ethnic identity; it can affect destination-State entitlement. President initially specifies the list after consultation; subsequent inclusion/exclusion requires Parliamentary law, not a fresh executive notification alone.

Rehabilitation design

  • Centre and origin/destination States should jointly verify displaced families, documentation and claims; offer voluntary safe return or supported settlement, housing, livelihoods, schooling and health access; provide grievance review and time-bound monitoring.
  • A national IDP framework drawing on UN Guiding Principles could coordinate protection and durable solutions; India has no comprehensive dedicated IDP statute.
  • Bru–Reang settlement agreement: 16 January 2020, not 2019. Centre, Tripura, Mizoram and Bru representatives agreed permanent settlement in Tripura for roughly 34,000, with housing/financial support. This negotiated model offers lessons, not automatic legal equivalence or proof every family had completed rehabilitation by April 2025.
