# Women in STEM: recognition, retention and leadership

## Concepts
- **Matilda effect:** women's scientific contributions overlooked/credited to men.
- **Leaky pipeline:** attrition across career stages; **chutes/ladders:** structural barriers and supports that shape progression/re-entry.
- Biased hiring/promotion/grants, harassment, unequal care, poor mentoring and conference exclusion restrict advancement; do not attribute structural inequality merely to low confidence.
- Source 17%women faculty across 98 institutions is a study sample, not every Indian STEMinstitution.
## Supports
- Transparent recruitment/funding, pay equity, harassment redress, childcare, parental flexibility and fair career-break/re-entry criteria; mentoring and leadership representation.
- **DSTGATI2020, WISE-KIRAN/Women Scientists programmes, Bio CARe** support institutional change/research re-entry; ICMR initiatives have their own eligibility.
- Bias Watch India tracks inequities; measure retention, decision-making and credit, not only admission counts. Diversity improves opportunities without guaranteeing every team identical outcomes.

## World Youth Skills Day, July 15
- Editorial cites women as **~43% of Indian STEM graduates** versus **~27% of STEM workforce**; these are source-reported differently defined education/employment indicators, not a single cohort's measured transition rate. Retention, career progression and safe work need industry participation alongside education.
- **PLFS 2023–24, usual status, age 15+: female LFPR 41.7% overall, 47.6% rural, 28.0% urban**. Source's urban **25.4%** belongs to **2022–23**, not the same year. Participation is not identical to secure salaried technical employment. [MoSPI annual results](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2057970&lang=2&reg=48).
- Source's **WeSTEM** example brings UN Women, Madhya Pradesh/Gujarat and Micron Foundation together for technical skills, workplace safety, mentoring, role models and family/community engagement. Partnerships must address hiring, care transitions, pay, safety and leadership as well as training.
- Source Budget 2025–26 gender allocation **₹4.49 lakh crore/~8.8%** concerns gender-responsive budgeting; it should not be interpreted as money exclusively spent on women-only STEM schemes. GDP gains cited for greater women's employment are scenarios, not realised growth.
