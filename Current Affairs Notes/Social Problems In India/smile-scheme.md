# SMILE: rehabilitation and inclusive livelihoods

## Scheme design
- **SMILE = Support for Marginalised Individuals for Livelihood and Enterprise**, Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment; launched **12 February 2022**, a Central Sector umbrella scheme.
- Two distinct components: **comprehensive rehabilitation for transgender welfare** and **comprehensive rehabilitation of persons engaged in begging**. Identification, counselling, shelter, healthcare, education, skills and livelihood support form a rehabilitation continuum. [Ministry/PIB launch background](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2191532).
## 27 April 2025 review: identification versus outcomes
- Source review: **9,958 identified**, **970 rehabilitated (~9.7%)**, including **352 children**; coverage **81 cities**, expanded from an initial 30, including pilgrimage/tourist/heritage centres.
- A subsequent **5 May 2025 PIB explainer** corroborates the 9,958/970 figures and 81-city coverage as of December 2024. It describes **₹100 crore for the beggary component during FY2023–24 to FY2025–26**, not the whole SMILE umbrella, and ₹365 crore overall during 2021–22 to 2025–26. Its two allocation tables use different annual distributions; do not mix them. [PIB explainer](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?ModuleId=3&NoteId=154411&lang=1&reg=3).
- Review reports **₹14.71 crore expenditure through December 2024**, roughly 14.7% of the cited three-year ₹100 crore allocation; spending at an interim date is not a final utilisation rate. **8,000 rehabilitations by FY2025–26** is a target, not achievement.
- Source cites **3.72 lakh persons in the 2011 Census** and **6.62 lakh rural households dependent on begging/charity in SECC2011**. These measures need original-table verification and are not interchangeable: households ≠ persons; begging/charity ≠ identical census categories. Comparing selected-city identification with an old national total does not quantify present prevalence.
## Implementation and ethical lessons
- Constraints: dated baseline data; poverty/homelessness, mental illness, addiction and trafficking; inadequate shelters, trained social workers and skills centres; weak awareness, coordination and follow-up; urban concentration can leave rural deprivation underserved.
- Voluntary consent and dignity matter; low uptake calls for trusted outreach and viable livelihoods, rather than treating consent itself as a defect to override.
- Integrate child protection under the **Juvenile Justice framework**, schooling and family assessment; distinguish exploited/forced begging from poverty-driven survival. Equality and dignity under Articles **14 and 21** guide humane implementation.
- Priorities: updated surveys with clear definitions, health/de-addiction services, realistic job links, local-body/NGO convergence, timely funding and independent monitoring of sustained reintegration. Identification, rescue, rehabilitation and lasting economic independence are separate outcomes.
