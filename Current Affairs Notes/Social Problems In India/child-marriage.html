Social Problems In India

Child marriage: substantive equality and annulment

Editable notes

Ages and remedies

  • PCMA 2006, section 2(a): child = female below 18 / male below 21 for marriage. Majority Act adulthood generally 18 for both; source's “men attain majority at 21” confuses marriage age with general majority.
  • Section 3: child marriage generally voidable at the child party's option, not automatically void in every case; specified circumstances under sections 12/14 differ. A minor applies through guardian/next friend with Child Marriage Prohibition Officer.
  • Section 3(3) allows filing before completing two years after attaining majority; interpretation of male limitation has been contested. Act.

Judgment: reasoning versus outcome

  • T. Sivakumar (Madras HC, 2011) interpreted male filing limit as 23; Independent Thought (SC, 2017) includes observations relevant to that approach.
  • Sanjay Chaudhary v Guddan, Allahabad HC, 25 October 2024 reasoned for an equal majority-based limit and questioned advantage to adult men. However, its final decision followed binding Supreme Court precedent, accepted this man's filing before 23 and allowed the appeal. The PDF's “rejected extension” does not capture that outcome. Court's published judgment, paragraphs 69–76.
  • Do not treat the discussion as a nationwide enacted rule abolishing the male 23-year interpretation.

Substantive equality debate

  • 2021 Amendment Bill proposed women's marriage age 21; lapsed with dissolution of the 17th Lok Sabha. Proposal ≠ enacted amendment.
  • Higher common age: potential schooling, delayed pregnancy and formal equality; concerns: adult 18–21 autonomy, privacy, selective enforcement and criminalisation of consensual relationships.
  • Source 49.4% self-initiated marriages, Enfold/Civic Data Lab 2024 study: sample-specific, not a national prevalence estimate.
  • Options debated: equal age 18, clearer/equal extended annulment periods, accessible legal aid and timely courts. Suggested 3–5 years is a reform proposal.
  • Pair protection from forced marriage with education through 18, livelihoods/social security, safe healthcare/contraception, sexuality education, counselling and survivor support. Formal equal wording must deliver real agency and access to remedies.

Prevalence and prevention

  • Women aged 20–24 married before 18: 47.4% (NFHS-3, 2005–06) → 26.8% (NFHS-4, 2015–16) → 23.3% (NFHS-5, 2019–21). The latest interval represents 3.5 percentage points, not a 3.5% relative reduction. It is a retrospective age-cohort indicator, not the fraction of all marriages occurring in 2025. Health Ministry annual report.
  • NFHS-5 source comparisons: West Bengal, Bihar and Tripura exceed 40%; Madhya Pradesh 23.1%, Rajasthan 25.4%, compared with 32.4% and 35.4% in NFHS-4 respectively. Date the data; a recycled global-programme committee visit is not evidence of a new April 2025 visit.
  • Poverty, gender norms, school discontinuation, insecurity and weak livelihood options reinforce early marriage. Consequences include curtailed education/employment, reduced agency, violence risk and early pregnancy; adolescent maternal and child nutrition/health require support rather than stigma.
  • SDG target 5.3: eliminate child, early and forced marriage and female genital mutilation by 2030. The source's approximately 12 million girls married annually worldwide is an annual flow, not the stock of all women married as children. UNICEF data.

Measures and limits

  • PCMA 2006 and POCSO 2012 provide legal protection; combine timely prevention, Child Marriage Prohibition Officers, safe reporting, legal aid and survivor support with schooling, social protection and livelihoods. The source's 2021 proposal to raise women's marriage age to 21 did not become law; retain the existing legal-age discussion above.
  • Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (2015): addresses discrimination and girls' survival, protection and education; Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (2015): savings for girls' education/future needs, not an unconditional cash grant. Source-listed Balika Samriddhi Yojana links assistance with girls' schooling; distinguish an older scheme example from proof of current universal delivery.
  • Source image highlights safety/violence, educational loss, reproductive health, psychological burden and violation of childhood rights. Child-help services support rescue/referral; an old 1098 image should not be treated as a complete description of present emergency-response integration.
  • Mains framework: scale and causes → law/enforcement → education and economic agency → health/social protection → community engagement → implementation gaps and measurable outcomes. The source's 250-word question is undated practice.

December 2025: prevention beyond national averages

  • The source's “Prevention of Child Marriage Act” title is wrong: Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006. Existing notes give female below 18/male below 21 thresholds; “either spouse below 18” alone omits males 18–20. NFHS 47.4%→23.3% refers to women 20–24 married before 18, across 2005–06/2019–21; not all girls or marriages in 2025.
  • Editorial's 40% poorest versus 8% richest and 48% no-education versus 4% higher-education comparisons are source-reported NFHS/UNFPA associations with different wealth/education cohorts; they are not experimental proof education alone explains every marriage. West Bengal/Bihar/Tripura remain highlighted, with interstate/social variation.
  • Bal Vivah Mukt Bharat, launched 27 November 2024, marked one year with a 100-day intensive campaign, formally launched 4 December 2025, covering schools, community/faith leaders and local bodies. This is a new campaign stage, not a new marriage-age law. WCD announcement.
  • Editorial warns fear of criminalisation can deter adolescents from formal healthcare and push unsafe care. Enforce protection sensitively while maintaining access to confidential appropriate health services, education, counselling and legal support; low prosecution counts alone cannot measure prevalence or establish the success of a law.
  • Existing notes cover poverty, gender norms, early-pregnancy/education consequences and 2030 SDG 5.3. This issue stresses usable school toilets, safe transport, secondary-school retention, outreach and livelihoods, beyond cash incentives alone. 150–200-word Mains prompt is expressly practice, with no authentic exam year, so excluded from PYQ bank.
