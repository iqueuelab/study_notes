# NARI 2025: women’s perceptions of urban safety

## Survey findings and limits
- Source reports release by the **National Commission for Women** of the **National Annual Report and Index on Women’s Safety (NARI) 2025**, prepared with **GIA, Pvalue Analytics, NorthCap University and Jindal Global Law School**. Survey: **12,770 women in 31 cities**; do not infer all States are comprehensively represented or that it measures every Indian woman's experience.
- Issue-period **national safety benchmark 65%**; **60%** felt safe, **40%** not so safe/unsafe. These are composite/perception survey measures, not probabilities of avoiding a crime or police crime rates.
- Higher-safety group: **Kohima, Visakhapatnam, Bhubaneswar, Aizawl, Gangtok, Itanagar, Mumbai**; lower-safety group: **Patna, Jaipur, Faridabad, Delhi, Kolkata, Srinagar, Ranchi**. Group membership should not be converted into a precise ordinal ranking absent the report's table.
- Source reports **7%** experienced harassment in **2024**, **14%** among women under 24; verbal harassment **58%**, neighbourhood/transport hotspots **38%/29%**, and **86%** daytime safety in educational institutions. Percentages have different denominators; night-time public transport/recreation perceptions deteriorated sharply.
- Source describes low reporting/trust: roughly **one-third** of victims complained, **one-quarter** expected effective action, and **53%** lacked workplace POSH-policy awareness. Its additional **22% registered/16% action** figures need the underlying question and denominator; do not multiply or equate them to all women or all FIRs.
- Revision: safety supports mobility, learning and paid work; combine lighting/safe transport, responsive policing, accessible redress/POSH implementation, community participation and gender-equitable norms. Link **SDGs 5 and 11**; survey associations do not establish that one policy caused a city's score.
