Social Problems In India

Matrimonial laws: protection and due process

Editable notes

Legal framework

  • Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961: dowry-related offences; Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005: protection, residence, monetary and other remedies for domestic violence, including physical, sexual, verbal/emotional and economic abuse.
  • Source uses IPC 498A, cruelty by husband/relatives. For the new framework, BNS section 85 creates the offence and 86 defines cruelty; old cases may continue under the applicable earlier law. India Code.
  • Cognizable/non-bailable classification does not require automatic arrest. Arrest necessity and procedural safeguards remain distinct from the existence of an accusation.

Cases and source corrections

  • Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar, 2014, not 2017 as printed: no routine/automatic arrest; apply the statutory necessity checklist and judicial scrutiny. A later 2017 judgment cites the 2014 precedent. Supreme Court.
  • Law Commission Report 243, August 2012 considered section 498A and safeguards. Recommendations are not automatically enacted law. Report.
  • The PDF's 'arrest immediately on complaint' and 'no punishment for false allegations' are overbroad; do not learn them as legal rules.

Balanced Mains argument

  • Genuine abuse/dowry violence require accessible protection, prompt investigation, legal aid and safe support.
  • Proven malicious complaints or indiscriminate implication can cause stigma, livelihood loss, family distress and wasted judicial resources; unsupported allegations of misuse can also discourage genuine victims.
  • Acquittal or a withdrawn complaint alone does not prove fabrication; assess evidence, not gender stereotypes.
  • Responses: trained investigators, reasoned arrest decisions, timely trials, judicial oversight, survivor support, counselling where safe and voluntary, and action on demonstrated deliberate falsehood under applicable law.
  • Gender-neutral reform is a policy debate; do not present the PDF's recommendations as current enacted provisions or dilute protection for victims.

Marital sexual violence: February 2025 legal distinction

  • BNS section 63 exception 2: wife not under 18, not the PDF's obsolete literal 15-year IPC text. Independent Thought, 2017, addressed minor wives; POCSO protection applies to children. Official BNS.
  • BNS section 67: non-consensual intercourse by husband during separation is a distinct offence. The general marital rape exception does not erase possible other offences or Domestic Violence Act sexual-abuse remedies.
  • Navtej Singh Johar, 2018: decriminalised consensual adult same-sex relations under IPC 377; it did not universally legalise sexual assault. BNS's omission of a direct 377 equivalent creates separate coverage concerns.
  • Source Gorakhnath Sharma discussion concerns an older IPC prosecution and a February 2025 decision; “2019 case” is not automatically the judgment year or statement of all present law.
  • Criminalisation arguments: dignity, equality, bodily autonomy, consent and effective protection. Objections about misuse/proof/family consequences require evidence and proportionate procedural safeguards, not the assumption marriage creates perpetual consent.
  • Reform: survivor services, trained investigation, fair trials, clear consent standards and domestic-violence remedies. Jaya Jaitley proposal to raise women's marriage age to 21 was a recommendation/proposal; source does not establish enactment.
  • Global country counts lack a clear date/legal definition; source includes vanished countries, so avoid memorising the list as current law.

Verified PYQ connection

  • 2014 GS-IV: innovative measures against sexual violence; official 150 words /10 marks, correcting the source's 250/15. Address prevention, safe reporting, accountable institutions, survivor support and measurable behavioural change.
