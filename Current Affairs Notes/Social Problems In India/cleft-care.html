Cleft lip and palate: child nutrition and equitable care

Editable notes

December 2025: public-health approach to cleft care

  • Cleft lip/palate are congenital orofacial anomalies from incomplete tissue fusion, involving genetic and environmental risk factors. They can impair feeding, nutrition, speech and hearing and cause stigma; treatment requires more than cosmetic repair.
  • Editorial estimates about 1 in 700 births/~36,000 affected births annually in India,17.5 lakh unrepaired clefts and cites 1.5×severe malnutrition risk. These are source period estimates, not findings from a complete national registry. Do not infer 1.5×infant mortality from a malnutrition risk ratio; the worksheet makes that invalid substitution in its practice guidance.
  • NGOs such as Smile Train, Mission Smile, Operation Smile provide surgery/training; source 7.5 lakh Smile Train surgeries/25 years/~30,000 annually measures procedures over distinct periods, not unique untreated patients or national coverage. Claims of highest global burden and exact preventable death share need comparative definitions.
  • Integrate birth screening, RBSK/ASHA referral, nutrition support, surgery, hearing/speech therapy, dental care, counselling and long-term follow-up into public health services. Timely treatment must follow individual clinical assessment; lip and palate repairs do not share one universal 3–6 month schedule.
  • Improve district referral/transport support, trained teams, public hospital capacity, registry/surveillance and NGO coordination; rural outreach and financial protection support SDG 2/3 and equity. Source's 68.8% rural is an old Census period share, not a measured December 2025 population estimate; alleged August 2024 national awareness month date is unverified.
  • Unyearred 250 word practice: public health impact → fragmented/urban-biased access → integrated nutrition/surgical/rehabilitation services → equitable outcomes. Exclude from PYQ banks.
