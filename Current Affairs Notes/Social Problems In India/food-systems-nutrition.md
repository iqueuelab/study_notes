# Food systems: nutrition, access and sustainability

## Triple burden and indicator caution
- Food security includes nutritious affordable diets, not cereal output alone: **undernutrition, micronutrient deficiencies and overweight/obesity** can coexist. Climate stress, biodiversity loss, low incomes and poor storage compound inequity/NCD risks.
- **NFHS-5 (2019–21), under 5:** stunting **35.5%** (low height-for-age), wasting **19.3%** (low weight-for-height), underweight **32.1%** (low weight-for-age). Source lists adult overweight/obesity **women 24%, men 22.9%**, and anaemia **57% women 15–49**. These are distinct indicators/cohorts, not proportions all suffering the same condition. [Official NFHS nutrition figures](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1779258&lang=2&reg=3).
- Source's **194 million undernourished** is not an NFHS national calorie-undernourishment count; it needs a separate FAO estimate/year. **GHI 2024: 105/127** is a composite rank, not a direct census of hunger or every nutritional condition.
- Article cites **55.6% unable to afford healthy diet in 2022 at PPP US$3.36/day**; dollar-PPP diet cost is not a nominal exchange-rate rupee price, and affordability estimates depend on methodology/reference year. Global **US$12 trillion/year food-system costs** are an estimated hidden-cost concept, not India's fiscal spending or an audited invoice.
## Transformation priorities
- **Nutrition-sensitive agriculture:** diverse/biofortified crops, nutrition goals in farm policy, better storage/processing and locally suitable foods; supplementation/fortification complements dietary diversity rather than replaces it.
- **Nutrition-Sensitive Community Planning:** connect food with WASH, soil/water conservation and healthcare. Source's **Nutri-Pathshala** model combines nutrition education, biofortified grains and local sourcing to support small farmers; programme examples are not proof every implementation succeeded.
- Diversify PDS and **PM-POSHAN (mid-day meals)** appropriately; strengthen safety nets and healthy-diet behaviour campaigns. Private sector: understandable labels, responsibly fortified foods, suitable plant alternatives and digital/QR education; innovation needs nutrition/equity safeguards.
- Climate-smart, gender-inclusive farming and rural opportunities; nutrition literacy via interactive tools (source example **MyPlate Blast Off**) and radio for remote areas.
- Source's **TERI–FOLU Himalayan model:** region-specific micronutrient-rich crops, local food networks and decentralised processing; assess measured outcomes rather than asserting universal success. Integrate nutrition/health expertise with planning; links **SDG 2, 3, 12**. [FAO dietary guidance](https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/dietary-guidelines/regions/india/en/).
