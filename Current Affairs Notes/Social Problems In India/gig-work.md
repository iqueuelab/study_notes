# Gig work, algorithmic management and labour rights

## Opportunity and precarity
- Platforms match workers and customers; flexible entry and demand aggregation coexist with uncertain earnings, unpaid waiting, expenses and weak social protection.
- Ratings, task allocation, pricing and deactivation can create employer-like control despite the label “partner”. **Legal employment status depends on law and facts**, not branding alone.
- The source's low-cost instant domestic-work example raises dignity, safety and bargaining-power concerns; advertised customer price is not necessarily workers' net hourly pay.
- Code on Social Security 2020 recognises gig/platform workers, but enactment, commencement and actual benefit delivery are distinct. Rajasthan's 2023 legislation provides a welfare framework; draft Karnataka proposals in March 2025 were not already implemented law.
- International rulings vary by jurisdiction and category: UK's “worker” status is not identical to full employee status; do not generalise one rule to all platforms.
- Reform priorities: clear earnings/fees, safety and insurance, portable welfare, access to relevant personal work data, fair deactivation appeals, representation and collective bargaining.
- Protect privacy when increasing algorithmic transparency; assess net earnings and coverage rather than registrations alone.
## 16 May 2025 : workers’ roundtable
- **15 May Delhi meeting**, organised by **Janpahal/Gig Workers Association**, sought **minimum earnings, social security and legal protection**, plus State/Central tripartite welfare boards including workers, platforms and government. Recommendations were to be forwarded to policymakers; these are demands, not newly enacted rights.
- Workers described blocked IDs/deactivation without notice/remedy, payment/per-task reductions, platform surcharges, poor support and resistance to organising. Testimonies illustrate alleged practices rather than adjudicated findings against every platform.
- They also sought an explicit Central policy; evaluate effective earnings after costs/unpaid waiting, worker representation, transparent deductions and accessible appeal systems.

## Visibility in PLFS
- Gig/platform workers can be counted as employed in **PLFS** while remaining indistinguishable in published broad categories such as self-employment, own-account work or casual labour. **Inclusion in total employment≠separate identification**.
- Multi-platform work, task rates, algorithmic allocation/deactivation, unpaid waiting, net costs, income volatility and access to protections need survey modules/identifiers. Legal employment status cannot be decided by a survey label or universally denied to every platform worker.
- **Code on Social Security 2020** definitions/framework, e-Shram registration and Budget 2025 ID/health-cover announcements are relevant; the Code's provisions for boards/funds are not proof all were already notified, operational and paying benefits by July 2025.
- The **January 2025 PLFS redesign** broadens frequency/rural coverage, but frequency alone does not create gig-specific classifications. Improve occupation/platform questions, multiple-job reporting and benefit/contract data with privacy safeguards; reconcile survey and administrative records rather than equate registrations with all active workers.

## January 2026: removing the ten-minute delivery promise
- Following **31 December worker protests** and Labour Minister **Mansukh Mandaviya's** discussions with aggregators, **Blinkit removed its ten-minute advertising promise**; other platforms were expected to follow. This is a change to branding/service assurances, **not a statutory nationwide ban on all quick delivery or proof every platform had already changed**.
- Quick commerce uses **nearby dark stores, app-based workers and algorithmic allocation**. Warehouse proximity can shorten trips, but speed branding, incentive thresholds, ratings/deactivation and income insecurity can still create implicit pressure; the competing explanations require worker-level evidence rather than assuming no risk because stores are near.
- Workers raised road safety, stress/fatigue and protection concerns. A changed tagline alone does not establish reduced accidents, improved net earnings or effective insurance. Assess realistic routes/timings, incentives and penalties, safety equipment, rest, transparent pay, accident/health protection and deactivation appeals.
- The **Code on Social Security 2020** recognises gig/platform work, but recognition is distinct from funded schemes, registration, eligibility and actual benefit delivery. Existing notes on worker representation, bargaining, algorithmic control and net income after costs remain applicable; labels such as “partner” do not resolve employment status for every legal purpose.
