# Gig work, algorithmic management and labour rights

## Opportunity and precarity
- Platforms match workers and customers; flexible entry and demand aggregation coexist with uncertain earnings, unpaid waiting, expenses and weak social protection.
- Ratings, task allocation, pricing and deactivation can create employer-like control despite the label “partner”. **Legal employment status depends on law and facts**, not branding alone.
- The source's low-cost instant domestic-work example raises dignity, safety and bargaining-power concerns; advertised customer price is not necessarily workers' net hourly pay.
- Code on Social Security 2020 recognises gig/platform workers, but enactment, commencement and actual benefit delivery are distinct. Rajasthan's 2023 legislation provides a welfare framework; draft Karnataka proposals in March 2025 were not already implemented law.
- International rulings vary by jurisdiction and category: UK's “worker” status is not identical to full employee status; do not generalise one rule to all platforms.
- Reform priorities: clear earnings/fees, safety and insurance, portable welfare, access to relevant personal work data, fair deactivation appeals, representation and collective bargaining.
- Protect privacy when increasing algorithmic transparency; assess net earnings and coverage rather than registrations alone.
