# Milk, dairy cooperatives and nutritional inequality

## Production does not ensure access
- India is the world's largest milk producer; **White Revolution / Operation Flood**, cooperative organisation and **Verghese Kurien** transformed supply and rural livelihoods.
- Milk supplies protein, calcium and micronutrients in many diets; nutritional security also needs dietary diversity, safe food and access. Milk is not compulsory for every person or suitable for every age/allergy.
- January editorial consumption estimates: richest decile **3–4 times** poorest decile; poorest **30% of households consume 18%**. Household surveys can miss consumption outside home; estimates are not the same as national per-capita availability.
- Higher consumption examples **Rajasthan/Punjab/Haryana**, lower **Chhattisgarh/Odisha/West Bengal**; affordability, regional diets and access matter. Source **300 g/day** is a dietary reference, not a universal entitlement.
## Inclusive nutrition policy
- Prioritise children, pregnant/lactating women and vulnerable elderly; locally appropriate supplementary nutrition under **POSHAN/Anganwadi services (ICDS)**. Existing programmes do not guarantee universal daily milk everywhere.
- Reliable State funding, cold chains, quality testing, diversified nutrition, SHG/community engagement; CSR/social bonds are proposed financing additions, not substitutes for public accountability.
- Avoid excess sugar in flavoured milk/processed foods; food choice, lactose intolerance and allergies need consideration. Overnutrition cannot be inferred merely from a person's income.
- Mains: production success → unequal consumption → affordability/service gaps → targeted access and monitoring.

## Operation Flood and dairy value chains
- **NDDB, 1970**, **Anand cooperative pattern**, **Verghese Kurien**: village procurement → chilling/processing → urban markets; producer incomes and milk self-sufficiency.
| Phase | Historical summary |
|---|---|
| I, 1970–1980 | Donated European milk-powder/butter-oil sales financed infrastructure; 18 milksheds linked to cities |
| II, 1981–1985 | 136 milksheds, 290 urban markets; 43,000 cooperatives, 4.25 million producer members |
| III, 1985–1996 | Procurement, chilling, transport, feed, veterinary care and artificial insemination; 173 milksheds by 1988–89 |
- **Milk and Milk Products Order 1992**, Essential Commodities Act 1955, is historical regulation; do not treat every old provision as the current food-safety regime. Liberalisation expanded private participation; quality testing matters across ownership types.
- Source dairy exports **2022–23: 67,573 tonnes, US$284.65 million**. Its 24% global share (2021–22), 60% decadal production growth, 40% per-capita availability increase and 6% annual growth versus 2% globally are dated estimates. Rajasthan-first state ranking is not timeless.
- Schemes: **AHIDF**, National Programme for Dairy Development, livestock **KCC**, **Rashtriya Gokul Mission**, National Livestock Mission and food-processing infrastructure support; eligibility and sector scopes differ.
- Low yields, feed/veterinary gaps, FMD/Black Quarter, cold chains, adulteration and low producer margins require animal health, scientific breeding, testing, producer bargaining, processing and market access.
- **2015 Mains PYQ:** rural non-farm income/employment potential; include women/smallholders and sustainable husbandry.
- Embedded news trigger: dairy/agriculture market-access differences complicated India–US trade negotiations; producer livelihoods and food standards require attention alongside export opportunities.
