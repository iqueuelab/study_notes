# Cancer, caregiver costs and children’s education

## Burden beyond hospital bills

- Illness can remove a patient's/caregiver's earnings while increasing travel, accommodation, food and psychological-care costs; uninsured indirect costs can persist even where hospital treatment is covered.
- Children may lose schooling, assume unpaid care or paid work, and experience distress; reduced education can damage future earnings and deepen poverty. Individual stories illustrate mechanisms, not a measured national effect size.
- Source **Sridevi, Hyderabad /Meena, Pune** are reported examples; protect dignity and do not treat every cancer family as having identical outcomes.

## Existing support and gaps

- **PM-JAY** eligible hospital care, State financial assistance, pensions and travel concessions can help, but eligibility/availability differs; source's State/airline list is not a universal guaranteed entitlement.
- Formal employees and informal workers have unequal leave/income protections. Health-insurance coverage does not automatically reimburse all caregiver wages or children's educational losses.

## Policy response

- Need-based caregiver/income support, safe employment protection, accommodation/travel assistance and informed insurance design.
- School retention grants/scholarships, flexible attendance/learning, counselling and teacher coordination; digital learning alone can exclude families without devices or connectivity.
- Link oncology/social work with welfare referral and school support; monitor access, dropout, household financial hardship and follow-up rather than only hospital claims.
- Connect **SDGs 3,4,10**: integrated health, education and inequality policy. Proposals for paid leave/non-medical reimbursement are recommendations, not already universal legal guarantees.
