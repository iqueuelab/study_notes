# Heart failure registry: disease burden and health-system gaps

## Registry findings
- **National Heart Failure Registry, India**: hospital-based acute heart failure study, not a census of every Indian; original recruitment **10,851 patients, 53 tertiary hospitals, 21 states**, mean age about **60**. [Registry study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36214477/).
- January 2025 etiology analysis: **21.1% one-year mortality**; ischemic heart disease **71.9%**, dilated cardiomyopathy **17.3%**, rheumatic heart disease **5.4%** of etiologies. [Primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39746979/).
| Etiology | Reported one-year mortality |
| --- | --- |
| Dilated cardiomyopathy | 23.7% |
| Restrictive cardiomyopathy | 36.8% |
| Infective endocarditis | 50% |
| Rheumatic heart disease | 27.5% |
| Congenital heart disease | 34.6% |
- Rare subgroup estimates have small denominators; do not rank individual prognosis from these percentages. Source's **“die ten years earlier”** headline conflates younger presentation/diagnosis with a measured ten-year loss of survival.
## Prevention and care gaps
- Heart failure = impaired ability to meet circulation needs; not synonymous with heart attack or cardiac arrest. Late presentation, affordability, adherence and uneven specialist access affect outcomes.
- **Rheumatic disease:** immune-mediated valve damage after group-A streptococcal infection. Appropriate antibiotic treatment and secondary prophylaxis are central; source's routine “streptococcal vaccination programme” is not an established available prevention programme. [WHO guideline](https://www.who.int/westernpacific/publications/i/item/9789240100077).
- Strengthen primary-care risk detection/referral, evidence-based treatment/follow-up, rehabilitation and financial protection; rural training and reliable drug access matter. Lifestyle prevention complements disease-specific care.
