# Liver disease and patterns in certified mortality

## Certified deaths: interpret the denominator
- The clipping analyses **annual Medical Certification of Cause of Death (MCCD)** data, **2012–2022**. Digestive-system diseases ranked **eighth among certified causes in 2022**; these are not automatically nationally representative all-death rates.
- Male certified digestive deaths were about **3–3.5 times** female deaths; **3.5:1 in 2022**. Liver-related causes represented **70–75% of male** versus **52–57% of female** digestive-disease deaths across the period; digestive disease and liver disease are not identical categories.
- **2022 age distribution:** about **30% of female digestive deaths at 65+**; male concentration greatest at **45–54**, followed by **35–44**. These are shares of deaths, not age-specific population mortality risk.
- Map: **Sikkim ~19.7%, Mizoram ~17.9%, Manipur ~16.1%, Meghalaya ~12.3%** of certified deaths due to digestive diseases. The text reports five northeastern States above 10%, while only four are labelled in this map. Certification coverage and age/comorbidity differences affect comparisons.
- NFHS alcohol-use comparison cited: **~19% men /1% women**. Alcohol is an established liver-disease risk, but cross-State/sex associations here cannot establish the causes of individual deaths. The source's meat-intake explanation is a **hypothesis requiring clinical/epidemiological testing**, not proof meat caused these regional patterns.
- Revision: improve cause-of-death certification, separate age-standardised rates from raw counts/shares, and combine metabolic-risk, alcohol-use and viral-hepatitis prevention with timely diagnosis.
