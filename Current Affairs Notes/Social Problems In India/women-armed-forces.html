Social Problems In India

Women in the armed forces: inclusion and institutional reform

Editable notes

Source-period inclusion review

  • Defence Minister highlighted expanding women's participation. Source 2024 officer counts: Army 1,735, IAF 1,614, Navy 674, compared with 767/574/154 in 2005. Its 13.4% IAF/6.85% Army/6% Navy figures should be read with officer-category denominators, not as shares of all ranks or nursing/medical personnel combined.
  • 30 May 2025: first 17 female NDA cadets passed out, 148th Course, after entry in 2022. Graduation from NDA precedes further service-academy training/commissioning. MoD milestone.
  • Women serve across Air Force branches; Navy opened branches apart from source-period submarine restrictions; Army entry/PC remains arm/service-specific, not universal entry into every combat role. The PDF's '12 branches including combat' cannot be read as opening infantry/armoured combat arms without restriction.
  • Permanent Commission opportunities expanded, Army aviation helicopter-pilot entry from 2021, women at sea and eligible Agniveer routes are distinct reforms. Training/physical eligibility is role/service-specific; 'identical standards everywhere' is too sweeping.

Equality and operational capacity

  • Articles 14,15,16, broader talent pool and community engagement support inclusion; women exemplified by Col. Sofiya Qureshi/Wing Commander Vyomika Singh challenge stereotypes.
  • Priorities: merit and role-specific standards, gender-sensitive accommodation/equipment, predictable career/command progression, childcare/posting support, fair grievance processes and prevention of harassment. Technology does not eliminate physical demands; care responsibilities should not be assumed exclusively female.
  • Inset chronology: 1888 military nursing, 1958 regular medical commissions, 1992 Women Special Entry, 2005 SSC replacing WSES, 2020 Babita Puniya, 2021 NDA litigation (Kush Kalra). Distinguish medical/nursing, officer and other-rank pathways.
