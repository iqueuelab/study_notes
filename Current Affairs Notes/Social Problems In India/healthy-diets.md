# Healthy diets: affordability and hidden hunger

## Calories are not complete nutrition
- A staple-heavy diet may meet energy needs while lacking protein quality, vitamins and minerals. A healthy diet requires **diversity, adequacy, balance and moderation**; cheap calories alone do not ensure nutritional security.
- **SOFI 2024, using 2022 data:** **2.826 billion people / 35.4%** could not afford a healthy diet; average cost **3.96 PPP dollars per person per day**. These are dated estimates, not a current headcount or US retail-dollar price. The source's “three billion” and $3.67 figures should not be mixed with this dataset. [FAO](https://fao.sitefinity.cloud/newsroom/detail/healthy-diets-remain-unaffordable-for-a-third-of-the-world-s-population/en).
- Affordability depends on income after essential non-food needs and local food prices; the statement that everyone in India must spend all income on food is false. Subsistence farming also need not supply a nutritionally diverse diet.
## Hidden hunger and unequal access
- **Hidden hunger:** micronutrient deficiency, e.g. iron, iodine or vitamin A, even where calories or body weight appear adequate.
- Effects include anaemia, impaired growth/cognition, weaker immunity and lower productivity; maternal and child deprivation can perpetuate disadvantage across generations. Undernutrition and obesity can coexist.
- Poverty, gender inequality, price shocks, weak cold chains, limited local markets and incentives favouring staple monocultures restrict access to diverse foods.
## Policy: nutrition security
- Diversify **PDS, Anganwadi/ICDS services under POSHAN and school meals / PM POSHAN** with locally appropriate nutrient-rich foods; fortification supplements dietary diversity rather than replacing it.
- Promote pulses, millets, fruits/vegetables, kitchen gardens and nutrition-sensitive agriculture; **2023 was the International Year of Millets**. Align incentives with nutritional and ecological outcomes.
- Improve storage/cold chains, reduce perishables loss, support farmer-market links, protect household purchasing power and strengthen nutrition education.
- **SDG 2** (Zero Hunger), **SDG 3** (health), **UDHR Article 25** (adequate standard of living including food); EAT-Lancet connects dietary health with environmental sustainability, not a binding food entitlement.
- Revision chain: low income + high nutritious-food prices → poor dietary diversity → hidden hunger → poorer learning/productivity → persistent poverty. Break the cycle through income support, diverse supply and accessible nutrition services.
