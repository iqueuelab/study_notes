# Edible insects: food culture, nutrition and acceptance

## Food norms and entomophagy
- **Entomophagy = eating insects**. Crickets, grasshoppers and silkworms feature in some food traditions, including **Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh and other northeastern/tribal communities**; practices vary within communities and are not a new Western import.
- The article uses a public food stall to show how familiarity, urbanisation, social status and stigma shape what people call “normal food”. Disgust can be culturally learned and altered through experience; avoid its absolute claim that all aversion is entirely social or that every urban Indian rejects the same foods.
- Insects can supply protein, fats, vitamins and micronutrients, and some production systems use less land/feed/water than conventional livestock. **Nutrition, edible fraction, emissions and resource efficiency vary by species, life stage, feed and processing**; source **80% edible versus ~55% poultry** is an illustrative comparison, not a universal rule. [FAO overview](https://www.fao.org/edible-insects/en).
- Respect indigenous food knowledge without coercing dietary change or presenting it as backward. Cultural acceptability, affordability, livelihoods and informed choice matter alongside sustainability; low-pressure tasting/education can facilitate exchange.
- Safe harvesting/farming, hygiene, processing, traceability and allergen management are necessary; “traditional” and “high protein” do not establish that every insect is edible or safe. [FAO food-safety guidance](https://www.fao.org/edible-insects/our-work/en).
