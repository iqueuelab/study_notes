Social Problems In India

Tobacco affordability, taxation and cancer prevention

Editable notes

Dated burden and affordability

  • GATS-2, India 2016–17, adults 15+: 42.4% men, 14.2% women, 28.6% overall currently used tobacco. These are survey-period rates, not measurements made in May 2025. Smoking and smokeless products have different patterns; the source's global 70% smokeless-user share needs a defined dataset/year. WHO factsheet.
  • WHO-supported study estimates ₹177,341 crore economic costs from tobacco-attributable disease/deaths among people aged 35+ in 2017–18, about 1.04% of GDP: roughly 74% smoking/26% smokeless. Direct care, morbidity/productivity and premature mortality costs are included. This is neither a measured 2025 annual loss nor the cost of all cancer alone. WHO account.
  • Low unit prices, single-stick/sachet sales, rising purchasing power, product substitution and tax undershifting can sustain affordability. Prices by pack, stick and sachet are not interchangeable; source ₹1–₹5, ₹5–₹95, 87% vendors, 450-million middle class and 88-country ban claims lack a consistent national sample/year.
  • Tobacco causes oral, lung and other cancers and cardiovascular/respiratory disease; second-hand smoke harms non-users. India is not first worldwide for all male cancer incidence/mortality—the source conflates site-specific oral-cancer burden with all cancers.

Tax and wider policy

  • WHO benchmark at least 75% of retail price in total taxes is a tax-share indicator. A proposed 35% GST slab is an ad-valorem rate with a different denominator and ignores other taxes/cess; the PDF's direct 35-versus-75 comparison is invalid. Proposal reporting does not establish enactment as of this issue. WHO tax benchmark.
  • Taxes should raise real prices faster than income growth, cover product substitutes, and limit evasion; assess burden alongside reduced disease/household spending and accessible cessation. Manufacturer price responses mean tax-rate change does not ensure equal retail-price change.
  • Single-stick restrictions, plain packaging, stronger zoning and earmarking taxes are recommendations here, not all newly enacted universal rules. COTPA already restricts sales to minors and within 100 yards of educational institutions; “near every tea stall” is not the existing nationwide statutory rule.

August 2025: smokeless tobacco and implementation

  • Editorial argues the control system underaddresses smokeless products, surrogate branding, films/OTT/social promotion, single-unit affordability and limited cessation access. COTPA 2003 does cover scheduled smokeless tobacco products; a claim of inadequate enforcement is not absence of legal coverage.
  • 85% of front/back principal display areas carry health warnings under packaging rules; do not interpret this as 85% of every surface of a package. 2019 electronic-cigarette prohibition covers manufacture, import/export, transport, sale/distribution, storage and advertising; online availability indicates an enforcement gap, not legalisation.
  • Source estimates ₹1,773.4 billion tobacco-use burden and ₹566.7 billion second-hand-smoke burden, tax shares of 22% bidis/50% cigarettes, and global 8 million/1.3 million deaths require their underlying study/reference years. Do not add costs from unlike studies into one current national total. Earlier notes preserve the dated 2017–18 tobacco-cost study.
  • Strengthen NTCP, tobacco-free educational institutions, family/teacher participation from early schooling, cessation services and regular evaluation. Poverty/stress and commercial determinants need attention alongside awareness. Independent research/oversight, stronger indirect-ad bans, plain packaging and multi-ministry “endgame” strategy are recommendations, not all newly enacted rules.
