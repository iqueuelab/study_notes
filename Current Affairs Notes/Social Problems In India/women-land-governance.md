# Women, land rights and environmental governance

## May 2025 editorial: agency without authority
- Adivasi, Dalit and rural women organise environmental/anti-displacement resistance yet may lack title, consultation influence, compensation eligibility and formal leadership. Examples: **Sijimali, Odisha** bauxite; **Jharkhand coal-mining resistance**; **Kudankulam, Tamil Nadu** fishing communities/nuclear plant; **Phulbari, Bangladesh** open-pit mining; **Narmada Bachao Andolan/Medha Patkar** dam displacement. The rewritten source's **“Dewas, Jharkhand”** is geographically unreliable; Dewas city/district is in Madhya Pradesh, so retain only the supported Jharkhand example without inventing a corrected village.
- **Free, prior and informed consent (FPIC)** demands timely, understandable information and freedom from coercion; presence in a male-dominated meeting does not prove meaningful influence. Women's ecological knowledge can be dismissed as emotional while title/household-head rules obscure unpaid work and resource dependence.
## Legal safeguards and limits
- **Forest Rights Act 2006**: individual rights registered jointly for married spouses; **FRA Rules** require women's representation in Forest Rights Committees and Gram Sabha quorum. Implementation and actual voice still need monitoring. [Tribal Ministry framework](https://tribal.nic.in/FRA.aspx), [Rules](https://tribal.nic.in/downloads/acts-and-rules/FRARulesBook.pdf).
- **PESA 1996** protects Scheduled-Area Gram Sabha/community powers; distinguish consultation and consent requirements under particular laws rather than claiming every development project has identical FPIC procedure.
- **Hindu Succession Amendment 2005** grants equal daughter coparcenary rights within its applicable framework; **Section 2(2)** generally excludes Scheduled Tribes absent a Central notification. It cannot be assumed to replace all tribal customary inheritance. [Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1713/1/AAA1956suc___30.pdf).
- Nepal joint-ownership and Bangladesh **khas-land distribution** are comparative arrangements cited by the editorial, not uniform guaranteed individual entitlements for every woman.
## Climate justice and reform proposals
- Resource depletion can increase water/fuel collection, care work and livelihood insecurity; exposure differs by caste, class, community and place. **Ecofeminism** links domination of women/nature as an analytical lens, not a substitute for evidence.
- Accessible local-language/translated technical information, safe meeting spaces/timing, legal literacy/aid and skilled facilitation; women-only preparatory consultations where helpful, followed by meaningful authority in legally required decision bodies.
- Joint/individual titles, compensation recognising independent livelihood and non-titleholder losses, inclusion of single women/widows/caregivers, representative negotiation/rehabilitation boards and funded leadership training. These are recommendations; assess actual consent, influence and benefit distribution rather than attendance alone.
