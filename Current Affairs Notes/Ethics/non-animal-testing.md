# Non-animal biological models and research ethics

## Ethical and scientific case
- Editorial urges replacement of avoidable live-animal experiments with **human-relevant biological models**: cultured tissues/organoids, engineered skin/muscle/cartilage, organ-on-chip systems, 3D models and computational methods.
- Animals can suffer; human welfare and scientific validity must be balanced with compassion and minimisation of harm. Species differences can limit prediction, but this does not prove every animal experiment is scientifically useless or every alternative already replaces a whole organism.
- **Replacement, reduction and refinement (3Rs)** provide the practical frame: use validated alternatives where suitable, minimise animal numbers and suffering when still necessary, and scrutinise study design.
- Proposed amendment to **Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act 1960** would encourage alternatives; it is the author's reform recommendation, not a newly enacted clause. Tissue engineering/regenerative medicine offers experimental models, not proof all listed full organs are routinely manufactured for testing.
- Ethical action: fund validation and shared facilities, train researchers, integrate alternatives in education, transparent review and welfare oversight; change scientific incentives as well as legal language.
