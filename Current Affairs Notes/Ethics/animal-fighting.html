Ethics

Cockfighting: law, tradition and animal ethics

Editable notes

Issue and law

  • Cockfighting pits roosters against each other, sometimes with sharp blades/spurs; injury/death and associated unlawful betting. Source discusses Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and Kerala; customary popularity does not confer legal exemption.
  • Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960, notably section 11(1)(m)/(n) provisions concerning entertainment involving fighting and organising places for animal fights. Act text.
  • Source cites IPC 428/429: these dealt with mischief by killing/maiming animals, not every unnecessary-pain offence. For January 2025, IPC references are historical: Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, section 325, operates from 1 July 2024, subject to offence facts. BNS.
  • Source's 2017 Supreme Court narrative concerns enforcement of the Andhra Pradesh prohibition; avoid reading it as an independently verified blanket new national ruling on every animal sport.
  • PETA = People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals; PETA India and its expanded name are not two unrelated organisations.

Ethics: values → action

Competing claimAssessment
Cultural continuity / livelihoodsRespect communities; provide lawful alternatives
Animal welfarePrevent avoidable suffering and lethal entertainment
Rule of lawConsistent enforcement, fair procedure, curb unlawful gambling
Public responsibilityArticle 51A(g): compassion for living creatures
  • Engage local leaders, run awareness campaigns, support nonviolent festival competitions and alternative livelihoods, and enforce anti-cruelty laws without selective tolerance.
  • Tradition is relevant context, not a sufficient ethical defence of cruelty; evaluate harm, legality, compassion and practical alternatives together.
