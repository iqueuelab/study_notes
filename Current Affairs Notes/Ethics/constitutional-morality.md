# Constitutional morality: values, methods and limits

## Meaning and origin

- Fidelity to constitutional forms, lawful authority, rights and democratic restraint; liberty, equality, dignity, pluralism and accountability guide public conduct.
- **George Grote**'s account influenced **Ambedkar's 4 November 1948** Constituent Assembly speech. Constitutional habits must be cultivated; institutional loyalty and lawful dissent matter amid partisan disagreement. [Assembly debate](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/04-nov-1948/).
- Not explicitly named as a defined constitutional provision; the idea draws from constitutional text, structure and commitments, not a decision-maker's personal moral preference.

## Application and critique

- Officials: act within powers, give reasons, treat opponents/citizens equally and resist partisan pressure. Citizens: participate, scrutinise government and respect lawful institutions.
- Benefits: minority protection, rule of law, accountable institutions and inclusive citizenship. Critiques: vagueness, selective use, judicial overreach and weak popular legitimacy.
- Response: transparent reasons grounded in text/precedent, consistent standards, context-sensitive proportionality, institutional restraint and active democratic oversight.
- **Constitutional morality ≠popular approval of every judgment**; protect rights while explaining how the relevant institution has authority to act.
