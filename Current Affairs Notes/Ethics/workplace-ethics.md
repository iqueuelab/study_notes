# Workplace ethics, overwork and duty of care

## Case and ethical questions
- Source discusses **Anna Sebastian Perayil, 26, EY employee**: her mother's allegations of excessive work pressure and insensitive organisational response. Treat alleged causation/responsibility as allegations, not a proved medical or judicial finding.
- Long hours, unrealistic expectations, fear of retaliation and insecure performance assessment can erode dignity and health; wellness branding must match daily management practices.
- Relevant values: **duty of care, empathy, fairness, integrity, accountability, dignity and confidentiality**. Credit misappropriation and silencing grievances undermine trust.
## Claim → evidence → judgement
- Source debate over **18 hours/day for 4–5 years** and demands for **70-hour weeks**: productivity cannot be assumed proportional to hours; fatigue, error, inequality and health costs matter.
- Entrepreneurial autonomy/reward and salaried employees' bargaining power differ; entrepreneurship is not automatically a safe or feasible remedy for overwork.
- **WHO–ILO study published 2021** estimated **745,000 deaths in 2016** from stroke/ischaemic heart disease attributable to **≥55 working hours/week**, globally—not India alone or a count of every cause of overwork death. [WHO–ILO findings](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2021-long-working-hours-increasing-deaths-from-heart-disease-and-stroke-who-ilo).
## Ethical management flow
- **Identify excessive demand → assess health/workload risks → redistribute or resource work → protect rest and grievance access → review outcomes**.
- Leaders: realistic deadlines, adequate staffing, fair credit/compensation, confidential counselling, non-retaliation, documented escalation and empathetic support after crises.
- Employees: communicate constraints, record concerns, seek support and use safe reporting channels; individual resilience cannot replace organisational duty.
- Evaluate actual workload, grievance resolution, turnover and wellbeing rather than counting wellness events. Voluntary overtime/pay does not remove the need for safe conditions.
- Mains conclusion: success includes health and dignity; reconcile performance with sustainable work and accountable leadership.
