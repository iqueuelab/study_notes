Ethics

Disease names, scientific precision and stigma

Editable notes

Naming principles

  • Geographic labels can wrongly imply origin and stigmatise communities. Spanish flu describes reporting history, not an established Spanish origin; legacy examples include Delhi boil and Madura foot.
  • WHO's 2015 guidance for new common human disease names avoids places, persons, animals and offensive terms. It does not govern scientific pathogen taxonomy or require every legacy name to change. WHO guidance.
  • Trichophyton indotineae is a dermatophyte fungus associated with difficult, often terbinafine-resistant ringworm; its name does not establish where it originated.
  • Mpox naming illustrates stigma reduction. Reactive arthritis replacing Reiter's eponym also reflects concerns about the person's Nazi associations; this was not simply a WHO order.
  • Ethical test: scientific clarity → neutral communication → public trust → cooperation with surveillance and treatment. Criticise nomenclature without blaming patients or populations.
