Blue Ghost, Resilience and commercial lunar missions

Editable notes

One rocket, two missions

  • 15 January 2025, SpaceX Falcon 9, Kennedy Space Center, Florida: Firefly Aerospace Blue Ghost (USA) and ispace Resilience (Japan) rideshare launch.
LanderJanuary plan
Blue GhostRoughly 45-day journey, 10 NASA instruments, Mare Crisium near Mons Latreille, northeastern near side
ResilienceRoughly 4–5 months, Mare Frigoris, northern lunar region; Tenacious micro-rover, ispace-Europe
  • Blue Ghost is a NASA CLPS delivery, supporting science/technology and Artemis goals; sharing its launcher does not make the Japanese mission itself a NASA CLPS contract.
  • Investigations: dust, lunar interior, Earth magnetosphere interactions, radiation-tolerant navigation/computing; rover/regolith handling for Resilience. NASA payloads.

Landing and dated outcomes

  • Essentially no atmosphere → parachutes cannot provide Earth-like braking; powered descent/navigation must avoid rocks/craters. Rideshare saves launch cost but does not guarantee landing success.
  • Later: Blue Ghost landed 2 March 2025; Resilience's June 2025 attempt failed, communication lost/no successful landing. Keep launch intentions separate from outcomes. ispace update.
  • Intuitive Machines achieved the first commercial soft landing in 2024; commercial providers complement national agencies, not eliminate their role in science/funding/regulation.

Country recall

  • Intuitive Machines is American, not Chinese; Blue Ghost → Firefly/USA, Resilience → ispace/Japan. Nationality of company differs from launch provider.
