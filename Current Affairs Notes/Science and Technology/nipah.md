# Nipah virus and zoonotic surveillance

## May 2025 Kerala report
- Embedded clipping reports a **42-year-old woman** testing positive in **Malappuram**, treated at **Perinthalmanna**; health authorities traced contacts and imposed surveillance/restrictions around her Valanchery residence. Initial seven close-contact tests were negative. The infection source was **unknown**; investigating local-fruit exposure did not establish a confirmed transmission route.
## Pathogen and transmission
- **Nipah (NiV): RNA virus, Paramyxoviridae, Henipavirus**; related to Hendra. Identified in **1998–99 Malaysia/Singapore** outbreaks involving pigs; pigs were amplification hosts, **Pteropus fruit bats** the natural reservoir. Other domestic animals can be infected.
- Bat-secretions-contaminated food, including **raw date-palm sap**, and infected-animal exposure can transmit infection. Close contact with infected patients/fluids, including healthcare exposure, supports human-to-human spread; bat urine/saliva contamination is especially relevant.
- Clinical spectrum: fever/headache, drowsiness/confusion and severe **encephalitis/coma**, sometimes acute respiratory illness. Case fatality typically **40–75%**, varying by outbreak/care; source's ~70% is not universal. [CDC clinical overview](https://www.cdc.gov/nipah-virus/hcp/clinical-overview/index.html).
- **RT-PCR** and serology support diagnosis. No licensed specific antiviral/vaccine in the source period; intensive supportive care is central. Experimental candidates are not approved routine treatment.
- Kerala outbreaks since **2018**; India also has earlier West Bengal outbreaks. ICMR/NIV diagnostics, human/animal surveillance, rapid isolation/contact follow-up, PPE, hospital infection control and food-safety awareness are priorities. Avoid contaminated/partly eaten fruit and unprotected sap collection; protecting bat habitats is compatible with reducing risky exposure.
- WHO priority R&D disease: zoonotic spillover, severe disease and limited countermeasures support a **One Health** approach; priority status is not evidence a pandemic has begun.

## October–November 2025: indigenous antibody development call
- ICMR invited an **Expression of Interest, E/252645/CD/2025**, for development/manufacture of **monoclonal antibodies against Nipah**, closing **6 November 2025**. This is a collaboration/development call, not approval or commercial availability of an indigenous therapy. [ICMR call](https://www.icmr.gov.in/post/invitation-for-expression-of-interest-eoi-eoi-no-e-252645-cd-2025-for-development-and-manufacturing-of-monoclonal-antibodies-against-nipah-viral-disease-by-icmr-hq-new-delhi-last-date-november-06-2025).
- **mAbs** are laboratory-produced antibodies directed at particular antigens; anti-Nipah candidates seek to block viral entry and are being investigated for post-exposure prophylaxis/treatment. Do not translate preclinical potential into guaranteed prevention, reduced mortality or a licensed routine treatment. The issue's “only viable countermeasure” overstates evidence: supportive care, infection prevention and other investigational approaches remain important.
- Domestic manufacturing/stockpile capability could reduce import dependence and improve outbreak readiness if safety, efficacy, regulatory approval and deployment are established. **ICMR–NIV, Pune** provides virology expertise; One Health surveillance, PPE/contact follow-up and safe food practices remain essential. Existing notes retain reservoir, transmission, symptoms and **40–75% case-fatality** qualification.

## January 2026: West Bengal health response
- Embedded clipping reports **two healthcare workers at a private hospital in Barasat, North 24 Parganas**, with preliminary Nipah-positive tests, critical-care treatment, contact tracing and Central/State coordination. A central multidisciplinary team and technical support were deployed; **AIIMS Kalyani screening and NIV Pune confirmation are separate diagnostic stages**, not proof every suspected contact was infected.
- WHO later documented that NIV confirmation occurred **13 January**; this retrospectively clarifies the diagnostic date without importing later contact outcomes into this issue. [WHO outbreak chronology](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON593).
- Earlier West Bengal outbreaks **Siliguri 2001 / Nadia 2007** precede Kerala's 2018 emergence. The source's old outbreak counts and ~70% fatality are historical comparisons; the common **40–75% outbreak-dependent case-fatality** qualification remains. Pigs were amplification hosts in the original Malaysian outbreak, while **Pteropus bats are the reservoir**; no particular spillover route for these Bengal cases is established here.
- Existing pathogen, RT-PCR/serology, supportive-care and One Health notes apply. Clinical suspicion calls for isolation/contact follow-up and hospital infection prevention, not a claim a pandemic has begun or that experimental antibodies are licensed routine therapy.
