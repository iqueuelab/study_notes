# Nipah virus and zoonotic surveillance

## May 2025 Kerala report
- Embedded clipping reports a **42-year-old woman** testing positive in **Malappuram**, treated at **Perinthalmanna**; health authorities traced contacts and imposed surveillance/restrictions around her Valanchery residence. Initial seven close-contact tests were negative. The infection source was **unknown**; investigating local-fruit exposure did not establish a confirmed transmission route.
## Pathogen and transmission
- **Nipah (NiV): RNA virus, Paramyxoviridae, Henipavirus**; related to Hendra. Identified in **1998–99 Malaysia/Singapore** outbreaks involving pigs; pigs were amplification hosts, **Pteropus fruit bats** the natural reservoir. Other domestic animals can be infected.
- Bat-secretions-contaminated food, including **raw date-palm sap**, and infected-animal exposure can transmit infection. Close contact with infected patients/fluids, including healthcare exposure, supports human-to-human spread; bat urine/saliva contamination is especially relevant.
- Clinical spectrum: fever/headache, drowsiness/confusion and severe **encephalitis/coma**, sometimes acute respiratory illness. Case fatality typically **40–75%**, varying by outbreak/care; source's ~70% is not universal. [CDC clinical overview](https://www.cdc.gov/nipah-virus/hcp/clinical-overview/index.html).
- **RT-PCR** and serology support diagnosis. No licensed specific antiviral/vaccine in the source period; intensive supportive care is central. Experimental candidates are not approved routine treatment.
- Kerala outbreaks since **2018**; India also has earlier West Bengal outbreaks. ICMR/NIV diagnostics, human/animal surveillance, rapid isolation/contact follow-up, PPE, hospital infection control and food-safety awareness are priorities. Avoid contaminated/partly eaten fruit and unprotected sap collection; protecting bat habitats is compatible with reducing risky exposure.
- WHO priority R&D disease: zoonotic spillover, severe disease and limited countermeasures support a **One Health** approach; priority status is not evidence a pandemic has begun.
