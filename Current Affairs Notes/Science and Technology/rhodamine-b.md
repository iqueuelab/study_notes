# Rhodamine B and food adulteration

## Industrial dye ≠ food colour
- Water-soluble **fluorescent red/pink dye**, used in textiles, paper, leather/paints and laboratories. Source green-powder description is not a dependable identification test for every formulation.
- **Not a permitted food colour in India**; adulterated cotton candy and other foods illustrate enforcement concern. Applicable law: **Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006**, with food-additive regulations; penalties depend on offence and harm, not one universal sentence.
- Toxicological concerns include oxidative/cellular injury and possible organ/neurotoxicity/carcinogenicity; animal/laboratory hazard evidence does not establish every exposure causes human cancer.
- Source blanket “banned worldwide / global consensus” is not a verified survey of all countries' law. **Bright colour alone cannot identify the chemical**; laboratory testing and enforcement are necessary.
- Licensing, supply-chain checks, approved additives, targeted inspection/testing and public awareness; avoid equating an industrial dye with a safe edible additive.
