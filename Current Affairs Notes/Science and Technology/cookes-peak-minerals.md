# Cookes Peak: new minerals and crystal polymorphism

## Cookes Peak mineral discoveries
- **Raydemarkite, virgilluethite and stunorthropite** occur at **Cookes Peak, Luna County, New Mexico, USA**, a mineral-rich mountain with a granodioritic core. Mineral species are naturally occurring crystalline solids distinguished by composition and structure; finding a natural analogue of a synthetic compound establishes a mineral occurrence, not a previously unknown chemical element.
- Raydemarkite: flexible, slender needle-like crystals, about **1 mm**; **MoO₃·H₂O**. Virgilluethite has the **same composition but different crystal structure** (polymorphism), with sheet-like architecture. Stunorthropite forms milky, short blade-like crystals.
- Correct the diagram wording: raydemarkite contains double chains of **MoO₆ / MoO₅(H₂O) coordination octahedra**, linked through hydrogen bonding; “MoO₅ octahedra” omits the sixth coordinating oxygen. [Original mineral description](https://www.geo.arizona.edu/xtal/group/pdf/TCJoMaP61_2023_203-213.pdf).
- Natural–synthetic comparisons clarify crystallisation, hydration/dehydration and mineral evolution. Related molybdenum oxides attract battery, sensing and catalytic research; this is **not proof the three rare natural minerals are commercially mined for those uses**.
- June coverage revisits discoveries already documented before 2025, including the **2023** mineral descriptions. Cookes Peak is **8,404 ft** high; raydemarkite is the natural analogue of a compound synthesised in **1903**. The source's **5,998** world-mineral count is a dated snapshot, not a fixed total. [New Mexico Bureau report](https://geoinfo.nmt.edu/news/home.cfml?id=750); [2023 symposium](https://geoinfo.nmt.edu/museum/nmms/abstracts/2023/2023-NMMS_p17.pdf).
