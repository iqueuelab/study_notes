ATAGS and indigenous artillery procurement

Editable notes

System and procurement

  • Advanced Towed Artillery Gun System: DRDO-led development with Bharat Forge and Tata Advanced Systems; initiated in 2013, unveiled in 2016.
  • 155 mm, 52-calibre towed howitzer: calibre length here means barrel length relative to bore diameter, not 52 mm ammunition.
  • Source March 2025 plan: procurement of 307 guns, roughly ₹7,000 crore; approval/signature differs from delivery and induction.
  • Trials demonstrated ranges around 47–48 km with appropriate ammunition; this is not every shell's guaranteed range or proof of superiority on every metric.
  • Electric drives, electronic controls, burst firing and repositioning support use. “All-electric” does not mean maintenance-free equipment.
  • Weight above 18 tonnes creates transport/high-altitude constraints; a separate lighter TGS requirement is distinct. Assess reliability, ammunition compatibility, mobility, production capacity and life-cycle costs, not just unit-price comparisons.
  • Reported explanations for a trial accident require investigation evidence; do not convert an unsupported attribution into a universal safety conclusion.

Capability revision

  • ARDE, Pune, DRDO laboratory, led design with Bharat Forge/Tata partners. Source table distinguishes rapid firing, sustained fire and shoot-and-scoot mobility; five rounds/minute and up to 60/hour should not be treated as one continuous firing rate.
  • High-angle short-range engagement, electric drives and compatibility with different 155-mm ammunition are operational features; guided-munition integration is a capability/development claim requiring matching ammunition, not proof every shell is precision guided.
