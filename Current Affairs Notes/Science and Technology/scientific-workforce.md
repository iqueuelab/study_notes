# Scientific workforce and the leaky research pipeline

## 1 May 2025: research staffing concern
- The issue cites a PSA-commissioned study of **244 public R&D institutions**, with **19,625 contractual** against **12,042 permanent staff in 2022–23**; contractual personnel rose **14%** over 2021–22 and fewer institutions reported permanent hiring.
- The newspaper clipping expressly says the study **excluded strategic sectors** such as defence, atomic energy and space. Do not mistake this sample for the entire Indian scientific workforce.
- Separately, a parliamentary committee reported roughly **three-fifths of TIFR sanctioned scientific posts vacant** and around **one-quarter of sanctioned posts** at major atomic-energy research institutions/nuclear power plants vacant. TIFR is supported primarily by DAE. These are separate data sources, not subgroup totals from the 244-institution study.
- “Leaky pipeline” here means attrition and difficulty retaining researchers through career stages, not exclusively women's exit from STEM. Insecure contracts, low incentives, vacancies and inadequate equipment disrupt continuity and mentoring.
- Quantum/AI missions and IISER/degree expansion require sustained human capacity. Priorities: timely transparent recruitment, stable career pathways, reasonable pay, research funding/equipment and protection of long-term basic research; these are recommendations, not proof missions have failed.
