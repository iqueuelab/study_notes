# CMS-03 and LVM3: maritime communications

## October 2025: launch preparation
- **CMS-03**, reported in the issue as **GSAT-7R**, was scheduled for **2 November 2025**, on **LVM3-M5** from Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota. As of this issue, launch was prospective. ISRO's pre-launch description confirms a roughly **4,400-kg multiband communications satellite**, serving a broad oceanic region and Indian landmass, and movement to the launch pad on **26 October**. [ISRO mission record](https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO_EN/LVM3_M5_CMS_03_MISSION.html).
- Source frames it as secure naval/Armed Forces connectivity, building on **GSAT-7 (2013)** and **GSAT-7A (2018)**: ships, aircraft and ground stations need resilient links for coordination, HADR and maritime-domain awareness. A communications satellite supports information exchange; it is not itself proof of a new radar-imaging or weapons capability.
- LVM3: **two S200 solid boosters + L110 liquid core + C25 cryogenic upper stage**. **GTO** is an elliptical transfer orbit, distinct from the final geostationary operating orbit. Payload capability depends on target orbit/configuration; the PDF's 4–8-tonne range is not one GTO capacity.
- The heaviest-communications-satellite claim is specifically **launched to GTO from Indian soil**, not India's heaviest satellite ever built or every orbit's heaviest launch. Exact military payload bands, encryption and tri-service integration should not be inferred from a generic public multiband statement.

## 2 November 2025: LVM3-M5 launches CMS-03
- **CMS-03**, called **GSAT-7R** in the issue's naval-communications reporting, launched from **SDSC, Sriharikota**, into **GTO** on **2 November 2025**. ISRO's mission brochure gives **about 4,410 kg**; the record is the heaviest communication satellite launched to GTO **from Indian soil at that time**, not the heaviest Indian satellite ever built or launched anywhere. Multiband services cover a wide oceanic region and Indian landmass. [ISRO mission and brochure](https://www.isro.gov.in/LVM3_M5_CMS_03_MISSION.html).
- **Fifth operational flight of LVM3**: the launch-preparation note already retains vehicle stages, GTO versus final geostationary orbit, naval applications and the record qualification. This update records the successful launch, not another prospective schedule.
- Heavy lift supports launch autonomy and experience relevant to human spaceflight, but the **human-rated Gaganyaan launcher requires separate qualification**. Do not repeat the source's implication that ordinary LVM3 had already flown Gaganyaan crew/test missions. **IN-SPACe facilitates non-government space activity; Defence Space Agency has a distinct military role**.
