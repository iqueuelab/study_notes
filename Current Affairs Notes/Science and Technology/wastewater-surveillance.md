# Wastewater surveillance and sewage treatment

## ICMR expansion proposal
- August source reports **10 viruses across 50 cities within six months**, from an existing five-city effort. This is a planned expansion, not evidence all sites were operational at publication. Includes viruses associated with fever, diarrhoea, encephalitis/respiratory disease and avian-influenza surveillance in risk areas; complements **ILI, SARI and IDSP** surveillance.
- Infected people shed pathogen material → sewage sampling before treatment → laboratory detection/trend analysis → targeted clinical testing and public-health response. Can include asymptomatic/pre-symptomatic infections and reveal trends before clinical reports.
- **Community signal, not individual diagnosis**: dilution, sewer coverage, shedding biology, sampling/lab delay and changing population affect interpretation. The source's “5–7 days” is neither universal lead time nor instantaneous real-time measurement.
## Wastewater impacts and treatment
- Household sewage, industrial effluent and agricultural runoff have different contaminants. Excess N/P → eutrophication/algal growth → oxygen depletion/fish deaths; sewage pathogens threaten water supplies.
- Primary treatment: physical separation/sedimentation. Secondary: biological oxidation, including **activated sludge**. Tertiary: targeted polishing, e.g. **ion exchange, reverse osmosis, adsorption, chemical oxidation/disinfection**. A process train must match contaminants and reuse standards.
- Organic wastes can supply biogas; treated water supports safe industrial/agricultural reuse. Decentralised plants complement sewers; **Namami Gange, AMRUT 2.0 and sanitation programmes** address different infrastructure links. Source **~72,000 MLD/~40% treated** needs reference-year/capacity-versus-actual-treatment qualification.
- **BOD** measures oxygen microorganisms require for degradable organic matter under specified conditions (often five days, 20°C); high BOD usually signals organic pollution. It is not oxygen concentration itself. **2017 CSE BOD PYQ (c)**.
- **2017 bioremediation PYQ: 1 and 3 (c)**. Microbes can degrade some organic pollutants and be engineered; heavy metals are elements, cannot be biodegraded into nothing, though immobilisation/transformation/concentration can help remediation. The separate tertiary-treatment question is **ESE**, not CSE.
