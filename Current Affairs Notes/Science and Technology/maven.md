# MAVEN: Martian atmospheric escape and relay communications

## December 2025: signal loss under investigation
- NASA's **MAVEN—Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution** lost contact **6 December** after passing behind Mars. Routine telemetry last received **4 December**; recovered tracking fragment from 6 December suggested unexpected rotation and possible trajectory change, **not a confirmed cause or proven recovery**. [NASA's 15 December update](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/maven/2025/12/15/nasa-continues-maven-spacecraft-recontact-efforts/).
- Studies upper atmosphere/ionosphere, atmospheric escape and interaction with sunlight/solar wind, explaining Mars's climate/water evolution. Rover UHF relay links **Curiosity/Perseverance** with Earth; NASA adjusted operations using **Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, Mars Odyssey and ESA ExoMars Trace Gas Orbiter**.
- Launched **18 November 2013, Cape Canaveral**; Mars insertion **21 September 2014 US time**. Primary science phase was **one Earth year**, not the PDF's two years; ten-plus years of operation was an extended mission. [NASA mission record](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/maven/).
- ISRO's **Mars Orbiter Mission/Mangalyaan** entered orbit **24 September 2014**, primarily a technology demonstrator with scientific payloads. Its objectives differ from MAVEN; both illustrate orbital insertion, deep-space communications and the value of redundancy. Keep the December uncertainty separate from any later mission-ending decision.
