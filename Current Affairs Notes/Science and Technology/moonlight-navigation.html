Polarised moonlight and nocturnal bull-ant navigation

Editable notes

Physics and experiment

  • Polarisation concerns electric-field orientation; scattering creates patterned partial polarisation in sky light. It is not simply loss of brightness or conversion to infrared.
  • Moonlight reflects sunlight; its much weaker celestial polarisation pattern can supply orientation cues even when the Moon is obscured.
  • Myrmecia pyriformis /M. midas, Australian nocturnal bull ants: researchers rotated polarisation filters above returning ants; systematic heading shifts supported use of the e-vector compass.
  • Evidence extends across tested full/quarter/crescent conditions, with light level influencing performance. Source's “second animal” is a discovery-summary claim, not a complete count of all species capable of this behaviour.
  • Dung beetles also use nocturnal celestial/polarisation cues; bees/ants are known for solar cues. Species capabilities and experimental conditions should not be generalised to every insect.
  • Research published in eLife 2024, not necessarily a new study first conducted in February 2025. University account.

Recall

  • Ambient polarisation rotated →heading changes →evidence for directional use, not proof ants use no landmarks, smells or other navigation information.
  • Sensory adaptation, ecology and bio-inspired navigation; supplied questions are coaching practice, not official PYQs.
