# Tularemia / rabbit fever

## Agent and transmission

- **Francisella tularensis — bacterium**, not virus; zoonosis affecting animals and humans, notably rabbits, hares and rodents.
- Human exposure: **tick/deer-fly bites**, contact with infected animals, contaminated water/food, aerosols/dust and laboratory exposure; route determines manifestations.
- Fever, skin ulcers/lymph-node involvement, eye inflammation, sore throat or respiratory disease; can be severe.
- Antibiotics are used; source says no US vaccine available. Clinical treatment depends on appropriate medical assessment, not one generic remedy.

## January 2025 trigger and trap

- US surveillance: **2,462 cases during 2011–22**, incidence **56% higher than 2001–10**; highest incidence among **5–9-year-olds, older men, American Indian/Alaska Native persons and central-US residents**. These are surveillance patterns, not the only susceptible groups. [CDC MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm735152a1.htm).
- Tick/deer-fly exposure is important in the US context; do not infer from the PDF practice answer that all other transmission routes/vectors are impossible in every region.
