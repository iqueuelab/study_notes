# Quantum key distribution and entanglement

## June2025 demonstration
- **DRDO–IIT Delhi**, via DIA-CoE, demonstrated entanglement-based secure communication over a **free-space optical link exceeding1km** on the IIT campus; announced **16 June2025**, key rate **nearly240bits/s**, **QBER below7%**. [DRDO release](https://drdo.gov.in/drdo/en/documents/press-release/drdo-iit-delhi-demonstrate-quantum-entanglement-based-free-space-quantum).
- **QKD distributes encryption key material**, not the whole message. Photons encode quantum states; measurement correlations plus authenticated classical communication, error estimation/correction and privacy amplification establish a shared key.
- **Prepare-and-measure** protocols (e.g. BB84) differ from **entanglement-based** protocols. Entanglement creates statistical correlations; it does **not transmit usable information instantaneously**, nor does every disturbance establish deliberate spying.
- **QBER** is the fraction of differing bits; excessive error can reveal noise/eavesdropping and require aborting. It is not an infallible real-time intruder alarm. A qubit is a quantum two-level system that can be in superposition; a photon may carry a qubit.
- Earlier Indian milestones cited: **Vindhyachal–Prayagraj intercity fibre link2022** and **100km entanglement-based fibre QKD2024**. Free-space, fibre and satellite links face different loss and infrastructure constraints.
- Potential uses: defence, government, finance and future quantum networks. Limits: equipment cost, optical loss/weather/alignment, detector/source vulnerabilities, integration and denial-of-service. **Authentication is still required**; QKD does not itself authenticate a stranger. Security depends on protocol assumptions and implementation; 'future-proof/unhackable' overstates it.
