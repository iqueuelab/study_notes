# Burevestnik: nuclear propulsion and strategic signalling

## October 2025: Russian missile-test claim
- Issue reports Putin/Gerasimov announcing a **Burevestnik (Storm Petrel)** test: about **14,000 km and 15 hours**. These are Russian reported figures, not independently established combat performance. [Contemporary report of the Russian announcement](https://apnews.com/article/dd6a424d6c545ad42848416b77e93619). A successful test claim does not prove operational deployment, a completed doctrine or invulnerability.
- Ground-launched **nuclear-powered, nuclear-capable cruise missile**: nuclear propulsion supplies flight energy; nuclear-capable describes a possible warhead. These are distinct properties. Long endurance and low-altitude/changeable routes aim to complicate defence; 'unlimited' is a theoretical/design claim, not literally infinite endurance.
- Contrasting every conventional cruise missile as a predictable ballistic trajectory is wrong: cruise missiles generally use sustained powered aerodynamic flight and can manoeuvre, while ballistic missiles follow different flight profiles.
- Source frames development as deterrence messaging amid missile-defence projects, arms-control breakdown and the Ukraine war. The **INF Treaty (1987)** covered ground-launched ballistic/cruise missiles in the **500–5,500-km range**, including conventional as well as nuclear variants; it was not restricted to nuclear warheads.
- Nuclear propulsion creates accident, radiation and environmental concerns. The source associates the **2019 Arctic accident** with testing, but precise causation/system details require corroboration; do not treat alleged risks as proof a 2025 test caused contamination or as proof of imminent attack.
