Science and Technology

Gene-edited sheep and CRISPR livestock research

Editable notes

May 2025 research milestone

  • SKUAST-Kashmir, team led by Riyaz Ahmad Shah, reported India's first gene-edited sheep after four years of research. CRISPR-Cas 9 targets myostatin, a brake on muscle growth; ~30% more muscle is a reported/expected trait, not proof herd-wide commercial productivity or farmer-income gains. Similar muscling occurs naturally in Texel sheep. University technology register.
  • CRISPR = Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats; guide RNA directs Cas 9 nuclease to a DNA target, cutting it so cellular repair can disrupt/change sequence. Other tools include TALENs and zinc-finger nucleases.
  • Correction: editing can delete, alter or insert DNA, including introduced sequences; “never foreign DNA” is false. Transgenesis is a particular genetic-modification route; gene editing is not scientifically outside all genetic modification. Crossbreeding combines inherited traits through mating and selection. NHGRI.
  • Somatic edits generally affect treated body cells; embryo/germline edits may be inherited. Off-target changes, mosaicism, animal welfare, heritability and food-safety require evaluation. No foreign gene does not automatically confer safety, consumer acceptance or exemption from regulation; plant SDN exemptions cannot simply be applied to livestock.
  • Potential meat yield, disease resistance, wool/milk improvement and lower veterinary costs are broader research possibilities, not all traits demonstrated in this lamb. Research birth is distinct from authorised food-market release.
