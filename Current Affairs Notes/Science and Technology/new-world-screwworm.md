# New World screwworm and sterile insect control

## August 2025 travel-associated case
- CDC and Maryland investigated a case on **4 August 2025** in a traveller returning from **El Salvador**. Officials reported very low public risk and no evidence of local transmission; the issue's “first US human case” overstates the announcement. [HHS/USDA statement](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-cdc-usda-traveler-associated-screwworm-case-surveillance.html).
- **Cochliomyia hominivorax**, a parasitic blowfly: eggs at wounds → larvae feed on living tissue of warm-blooded animals, occasionally humans → soil pupation → adult flies. It causes **myiasis**, serious tissue damage and livestock losses; it is not a worm species or ordinary scavenging maggot.
- **Sterile Insect Technique:** mass-reared sterilised males mate with wild females → no viable offspring → population suppression. US eradication in **1966** did not prevent all later imported cases/reintroductions; surveillance remains necessary.
- Outbreaks moving north through Central America highlight animal-movement control, wound care, early detection, border surveillance and coordinated sterile-fly release. The source's claim that sterile strains have lost effectiveness is not established here; do not memorise the practice question's all-three answer on that basis.
