# H1N1 influenza

## Essential distinctions
- Influenza A subtype **H1N1**: haemagglutinin type 1 and neuraminidase type 1. The 2009 pandemic strain, A(H1N1)pdm09, now circulates seasonally.
- Human seasonal infection does not require contact with pigs. Respiratory particles are important; “swine flu” is an imprecise label for all current infections.
- Fever, cough, sore throat, myalgia and fatigue may occur; pneumonia and respiratory failure are possible complications. Influenza B is a separate type, not H1N1-B.
- **Seasonal influenza vaccines include an H1N1 component**; the source's no-vaccine claim is false. Antiviral treatments exist, with clinical decisions based on risk and severity. [CDC vaccine composition](https://www.cdc.gov/flu/about/season/vaccine-selection.htm).
- India's first confirmed pandemic H1N1 cases were reported in May 2009. Prevention combines vaccination, ventilation, respiratory hygiene and appropriate clinical care.

## 2015 PYQ recall
- **H1N1 → swine flu, option (d)** in the supplied 2015 question. That historical label does not mean current human seasonal infection requires contact with pigs; the existing prevention/vaccine distinctions apply.
