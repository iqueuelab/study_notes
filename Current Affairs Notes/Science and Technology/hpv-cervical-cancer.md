# HPV vaccination and cervical-cancer screening

## Prevention and vaccine basics
- Cervical cancer arises in the **cervix**, the lower uterus opening into the vagina; persistent infection with high-risk **human papillomavirus (HPV)** causes almost all cases. **HPV infection is sexually transmissible; cancer itself is not an STI.** [WHO overview](https://www.who.int/health-topics/cervical-cancer).
- **Cervavac**, Serum Institute of India's indigenous quadrivalent HPV vaccine, targets **6, 11, 16 and 18**: 16/18 are major oncogenic types; 6/11 principally cause genital warts. Virus-like particles containing **L1 capsid protein** stimulate protective antibodies without live infectious virus.
- The PDF recycles an earlier market-authorisation account; approval is not a fresh April 2025 achievement. Its “50 million girls aged 9–14” figure was a prospective procurement opportunity, **not completed vaccination coverage**. [IARC vaccine evaluation](https://www.iarc.who.int/news-events/evaluation-of-new-hpv-vaccine-by-iarc-and-partners-supports-recommendation-to-grant-marketing-authorization-for-the-vaccine/).
- Vaccinate preferably **before HPV exposure**; vaccination is preventive, not treatment for existing infection/cancer. The claim it is effective “only before first intercourse” is too absolute. Screening and treatment of precancer remain necessary; vaccine protection does not cover every oncogenic type. [WHO position paper](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/who-wer9750-645-672).
- Bivalent Cervarix and quadrivalent Gardasil are examples, not the entire global vaccine catalogue. Affordability, procurement, cold chain, awareness and equitable adolescent access determine programme reach; approval alone does not establish national immunisation rollout.
## April 2025: indigenous diagnostic tests
- The embedded newspaper clipping concerns **HPV test kits**, a separate development from vaccination. **23 April 2025** official review described validation of indigenous rapid, point-of-care **RT-PCR-based HPV tests**, under **Grand Challenges India–BIRAC–DBT**, with AIIMS/ICMR, industry and IARC involvement. Companies named in the clipping: **Molbio Diagnostics** and **Mylab Discovery Solutions**. [DBT/PIB review](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2123856&lang=2&reg=48).
- Detecting high-risk HPV supports screening and follow-up; a positive HPV result is **not by itself a cancer diagnosis**. Validation does not prove universal availability or that screening has reached all eligible women.
- Prevention chain: **vaccination → screening → assessment/treatment of precancer → timely cancer care**. The PDF's older India burden figures should not be combined with its separately labelled 2022 cancer table; see the cancer-burden topic for a consistent dataset.
