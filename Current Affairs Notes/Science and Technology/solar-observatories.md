# NLST and NLOT: planned astronomy facilities in Ladakh

## Budget 2026–27 and telescope proposals
- **NLST**, a proposed **2-metre ground-based optical/near-infrared solar telescope**, is planned near **Merak, Pangong Tso, Ladakh, about 4,200 m**. It studies solar magnetic fields, dynamics, energetic eruptions and space weather, complementing **Aditya-L1** and ground instruments. [Indian Institute of Astrophysics](https://www.iiap.res.in/projects/nlst/).
- High altitude, dry/transparent skies and stable daytime atmospheric conditions improve observations; adaptive optics mitigates turbulence. Low night-time light pollution is not the main reason a daytime solar telescope resolves the Sun. **Kodaikanal (1899)** and **Udaipur (1975)** are existing solar-observatory traditions; calling NLST “third” does not count every Indian solar instrument.
- **NLOT**, National Large Optical/Near-Infrared Telescope: the source proposes **Hanle** and a segmented primary mirror for faint objects, exoplanets, stellar/galactic evolution and cosmology. Design-stage publications use different aperture figures; the PDF's **13.7 m/90 segments** graphic remains a proposal, not commissioned hardware. [IIA project](https://www.iiap.res.in/projects/nlot/).
- Budget support or establishment announcements differ from completed construction and actual observations. Space-weather research supports forecasting and risk management; it does not physically shield satellites from every eruption.
