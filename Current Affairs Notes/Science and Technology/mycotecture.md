# Mycotecture and fungal construction materials

## NASA's off-planet concept
- **Mycotecture Off Planet**, led by **Lynn Rothschild,NASA Ames**: dormant fungi in compact lightweight frameworks could grow structures at destination using water/nutrients, reducing launch mass. **NIAC PhaseIII,2024 award** supports continued development; this is research toward demonstration, not a working lunar/Martian settlement. [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-advances-research-to-grow-habitats-in-space-from-fungi/).
- **Mycelium is a network of fungal hyphae**, not a true root; fungal fruiting body is distinct. Containment/sterilisation and controlled growth protect both habitat reliability and planetary environments.
- Research investigates mouldability, insulation and potential radiation protection. Strength, fire behaviour, shielding and durability depend on composite/design/testing; no blanket guarantee every fungal brick withstands space conditions. NASA also notes Earth uses in filtration/mineral extraction from wastewater.
## Mycelium composites on Earth
- Agricultural residues **husk,straw,sawdust** + fungal inoculum → growth binds substrate → moulding → drying/heat treatment stops growth. The PDF's five-day cycle is illustrative, not universal.
- Potential products: insulation/interior panels, packaging alternatives, furniture/sports goods, leather-like materials, filtration/bioremediation and fungal chitin/chitosan for medical research. Species-specific termite biocontrol does not make every dried mycelium brick a pesticide.
- Low density, thermal/acoustic insulation, residue use and biodegradability can reduce material impacts, but coatings/additives, production energy and end-of-life affect results. **Biodegradable does not automatically mean carbon-neutral or pollution-free**.
- Constraints: limited load-bearing strength versus concrete/steel, moisture sensitivity, reproducibility, fire/building certification, cost and manufacturing scale. Source's universal '100times weaker', zero-waste and emissions figures are not general verified performance specifications.
