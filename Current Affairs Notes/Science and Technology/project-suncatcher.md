# Project Suncatcher: proposed orbital AI computing

## November 2025 research proposal
- Google announced **Project Suncatcher on 4 November 2025**: explore **solar-powered satellite constellations with Tensor Processing Units and optical inter-satellite links** for distributed AI. **Two prototype satellites with Planet, targeted early 2027**, are a planned learning mission, not an operational commercial orbital data centre. [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/exploring-a-space-based-scalable-ai-infrastructure-system-design/).
- Proposed **dawn–dusk sun-synchronous LEO** offers near-continuous sunlight; onboard arrays power TPUs, while laser links connect closely spaced satellites. A sun-synchronous orbit alone does not guarantee sunlight at all times. Target tens-of-terabits/s links differ from achieved full-scale service.
- **Trillium TPU radiation tests** were encouraging but do not establish immunity of an entire system. Formation control, pointing, bandwidth to ground, radiation resilience, repair/reliability, debris and launch economics remain challenges.
- Correct source: vacuum provides **no free convective cooling**; heat must be transferred to radiators and emitted. Solar power does not establish **zero lifecycle carbon footprint/no ecosystem impact**—manufacture, launch and orbital debris matter. Claimed lower terrestrial land/water demands and scalability remain prospective advantages, not proven net environmental savings.
- Governance issues include orbital coordination, spectrum/laser safety, cybersecurity, data jurisdiction and concentration of infrastructure ownership. Economic comparisons depend on future launch-cost assumptions; the project is a feasibility study rather than a guaranteed replacement for ground data centres.
