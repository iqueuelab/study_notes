# NavIC, NVS-02 and satellite navigation

## Navigation fundamentals
- **NavIC, Navigation with Indian Constellation**, previously **IRNSS**; regional **position/velocity/time** services over India and roughly **1,500 km beyond**.
- Original architecture **7 satellites: 3 geostationary + 4 inclined geosynchronous**. All geostationary orbits are geosynchronous; inclined ones do not stay above one ground point. Design count ≠ number healthy/operational on any date.
- **SPS** civilian and **Restricted Service** authorised/strategic; stated service-area accuracy **better than 20 m**, timing **better than 40 ns**, under specified conditions.
- Other systems: **GPS—US; GLONASS—Russia; Galileo—EU; BeiDou—China; QZSS—Japan**. Interoperability ≠ all systems having identical coverage.
## January 29 launch and later clarification
- **GSLV-F15** inserted **NVS-02**, second NVS-series satellite, into intended **geosynchronous transfer orbit**, on **29 January 2025**, **100th launch from Sriharikota** (not ISRO's 100th mission overall).
- GSLV three stages, indigenous upper cryogenic stage; **Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Andhra Pradesh**. Agency established **15 August 1969**, roots in **INCOSPAR 1962**, Bengaluru HQ.
- Source **1979 SLV-3 experimental flight** failed to reach orbit; first successful SLV-3 orbital launch **18 July 1980**. Sriharikota launch history is wider than SLV-3 alone.
- **2 February 2025 ISRO update**: launch successful, but oxidiser valves did not open for orbit raising; satellite in elliptical orbit, alternative strategies considered. Launch insertion ≠ operational placement in final slot. [ISRO mission and update](https://www.isro.gov.in/GSLV-F15_NVS-02_Mission.html).

## July 2025 replenishment plan
- **23 July parliamentary reply**: **NVS-03 planned by end 2025**, NVS-04/05 at approximately six-month intervals. Source's 2026 completion was a schedule expectation, not evidence all launches occurred. [Space Department reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/Press Release Page.aspx?PRID=2147284&lang=2&reg=48).
- Source snapshot: **11 launched including IRNSS-1 H launch failure**, one retired, four navigation-capable, four usable for one-way messaging, plus NVS-02 orbit-raising problem. Constellation design, satellites launched and satellites providing navigation are different counts. Atomic-clock health affects ranging; message broadcasting can remain useful for disaster alerts.
- GPS timing synchronises **mobile networks, banking transactions and power grids** in addition to locating users;2018 applications PYQ includes all three. Old infographic's constellation sizes/accuracy are historical, not a fresh 2025 global inventory.
