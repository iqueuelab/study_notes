# Ancient Egyptian whole-genome research

## Nuwayrat genome
- **Nature,2 July 2025:** whole genome from a man buried at **Nuwayrat, about 265 km south of Cairo**, around 4,500–4,800 years ago; Old Kingdom/early dynastic context. Ceramic-pot burial in a rock-cut tomb may have aided preservation; DNA extracted from tooth roots.
- Estimated age 44–64, height≈157–161 cm; brown-eye/hair and pigmentation predictions are probabilistic, not a photograph or proof of a racial category.
- Best-fit ancient comparison model: **≈78% North-African-related ancestry and≈22% eastern-Fertile-Crescent/Mesopotamian-related**. This is one individual's model using available ancient reference samples—not a literal percentage of modern nationalities or representative sample of all ancient Egyptians. Direct/Levantine migration route remains uncertain. [Primary study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09195-5).
- Hot climate and preservation conditions obstruct ancient-DNA research; the result demonstrates regional ancestry links but cannot establish a uniform Egyptian population across millennia.
- Map recall: Egypt's Mediterranean coast/Nile delta in north, Red Sea/Sinai east, Libya west, Sudan south; Suez Canal connects Mediterranean and Red Sea. Levant is eastern Mediterranean (Syria, Lebanon, Israel/Palestine, Jordan;definitions vary), not synonymous with all Mesopotamia.
