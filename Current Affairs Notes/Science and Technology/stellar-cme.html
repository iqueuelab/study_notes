Stellar coronal mass ejections and exoplanet habitability

Editable notes

November 2025: first characteristic radio signal beyond the Sun

  • StKM 1-1262, an M dwarf / red dwarf, about 130 light-years away (source rounds to 133), produced a drifting Type II radio burst observed with the European LOFAR (Low-Frequency Array) network. Radio emission from a shock provides evidence of plasma escaping into interplanetary space; this is not a resolved photograph of the ejected cloud. Research paper.
  • Source reports archival observation on 16 May 2016, analysed with improved processing and reported in 2025. It calls the burst one minute; related research describes roughly two minutes, so distinguish the bright interval from total radio-event duration rather than asserting an exact one-minute physical eruption. Related search study.
  • CME = magnetised plasma expelled from a stellar corona. Flare radiation and CME material are different phenomena. Solar CMEs can produce geomagnetic storms, auroras and satellite/radio/GNSS/grid disruption when conditions permit.
  • The 10,000-fold comparison concerns radio brightness relative to comparable solar signals, not a measured universal energy, speed or destruction factor. Modelling suggests a severe eruption could compress an Earth-like magnetosphere to the surface, exposing a close-orbiting planet to atmospheric erosion. One event does not establish that all red-dwarf planets lack atmospheres or life. UCL research announcement.
  • Red dwarfs are common and their temperate zones lie close to the star; habitable-zone temperature alone is insufficient. Assess stellar activity, eruption frequency, orbital distance and planetary magnetic/atmospheric protection. Existing solar-weather notes cover Earth's effects; the new observation extends such study to other systems.
