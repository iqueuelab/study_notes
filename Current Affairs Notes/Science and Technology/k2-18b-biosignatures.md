# K2-18b and possible atmospheric biosignatures

## April 2025 finding
- Cambridge-led researchers used **JWST's Mid-Infrared Instrument (MIRI)** to report evidence for **dimethyl sulphide (DMS) and/or dimethyl disulphide (DMDS)** in K2-18b's atmosphere at approximately **3-sigma** significance. Overlapping spectral signatures mean both molecules were not independently established.
- DMS/DMDS have biological sources on Earth, including marine microorganisms. A possible atmospheric biosignature motivates follow-up observations and chemical modelling; it is not proof of extraterrestrial organisms. **3-sigma is not a probability that life exists.** [Original research, 16 April 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12267).
## Planet and observing method
| Feature | Recall |
| --- | --- |
| Discovery | 2015, Kepler's K2 mission |
| Distance | Approximately 120–124 light-years |
| Size | Approximately **2.6 Earth radii** |
| Mass | Approximately **8.6–8.9 Earth masses**, a mass estimate rather than “8.6 times bigger” |
| Orbit | About 33 days around a cool M-type red dwarf, in its habitable zone |
- Often described as a **sub-Neptune** and possible **Hycean** candidate: hydrogen-rich atmosphere with a proposed ocean beneath it. Habitable-zone location allows potential liquid water under suitable conditions; the actual ocean, surface conditions and habitability remain unresolved.
- Earlier Webb spectra found **methane and carbon dioxide**. Transit spectroscopy measures wavelength-dependent starlight passing through the atmosphere; researchers infer molecules from absorption features and atmospheric models. [Cambridge announcement](https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/strongest-hints-of-biological-activity).
- **JWST:** launched in **2021**, NASA–ESA–CSA collaboration; infrared observations from an orbit around **Sun–Earth L2** enable early-universe studies and exoplanet spectroscopy.
## Scientific reasoning and India
- Repeat observations, instrument/systematic checks, alternative atmospheric models and laboratory studies of abiotic production are essential. Methane reports on Mars and debated phosphine claims on Venus illustrate that chemical detection and biological interpretation are separate questions.
- Indian opportunities: spectroscopy/infrared instrumentation, planetary and astrobiology research, international collaboration, education and planetary-protection standards. **AstroSat** and **XPoSat** expand astronomy capability; XPoSat launched in **January 2024**, rather than being an upcoming mission in April 2025. Gaganyaan develops human-spaceflight capability rather than directly detecting exoplanet life.
- **Spectral feature → molecular inference → possible biosignature → independent verification** is the evidence chain; subsequent findings should be dated separately from this April report.
