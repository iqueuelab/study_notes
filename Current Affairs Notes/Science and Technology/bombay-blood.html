Science and Technology

Bombay blood phenotype and cross-group kidney transplantation

Editable notes

Blood-group distinction

  • Bombay phenotype, Oh / hh, identified 1952 by Y.M. Bhende and colleagues: absence of H antigen, precursor for A/B expression; can resemble O on routine forward typing but anti-H antibodies complicate compatibility.
  • Red-cell transfusion generally needs appropriately compatible Bombay-phenotype blood; ordinary O is not automatically safe. Source notation “HH phenotype” is misleading; rare prevalence varies by population/study.

Kidney-transplant report

  • February report describes MIOT International, Chennai, cross-group kidney transplant with specialised desensitisation, double-filtration plasmapheresis (DFPP) and B-cell-directed immunosuppression.
  • DFPP reduces circulating antibodies; immunosuppression reduces immune response/rejection risk. Blood transfusion compatibility ≠organ-transplant eligibility: the source's “can receive organs only from Bombay donor” contradicts the reported cross-group transplant.
  • A reported innovative case does not establish a universally safe protocol or benefit to “millions” of rare-phenotype patients. Source's exact journal/world-first attribution needs case-publication verification; do not confuse a Bombay donor report with a Bombay recipient.

Ethics and governance

  • Informed consent, independent clinical/ethics oversight, infection/rejection follow-up, donor welfare, lawful allocation and affordability.
  • Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 1994, donor registries, rare blood banking and referral networks; documented outcomes and reproducibility matter more than novelty claims. Earlier primary Bombay-donor case.
