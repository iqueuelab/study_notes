# Auramine O: industrial dye and food adulteration

## November 2025: non-permitted yellow food colour
- **Auramine O** is a synthetic yellow industrial dye used in textiles/leather, printing/paper and fluorescent microbiological staining. It is **not an approved food colour in India**; low cost and bright colour encourage illicit addition to foods such as turmeric/spices, pulses and sweets. Food-grade approval is distinct from legal industrial/laboratory use.
- Source links ingestion/exposure with gastrointestinal irritation and potential liver/kidney, genotoxic and other toxic effects. Risk depends on dose/exposure and evidence; do not diagnose a consumer or assert every detected trace causes cancer.
- **IARC distinguishes auramine (Group 2B, possibly carcinogenic) from occupational auramine production (Group 1, carcinogenic)**. Hazard classification is not a numerical cancer risk from a particular serving, and manufacturing exposure is not identical to food exposure. [IARC classifications](https://monographs.iarc.who.int/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/ClassificationsAlphaOrder.pdf).
- Detection requires validated laboratory tests, traceable suppliers, sampling and enforcement, recalls where warranted and consumer awareness. Cheap adulteration, fragmented informal supply and weak compliance sustain recurrence; vivid yellow colour alone is not a definitive identification test. The source's universal “all countries ban every use” implication should be restricted to non-permitted food use, not industrial uses.
