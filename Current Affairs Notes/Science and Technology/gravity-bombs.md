# Gravity bombs, JDAM and standoff munitions

## March 2026: conventional gravity bombs and JDAM
- Source reports US plans to use more gravity bombs after claims Iranian air defences were degraded. This is a stated operational assessment/plan, not independent proof all air defences were neutralised or every listed aircraft/munition was used.
- **Gravity bomb:** unpowered after release; trajectory depends on gravity/aerodynamics and release conditions. **Guidance does not require propulsion**: JDAM adds **GPS-aided inertial navigation and tail controls** to conventional bomb bodies. Unguided gravity bombs are not automatically precision weapons.
| General-purpose body | Nominal weight class | JDAM designation |
|---|---|---|
| Mk 82 | 500 lb | GBU-38 |
| Mk 83 | 1,000 lb | GBU-32 |
| Mk 84 | 2,000 lb | GBU-31 |
- A **Mk 84 general-purpose bomb is not interchangeable with the specialised BLU-109 penetrator**, although either can form a GBU-31 variant. Fixed crater/depth claims vary with soil, fuze and conditions. [USAF JDAM fact sheet](https://www.minot.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/264282/joint-direct-attack-munitions-gbu-313238/).
- Standoff weapons can reduce aircraft exposure; closer delivery trades cost against air-defence risk, but modern glide kits complicate a simple “must fly directly overhead” rule. **LUCAS** is the correct unmanned-system acronym, not the sheet's LUAS. Conventional Mark 80-series bombs differ from nuclear B61/B83; the report does not establish nuclear use.
