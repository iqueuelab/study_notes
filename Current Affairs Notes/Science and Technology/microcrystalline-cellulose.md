# Microcrystalline cellulose: excipients and food uses

## Material and manufacture
- **MCC**: purified, partially depolymerised cellulose derived from plant fibres/refined wood pulp; cellulose is a structural plant-cell-wall polymer, **(C₆H₁₀O₅)n**. Acid hydrolysis preferentially removes less-ordered regions, followed by drying/milling; residual microcrystalline regions are stabilised by molecular packing/hydrogen bonding.
- White free-flowing powder, insoluble in water; humans lack enzymes to digest cellulose. Food-additive safety at authorised uses is different from universal harmlessness at every dose/exposure; inhalable manufacturing dust has separate occupational risks. [EU specification](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32018R0075).
- Pharmaceutical **excipient**, not the active drug: binder, filler/bulking agent and disintegrant support tablet manufacture/drug release. Food uses include texture, bulk, fat replacement, stabilisation and anti-caking under applicable standards.
- **Cellulose**, **MCC/E460(i)** and **carboxymethylcellulose(CMC)** are not identical: CMC is chemically modified and commonly used for thickening/stabilisation.
- The Sigachi accident is covered in industrial-process safety. Safety of the finished excipient does not establish safety of drying, pressure, dust/solvent or acid-handling operations; the PDF's possible causes are not concluded findings.
