# Black-hole mergers and GW231123

## Gravitational waves and detection
- A black hole has an **event horizon** beyond which light cannot escape. Massive-star collapse is one formation route; “cosmic vacuum cleaner” is a misleading analogy because distant orbital gravity follows the mass, not indiscriminate suction.
- Binary inspiral loses orbital energy through **gravitational waves**, accelerating toward merger and ringdown. These spacetime ripples were predicted by general relativity; **LIGO (US), Virgo (Italy), KAGRA (Japan)** form a detector network.
## GW231123
- Detected **23 November 2023 by the two LIGO observatories**, reported in July 2025. Inferred component masses roughly **137 and 103 solar masses**, with broad uncertainty and high inferred spins; do not treat them as exact measurements.
- Masses challenge simple stellar-collapse/pair-instability expectations; hierarchical mergers are one possible origin, not proven history. The event does not mean the theory of gravity failed. [LIGO discovery paper](https://dcc.ligo.org/LIGO-P2500026-v6/public).
- The remnant mass is below the sum because energy is radiated as gravitational waves. Signal inference requires waveform models and uncertainty assessment; detector operation at the time differs from the membership of the full collaboration.
