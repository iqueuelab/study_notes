# Architectural immunity: ants redesign nests against disease

## October 2025: nest design as collective defence
- **Black garden ants (Lasius niger)** exposed to a fungal pathogen altered the three-dimensional networks they excavated: entrances farther apart, altered connectivity and reduced chamber clustering; the study also observed vulnerable brood in less connected locations.
- **Architectural immunity** means changing built space to reduce infection risk; it complements grooming, antimicrobial defences and other forms of **social immunity**. It is a collective, self-organised response, not evidence of a centrally planned ant public-health authority.
- Researchers compared pathogen-treated and control ants and analysed nest growth/networks. Reduced simulated transmission through the changed networks supports a protective mechanism; do not claim architecture eliminates all infections or that identical responses occur in every ant species. [University of Bristol, 16 October 2025](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/biology/news/2025/ants-alter-their-nest-networks-to-prevent-epidemics-study-finds.html).
- Useful analogy: spacing, movement networks and isolation of vulnerable groups can influence transmission in human buildings. Translating insect findings into urban design requires human epidemiological evidence; an analogy is not a ready-made building standard.
