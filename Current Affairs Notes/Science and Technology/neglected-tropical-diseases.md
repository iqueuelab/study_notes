# Neglected tropical diseases and integrated public health

## NTD framework
- Diverse diseases concentrated in poor/marginalised tropical/subtropical settings; neglect reflects limited resources/attention and stigma. **Not all are communicable:** snakebite envenoming is toxin injury.
| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Helminthic | Taeniasis, lymphatic filariasis |
| Protozoal | Leishmaniasis/kala-azar |
| Viral | Rabies, dengue |
| Bacterial | Leprosy, trachoma, yaws |
| Fungal | Mycetoma/chromoblastomycosis and other deep mycoses |
| Ectoparasitic | Scabies |
| Non-infectious | Snakebite envenoming |
- **India guinea-worm-free certification: 2000**; yaws national elimination declared **2006**, WHO verified interruption/free status **2016**; **trachoma validation 2024**, not 2023. These national milestones do not mean all three diseases eradicated worldwide. [NCDC yaws timeline](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/includes/About/CentresAndDivision/yep.php).
- Global instruments: WHO NTD **2021–2030 road map**, **Global NTD Annual Reporting Form (GNARF)** for monitoring, **Global Vector Control Response 2017–2030**, **Kigali Declaration 2022** political commitments. Different diseases require prevention/control/elimination/eradication targets; declarations are not achievements.
- Indian vector-borne programme covers malaria, dengue, chikungunya, kala-azar and lymphatic filariasis; **malaria is not itself on WHO's NTD list** simply because it shares control infrastructure. Swachh Bharat, Jal Jeevan Mission and health-system access support prevention through WASH, surveillance and care; one intervention cannot eliminate every NTD.
