Science and Technology

Anaemia and AnemiaPhone

Editable notes

Mechanism and causes

  • Anaemia: low haemoglobin/oxygen-carrying capacity for the relevant group; iron deficiency is a major cause, not the only cause.
  • Other contributors: B12/folate deficiency, poor intake, infections, blood loss and inherited blood disorders; nutrition and access inequalities matter.
  • Fatigue, weakness, breathlessness, impaired function; severe cases can be life-threatening. Pregnancy risks include prematurity/low birthweight; signs alone do not establish the cause.

AnemiaPhone: point-of-care iron assessment

  • Cornell University technology licensed to ICMR for public-health use; announced December 2024, discussed in January 2025 notes. Integrates into anaemia/women's/maternal-child-health programmes; transfer is not proof of universal rollout.
  • Finger-prick blood → test strip + portable reader → result in minutes → phone/tablet/computer-connected clinical record; source describes Bluetooth/Wi-Fi-enabled readers.
  • Enables decentralised iron-status screening, potentially before clinical anaemia, and appropriate referral/differential diagnosis; not symptom-only smartphone diagnosis. Cornell.

NFHS-5 and programme recall

  • 2019–21 survey: pregnant women 15–49 52.2% anaemic; children 6–59 months 67.1%; all women 15–49 57%. PDF's general '50–70% pregnant / 50% children' should not replace specified survey figures. Health Ministry.
  • Anaemia Mukt Bharat, 2018, life-cycle approach: supplementation, testing/treatment, diet/behaviour change and attention to non-nutritional causes; public-health support does not justify self-prescribing iron for every anaemia.
  • Clinical workup may use CBC/haemoglobin, iron indicators and other cause-specific tests. Digital screening needs quality control, trained workers, reliable supply chains, follow-up and privacy.

May 2025: preconception prevention

  • Begin screening/nutrition and cause-specific treatment before pregnancy, supported by ASHA/Anganwadi outreach, families and affordable follow-up. NFHS-5 57% women 15–49 anaemic does not establish that all were undiagnosed/untreated. Source's MMR 93 is a maternal-health indicator, not an anaemia-specific death rate.
  • Iron deficiency, B12/folate deficiency, blood loss, inflammation/infection, inherited disorders and thyroid-related conditions need differential assessment. Inset also highlights heavy menstrual/pregnancy blood loss and parasites. Source's 49% B12 deficiency comes from an unspecified study, not a verified all-India census.
  • Low haemoglobin/iron deficiency can be associated with prematurity, low birthweight, impaired development and reduced tolerance of delivery blood loss; anaemia is not proof it directly caused every listed complication, including pre-eclampsia.
  • WHO 2024 thresholds at relevant reference conditions: non-pregnant women <12 g/dL; pregnancy <11 g/dL first/third trimester, <10.5 g/dL second. Altitude/clinical context and measurement matter. WHO guideline.

Treatment and policy caution

  • Oral iron/folic acid can be limited by gastrointestinal effects, adherence and absorption; hepcidin regulates iron availability. IV ferric carboxymaltose bypasses intestinal absorption and can be considered by clinicians for indicated iron deficiency/intolerance or inadequate response; it is not a universal first-line treatment or blanket first-trimester recommendation. Infusion supervision and adverse-effect assessment remain necessary. WHO-hosted clinical discussion.
  • B12/folate replacement and thyroid/metabolic screening depend on individual indications; gestational diabetes is distinct from anaemia. Article's proposal for injectable B12+IV iron for everyone should not become standard self-treatment advice.
  • Proposed standardised/subsidised preconception care complements antenatal services and reproductive autonomy. SDG 3.1 global MMR<70/100,000 requires comprehensive obstetric/health-system improvements; anaemia prevention alone does not guarantee it. Supplied 250-word 15-mark question is undated practice.

Anaemia Mukt Bharat PYQ recap

  • Iron–folic acid, not prophylactic calcium, is the supplementation intervention. Delayed cord clamping, periodic deworming and attention to non-nutritional causes (malaria, haemoglobinopathies, fluorosis) are included. Supplied 2023 answer: only three (c); statements2,3,4 are correct.
