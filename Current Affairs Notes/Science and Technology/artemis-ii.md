# Artemis II: crewed lunar flyby test

## January 2026: planned crewed test flight
- **Artemis II**, NASA-led programme's **first crewed flight**, was planned as an approximately **ten-day lunar flyby**, the first crewed journey to the Moon's vicinity since **Apollo 17, 1972**. **No lunar landing** is part of this mission; it tests the integrated **SLS rocket /Orion spacecraft**, life support, communications, navigation and crew operations.
- Crew: **Reid Wiseman, commander; Victor Glover, pilot; Christina Koch and Jeremy Hansen, mission specialists**. Hansen represents the **Canadian Space Agency**; three NASA astronauts plus one Canadian illustrates international cooperation.
- Planned profile: launch from **Kennedy Space Center Pad 39B**, initial Earth-orbit system checks and manual-handling demonstrations, translunar journey around the far side on a **hybrid free-return trajectory**, then Pacific splashdown. Source distance **~10,300 km beyond the Moon** is a planned far-side geometry, not an already flown record.
- At the issue date, **February 2026 was an earliest launch opportunity, with March/April windows**, dependent on testing and readiness. **Rollout to the pad was a planned mid-January step**, not already completed as the supplementary summary suggests; the original question-corner clipping uses future tense. A launch window is not an actual launch date.
- Artemis seeks sustained lunar capability and eventual Mars preparation. At this source date, **Artemis III lunar-surface ambitions** were prospective; successful Artemis II alone would not certify every landing system or guarantee the next mission's timing. **Artemis Accords are cooperation principles, distinct from these flight designations**.
- [NASA planned hybrid free-return trajectory](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190033324/downloads/20190033324.pdf?attachment=true).

## February 2026: wet dress rehearsal and hydrogen leak
- Artemis II's **2–3 February wet dress rehearsal** encountered a **liquid-hydrogen leak at the ground–rocket interface**, ending the countdown at **T−5:15**. NASA moved the earliest launch opportunity toward **March**, subject to repairs/testing; this is not an actual launch or lunar landing. [NASA test update](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2026/02/03/artemis-ii-wet-dress-rehearsal-test-terminated-at-t-515/).
- **Dry rehearsal:** procedures/countdown and personnel/software coordination without loading propellants. **Wet rehearsal:** actual propellant loading, integrated ground/rocket checks and terminal-countdown practice, stopping before ignition/launch, then safe detanking. It exposes temperature-dependent leaks, contraction, valve/seal or connection problems that simulations can miss.
- For **SLS**, **liquid hydrogen is fuel; liquid oxygen is oxidiser**, both cryogenic propellants. Calling oxygen a fuel is incorrect; not every rocket uses this pair. Typical sequence: chill lines → load/pressurise and replenish boil-off → monitor leaks/systems → practise holds/countdown → stop safely → drain.
- Wet rehearsals test hardware, software, people and timelines together. Dry tests still have operational hazards; the PDF's “zero risk” language is too absolute. Mission readiness depends on resolving findings, not merely conducting a rehearsal.
