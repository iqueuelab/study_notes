Saras: revision anchors

FeatureFact
DeveloperCSIR–National Aerospace Laboratories (NAL), Bengaluru
Original Saras14-seat prototype; first flight in 2004
Mk2Planned 19-seat light transport/commuter aircraft
UsesPassenger/charter transport, troop/VIP support and casualty evacuation/air ambulance
Design aimRegional connectivity; short, hot/high and semi-prepared airfields

NAL project background; PIB design and certification account.

13 April 2025 development

  • The supplied report quotes an official expecting the first Mk2 test flight in December 2027, after delay from a 2026 schedule. This is a forecast, not a completed flight or certification.
  • It reports IAF interest in at least 15 airworthy aircraft. Procurement interest, a test flight and operational induction are separate milestones.
  • Production plan reported: NAL wings/composites; outsourced metallic parts including HAL participation; large vendors for fuselage work. Genesis avionics and NAL brake-management/environmental-control systems are reported; an older Saras serves as a test bed.
  • Constraints: pending engineering drawings despite material procurement, certified test-pilot availability, rigorous airworthiness testing and production coordination.

Programme and policy significance

  • 2009 test-flight crash killed three IAF personnel; development later suffered funding interruption and revival towards end-2016. NAL-hosted historical coverage.
  • Design → ground tests → flight tests → type certification → serial production → induction. DGCA civil and CEMILAC military certification are distinct regulatory tracks.
  • Indigenous design, avionics/system integration and manufacturing supply chains can support regional connectivity under UDAN — Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik, skilled jobs and reduced dependence on imported commuter aircraft. These benefits depend on safety, affordability and reliable production.
  • Civil transport with possible military utility does not make Saras a fighter aircraft. Earlier official flight schedules are plans that can be superseded.
