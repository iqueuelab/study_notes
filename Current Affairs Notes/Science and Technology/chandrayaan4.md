# Chandrayaan-4: lunar sample return and landing-site selection

## February 2026: Mons Mouton candidate region
- Source reports SAC researchers identified **Mons Mouton** as a candidate region for **Chandrayaan-4**, India's planned lunar sample-return mission. A site study is not a completed mission or final landing authorisation. ISRO describes the objective as collecting samples, lunar ascent and protected return to Earth for laboratory analysis. [ISRO mission configuration](https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO_EN/UnionCabinetApprovesIndiasMission.html).
- **Mons Mouton** is a flat-topped/mesa-like mountain near the Moon's south pole, beside **Nobile crater**, named for NASA mathematician **Melba Roy Mouton**. Relatively flat terrain, low slopes, manageable boulders, illumination, thermal conditions and Earth visibility influence landing safety. NASA describes **long stretches** of communication/sunlight, not guaranteed uninterrupted illumination or communications. [NASA geographic description](https://www.nasa.gov/people-of-nasa/moon-mountain-name-honors-nasa-mathematician-melba-mouton/).
- Nearby permanently shadowed depressions can preserve cold-trapped volatiles; illuminated elevated sites support solar power. This is a trade-off between access to scientific targets and operational constraints, not proof every crater contains readily recoverable ice.
- **South Pole–Aitken basin** is a vast ancient impact feature mostly on the lunar far side, important for crust/mantle and early impact-history studies. The source's “on its rim” linkage is tentative; do not identify Mons Mouton with the whole basin or claim sampling automatically reaches the mantle.
- **LUPEX/Chandrayaan-5** is the separate India–Japan polar mission: **ISRO lander, JAXA rover and planned H3 launcher**. Source **UPPSC 2024** question reverses those roles; only statement 1 is correct, and it is not a CSE PYQ.
