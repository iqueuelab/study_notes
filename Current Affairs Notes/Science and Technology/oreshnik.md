# Oreshnik: Russian intermediate-range ballistic missile

## Source-period Russian announcement
- **Oreshnik** (hazel tree) is Russia's mobile, solid-fuelled **intermediate-range ballistic missile**; first combat use reported **21 November 2024, Dnipro, Ukraine**. August 2025 announcement described serial production and planned deployment in **Belarus by end-2025**; that was a prospective deployment at this issue date.
- Source reports roughly **5,000-km range**, speeds up to **Mach 10**, nuclear/conventional capability and multiple re-entry vehicles. Treat these as reported/claimed capabilities, not independently demonstrated exact performance or proof of immunity to interception.
- **MIRV** means independently targetable re-entry vehicles; multiple payloads/submunitions alone do not establish independent targeting. The worksheet's confident MIRV and mid-air manoeuvrability claims should not be learned as verified specifications. A ballistic weapon reaching hypersonic speeds is distinct from a powered hypersonic cruise missile.

## 29 December 2025: Belarus deployment report
- Embedded clipping reports **US researchers' assessment that Russia was likely placing Oreshnik in Belarus**. This is a source-period assessment, not independent confirmation from the worksheet of completed deployment or exact basing.
- Existing notes retain first use **21 November 2024, Dnipro**, mobile solid-fuel IRBM, reported roughly 5,000 km/Mach 10 and dual conventional/nuclear capability. The claim that its payloads are independently targetable **MIRVs**, or that it has demonstrated exceptional mid-course manoeuvring/immunity to interception, is unverified; multiple re-entry vehicles alone do not prove independent targeting.
- Many ballistic missiles reach hypersonic speed. Distinguish ballistic missiles, hypersonic glide vehicles and powered cruise missiles. Strategic issues are shortened warning, nuclear/conventional ambiguity and escalation; do not learn the source's simplistic traditional-ballistic-versus-Oreshnik table as a technical classification.
