# Space surveillance and domestic capacity

## January 2025 launch
- **SCOT = Space Camera for Object Tracking**, **Digantara**; commercial **space situational awareness (SSA)** satellite on **SpaceX Transporter-12**, **14 January**, Vandenberg, California; **sun-synchronous orbit**.
- Tracks **resident space objects** to support collision avoidance, safe operations and security. Developer's reported **5 cm** tracking capability needs observing conditions; no sensor provides unrestricted detection of every object at every instant.
- Space-based observation reduces terrestrial weather/geographic constraints but retains field-of-view, illumination, orbital geometry and processing limitations. Backers listed: **Aditya Birla Ventures, SIDBI**.
- Co-launches: **Pixxel's first three Firefly hyperspectral satellites**; Hyderabad **XDLINX Spacelabs' ELEVATION-1** for US **Almagest Space Corporation**.
- **SSA = know/track orbital environment; Earth observation = observe Earth's surface**. Different missions can share a launch.
## 18 May 2025: PSLV-C61/EOS-09 failure
- **PSLV-C61**, the PSLV's **63rd flight**, attempted launch of **EOS-09** from Sriharikota; performance normal through stage two, anomaly observed in the third stage, mission not accomplished. **EOS-09 synthetic aperture radar** was intended for all-weather/day-night Earth observation. No successful satellite deployment should be inferred from launch. [ISRO record](https://new1.isro.gov.in/Mission_PSLV_C61_EOS_09.html?timeline=timeline).
- Civilian uses include land/water/resource monitoring; radar observation also has strategic utility. Satellite revisit, resolution, coverage and processing constrain surveillance: an all-weather payload does not guarantee continuous real-time observation of every place.
- **Operation Sindoor was real**, contrary to the PDF's “possibly fictional” aside. The editorial links reliance on foreign commercial imagery with security/dependence concerns; this does not prove India lacked all indigenous surveillance.
## Capacity and policy argument
- Source describes **Space-Based Surveillance-3: planned 52 satellites, 31 through private sector**. Treat these as programme plans/reported allocation, not a completed constellation.
- Constraints form a **cost–reliability–time** triangle: more funding alone cannot eliminate complex engineering failures; accelerated schedules need quality assurance and failure analysis.
- ISRO balances launch operations, manufacture, R&D, data processing, national applications and **Gaganyaan**, with finite staff/budgets. Increase institutional resources, manufacturing capability, qualified suppliers/private collaboration and long-term R&D rather than count only launch targets.
- Security infrastructure requires resilient domestic capability, redundancy, data protection and coordinated user requirements. Private participation does not remove ISRO's technical/infrastructure role or automatically privatise ISRO.

## SBS-III acceleration report
- The issue reports faster construction for the **52-satellite Space-Based Surveillance-III programme**, approved in October 2024: **21 ISRO-built and 31 private-sector satellites**; these are plans, not a completed operational constellation.
- **SAR** offers day/night and cloud-penetrating imaging; electro-optical sensors complement it with high-resolution imagery but have different weather/illumination constraints. Revisit frequency, coverage, downlink and analysis determine usefulness; LEO alone does not guarantee continuous observation of every location.
- Named firms: **Ananth Technologies (Hyderabad), Centum Electronics and Alpha Design Technologies (Bengaluru)**. Reported 12–18-month acceleration is a programme aspiration; source clipping says an initial satellite set was sought by **2026**, so the simplified “by 2025” assertion should not be memorised.
- Defence Space Agency/user coordination, launch/engineering supervision, AI-assisted analysis and domestic data sovereignty support border/coastal/IOR surveillance and reduced commercial-foreign dependence. Speculative SpaceX/LVM3 launch choices are not confirmed contracts. Private end-to-end manufacturing needs security, reliability and quality control.
