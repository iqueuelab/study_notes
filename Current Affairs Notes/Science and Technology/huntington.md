# Huntington disease and inherited neurodegeneration

## Genetic mechanism
- **HTT gene CAG-repeat expansion** → abnormal **huntingtin** protein → progressive neuronal dysfunction/loss, particularly **basal ganglia and cortex**.
- **Autosomal dominant**; affected heterozygous parent gives each child a **50% inheritance risk**. Not contagious and not a prion infection.
- **Chorea**, abnormal posture/movements, cognitive decline and behavioural/emotional changes; unusual eye movements/tremors may occur.
- Source **3–7 per 100,000**, more common in European ancestry; prevalence varies. Often **15–20 years survival after onset** is an average, not an individual prediction.
- No cure reversing the disease in the January account; specialist medicines and supportive care manage symptoms, with genetic counselling where appropriate. [NIH clinical genetics](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1305/).

## August 2025: rare-disease policy demand
- Patients/caregivers seek inclusion/support for Huntington disease under the **National Policy for Rare Diseases, 2021**. Reported demand is not proof a new inclusion notification or universal treatment entitlement already exists.
- Existing HTT/CAG mechanism, autosomal-dominant inheritance, movement/cognitive/psychiatric symptoms and supportive management apply. A heterozygous affected parent gives each child 50% inheritance risk; both parents need not be affected.
