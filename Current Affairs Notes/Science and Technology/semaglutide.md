# Semaglutide and GLP-1 receptor agonists

## Mechanism and significance
- **Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist**, mimicking an incretin hormone. It increases **glucose-dependent insulin secretion**, reduces glucagon secretion, delays gastric emptying and influences appetite/satiety.
- Mechanism: GLP-1 receptor activation → greater insulin response when glucose is elevated + less hepatic glucose output + reduced appetite → improved glycaemic control and weight reduction.
- Evidence supports glucose control, weight-management benefits and reduction of cardiovascular or renal risk in specified patient groups. Benefits and eligibility depend on indication, formulation and trial population; an average “4 kg loss” is not a universal outcome.
## Milestones and a correction
- **Ozempic: US FDA approval for type 2 diabetes in 2017**; **Wegovy: approval for chronic weight management in 2021**, alongside diet and activity. Brand/formulation indications should not be assumed interchangeable. [FDA](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USFDA/bulletins/2e2adc7).
- **Later development — 5 September 2025:** WHO added semaglutide and other GLP-1 therapies to its Essential Medicines List for specified adults with type 2 diabetes and relevant cardiovascular/renal disease and obesity. The source's **“WHO EML in 2023” is incorrect**; this had not happened by the April article date. EML inclusion guides national selection and procurement, not automatic free supply or approval for every use. [WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/05-09-2025-who-updates-list-of-essential-medicines-to-include-key-cancer--diabetes-treatments).
## Access and safety
- Common adverse effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea and constipation. Important warnings include pancreatitis and gallbladder problems; dehydration can affect kidney function. Hypoglycaemia risk is generally lower than with insulin alone but **not zero**, particularly with insulin/sulfonylureas. [FDA safety information](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-patients-dosing-errors-associated-compounded).
- High cost, uneven access, supply quality and long-term adherence matter. Clinical follow-up and nutrition/activity remain important; medicine does not replace prevention or equitable primary care.
- UPSC angle: India’s combined diabetes-obesity burden, NCD expenditure, essential-medicine policy, affordability, generic competition and **SDG 3**.

## June 2025: compare tirzepatide
- **Tirzepatide activates both GIP and GLP-1 receptors**; semaglutide activates **GLP-1** receptors. Both are synthetic peptide medicines; incretin signalling links intestinal hormones to glucose regulation and satiety. [FDA tirzepatide overview](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-new-medication-chronic-weight-management).
- Source describes injections, but semaglutide also has an **oral formulation**; brand, route, indication and approval jurisdiction must be distinguished. Access, affordability, regulated supply and continuing clinical care matter to public-health policy; drug effects are not a substitute for population-level prevention.
- Source's broad BMI thresholds are indication-specific eligibility descriptions, not personalised treatment rules. Its undifferentiated obesity count/diabetes rank should not be learned without survey/year definitions.
- Adjacent PYQ recall: **ACE2 → spread of viral diseases (2021, d); Bollgard I/II → GM crops (2022, b); B and T cells → protection against pathogens (2022, d)**. These are separate biotechnology/immunology concepts, not mechanisms of the two medicines.

## 1 December 2025: WHO obesity guideline
- WHO issued its first guideline on **GLP-1 therapies for chronic, relapsing obesity**, addressing liraglutide, semaglutide and tirzepatide. It conditionally supports long-term use in **adults with obesity, excluding pregnant women**, within comprehensive care. [WHO guideline announcement](https://www.who.int/news/item/01-12-2025-who-issues-global-guideline-on-the-use-of-glp-1-medicines-in-treating-obesity).
- Conditional status reflects limited long-term/discontinuation evidence, costs, health-system readiness and equity concerns. A second conditional recommendation supports offering **intensive behavioural intervention** alongside prescribed therapy. This does not mean compulsory use for every adult or that medication replaces diet/activity and clinical care.
- WHO's **US$3 trillion annual global cost by 2030** is a projection, not a current measured Indian burden. Affordable access, quality-assured supply and continuing care determine policy value.
- Mechanism, tirzepatide's dual **GIP/GLP-1** action, oral semaglutide and dated infographic statistics repeat existing notes. **ACE2 2021 (d), Bollgard 2022 (b), B/T cells 2022 (d)** are already banked; they are biotechnology/immunology revision anchors, not obesity-drug mechanisms. The unyearred tirzepatide practice item is not a PYQ; its ‘neither 1 nor 3’ option is a typographical error.
