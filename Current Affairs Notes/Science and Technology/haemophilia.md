# Haemophilia and preventive care

## Deficiency → bleeding → prevention
- Inherited haemophilia impairs coagulation: **A = factor VIII deficiency; B = factor IX deficiency**. Spontaneous joint/muscle bleeds cause pain, chronic arthropathy/disability; intracranial bleeding can be life-threatening.
- **Prophylaxis** means regular preventive haemostatic treatment before bleeding; **on-demand/episodic** treatment follows a bleed. WFH recommends sustained prophylaxis for severe bleeding phenotypes to protect joints, activity and quality of life. Factor replacement is commonly intravenous; some non-factor treatments are subcutaneous. Treatment choice requires specialist assessment. [WFH 2020 guidelines](https://guidelines.wfh.org/guidelines/).
- Early diagnosis, district testing/referral, high-risk-family screening, reliable affordable factor supply, trained clinicians, physiotherapy and a patient registry reduce avoidable disability. School/work participation, mental health and household costs are policy outcomes alongside hospital admissions.
- Source estimates **1–1.5 lakh expected Indian cases versus~29,000 diagnosed**; these are approximate source figures, not a complete prevalence census. Rural access, cost, stigma and low awareness contribute to underdiagnosis. Its “each untreated bleed shortens life by16 days” is an unsupported universal calculation and should not be memorised.
- Source proposes subsidised treatment/NHM–Ayushman Bharat integration, domestic production and non-factor/gene-therapy research; these recommendations do not establish universal national coverage. Practice answer **2 only**: factor IX causes B, not A.
