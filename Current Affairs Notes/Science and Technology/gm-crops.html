Science and Technology

GM crops, mustard and biotechnology governance

Editable notes

Technology and regulatory status

  • GM changes a plant's genetic material through engineering; trait-specific benefits/risks cannot be generalised across all crops.
  • Bt cotton, approved in 2002, was India's commercially cultivated GM crop in this source period.
  • DMH-11 mustard uses barnase–barstar male-sterility/restoration technology for hybrid breeding; bar is a selectable herbicide-tolerance marker. It is not simply a Bt-style insect-resistant mustard.
  • The 2022 conditional environmental-release decision involved DMH-11/parental lines and further requirements; do not call it unrestricted nationwide commercial cultivation. ICAR clarification.
  • July 2024 Supreme Court split verdict left legal issues unresolved; formulation of a national policy was directed. Scheduled hearings in 2025 are not a final adjudication.
  • GEAC is under MoEFCC, not DBT; EPA 1986 and the 1989 rules govern relevant biosafety regulation. DBT, under Science and Technology, supports research.

Assessing claims

  • Potential productivity/resilience gains require field evidence; assess allergenicity, gene flow, non-target effects, herbicide practices, resistance and seed-access economics case by case.
  • Public-sector research and transparent regulatory evidence can coexist with precaution and farmer participation; neither all GM crops are dangerous nor all are automatically safe/high-yielding.
  • Source bioeconomy estimate US$165.7 billion, bio-agriculture US$13.5 billion/8.1%, is a dated sector estimate, not GDP. BioE3 (2024) concerns biotechnology for economy, environment and employment/high-performance biomanufacturing.
