# Fighter aircraft escape systems and rocket-sled testing

## December 2025: dynamic escape-system test
- **DRDO**, with **ADA and HAL**, conducted a high-speed rocket-sled test at controlled velocity, announced **2 December 2025**, at **Rail Track Rocket Sled (RTRS), Terminal Ballistics Research Laboratory (TBRL), Chandigarh**. [Ministry of Defence](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2197771&lang=2&reg=3).
- Validated sequence: **canopy severance → timed seat ejection → stabilisation/parachute deployment → aircrew recovery**. Canopy clearance prevents the seat/pilot striking the cockpit cover; correct sequencing is critical at fighter speeds.
- A rocket-propelled rail sled recreates dynamic loads on the ground while instruments record timing and performance. Dynamic ejection tests are more demanding than static/net or zero-zero tests. A successful ground trial demonstrates a testing milestone, not completed certification or fleet deployment on every Tejas/AMCA variant.
- Indigenous capability supports pilot survival, aviation safety, DRDO–HAL–ADA collaboration and reduced overseas dependence. The source links future use to Tejas and advanced combat aircraft; retain this as relevance rather than a delivery announcement.
- Practice key: RTRS tests **aircraft escape/ejection mechanisms (c)**, rather than satellite navigation, underwater torpedoes or a hypersonic wind tunnel.
