# Deinococcus radiodurans and impact survival

## March 2026 experiment
- **Deinococcus radiodurans** is an exceptionally radiation/desiccation-resistant bacterium with strong DNA repair and protective cellular structures. It is a bacterium, not a tardigrade or a virus.
- Johns Hopkins researchers simulated impact ejection using microbes between metal plates struck by a gas-gun projectile: **1–3 GPa** shock pressures. Survival was near complete at **1.4 GPa** and about **60% at 2.4 GPa**, roughly **14,000–24,000 Earth atmospheres**. Survivors activated DNA repair/iron-transport responses; injury increased with pressure. [University report,3March 2026](https://hub.jhu.edu/2026/03/03/life-forms-can-planet-hop-on-asteroid-debris-and-survive/).
- Implication for **lithopanspermia**: organisms shielded in impact-ejected rocks might survive one stage of interplanetary transfer. This is not evidence that life on Earth actually came from Mars, that Mars has life, or that full ejection→long space transit→atmospheric entry→establishment has been demonstrated in this experiment.
- **Planetary protection** must consider forward contamination of other worlds and backward contamination of Earth; resistance under controlled laboratory shocks informs assessment without establishing survival under every natural impact condition.
