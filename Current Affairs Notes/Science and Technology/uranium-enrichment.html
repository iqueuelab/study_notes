Science and Technology

Uranium isotopes, reactor fuel and safeguards

Editable notes

Conceptual recall

  • Natural uranium: about 99.3% U-238, 0.7% U-235, plus trace U-234. U-235 is fissile; U-238 is fertile and can produce plutonium-239 through reactor processes.
  • Enrichment increases U-235's proportion by isotope separation; it does not chemically turn U-238 into U-235. Centrifuges exploit small mass differences; uranium isotopes have essentially the same chemistry.
  • Light-water reactor fuel typically has roughly 3–5% U-235; India's conventional PHWRs use natural uranium. HALEU means enrichment above 5% and below 20%; reactor designs vary.
  • Do not infer that every nuclear reactor requires enriched uranium or that civilian fuel use equals a weapons programme.

Governance

  • IAEA safeguards verify relevant nuclear-material commitments and peaceful-use arrangements. NPT governs non-proliferation obligations; India is not an NPT party.
  • CTBT concerns nuclear explosive testing, not ordinary reactor-fuel enrichment; India has not signed it, and the treaty has not entered into force.
  • India–US civil nuclear cooperation and designated civilian-facility safeguards differ from blanket safeguards on all Indian facilities.
  • Mains: energy security, fuel supply, safety, waste management and non-proliferation; distinguish physical fuel concepts from international legal obligations.

June 2025 revision

  • Centrifugation uses the small mass difference between uranium isotopes, rather than different chemistry. The schematic force relationship is F = m r ω²; high-strength rotor materials withstand large mechanical stresses.
  • 2023 PYQ: India's coal dependence is correct; the assertion that electricity generation requires at least 60% enriched uranium is incorrect (c). Conventional Indian PHWRs use natural uranium, while typical LWR fuel is around 3–5% U-235.
