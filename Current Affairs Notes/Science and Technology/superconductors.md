# Superconductors: nickel oxides and the LK-99 claim

## Properties
- Below relevant critical temperature/field/current limits: **zero electrical resistance** and **Meissner effect** (magnetic-field expulsion). Perfect conductivity alone is not the full definition.
- Kamerlingh Onnes observed mercury superconductivity in **1911**. Bednorz–Müller's cuprate work began in **1986**, rewarded with **1987 Nobel Prize**.
- “High temperature” is relative to other superconductors; **40 K ≈ −233°C**, still very cold, not room temperature.
## March 2025 nickelate result
- NUS synthesised **(Sm–Eu–Ca)NiO₂**, copper-free oxide with transition near **40 K at ambient pressure**, reporting zero resistance **above 30 K**. It extends oxide superconductivity beyond cuprates, not the first copper-free superconductor of any kind. [NUS](https://news.nus.edu.sg/nus-physicists-copper-free-high-temperature-superconducting-oxide/).
- Onset temperature and fully zero-resistance temperature are different. Interlayer interactions and predictive material models guide research; bulk device production remains a separate engineering challenge.
## LK-99: failed replication
- Copper-substituted lead-apatite claim arose in **2023**, not as a confirmed 2025 breakthrough. Independent studies did **not establish room-temperature superconductivity**. [Replication study](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsomega.3c06096).
- Levitation or an apparent resistance change alone is insufficient; reproducible resistance, magnetic response and sample purity matter.
- Applications: MRI magnets, maglev, selected quantum circuits and power equipment. Cryogenic cost, critical limits and alternating-current losses mean entire devices are not automatically loss-free.
