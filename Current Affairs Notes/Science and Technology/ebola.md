# Ebola: transmission, prevention and treatments

## Disease recall
- Severe viral disease; **Orthoebolavirus genus, Filoviridae**. First recognised **1976**, outbreaks in **Yambuku, now DRC**, and **Nzara, now South Sudan**; Ebola River gave the name.
- Fruit bats, particularly **Pteropodidae**, implicated as natural hosts; humans and other primates can become ill.
- Infected animal exposure → spillover; human transmission through **direct contact with infected bodily fluids/tissues**, sick/dead persons or contaminated materials. Not principally mosquito-borne or ordinary airborne transmission.
- Incubation **2–21 days**; fever, weakness, vomiting/diarrhoea and sometimes bleeding; bleeding is not universal. Average case-fatality **about 50%**, varies widely by outbreak/access to care.
## Correct the treatment claim
- Source's 'no fully tested treatments' conflicts with established therapies: **Inmazeb / REGN-EB3** and **Ebanga / ansuvimab / mAb114**, monoclonal-antibody treatments for **Zaire/Ebola virus disease**, recommended by WHO in **2022**.
- These do not constitute an approved treatment for every orthoebolavirus disease. Licensed vaccines also target particular viruses; **Ervebo** is not a universal all-strain Ebola vaccine. [WHO treatment guidance](https://www.who.int/news/item/19-08-2022-who-makes-new-recommendations-for-ebola-treatments-----calls-for-improved-access), [WHO disease distinctions](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/ebola-disease).
- Early supportive care, fluids/electrolytes and symptom management improve survival; blood products used when clinically appropriate, not compulsory for every patient.
- Source reports **nanobody-based inhibitors** as research; experimental discovery is not proof of approved population-wide therapy.
- Response: detection/testing, isolation with safe care, contact tracing, PPE/infection control, safe dignified burials and vaccination where relevant; survivors need follow-up and non-stigmatising support.
