Science and Technology

Malaria: parasite cycle, vaccines and elimination

Editable notes

Organism and life cycle

  • Protozoan Plasmodium, transmitted principally by infected female Anopheles; important human species include P. falciparum /P. vivax. Not bacterial and not spread by every mosquito species.
  • Mosquito injects sporozoites →liver stage →merozoites infect red cells →some develop into gametocytes →mosquito ingests them →sexual development →sporozoites.
  • Repeated red-cell infection causes illness; falciparum can cause severe disease. Vivax dormant liver stages complicate relapse/elimination.
  • Laveran 1880 identified parasites; Ronald Ross 1897 demonstrated mosquito-linked development; Grassi and colleagues 1898 clarified human-malaria transmission. Science's colonial setting does not imply equitable health delivery.

Vaccines: recommendations versus experiments

  • WHO recommended RTS,S/AS01 in 2021; R21/Matrix-M in October 2023, for children against P. falciparum in endemic settings. WHO vaccine explanation.
  • Liver/pre-erythrocytic targeting complements nets, vector control and treatment; neither vaccine is 100% protection against every species.
  • Source 36% over four years /77% over one year arise from different trial settings/timeframes; do not compare as identical head-to-head effectiveness measures.
  • PfSPZ, blood-stage RH5 and transmission-blocking approaches are research strategies at different stages; not all licensed routine vaccines.

Elimination and public health

  • Georgia certified malaria-free, 23 January 2025; WHO certification requires at least three consecutive years without indigenous transmission plus prevention-of-re-establishment capacity. WHO announcement.
  • National elimination ≠global eradication. Imported infections do not automatically negate absence of indigenous transmission.
  • Surveillance, timely diagnosis/treatment, insecticide-treated nets, appropriate spraying, vector management and sustained funding; resistance, climate suitability, migration and weak health systems threaten progress.
  • Equity matters: reach remote/marginalised communities, sustain surveillance after case declines and integrate vaccination with existing services.

June 2025: RTS,S affordability and supply

  • 25 June 2025 announcement: GSK, Bharat Biotech and Gavi planned lower-cost RTS,S, with Bharat Biotech supply from 2026 and a progressive price reduction of over half to below US$5/dose by 2028. These are announced supply/price commitments, not delivered 2025 outcomes. Technology transfer, manufacturing efficiencies and capacity support affordability during Gavi 6.0 (2026–2030). Joint announcement.
  • Source's “RTS,S is the only malaria vaccine” was obsolete: WHO also recommended R21/Matrix-M in 2023, already recorded above. Vaccines complement nets, spraying, diagnosis and antimalarial treatment.
  • Other human-malaria species recalled here: P. malariae, P. ovale and P. knowlesi, alongside falciparum/vivax. Common early fever, headache/chills may begin 10–15 days after infection; severe disease disproportionately harms young children and pregnant people.
  • 2010 vaccine-difficulty PYQ: source answer (b). Understand this as absence of reliably protective natural immunity, rather than a literal claim humans develop no partial immunity: exposure can generate incomplete, non-sterilising protection, while complex life stages/immune evasion impede vaccination. Mosquito bites are the main route, not the only possible route; transfusion/congenital transmission can occur.

July 8: Coartem Baby approval

  • Swissmedic approved Coartem Baby (Riamet Baby), developed by Novartis with MMV, for infants/neonates 2 to less than 5 kg with acute uncomplicated P. falciparum malaria or mixed infections including falciparum. Artemether + lumefantrine, with a ratio/dose adapted to infant metabolism; not a vaccine or treatment for every severe case. Developer announcement.
  • Approval rested on the Phase II/III CALINA study. Burkina Faso, Côte d'Ivoire, Kenya, Malawi, Mozambique, Nigeria, Tanzania and Uganda participated in the regulatory assessment and were expected to grant rapid approvals; the sheet incorrectly calls all eight trial countries. Anticipated approval/access is distinct from completed rollout.
  • Dispersible infant formulation helps avoid unsafe extrapolation from older children's medicines; body weight/clinical eligibility matter more than a blanket age label such as “up to six months”.

August 2025 vaccines and elimination

  • AdFalciVax, ICMR-RMRC Bhubaneswar-led recombinant P. falciparum candidate: PfCSP pre-erythrocytic target fused with PfsPro6C(Pfs230/Pfs48/45 transmission-blocking subdomains), produced in Lactococcus lactis. July technology-transfer invitation sought partners; preclinical, not licensed or completed human trials. ICMR.
  • Source's room-temperature stability, mice protection and 7–8 year development window are preclinical claims/estimates, not guaranteed human efficacy/approval. GMP, clinical comparisons, immune markers and scale-up remain.
  • PfSPZ/PfSPZ-LARC2 whole-parasite approaches; PfRH5 blood-cell invasion target; Pfs230D1(Mali)/Pvs230D1M(Thailand) transmission blocking. Source 96% antibody response, 79% protection, 78%/96% transmission figures represent different trials/endpoints, not interchangeable routine effectiveness.
  • Both RTS,S/R21 use four-dose WHO schedules; “R21 requires fewer doses” is misleading. WHO 2024 position paper.
  • WHO 2024 report estimated 263 million cases/597,000 deaths in 2023, not 294 million. WHO report.
  • India 2030 elimination remains a target; asymptomatic reservoirs, vivax relapse, mixed infections, resistance and remote/tribal access complicate it. Jharkhand 20% mixed infections is source-specific study context, not national proportion. Vaccines complement nets, spraying, diagnosis, treatment and surveillance.

September 2025: AdFalciVax technology licensing

  • The embedded report states ICMR licensed AdFalciVax technology to five firms: Indian Immunologicals, Techinvention Lifecare, Panacea Biotec, Biological E and Zydus Lifesciences. These are technology-transfer/commercial-development rights, not regulatory approval for routine human vaccination. The July ICMR document explicitly describes a vaccine candidate. ICMR EoI.
  • Targets P. falciparum with pre-erythrocytic and transmission-blocking components. Preclinical validation involves ICMR-RMRC Bhubaneswar, NIMR and National Institute of Immunology; do not omit RMRC or equate animal findings with demonstrated clinical effectiveness.
  • Source's over nine months at room temperature concerns formulation stability/preclinical development, not nine months of proven human protection. Affordability, scale and impact depend on further development, trials, approval and deployment.
  • The issue's India 1.4% global /66% WHO South-East Asia-region burden lack a clear common reference year and must not be treated as newly measured September 2025 statistics. Elimination by 2030 remains a target; vaccination complements vector control, diagnosis and treatment.

24 November 2025: R21 price agreement

  • Gavi and UNICEF announced an R21/Matrix-M procurement agreement: future price US$2.99 per dose, anticipated to take effect in about one year, not immediate November 2025 pricing. Gavi backing and UNICEF execution use an advance payment enabled by IFFIm, which raises vaccine finance through bond markets.
  • Projected savings up to US$90 million over five years could fund over 30 million extra doses, fully vaccinating nearly seven million additional children. These are expected programme effects, not children already vaccinated by this deal. Gavi announcement.
  • Gavi’s dated announcement reports over 40 million malaria-vaccine doses delivered and routine vaccination in 24 African countries that together represent over 70% of global malaria burden. The PDF incorrectly transfers that latter figure to Africa as a whole. Delivered doses, fully immunised children and burden shares measure different things.
  • Existing notes distinguish WHO-recommended/prequalified R21/Matrix-M (Oxford/SII, Novavax Matrix-M adjuvant) and RTS,S/AS01 (GSK/PATH), four-dose schedules, and vaccines as complements to nets, diagnosis and treatment. The new agreement concerns R21; do not merge it with June’s separate RTS,S price/supply announcement.

December 2025: urban malaria and elimination gaps

  • Issue discusses the Malaria Elimination Technical Report 2025: national 2030 elimination goal, with zero indigenous cases by 2027 as an intermediate ambition. National progress does not mean every district has interrupted transmission; imported cases and indigenous transmission require separate surveillance.
  • Major correction: Anopheles stephensi is native in parts of South Asia, including India, and the Arabian Peninsula. It is an invasive threat in newly colonised regions, notably Africa; the sheet wrongly makes Africa its origin and labels it newly invasive throughout India. It transmits P. falciparum and P. vivax and is a longstanding urban-malaria vector. WHO vector database.
  • Artificial water containers/overhead tanks and construction-associated storage can sustain breeding. Dense settlements, water-storage practices, construction, private-sector reporting gaps and fragmented urban services require city-specific larval-source management, vector surveillance and coordinated municipal/health action.
  • Source highlights Odisha, Tripura and Mizoram, tribal/forested/remote areas, occupational exposure, mobility and border links with Myanmar/Bangladesh. Combine regional data sharing, active detection, reliable diagnostics/treatment and last-mile supplies; do not blame every case on cross-border transmission.
  • Asymptomatic infections, vivax relapse, drug/insecticide resistance, inadequate entomological capacity and inconsistent private reporting can sustain hidden transmission. Operational research must test locally appropriate interventions.
  • Date correction: 2,27,564 reported cases belongs to 2023, not 2024 as the sheet says; comparator 11,69,261 in 2015. MoHFW's 2024–25 report dates these figures and 83 deaths in 2023 versus 384 in 2015. These reported counts differ from WHO modelled burden. MoHFW annual report.
