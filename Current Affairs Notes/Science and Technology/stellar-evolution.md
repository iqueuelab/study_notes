# Star formation, stellar evolution and variable stars

## July 2025 protostar finding
- IIST-led researchers reported first radio-continuum **circular polarisation** toward massive protostar **IRAS 18162−2048**, using the **Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array at 4–6 GHz**. It probes magnetic/particle processes near the forming star; proposed mechanisms include gyrosynchrotron emission and Faraday conversion. Do not present the PDF's “100 times Earth's field” as a uniquely established direct measurement. [Research preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04913).
## Revision chain
- Gas/dust nebula → gravitational collapse → accreting protostar → stable core hydrogen fusion on main sequence. **T Tauri** describes young low-mass stars; it is not a compulsory stage for every massive star.
- Sun-like stars: red giant → shed outer layers/planetary nebula → white dwarf. Massive stars: advanced burning, core collapse/supernova → neutron star or black hole, depending on remnant properties. A simple single birth-mass cut-off does not capture every outcome.
- Pulsars are rotating neutron stars observed through pulsed emission; magnetars have exceptionally strong magnetic fields. Black-hole accretion can emit observable radiation outside the horizon.
- Intrinsic variability reflects stellar processes; extrinsic variability includes eclipsing binaries. **Cepheids are pulsating variable stars; nebulae are gas/dust clouds**. In the supplied **2023 PYQ**, only pair 3 (pulsars—neutron stars) is correct: **(a)**. The ordinary Sun being eclipsed does not make every solar-eclipse event a separate stellar-evolution stage.
