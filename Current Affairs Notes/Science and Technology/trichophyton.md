# Trichophyton indotineae and antifungal resistance

## Pathogen and transmission
- **Dermatophyte fungus**, named **Trichophyton indotineae in 2020**, following study of isolates from India/Nepal. A geographic name does not establish where all infections originated.
- Causes itchy, inflamed **tinea/ringworm** skin lesions, often widespread across groin, buttocks, trunk or face; dermatophytes as a group can affect skin, hair and nails. **Ringworm is fungal, not a worm**.
- Contact with affected skin and contaminated towels/clothes; source reports detection in over **40 countries** and renaming advocacy by over **30 dermatologists in 14 countries**, January 2025 figures.
## Resistance and public health
- Often **terbinafine-resistant**; not every isolate resists every antifungal. Misuse of topical steroid–antifungal–antibiotic combinations can mask/worsen infection and contributes to the problem.
- Correct diagnosis, species/resistance surveillance, appropriate clinician-led treatment and antifungal stewardship; avoid treating a source regimen as personal medical advice. [CDC investigation](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/wr/pdfs/mm7219a4-h.pdf).
- Naming ethics: precise scientific communication without stigmatising a country/community.
