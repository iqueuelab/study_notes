# Chemotherapy drugs and medicine-quality safeguards

## Treatment principle
- Cytotoxic chemotherapy damages DNA, DNA synthesis or cell-division processes; systemic treatment can reach disseminated cancer and blood malignancies. Administration may be IV, oral, injected or topical depending on the drug; surgery/radiotherapy mainly act locally.
- Rapidly dividing normal cells also suffer: marrow suppression (anaemia, infection risk, bruising/bleeding), gastrointestinal effects, hair loss and fatigue. Toxicity depends on drug, dose/regimen and patient; side-effect severity does not establish tumour response. [National Cancer Institute](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/chemotherapy).
| Drug | Main mechanism / examples | Important toxicity recalled |
|---|---|---|
| Cisplatin | Platinum DNA damage; testicular, ovarian, bladder, some lung cancers | Kidney and hearing injury, marked nausea/vomiting, marrow suppression |
| Oxaliplatin | Platinum DNA damage; colorectal regimens | Peripheral neuropathy, GI effects, marrow suppression |
| Cyclophosphamide | Alkylating DNA damage; breast cancer, leukaemia, lymphoma, sarcoma | Haemorrhagic cystitis, immunosuppression, alopecia |
| Doxorubicin | Anthracycline disrupting DNA-related processes; breast/haematological cancers, sarcoma | Cumulative cardiotoxicity, marrow suppression, hair loss |
| Methotrexate | Antifolate impeding nucleotide/DNA synthesis; leukaemia, lymphoma, selected solid tumours | Liver injury, mucositis, marrow suppression, lung/kidney toxicity |
| Leucovorin (folinic acid) | Companion drug: rescue from high-dose methotrexate; enhances 5-FU regimens | Distinguish supportive rescue/potentiation from a cytotoxic drug itself; rare allergy |
- Regimens/indications are selected clinically; the table is revision material, not a dosing guide. [NCI regimen safety tables](https://ctep.cancer.gov/protocolDevelopment/sideeffects/regimes/regimes.htm).
## Medicine quality and governance
- The clipping reports roughly **20% of tested chemotherapy samples failed quality standards**, with **16 of 17 implicated manufacturers Indian**, and distribution across **100+ countries**. These are an investigation's sample-specific findings; do **not** infer one-fifth of all Indian cancer medicines fail or that every product from each manufacturer is defective.
- Quality assurance requires good manufacturing practice, independent batch testing, procurement traceability, pharmacovigilance, recalls and transparent regulator follow-up. Affordable generic access must retain potency and safety.
