Identification after disasters

  • June 2025 Air India crash illustrates DNA identification when visual recognition/fingerprints fail. DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) profiles compare remains with direct reference material (e.g. toothbrush) or biological relatives; results require statistical kinship/match assessment and corroboration, not a bare “match means certainty”.
  • Collect/document specimens, avoid contamination, maintain chain of custody and preserve material promptly; DNA degrades with heat, moisture and time. Bones/teeth can protect DNA better than exposed soft tissue. Source suggests ethanol preservation/cold storage; actual protocols depend on tissue and laboratory requirements.
MethodUse and limitation
Nuclear STRVariable short tandem repeats; routine individual/kinship profiling; degraded samples can require shorter targets
Mitochondrial DNAMany copies; useful in damaged remains and maternal-line comparison, but shared maternal relatives limit individual discrimination
Y-chromosome markersPaternal male-line comparison; paternal relatives may share the profile
SNPsSingle-base variants; short targets help degraded DNA, but no single SNP uniquely identifies each person
  • Mature human red cells lack nuclei/mitochondria: PDF's “every cell has mitochondria” is incorrect. Identical twins usually share routine DNA profiles. NIST disaster identification, NIST SNP review.

DNA barcoding: different purpose

  • Short standardised DNA regions identify species, especially look-alike/cryptic species, and detect undesirable plant/animal material in processed foods. It does not determine organism age.
  • 2022 PYQ: statements 2 and 3 are correct (d). Species barcoding differs from human individual/kinship profiling.
