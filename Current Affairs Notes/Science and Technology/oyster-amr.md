# Oyster proteins and antimicrobial resistance research

## January research finding

- **Southern Cross University**, oyster **haemolymph** proteins: laboratory antibacterial activity and improved action of conventional antibiotics against selected bacteria.
- **Antimicrobial proteins/peptides** are innate defences against microbes in marine habitats; potential drug leads require isolation, mechanism, toxicity, dosage and clinical validation. **Not an approved human treatment or evidence eating oysters cures resistant infection**. [University's 21 January account](https://www.scu.edu.au/news/2025/video---oyster-blood-proteins/).

## Biology traps

- **Mollusca → Bivalvia**; two irregular calcified shell valves, marine/brackish filter feeders drawing algae/particles across gills.
- Filtration depends on size, species and conditions; source **1.3 gallons/hour** is not a universal fixed rate. Many reproduce by broadcast spawning, with sex change in some species.
- Oysters have **nervous ganglia**, despite lacking a vertebrate-like brain/spinal cord. That alone does not establish a definitive capacity/absence of pain.
- Source **eyes all over body** confuses oysters with eye-bearing bivalves such as scallops; shell closure/sensory response does not require that anatomy.
- University describes **Sydney rock oyster** proteins disrupting/preventing bacterial **biofilms**, one reason for improved antibiotic action in laboratory work.
