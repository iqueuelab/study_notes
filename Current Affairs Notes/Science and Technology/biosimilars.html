Science and Technology

Biosimilars: comparability, testing and affordable biologics

Editable notes

Scientific distinction

  • Small-molecule generics reproduce a relatively simple chemical active substance; biologics are complex products made using living systems, such as insulin and monoclonal antibodies. Biosimilars are highly similar to a reference biologic, with no clinically meaningful differences in relevant safety/efficacy; they are not ordinary chemically identical generics.
  • Manufacturing complexity and costly comparative testing contribute to development cost; increased competition can improve access to expensive therapies.

August 2025 regulatory debate

  • Editorial proposes proportionate Indian requirements, using strong analytical/functional comparability and clinical studies only where remaining uncertainty justifies them. Do not record the proposal as a completed universal Indian waiver.
  • UK MHRA February 2025 guidance does not request animal in-vivo studies for biosimilar comparability and generally permits omission of comparative efficacy trials when justified; quality and appropriate human evidence still matter. MHRA guidance.
  • US FDA April 2025 roadmap announced staged adoption of human-relevant methods, including computational and organ-based models, especially for monoclonal antibodies. A roadmap is not proof all animal/clinical testing of every US drug was abolished by August. FDA April announcement.
  • Reform must validate alternatives and preserve manufacturing consistency, immunogenicity assessment and post-marketing surveillance; lower testing burden does not mean lower safety standards.

September EU recommendation for denosumab biosimilars

  • Embedded clipping: EMA committee recommended Dr Reddy-linked denosumab biosimilars. CHMP opinions adopted 18 September 2025 for Acvybra (osteoporosis/bone loss) and Xbonzy (specified skeletal complications/giant-cell tumour indications), applicant Reddy Holding GmbH. Recommendation was not yet European Commission marketing authorisation. EMA meeting record.
  • Existing notes distinguish highly similar biologics from identical small-molecule generics, manufacturing consistency, immunogenicity and appropriate comparability. Affordability/competition can expand NCD care; regulatory clarity, research capacity, investment and surveillance determine access.
  • Source ₹100-plus crore /6–7 years development figures are indicative industry estimates, not mandatory universal thresholds. Zydus's 2014 adalimumab biosimilar example does not mean it invented the world's first biosimilar of any biologic; patent expiry alone does not confer regulatory approval.
