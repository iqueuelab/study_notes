Science and Technology

Bullseye Galaxy and collisional rings

Editable notes

Discovery and mechanism

  • LEDA 1313424, nicknamed Bullseye, is a collisional ring galaxy. February 2025 observations identified nine star-filled rings: Hubble resolved eight and the W. M. Keck Observatory confirmed the ninth.
  • A blue dwarf galaxy passed through its centre about 50 million years ago. Gravitational disturbance → outward/inward density waves in gas → compressed regions and star formation → rings.
  • A gas trail links the galaxies, now separated by about 130,000 light-years. Bullseye's diameter is about 250,000 light-years. A faded tenth ring is a hypothesis.
  • Ring positions test dynamical models; this is an unusually numerous ring system, not the first ring galaxy. NASA discovery account.

Galaxy evolution and dark matter

  • Authors discuss a possible route towards a giant low-surface-brightness galaxy: an extended diffuse disk with substantial gas. This is an evolutionary interpretation, not an observed completed transformation.
  • Gas motions and mass distribution can constrain the gravitational dark-matter halo. Rings do not by themselves demonstrate non-gravitational dark-matter interactions or directly detect dark-matter particles. Research paper.
