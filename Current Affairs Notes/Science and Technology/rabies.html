Rabies: transmission, prevention and public health

Editable notes

Infection and prevention

  • Rabies virus, an RNA virus (Rhabdoviridae), causes a zoonotic neglected tropical disease of the central nervous system. Infected mammal saliva enters via bites/scratches or broken skin/mucosa; dogs cause up to 99% of human cases.
  • Virus moves from exposure tissues along peripheral nerves → brain → salivary glands. Incubation commonly 2–3 months, varying from about a week to a year. Early fever/pain/tingling may progress to furious disease (hydrophobia, agitation) or paralytic disease; clinically apparent rabies is virtually always fatal.
  • Prompt post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) prevents disease: thorough wound washing for at least 15 minutes, urgent professional care, a validated vaccine schedule and RIG when indicated. Do not delay for symptoms. The source's universal “HRIG +4–5 doses” is too rigid: route/regimen/prior immunisation and exposure category determine treatment.
  • Mass dog vaccination, bite prevention, surveillance and accessible PEP form a One Health response; pre-exposure vaccination serves selected high-risk groups. WHO's ~59,000 annual deaths is a modelled global estimate, not reported cases; under-reporting is substantial. WHO fact sheet.

December 2025: unequal access to prevention

  • Editorial frames preventable deaths as an equity/health-system failure: rural/remote people, children, daily-wage workers and people exposed to free-roaming dogs face risks aggravated by poor awareness, travel/cost, vaccine shortages and scarce passive immunisation. The Assam case illustrates reported delay/referral failures; one case does not quantify all national causes.
  • Burden correction: the frequently repeated ~20,000 Indian deaths/year is an older estimate, not a newly measured December 2025 annual toll. A 2022–23 survey/modelling study, online September 2024 and in the January 2025 Lancet Infectious Diseases, estimated 5,726 annual deaths (95% uncertainty interval 3,967–7,350). Estimated mortality, reported suspected deaths and confirmed surveillance deaths differ. Study.
  • Same study: 20.5% of dog-bite victims received no anti-rabies vaccine; 49.1% of those who received at least one dose did not complete the course. The second percentage's denominator is vaccinated starters, not every bite victim.
  • Existing notes cover nerve spread and prompt 15-minute wound washing + urgent clinical assessment + appropriate vaccination, with RIG/passive antibodies when indicated. Tetanus care is an additional wound-care issue, not an anti-rabies substitute; vaccination must not wait for symptoms. Clinically apparent rabies is virtually always fatal, more precise than the sheet's absolute 100% claim.
  • Source's 60 million doses needed/50 million produced/15 million exported, ₹5,000–₹20,000 RIG cost, 80 million dogs/20 million bites are inadequately dated/specified estimates; they do not establish a matched national supply balance. Indigenous rabies monoclonal antibodies may improve access, subject to approved indications/guidelines and surveillance; not all products share one price or stage of approval.
  • The issue recalls November 2025 court directions concerning dogs in institutional/public premises and sheltering after sterilisation/vaccination. Treat their scope/status carefully rather than reading them as an unqualified new December ban on every stray dog in all public space.
  • Response: reliable free/affordable PEP and passive immunisation, trained primary-care/referral staff, stock monitoring, clear communication against harmful remedies, integrated human/animal surveillance, mass dog vaccination and humane evidence-based population management. Public-health protection and animal welfare require implementation capacity, not slogans alone.
