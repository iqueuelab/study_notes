# Bio-based chemicals and industrial enzymes

## Inputs, processes and policy
- **Bio-based chemicals** use biological feedstocks—sugars, starch, agricultural residues and other biomass—instead of fossil carbon. Microbial fermentation, metabolic engineering and enzymatic conversion can produce lactic acid, bio-alcohols, solvents, surfactants and chemical intermediates for plastics, cosmetics and pharmaceuticals.
- **Enzymes are biological catalysts**: they accelerate reactions without being consumed in the net reaction. Their specificity and operation under milder conditions can save energy in detergents, food processing, pharmaceuticals, textiles and pulp/paper; performance depends on enzyme/process conditions.
- DBT's **BioE3 (Biotechnology for Economy, Environment and Employment)** policy, approved **24 August 2024**, includes high-value bio-based chemicals, biopolymers and enzymes. Bio-AI hubs, biofoundries and biomanufacturing hubs support research, scale-up and commercialisation. [Cabinet approval](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2048569&lang=2&reg=3).
- India's agricultural feedstocks and fermentation expertise offer residue value addition, rural markets and petrochemical-import substitution. Source examples include Praj, Godrej Industries, Godavari Biorefineries and Jubilant Ingrevia; examples do not establish market leadership or current market shares.
- Main constraints: cost competitiveness, dependable biomass collection, product purification, pilot/demonstration infrastructure, industrial compatibility and demand creation. EU bioeconomy measures and US BioPreferred procurement illustrate market support; lifecycle land/water/energy impacts matter before claiming all bio-based products have lower emissions.
