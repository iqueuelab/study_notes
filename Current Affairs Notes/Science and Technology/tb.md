# Tuberculosis: elimination and health-system gaps

## Disease and resistance
- **Mycobacterium tuberculosis**, chiefly airborne from infectious pulmonary TB; lungs commonly affected, but **extrapulmonary TB (EP-TB)** involves other organs. **Latent infection is not contagious active disease**.
- Symptoms: persistent cough/sputum, weight/appetite loss, fever/night sweats and fatigue. Risks include **undernutrition, HIV, diabetes, smoking/alcohol**, and occupational exposure/social barriers.
- **MDR-TB**: resistance to at least **isoniazid + rifampicin**. **Pre-XDR**: MDR/rifampicin-resistant TB plus any fluoroquinolone resistance. **XDR** adds resistance to **bedaquiline or linezolid**. Source graphic's “isoniazid, rifampicin, fluoroquinolone and second-line injectable” reflects the **old definition**. [WHO 2021 update](https://www.who.int/news/item/27-01-2021-who-announces-updated-definitions-of-extensively-drug-resistant-tuberculosis).
- **BPa LM = bedaquiline + pretomanid + linezolid + moxifloxacin**; shorter regimen for eligible drug-resistant cases under clinical/programme criteria. Other named drugs **isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide, ethambutol, streptomycin** are not an instruction to self-combine treatment.
## Dated targets and burden
- India **2025 elimination ambition**, global **2030 End TB/SDG horizon**; ambition is not proof disease eradicated. **NTEP**, **Pradhan Mantri TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan/Ni-kshay Mitras**, nutrition/community support and preventive therapy for eligible high-risk contacts; source **Project Akshaya Plus** contact-tracing/testing example.
- Source report **~26% global burden**, **EP-TB 24% notified cases**, **>95% treatment initiation**, **24% mortality reduction from 2015**: retain indicator/period labels; initiating notified cases differs from treatment coverage among all estimated incident cases.
- Graphic world deaths **1.32 million (2022) → 1.25 million (2023)**, about **3, 500 daily**; low/middle-income countries **87% burden**, Indonesia/China/Philippines/Pakistan plus India account for a major share. Notifications reflect diagnosis/reporting, not automatic incidence increase.
## Gaps and response
- Drug/diagnostic supply interruptions, including source **2023 shortages**; delayed EP-TB diagnosis, cartridge/staff shortages and underused **CBNAAT/Truenat**; high costs and migrant/occupational barriers.
- Decentralised rapid molecular testing and referral, trained workers, uninterrupted procurement, adherence/nutrition support and portable migrant records.
- **Idukki–Kudumbashree SHGs**: community participation; **Vietnam active case finding** in high-risk groups: adapt rather than copy uncritically.
- Prevention → find cases early → test resistance → complete appropriate treatment → support contacts/nutrition → monitor outcomes. COVID disruption and social determinants require sustained action beyond targets.

## Gender-sensitive TB services
- Higher male notifications reflect exposure and detection patterns; they do not prove women face no burden. Stigma, financial dependence, unpaid care and fear of rejection can delay women's diagnosis/treatment.
- COVID disruption, weak outreach and benefit delays compound barriers. Disaggregate notifications and treatment outcomes while recognising under-detection.
- Strengthen confidential counselling, accessible screening/referral, nutrition, mental-health support, workplace protection and timely entitlements; SHGs can support adherence without exposing private diagnoses.
- Nikshay Poshan assistance was **₹1,000/month** in this source period. Proposed extra incentives or microcredit are recommendations, not universally available scheme benefits.

- The practice sheet's answer including a 2023 launch is therefore wrong: with its printed statements, **3 and 4 only** is the correct combination.

## Genomic surveillance
- **Dare2eraD TB**, launched in 2022: DBT-supported work with CSIR/ICMR laboratories. Source milestone: 10,000 bacterial genome samples sequenced toward a **32,500** target through the Indian TB Genomic Surveillance consortium.
- Sequencing identifies *M. tuberculosis* diversity/resistance-associated mutations and informs diagnostics/transmission analysis; this is not sequencing 10,000 human genomes.
- Reported 7% resistance in the sequenced sample is not automatically national prevalence. Sampling and representativeness matter; AI-assisted diagnostic-time improvements require validation against appropriate tests.
- **Latent TB infection does not transmit TB**; progression to active infectious disease creates future risk. Subclinical active TB is a separate category. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/tb/about/inactive-tuberculosis.html).
- Elimination threshold (<1 case per million per year), India's 2025 ambition and WHO End TB percentage-reduction targets are distinct; the source's incidence comparison is not proof elimination was reached.

## BPaL and vaccination revision
- **BPaL = bedaquiline + pretomanid + linezolid**; distinguish **BPaLM**, which adds moxifloxacin. Six-month all-oral regimens replace much longer treatment for eligible resistant-TB patients; selection depends on resistance, eligibility and programme guidance, not a blanket prescription for every XDR case.
- **BCG**, developed by Calmette and Guérin from *M. bovis* and first used in 1921, is especially important against severe childhood TB. India's limited introduction began in 1948; National TB Programme followed in 1962. Protection against pulmonary TB varies; latitude alone is not a universal rule.
- Source repeats old burden figures and a suspect **2021 notification count of three lakh**; do not treat these as July 2025 surveillance. VPM1002/MIP are investigational vaccine references in the sheet, not proof of routine approval. Its broad claims about BCG preventing Buruli ulcer, neonatal respiratory infections or treating melanoma should not be read as standard universal indications.

## Global TB Report 2025: interpret rates and coverage
- **2015–2024**, India's estimated TB incidence fell **237 → 187 per lakh**, about **21% cumulatively**, compared with **12% global decline**. Correct the embedded headline's “21% a year”: this is a nine-year comparison. Mortality rates in the sheet **28 → 21 per lakh** are also period comparisons, not proof elimination. [Health Ministry, 12 November 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2189415&lang=1&reg=1).
- Ministry reports **treatment coverage 53% (2015) → 92% (2024)**, **26.18 lakh diagnosed** against **~27 lakh estimated incident cases**, and **90% treatment success versus 88% global**. Estimated incidence, diagnosis/notification, coverage and successful outcomes are distinct metrics/cohorts; do not force their rounded numbers into an exact arithmetic identity.
- December 2024-started intensified campaign: ministry's November update reports **24.5 lakh detected, including 8.61 lakh asymptomatic cases**; the issue adds **19 crore screened** as a campaign snapshot. These cumulative programme counts differ from calendar-2024 burden. Asymptomatic/subclinical active disease is not the same as noninfectious latent infection.
- The sheet's **15 lakh → under one lakh missing cases** is a source-period estimate with denominator uncertainty; “missing” means undiagnosed/unreported relative to estimates, not proof every remaining patient is untreated. Stable drug-resistant estimates do not justify calling MDR-TB “under control”.
- Active case finding, **Truenat/GeneXpert molecular diagnosis and resistance testing**, AI-assisted portable X-rays, decentralised care, ASHAs, nutrition and adherence support are complementary. Screening technology does not substitute for confirmatory diagnosis or continuity of treatment.
- **BPaL/BPaLM and drug-resistance definitions** are already covered; source graphic repeats the obsolete injectable-based XDR definition and must not override WHO's newer bedaquiline/linezolid-based definition. BPaL is not universally suitable for every MDR/XDR patient. **2025 elimination remained an ambition**, not a result established by this report. Practice: Truenat **rapid TB diagnosis (c)**, no exam year.
