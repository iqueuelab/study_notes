# Malaria: parasite cycle, vaccines and elimination

## Organism and life cycle
- Protozoan **Plasmodium**, transmitted principally by infected **female Anopheles**; important human species include **P. falciparum /P. vivax**. Not bacterial and not spread by every mosquito species.
- Mosquito injects **sporozoites →liver stage →merozoites infect red cells →some develop into gametocytes →mosquito ingests them →sexual development →sporozoites**.
- Repeated red-cell infection causes illness; falciparum can cause severe disease. Vivax dormant liver stages complicate relapse/elimination.
- **Laveran 1880** identified parasites; **Ronald Ross 1897** demonstrated mosquito-linked development; **Grassi and colleagues 1898** clarified human-malaria transmission. Science's colonial setting does not imply equitable health delivery.
## Vaccines: recommendations versus experiments
- WHO recommended **RTS,S/AS01 in 2021; R21/Matrix-M in October 2023**, for children against **P. falciparum** in endemic settings. [WHO vaccine explanation](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/q-a-on-rts-s-malaria-vaccine).
- Liver/pre-erythrocytic targeting complements nets, vector control and treatment; neither vaccine is 100% protection against every species.
- Source **36% over four years /77% over one year** arise from different trial settings/timeframes; do not compare as identical head-to-head effectiveness measures.
- **PfSPZ**, blood-stage **RH5** and transmission-blocking approaches are research strategies at different stages; not all licensed routine vaccines.
## Elimination and public health
- **Georgia certified malaria-free, 23 January 2025**; WHO certification requires at least **three consecutive years without indigenous transmission** plus prevention-of-re-establishment capacity. [WHO announcement](https://www.who.int/laos/news/detail-global/23-01-2025-georgia-certified-malaria-free-by-who).
- National elimination ≠global eradication. Imported infections do not automatically negate absence of indigenous transmission.
- Surveillance, timely diagnosis/treatment, insecticide-treated nets, appropriate spraying, vector management and sustained funding; resistance, climate suitability, migration and weak health systems threaten progress.
- Equity matters: reach remote/marginalised communities, sustain surveillance after case declines and integrate vaccination with existing services.

## June 2025: RTS,S affordability and supply
- **25 June 2025 announcement:** GSK, Bharat Biotech and Gavi planned lower-cost **RTS,S**, with Bharat Biotech supply from **2026** and a progressive price reduction of **over half to below US$5/dose by 2028**. These are announced supply/price commitments, not delivered 2025 outcomes. Technology transfer, manufacturing efficiencies and capacity support affordability during **Gavi 6.0 (2026–2030)**. [Joint announcement](https://www.bharatbiotech.com/images/press/World%27s%20First%20Malaria%20Vaccine%20%28RTS%2CS%29.pdf).
- Source's “RTS,S is the only malaria vaccine” was obsolete: **WHO also recommended R21/Matrix-M in 2023**, already recorded above. Vaccines complement nets, spraying, diagnosis and antimalarial treatment.
- Other human-malaria species recalled here: **P. malariae, P. ovale and P. knowlesi**, alongside falciparum/vivax. Common early fever, headache/chills may begin **10–15 days after infection**; severe disease disproportionately harms young children and pregnant people.
- **2010 vaccine-difficulty PYQ: source answer (b).** Understand this as absence of reliably protective natural immunity, rather than a literal claim humans develop no partial immunity: exposure can generate incomplete, non-sterilising protection, while complex life stages/immune evasion impede vaccination. Mosquito bites are the main route, **not the only possible route**; transfusion/congenital transmission can occur.

## July 8: Coartem Baby approval
- **Swissmedic approved Coartem Baby (Riamet Baby)**, developed by Novartis with MMV, for infants/neonates **2 to less than 5 kg** with acute uncomplicated *P. falciparum* malaria or mixed infections including falciparum. **Artemether + lumefantrine**, with a ratio/dose adapted to infant metabolism; not a vaccine or treatment for every severe case. [Developer announcement](https://www.novartis.com/news/media-releases/novartis-receives-approval-first-malaria-medicine-newborn-babies-and-young-infants).
- Approval rested on the **Phase II/III CALINA** study. Burkina Faso, Côte d'Ivoire, Kenya, Malawi, Mozambique, Nigeria, Tanzania and Uganda participated in the **regulatory assessment** and were expected to grant rapid approvals; the sheet incorrectly calls all eight trial countries. Anticipated approval/access is distinct from completed rollout.
- Dispersible infant formulation helps avoid unsafe extrapolation from older children's medicines; body weight/clinical eligibility matter more than a blanket age label such as “up to six months”.

## August 2025 vaccines and elimination
- **AdFalciVax**, ICMR-RMRC Bhubaneswar-led recombinant **P. falciparum** candidate: **PfCSP** pre-erythrocytic target fused with **PfsPro6C(Pfs230/Pfs48/45 transmission-blocking subdomains)**, produced in **Lactococcus lactis**. July technology-transfer invitation sought partners; **preclinical**, not licensed or completed human trials. [ICMR](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/Call/1752741763_eoinoicmreoipm05malariavaccineadfalcivax2025.pdf).
- Source's room-temperature stability, mice protection and 7–8 year development window are preclinical claims/estimates, not guaranteed human efficacy/approval. GMP, clinical comparisons, immune markers and scale-up remain.
- **PfSPZ/PfSPZ-LARC2** whole-parasite approaches; **PfRH5** blood-cell invasion target; **Pfs230D1(Mali)/Pvs230D1M(Thailand)** transmission blocking. Source 96% antibody response, 79% protection, 78%/96% transmission figures represent different trials/endpoints, not interchangeable routine effectiveness.
- **Both RTS,S/R21 use four-dose WHO schedules**; “R21 requires fewer doses” is misleading. [WHO 2024 position paper](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/who-wer-9919-225-248).
- WHO 2024 report estimated **263 million cases/597,000 deaths in 2023**, not 294 million. [WHO report](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/malaria/world-malaria-reports/world-malaria-report-2024.pdf).
- India 2030 elimination remains a target; asymptomatic reservoirs, vivax relapse, mixed infections, resistance and remote/tribal access complicate it. Jharkhand 20% mixed infections is source-specific study context, not national proportion. Vaccines complement nets, spraying, diagnosis, treatment and surveillance.
