EAST, nuclear fusion and the path to usable energy

Editable notes

Experiment and physics

  • China's EAST, Hefei, superconducting tokamak: magnetic confinement of plasma in a toroidal chamber; “artificial Sun” is a nickname.
  • 20 January 2025: 1,066 seconds high-confinement plasma operation at about 70 million°C. This is a confinement achievement, not commercial electricity or proof of net power gain; PDF's 100 million°C is not the reported figure for this record. ITER account.

Fuel, safety and engineering

  • Deuterium available from water; tritium scarce, must be produced/bred, commonly using lithium concepts. Both are not equally abundant off-the-shelf fuels.
  • Plasma cannot sustain an ordinary fission-like runaway chain reaction; nevertheless heat loads, neutron damage, activated components and tritium handling require safeguards.
  • Fusion is not automatically zero radioactive waste /zero lifecycle emissions /cheap. Commercial viability needs net electricity, long-duration reliability, fuel breeding, maintainable materials and competitive costs.
  • ITER, Cadarache, France: international experimental project; India one of seven members alongside China, EU, Japan, Korea, Russia and US. Not a commercial power station.
  • Magnets, cooling, plasma stability, materials, breeding blankets and international research are complementary tasks. A laboratory duration record does not by itself replace solar, wind or existing generation.

May–June 2025: central solenoid status

  • US ITER/General Atomics completed testing of the sixth production module in May; it was to ship in summer. Four modules were already delivered/stacked and the fifth en route. The PDF's completed-delivery narrative is premature; completion of manufacturing/testing differs from delivery and assembly. ITER, 5 May 2025.
  • Six cylindrical modules + a seventh spare; completed tower about 18 m high. Official module mass 110 tonnes, not the PDF's roughly 121 tonnes after pound conversion. Niobium–tin superconducting cable supplied by Japan, fabricated/tested in US; support exoskeleton resists electromagnetic forces.
  • Central solenoid acts as a transformer to induce/control plasma current, which contributes heating and a confinement field; toroidal/poloidal coils together provide confinement/control. It is not alone a device compressing plasma into fusion or proof the reactor is running.
  • Superconductivity means negligible electrical resistance below critical conditions; cryogenic magnets operate far colder than the ~150 million°C plasma. Plasma: ions/electrons; cryostat: insulated vacuum enclosure supporting a cold environment.
  • Q=10 target: 500 MW fusion power /50 MW injected plasma heating, not 500 MW electricity or tenfold gain over all facility energy. ITER is experimental and will not generate electricity. The updated research plan targets deuterium–tritium operation around 2039; the PDF's 2040 is an approximate future expectation, not completion achieved. ITER objectives.
  • India participates through ITER-India/IPR, Gujarat; in-kind work includes cryostat, cooling and diagnostics, with a construction-share formulation about 9.1%. Seven formal members matter more than the PDF's stale “35 countries” count. Engineering procurement diagrams show multinational supply rather than seven national self-contained reactors.
  • Challenges: sustained plasma, heat/neutron-resistant materials, fuel breeding, maintainability, cryogenics, costs and schedule; >$20bn cost is a source estimate with changing accounting. Existing EAST record and fusion/fission safety corrections remain applicable.

September 2025: proposed Indian fusion roadmap

  • The embedded newspaper clipping reports an IPR Gandhinagar research roadmap, distinct from the repeated central-solenoid explainer: Steady-state Superconducting Tokamak–Bharat (SST-Bharat) envisaged as a fusion–fission hybrid, source 130 MW total including 100 MW from fission, gain aspiration 5, estimated construction ₹25,000 crore. These are researchers' proposed design/cost figures, not a sanctioned operating plant.
  • Long-term source aspiration: a full-scale demonstration reactor by 2060, 250 MW, gain 20. Keep plasma gain, total facility input and net electricity separate; an aspirational gain ratio does not demonstrate commercial viability. SST-1 is an existing research machine and is not an electricity generator.
  • Proposed tools: digital twins to test designs before construction, machine-learning-assisted confinement and radiation-resistant materials. Magnetic confinement guides hot plasma with fields; inertial confinement rapidly compresses fuel, commonly with powerful lasers. India's ITER participation develops expertise but does not prove a domestic power plant is ready.
  • Comparative plasma-duration records show progress, not assured net electricity. Costs, realistic timelines, tritium/materials/maintenance, sustained funding and wider Indian industry participation remain open issues; spinoffs include magnets, plasma modelling and high-temperature engineering.
  • Repeated 2008 PYQ remains Southern France (b). Existing notes retain corrections to EAST temperature, scarce tritium, activated waste, central-solenoid delivery stages and ITER's Q=10 target.

January 2026: EAST density-limit experiment

  • EAST, Hefei experiments reported stable line-averaged electron density at 1.3–1.65 times the Greenwald limit, compared with a usual EAST range about 0.8–1.0. This is a particular experimental operating regime, not elimination of every density/engineering constraint. Research preprint, Chinese Academy of Sciences, 6 January.
  • Greenwald density limit is empirical and depends on plasma current/cross-sectional area; it is not one universal fixed density for every tokamak. Source compares about 5.6 × 10¹⁹ electrons/m³ with 3.4 × 10¹⁹. ECRH-assisted Ohmic start-up, controlled fueling and wall/divertor conditions support the density-free-regime interpretation; lowering impurity radiation/heat losses can improve stability. The source’s lithium-conditioned tungsten-wall description is experiment-specific, not every tokamak's compulsory design.
  • Fusion performance requires a sufficient density × temperature × energy-confinement-time combination. Changing one can relax other requirements in a specified regime, but no universal “double density automatically halves temperature” law follows. Burning plasma means fusion self-heating dominates; ignition is a stronger condition, not synonymous with any stable EAST discharge. This study did not demonstrate a commercial power plant or net electricity.
  • Pages 13–17 repeat ITER/central-solenoid background and the old duration record. Existing corrections remain: 1,066 seconds at roughly 70 million°C, not 100 million°C; central solenoid induces plasma current, works with other coils; Q=10 means 500 MW fusion /50 MW injected heating, not tenfold facility electricity gain; tritium is scarce and neutron-activated materials still require waste controls.
  • 2008 CSE PYQ: Southern France (b), Cadarache, already banked. Reprinted magnet-delivery text is not evidence of a new January shipment or that ITER now generates electricity.
