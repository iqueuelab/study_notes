# AIKosha and IndiaAI compute access

## Data and infrastructure
- **AIKosha**, India AI datasets platform, launched March 2025; supports datasets/models for researchers, startups and public applications, including Indian-language work.
- India AI Mission approved 2024, ~₹10, 372 crore; Compute Portal expands shared/subsidised GPU access. Source 316 datasets/14, 000 GPUs are launch-era counts, not permanent inventory. [Meit Y launch](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2108961&lang=1&reg=1).
- Data.gov.in already aggregates open government data; AIKosha is not India's first public-data portal. Departmental Chief Data Officers support sharing.
- Data quality, licences, consent/privacy, re-identification risk, security and representative languages matter; “non-personal” is not a guarantee all processing is unrestricted.
- Gopalakrishnan non-personal-data committee was constituted 2019, not 2018; recommendations do not create an automatic compulsory private-data sharing law.
## AI compute: 28 April 2025 editorial
- **Training** develops model parameters; **inference** runs trained models. Both require suitable chips, memory, networking, dependable electricity and data centres; GPU counts alone do not measure usable capacity or domestic chip manufacture.
- IndiaAI uses empanelled private providers and subsidised shared access to reduce barriers for startups/researchers. Official January 2025 announcement described **18,000+ GPUs** and **40% subsidy**; the issue's roughly **19,000** is an April-era estimate, not a permanent installed inventory. Source cites approximately **₹4,500 crore over five years for compute**, distinct from the whole mission's roughly ₹10,372 crore. [January announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx/PRID=2097709).
- Policy rationale: stimulate nascent demand, widen access, support public-priority applications and domestic capability, and prevent vendor concentration. “Sovereign compute” requires control/access/security arrangements; foreign GPUs or privately hosted infrastructure do not automatically mean sovereign hardware production.
- Editorial critique: lowest-bid matching can depress sustainable margins, quality and R&D; eligibility/application friction burdens small users; subsidies/price controls can distort private demand; limited scale and narrow application focus may constrain frontier research. The claim that only 25% of H100 demand is domestic is an **unverified editorial estimate**, not a census of India's compute market.
- Reform proposals: assess reliability/performance and innovation alongside price; simplify access; combine compute support with model/R&D grants and talent; scale public-private investment; adapt procurement to inference as well as training; improve chip imports and sustainable power/data-centre capacity.
- Balance affordability against fiscal efficiency, utilisation, competition and future hardware obsolescence. Source comparisons with Meta spending are illustrative and not like-for-like public programme budgets. These are policy arguments, not proof the mission has failed or that all proposed reforms were implemented.

## Strategy before implementation: editorial argument
- The editorial calls for an updated, democratically debated national AI strategy linking missions to **strategic autonomy, data control, jobs, inclusion, public-service accountability and environmental costs**. Mission execution and national strategic priorities serve different functions.
- “India has no AI strategy” is too absolute: **NITI Aayog published its National Strategy for AI discussion paper in June 2018**. The article seeks a more comprehensive Cabinet-backed/parliamentarily debated framework, not discovery that no prior policy work existed. [2018 official account](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=181007).
- Risks: foreign model/compute dependence in sensitive uses; platform/data concentration; opaque healthcare, police/welfare decisions; unequal access to gains. Protect privacy/consent, representative data, contestability and independent oversight.
- Source **65,000 jobs cut at TCS/Infosys/Wipro in 2024** is an employment-change claim, not proof AI caused all reductions. **26% workforce exposure** estimates potential task exposure, not realised job loss or certain replacement.
- Proposals: Cabinet strategy presented to Parliament, dedicated parliamentary oversight, national study of sector/region/group employment effects, reskilling and social protection. These were recommendations, not newly established institutions; Global South/GPAI leadership needs domestic credibility.

## February 2026: compute partnerships and indigenous models
- The issue reports **NVIDIA collaboration with Yotta, L&T and E2E Networks** for Indian AI cloud/data-centre capacity and model deployment. Distinguish announcements from installed/operational compute; hardware procurement, power/cooling, networking and trained staff all matter.
- **Yotta's 18 February announcement** envisaged **20,736 NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra GPUs**, over **US$2 billion investment**, with operations expected by August 2026. The announced plan is not evidence that this full capacity was already available in February. [Yotta announcement](https://yotta.com/press-releases/yotta-to-deploy-20000-nvidia-blackwell-ultra-gpus/).
- The clipping describes Sarvam's indigenous model announcement as **35B/105B**. Contemporary NVIDIA technical documentation identifies the smaller model as **Sarvam 30B**, not 35B; the larger announcement in the issue is **105B**. An announced model and promise of open weights do not establish that weights were already publicly downloadable. [NVIDIA technical account, 18 February](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-nvidia-extreme-hardware-software-co-design-delivered-a-large-inference-boost-for-sarvam-ais-sovereign-models/).
- A **parameter** is a learned model value, not a direct score of intelligence. More parameters alone do not guarantee better performance: data quality, training, architecture, task and evaluation matter. **Fine-tuning** adapts a trained model; **inference** uses it to generate an output. Domestic training capability, access/control and representative Indian-language data are distinct dimensions of technological autonomy.
