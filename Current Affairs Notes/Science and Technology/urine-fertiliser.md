# Urine recycling and percarbamide fertiliser

## Waste to resource
- Research uses porous graphitic carbon electrodes to reduce oxygen into hydrogen peroxide; interaction with recovered urea produces **percarbamide, a urea–hydrogen peroxide crystalline adduct**.
- Urea, CO(NH₂)₂, supplies nitrogen. It is not by itself a complete nitrogen–phosphorus–potassium fertiliser.
- Urine contains recoverable nutrients; the source estimates an adult's annual output at 450–680 litres, about 4 kg nitrogen and 0.3 kg phosphorus. Values vary with diet and collection.
- Reported near-100% product purity is an experimental result, not proof of commercial viability. Porosity and surface chemistry assist adsorption and reaction.
- Benefits: nutrient recovery and reduced wastewater nutrient loads. Test energy cost, pathogen/contaminant control, variable feedstock, crop response and environmental life-cycle performance before scale-up.
