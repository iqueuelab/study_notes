# Trisulphide metathesis: reversible sulfur-bond chemistry

## March 2026: sulfur exchange reaction
- Flinders-led researchers described **trisulfide/trisulphide metathesis**, exchange between molecules bearing **three linked sulfur atoms**, through a **thiosulfoxide intermediate**. The Nature Chemistry work offers rapid reversible S–S bond rearrangement under mild conditions, in some cases within seconds, without extra catalysts, heat or light.
- Demonstrations include modification of sulfur-containing **calicheamicin** without destroying the complex drug framework and chemically recyclable polyethylene-like materials. These are research demonstrations/potential tools, not proof an improved cancer therapy or an industry-wide recycling system is already available.
- The source notes use of **dimethylformamide (DMF)** solvent. Low reaction energy/clean exchange can be useful, but solvent hazards, recovery, scale, economics and full material lifecycle still determine environmental performance; “no reaction by-products” alone does not prove zero environmental impact.
- **Metathesis = exchange of molecular parts**; reversible bonds can enable repair/depolymerisation and circular materials. [Flinders research account](https://www.flinders.edu.au/research/articles/chain-reaction-chemistry-breakthrough).
