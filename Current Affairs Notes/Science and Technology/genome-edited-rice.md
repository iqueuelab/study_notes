# Genome-edited rice: varieties, regulation and farmers’ rights

## May 2025 release
- **4 May:** government launched **DRR Dhan 100 (Kamala)** and **Pusa DST Rice 1**. ICAR-IIRR Hyderabad developed Kamala from **Samba Mahsuri/BPT 5204**; ICAR-IARI developed Pusa DST Rice 1 from **MTU 1010**. [ICAR press note](https://www.iari.res.in/files/Latest-News/ICAR_Genome_Edited_Rice_04052025.pdf).
- Kamala: source trial yield **5.37 versus 4.5 tonnes/ha**, maturity about **130 days/20 days earlier**, improved resource-use traits/strong stalk. Pusa DST: inland/coastal salinity and alkalinity tolerance; reported **9.66–30.4% yield gains under specified stress conditions**. Trial performance is not guaranteed on every farm.
- Shorter crop duration can save water/fertiliser and reduce methane; actual benefits depend on management/conditions. Food security, climate resilience and input efficiency link SDGs 2/13 ; increased yield does not automatically double net farmer income.
## Editing and regulatory distinction
- **SDN 1 :** targeted cut followed by repair without a repair template; **SDN 2 :** targeted small template-directed change; **SDN 3 :** larger sequence insertion, potentially foreign DNA. Editing changes DNA even when it introduces no transgene; “not genetically modified” is regulatory shorthand rather than absence of genetic change.
- India’s **2022 exemption for qualifying SDN 1/SDN 2 plants free of exogenous introduced DNA** concerns specified Rules 1989 provisions; it does not abolish all safety/release requirements. Traceability and developer documentation matter.
- Government’s “world first” describes its public rice-variety launch; do not turn it into a claim that rice had never been genome-edited in research anywhere. Foreign examples include edited tomato/soybean/fish, with different regulatory frameworks.
- **PPVFR Act 2001** recognises farmers’ rights to save/use/sow/resow/exchange/share/sell farm produce including seed, **excluding branded seed sale of protected varieties**. Source Article 21 food/seed-sovereignty framing is an argument, not an unlimited standalone seed-IP exemption.
- Evaluate intellectual-property access, public breeding, seed affordability, transparent trials and agronomic/ecological performance alongside innovation.

## Phosphate-use efficiency in japonica rice
- NIPGR's **Nipponbare (japonica)** study targeted a **30-bp W-box region in the OsPHO1;2 promoter**, where repressor **OsWRKY6** binds. It did not simply delete 30 bp from the WRKY6 gene. Removing WRKY6 entirely caused unwanted effects; promoter editing reduced repression of root-to-shoot phosphate transport.
- Study reports approximately **20% yield gain under full phosphate and 40% gain at 10% recommended phosphate**, with more panicles/seeds under experimental conditions. Foreign editing DNA was segregated away. Off-target prediction is not proof no unintended change can ever occur.
- Potential benefits: phosphorus-use efficiency, lower fertiliser dependence and runoff; translation to Indian indica varieties and farmer-scale performance still require testing. A study does not establish immediate national DAP-import savings. [Plant Biotechnology Journal](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/pbi.70165).
