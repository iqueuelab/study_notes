# S-400 Triumf and layered air defence

## System and operational limits
- Russian **Almaz/Almaz-Antey S-400 Triumf**, mobile surface-to-air missile system against aircraft, UAVs, cruise missiles and selected ballistic threats. Command centre, acquisition/engagement radars (including **91N6E**), launchers and transport/reload units operate as a network.
- Source missile-family recall: **40N6 ~400 km; 48N6 variant ~250 km; 9M96E2 ~120 km; 9M96E ~40 km**. Maximum advertised range depends on missile variant, target altitude/profile and configuration; it is not a guaranteed interception radius against every target.
- Source advertises radar detection **up to 600 km**, engagement **up to 80 targets** and Mach **14–15**. Treat these as configuration-specific headline claims; detection, tracking and engagement differ, and maximum target speed is not automatically every interceptor's speed. “Anti-stealth” does not establish assured detection/destruction of all low-observable aircraft.
## India and strategic autonomy
- **October 2018** contract, roughly **US$5.43 billion**, five systems; initial deliveries **2021**. Source's full delivery “expected by 2025” is a forecast, not proof all five had arrived by May 9. Initial Punjab-sector deployment is source-reported; avoid reconstructing sensitive present locations.
- Supports layered protection of infrastructure/airspace and complements other systems; integration with indigenous sensors/command networks, logistics, maintenance, ammunition and Russian supply dependence affect effectiveness.
- **CAATSA: Countering America's Adversaries Through Sanctions Act** creates sanctions risk for significant Russian defence transactions. India's purchase reflects defence needs/Russia ties and strategic autonomy; risk is not proof sanctions had been imposed on this purchase.
- Comparators have different missions: **Patriot PAC-3** terminal missile defence, **THAAD** high-altitude terminal ballistic interception, **India–Israel Barak-8** land/naval air defence. Source's Patriot ~160-km number mixes interceptor families; do not rank all systems using a single unqualified “maximum range”.

## June 27 delivery headline
- Source's news list reports Russia's Defence Minister **Andrei Belousov assured timely delivery of S-400 systems**. Its generic body still says completion expected “2025”; retain the earlier **2026 reported timetable** as a forecast and avoid presenting assurances as completed delivery.
- Graphic's network sequence: **surveillance radar → command vehicle → engagement radar/launcher → guided interception**. Acquisition range, target speed and engagement envelope are different measures; previous qualifications and six defence PYQ explanations apply.

## November 2025: reported Russia talks and layered defence
- The issue previews a **4 December New Delhi defence-ministers meeting** during the Russian President's visit; on 29 November this was a **scheduled meeting**, not a completed acquisition decision. It reports discussion of **S-500**, shipbuilding and joint projects, alongside BrahMos, AK-203 and the proposed Ka-226T programme; discussion does not establish a signed order or completed technology transfer.
- Source delivery snapshot: **three of five S-400 systems**, with the other two **reported due in 2026 and 2027**. These are forecasts, superseding earlier source schedules, not evidence of completed deliveries. It also reports selection of an Indian firm for an **MRO facility** without naming it; treat this as a reported maintenance-development step.
- **S-500** is a different Russian air/missile-defence system. Source quotes **~600 km against ballistic targets / ~400 km against aerial targets** and hypersonic/high-altitude interception claims; these are advertised/reported capabilities, not guaranteed envelopes or proof that India has bought or deployed it. Do not equate tracking range, interceptor reach and immunity to attack.
- **Mission Sudarshan Chakra**, announced **15 August 2025**, aims to expand indigenous protection of important strategic/civilian infrastructure over the next decade, towards **2035**. This national programme is distinct from S-400's Indian nickname; it does not itself confirm an S-500 purchase or an already impenetrable shield. Indigenous sensors, layered interceptors, command integration and maintenance remain important. [Prime Minister's address](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2156749&lang=2&reg=3).
