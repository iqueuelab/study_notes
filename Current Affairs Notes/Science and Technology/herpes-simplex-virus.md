# Herpes simplex virus: latency, transmission and prison health

## HSV essentials
- **HSV-1** mainly causes oral herpes and can cause genital infection; **HSV-2** mainly causes sexually transmitted genital herpes. Both persist in nerve cells, with latency and possible recurrent episodes.
- Transmission occurs through direct contact with infected oral/genital skin or mucosa, sores and relevant fluids. **Asymptomatic shedding is possible**; risk is greatest with active sores. HSV is **not an airborne infection** spread simply because people share a crowded room.
- Many infections are asymptomatic/mild; painful blisters/ulcers may occur, with fever/body aches/swollen nodes in a first episode. Severe/disseminated infection and encephalitis are uncommon but important, especially with immunocompromise; neonatal herpes is a separate serious risk.
- Antivirals such as **acyclovir, valacyclovir and famciclovir** reduce severity/duration and may suppress recurrence/transmission; they **do not eradicate latent infection**. [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/Herpes-simplex-virus).
- Jalpaiguri's outbreak underscores timely clinical care, confidentiality, appropriate infection control and non-stigmatising access to care. Crowd reduction helps health services but is not a substitute for identifying the actual route and type of infection.
