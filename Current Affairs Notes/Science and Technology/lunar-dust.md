# Lunar dust simulants and astronaut health

## Cell-model study
- **Life Sciences in Space Research 2025** compared **LMS-1(mare) and LHS-1(highland) lunar simulants** with terrestrial urban dust in bronchial **BEAS-2B** and alveolar **A549** cell models. Simulants are engineered Earth materials, not samples proving every lunar region's hazard.
- Compared fine **≤2.5µm particles** at controlled doses/times. Earth dust produced more inflammation/toxicity; lunar simulants still caused dose-dependent loss of viability, especially fine particles. “Less toxic than this comparator” does not mean safe to inhale. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lssr.2025.02.005).
- Sharp/abrasive grains and electrostatic adhesion can transport dust on suits into habitats and allow re-aerosolisation. Use suit/cabin isolation, filtration and exposure control; cell experiments do not establish a safe lifelong human dose.
- PM 2.5 refers to fine airborne particulate size, not a particular chemical. Artemis/other lunar mission planning motivates habitat/astronaut protection; launch schedules in the issue are forecasts.
