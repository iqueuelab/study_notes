Maitri-II and Indian Antarctic research

Editable notes

October 2025: proposed replacement station

  • Source reports Finance Ministry clearance for Maitri-II near the ageing Maitri station in East Antarctica, with NCPOR, Goa, under the Ministry of Earth Sciences as nodal organisation. Treat ₹2,000 crore and January 2029 as source-reported project estimate/target, not completed expenditure or an operational station.
  • Background: Dakshin Gangotri established 1983, Maitri 1989 and Bharati 2012; Maitri-II is planned near/replacing Maitri rather than evidence India already operates four simultaneous bases. The official December 2023 schedule already targeted January 2029; the October PDF's “seven years” and “sanctioned October 2024” do not form a consistent freshly announced schedule. MoES parliamentary answer.
  • Modern infrastructure aims to improve year-round research/logistics and environmental performance. Ice sheets, atmospheric chemistry/ozone, ocean–climate links, ecosystems and monsoon understanding support science and international cooperation; design goals such as renewable energy are not verified final construction features.
  • Antarctic Treaty reserves Antarctica for peaceful purposes; military personnel/equipment may support peaceful scientific work. Madrid Protocol prohibits mineral-resource activity other than scientific research. Do not frame the station as an approved mineral-extraction project or assume an automatic future expiry of the mining prohibition. Indian Antarctic Act 2022 provides the domestic regulatory framework.
