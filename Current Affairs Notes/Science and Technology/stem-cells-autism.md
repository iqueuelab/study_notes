# Stem-cell therapy for autism: evidence and regulation

## January judgment revisited in February 2026
- Supreme Court's **30 January 2026 judgment, W.P.(C) 369/2022**, held stem-cell intervention for **autism spectrum disorder (ASD)** impermissible as routine clinical treatment, with research limited to duly approved, regulated clinical trials. Commercial claims of an established cure cannot substitute for evidence. [Judgment](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2022/14452/14452_2022_7_1501_68117_Judgement_30-Jan-2026.pdf).
- Stem cells **self-renew and differentiate**; embryonic, adult and induced pluripotent cells are distinct categories. Promise in regenerative medicine does not establish safety/efficacy for every disease; established haematopoietic transplantation should not be conflated with unproven ASD interventions.
- ASD is a neurodevelopmental condition affecting communication/social interaction and patterns of behaviour; individual support needs vary. Studies discussed did not establish a recognised effective stem-cell treatment. Risks, informed consent, vulnerable families, advertising and medical tourism require evidence-based oversight by research/drug regulators and professional bodies.
- Ethical focus: scientific validity, independent approval, honest uncertainty, affordability, protection against exploitation and enforcement. Patient demand or consent alone cannot make an unproven commercial treatment standard care.
