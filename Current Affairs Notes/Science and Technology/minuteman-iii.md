# Minuteman III: ICBMs, MIRVs and nuclear deterrence

## March 2026: routine US ICBM test
- **3 March, 11:01 pm Pacific time** (reported 4 March): **GT 255**, unarmed **Minuteman III** from **Vandenberg, California**, carried **two test re-entry vehicles**. USAF states it was scheduled years earlier and was not a response to current events. Test payload differs from an operational nuclear deployment. Historical descriptions of single-warhead deployment should not imply an ongoing New START treaty constraint: the treaty expired on **5 February 2026**. [Contemporary CRS account](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF12964/IF12964.5.pdf). [USAF release](https://www.afgsc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/4421875/icbm-test-launch-verifies-multiple-re-entry-vehicle-system-reliability/).
- **LGM-30G**: L=silo-launched, G=surface attack, M=guided missile; deployed **June 1970**. Contractor **Boeing**, not the source's Douglas attribution. Three solid-propellant motor stages; official data **36,030 kg**, range **6,000-plus miles**, burnout speed **~15,000 mph**. The source's universal 13,000 km and near 100% reliability overstate what this fact sheet establishes. [USAF specifications](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104466/lgm-30g-minuteman-iii/lgm-30g-minuteman-iii/).
- Land ICBMs, submarine-launched missiles and strategic bombers form the nuclear triad; **MIRV** means multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles. The sheet lists **400 missiles** as of **February 2019**; do not treat source 440 as a verified current deployment count.
- Replacement **LGM-35A Sentinel**, formerly GBSD: February 2026 official target **early 2030s initial capability**, superseding the source's **2029** expectation. [Contemporary programme update](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/4407679/delivering-deterrence-sentinel-restructure-to-complete-in-2026-initial-capabili/).
