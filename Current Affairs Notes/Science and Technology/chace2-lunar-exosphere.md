# CHACE-2: coronal mass ejections and the lunar exosphere

## October 2025: observed lunar response to a solar storm
- **ISRO's 18 October announcement** reports Chandrayaan-2 orbiter **CHACE-2 (Chandra's Atmospheric Composition Explorer-2)** measurements during the **10 May 2024 CME episode**: dayside exospheric pressure rose and derived neutral-particle number density increased **more than tenfold**. This was the reported first direct observation of this CME-driven effect, consistent with earlier models; it is not the first observation of any lunar atmosphere. [ISRO announcement](https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO_EN/Chandrayaan-2_Coronal_Mass_Ejections_Lunar_Exosphere.html).
- **Coronal mass ejection:** solar-coronal plasma/magnetic-field eruption. Particle bombardment knocks atoms off the lunar surface (**sputtering**) into its tenuous exosphere. The Moon lacks a dense atmosphere/global magnetic-field shield; its temporary response informs space-weather modelling and future lunar-base engineering. An exosphere is not a breathable atmosphere.
- Exospheric particles also arise from micrometeorite impacts and radiogenic processes; helium, argon, sodium and potassium are examples, not a fixed dense atmospheric mix. Charged-particle/dust/equipment hazards require engineering assessment, rather than assuming this measurement proves every suggested communications failure occurred.
- Chandrayaan-2 launched **22 July 2019, GSLV MkIII-M1**, with an orbiter, Vikram lander and Pragyan rover; its orbiter continued science after the landing attempt failed. Correct the PDF's **“8 payloads: 5 orbiter + 3 lander”**: ISRO lists **8 orbiter payloads, 3 lander payloads and 2 rover payloads**. [ISRO launch release](https://www.isro.gov.in/Press%20Release5.html).
