# BlueBird and direct-to-device satellite communications

## Planned commercial launch: August 2025
- **BlueBird Block 2**, **AST SpaceMobile (USA)**, communications satellite for **direct-to-device mobile broadband**, including ordinary phones through compatible networks; distinct from NISAR radar/Gaganyaan crew capsule.
- Issue reports **~6,500 kg**, **LVM3 (formerly GSLV Mk III)** launch from **Sriharikota**; satellite arrival expected September 2025 and launch late 2025/early 2026. Forecasts, not completed milestones in this issue.
- Heavy commercial launches support revenue, domestic skills and India–US cooperation. Coverage, spectrum authorisation, bandwidth, terrestrial integration and delivered service differ from launch success.
- **NISAR=NASA–ISRO**, not ESA; L/S radar observes Earth. LVM3 naming does not make every configuration human-rated.

## 22 December 2025: LVM3-M6 launch preparation
- **BlueBird Block-2 for AST SpaceMobile (USA)** scheduled for **24 December**, commercial **ISRO/NSIL LVM3-M6** mission to **LEO**; retain this issue as pre-launch reporting. ISRO’s dated brochure gives **~6,100 kg**, superseding the earlier August estimate of ~6,500 kg for this flight. [ISRO mission brochure, 20 December](https://www.isro.gov.in/media_isro/pdf/LVM3M6/LVM3M6_Brochure_201225.pdf).
- **Sixth operational LVM3 flight**, not sixth launch of every LVM3 developmental/experimental version. Vehicle: **two S200 solid boosters, L110 liquid core, C25 cryogenic upper stage**. Chandrayaan/OneWeb heritage does not mean this particular commercial configuration has flown astronauts.
- Direct cellular broadband to **standard compatible smartphones**, through mobile-operator partnerships, can support remote areas and disaster communication. No special satellite handset does not mean no terrestrial gateways, spectrum permission or network infrastructure.
- Source’s **five BlueBird satellites launched September 2024** is background. Its statement that these already ensure continuous internet throughout the USA/other countries overstates a developing constellation; service continuity/capacity requires sufficient satellites, integration and licences.
- LEO (~160–2,000 km) generally reduces propagation delay relative to GEO, but requires tracking/handover and constellation coverage. Largest-commercial-communication-satellite claims are mission-specific, not the largest spacecraft of all types. Existing notes already cover digital inclusion and regulatory constraints.
