# Blue–green ammonia: integrating hydrogen production

## Integrated blue–green ammonia study
- **NH₃** links fertilisers, chemicals and prospective hydrogen/energy transport. Hydrogen generation is distinct from subsequent **Haber–Bosch ammonia synthesis** with nitrogen; steam reforming alone does not make NH₃.
- Grey: fossil-derived hydrogen without CO₂ capture; blue: fossil hydrogen plus capture/storage; green: renewable-powered electrolytic hydrogen. Green production is not automatically zero across its whole lifecycle, and ammonia combustion needs NOₓ control.
- The **Energy & Fuels study, online 13 September 2025**, models **electrolysis oxygen → autothermal reformer**, together with air supplying nitrogen, avoiding a separate air separation unit; electrolytic hydrogen is integrated into ammonia production. This is **autothermal reforming**, not merely ordinary steam methane reforming. [Original study](https://pubs.acs.org/enfuem/article/39/38/18694/3696236/A-Comprehensive-Costing-and-Emissions-Analysis-of).
- Modelled **levelised ammonia cost about 7% lower than standalone blue/green plants; lifecycle GHG about 63% lower than conventional blue ammonia**. These use different comparison baselines and model assumptions, not universal measured savings or 63% versus every grey plant.
- Indian relevance: fertiliser feedstock/import costs and low-carbon industrial strategy. Natural-gas prices, methane leakage, electricity carbon intensity, capture performance, water and capital costs determine actual outcomes; lower modelled cost does not establish an already reduced fertiliser subsidy.
