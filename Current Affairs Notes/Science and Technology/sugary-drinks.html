Science and Technology

Sugary drinks and cardiometabolic disease burden

Editable notes

2025 study: modelled attribution in 2020

  • Study across 184 countries estimated 2.2 million new type-2 diabetes cases and 1.2 million cardiovascular-disease cases, 2020: 9.8% / 3.1% of global incident cases attributable to sugar-sweetened beverages.
  • These are modelled population-attributable burdens with uncertainty, not proof that a particular person's disease was caused solely by one drink. Added-sugar beverages include soft/energy drinks, sweetened fruit drinks/lemonade; categories/doses matter. Primary Nature Medicine study.

  • Sub-Saharan increase 1990–2020: 8.8 percentage points diabetes / 4.4 percentage points CVD; percentage points ≠ relative-percent increase.
  • Attributable shares vary by sex/age; higher estimates in men/younger adults do not mean older people have lower absolute cardiovascular risk in general. Income vulnerability is not a universal country-independent ranking.

Mechanism and policy

  • Liquid sugar/energy intake, limited satiety and weight gain can contribute to insulin resistance and metabolic/vascular disease; glucose spikes alone are not the entire causal pathway.
  • Water access, public awareness, school/advertising safeguards, clear labelling and appropriately designed taxes; Mexico tax, 2014, cited consumption reduction, not instant eradication of diabetes.
  • Evaluate substitution, affordability/equity, industry reformulation and long-term health outcomes alongside revenue.
