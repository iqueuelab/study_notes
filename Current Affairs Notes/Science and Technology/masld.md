# MASLD and metabolic liver disease

## Terminology and mechanism

- **MASLD:** metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. The 2023 nomenclature uses **liver steatosis plus at least one cardiometabolic risk factor**; it is more than simply replacing an old label in every case.
- **MASH** replaces NASH for the inflammatory form with liver-cell injury. Greater alcohol exposure alongside metabolic dysfunction has a separate **MetALD** category. [AASLD nomenclature](https://www.aasld.org/new-masld-nomenclature)
- Risk factors include excess adiposity, insulin resistance/type-2 diabetes, abnormal lipids and hypertension; genetics and other endocrine/metabolic conditions also contribute. Children and **lean people** can be affected—normal body weight does not exclude disease.

**Possible progression:** steatosis → MASH → fibrosis → cirrhosis → liver failure/cancer. Progression is **not inevitable**, and individuals differ substantially.

## Detection and care

- Complications extend beyond the liver: cardiovascular disease, diabetes-related problems and kidney disease. Early disease may be clinically silent.
- Assessment combines history, metabolic evaluation and blood tests with imaging; elastography assesses stiffness/fibrosis risk, and biopsy is reserved for selected uncertain or higher-risk cases. Normal liver enzymes do not alone exclude disease.
- Healthy diet, activity, appropriate weight management and control of associated metabolic conditions remain central; individual treatment requires clinical assessment.
- **Correction to the source:** on **14 March 2024**, the FDA approved **resmetirom (Rezdiffra)** for selected adults with **non-cirrhotic NASH and moderate-to-advanced fibrosis**, with diet/exercise, through accelerated approval. Thus “no FDA-approved treatment” was already outdated in April 2025. This is not an approval for every person with fatty liver or evidence that all disease is curable. [FDA approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-treatment-patients-liver-scarring-due-fatty-liver-disease)
- India integrated NAFLD into its NCD programme framework on **22 February 2021**. [PIB operational-guidelines launch](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1699904&lang=2&reg=48) Screening/referral and sustained metabolic-risk management are more useful than treating an unverified single prevalence percentage as nationally representative.
## June 2025: lifestyle measures and source cautions
- Core MASLD/MASH definitions, risk factors, possible progression, diagnosis and **2024 FDA resmetirom approval** are already covered; PDF's “no FDA-approved medication” remains obsolete. Approval applies to selected adults with non-cirrhotic disease/moderate–advanced fibrosis, not every person with steatosis.
- Lifestyle evidence differentiates targets in adults with excess weight: **≥5% sustained loss** helps liver fat; **7–10%** improves inflammation; **≥10%** may improve fibrosis. Tailored activity preferably ** >150 min/week moderate or 75 min/week vigorous**,appropriate diet and metabolic-risk management; these are general clinical-guideline targets, not a guaranteed cure or identical prescription for every patient. [EASL–EASD–EASO 2024 guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC 11299976/).
- **PCOS, sleep apnoea, hypothyroidism, family/genetic susceptibility** can accompany metabolic risks; lean people/children may be affected. Cardiovascular and kidney/diabetes complications matter alongside liver disease.
- Ultrasound/MRI detect steatosis differently; **FibroScan elastography** chiefly supports fibrosis-risk assessment, not a stand-alone universal biopsy replacement. **ALT/AST can be normal** despite disease. Progression to cirrhosis/cancer is not inevitable; percentages/arrows in the page graphic are not individual forecasts.
- Source **“ICMR-INDIAB nearly 30% urban NAFLD”** lacks an identifiable cohort/test definition and should not be memorised as nationally representative liver-disease prevalence. INDIAB's well-known diabetes/metabolic estimates cannot automatically establish this liver statistic.
- India integrated NAFLD into the NCD programme in **2021**; Ayushman Bharat primary-care screening/referral is an implementation opportunity,not proof every centre delivers universal liver imaging. Prevent obesity/metabolic disease and strengthen follow-up rather than rely only on a prevalence slogan.
