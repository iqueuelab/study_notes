# Polio vaccines and surveillance transition

## 4 June 2025: network transition debate
- **WHO–India National Polio Surveillance Project began 1997**. Source reports phased NPSN reduction **280 units (2024–25) → 190 (2025–26) → 140 (2026–27)**, beginning June with lower funding and eventual integration into **IDSP**. These are reported transition plans, not evidence all closures happened on 4 June.
- WHO describes preserving essential functions and wider disease surveillance; Dr Jacob John argues withdrawal is premature given **Pakistan/Afghanistan WPV1 circulation**, importation risks and job insecurity. Staff transition and replacement capacity must be verified rather than treating assurances as completed implementation. [WHO transition framework](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/india-polio-transition-snapshot).
- Priorities: acute flaccid paralysis investigation, environmental/laboratory surveillance, high vaccination coverage, rapid response, trained workforce and sustainable financing. **Polio-free certification is not global eradication**.
## Disease and vaccine distinctions
- Poliovirus: **single-stranded RNA**, principally **faecal–oral/person-to-person** spread; mostly under-fives, but susceptible people of other ages can be infected. Many infections are asymptomatic; possible fever, fatigue, headache/vomiting/stiffness and irreversible paralysis. WHO approximate **1 in 200 infections paralysed; 5–10% of paralysed patients die when breathing muscles are affected**. No curative antiviral treatment; prevention and supportive/rehabilitative care remain essential.
- **Wild type 2 eradicated 2015; wild type 3 eradicated 2019; WPV1 remains. India’s last wild case 2011; WHO South-East Asia certification 2014**. Vaccine-derived viruses are a separate risk. [WHO polio facts](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/poliomyelitis).
| Vaccine | Distinction |
|---|---|
| **IPV/Salk** | Inactivated injected vaccine, strong protection against paralytic disease; cannot replicate/revert or cause VAPP. Less intestinal immunity than OPV is not simply “less potent” overall |
| **OPV/Sabin** | Live attenuated oral vaccine; strong gut/systemic response, easy mass administration; rare VAPP and, with prolonged circulation in poorly immunised populations, vaccine-derived-virus risk |
- **VAPP** is rare paralysis in a recipient/contact; **cVDPV** means evidence of community circulation of genetically evolved vaccine virus. Not all VDPV is type 2, and VAPP ≠ circulating outbreak. Source “40% of polio cases” lacks a denominator and “India does not classify VAPP as polio” should not erase real vaccine-associated paralysis. [WHO vaccine explanation](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/poliomyelitis).
- Transition toward IPV/ultimate OPV cessation must be coordinated with eradication strategy; Dr John’s recommendation is not proof India stopped OPV in June. Both vaccine technologies require product-specific cold-chain/safety rules; no inactivated vaccine is automatically “risk-free”, and not every live vaccine is oral/nasal.
- Practice: **statement 1 only**; inactivated IPV cannot regain virulence, unlike replicating attenuated OPV-derived virus.
