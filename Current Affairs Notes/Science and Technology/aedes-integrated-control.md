# Aedes mosquitoes: integrated disease prevention

## Combine vector control and community action
- **Aedes aegypti** transmits dengue, chikungunya and Zika; domestic water containers and discarded waste create breeding sites. Daytime biting means night-time bed nets alone are insufficient; fogging has limited reach, particularly indoors, and resistance can reduce chemical effectiveness.
- Weekly empty/scrub/cover water containers; manage tyres, coconut shells, coolers and solid waste; ASHA-led mobilisation and dependable municipal services complement personal protection. Delhi's **10 weeks, 10 AM, 10 minutes** campaign is the issue's example, not a universal transmission-season calendar.
- **Wolbachia** introduced into Aedes reduces viral transmission; deployment requires breeding/release capacity, community acceptance and surveillance. **2023 PYQ: (a), controlling mosquito-borne viral diseases**. Method-specific trial results should not be promised universally.
- Source cites transfluthrin spatial emanators and studies in Peru, Camino Verde and Chennai. Its **34%, 29% and 94%** estimates measure different outcomes/settings; they are not directly comparable national effectiveness figures. Independent approvals, durability, cost and local evaluation matter before scale-up.
- Protective clothing and correctly used authorised repellents complement breeding-site removal; duration depends on formulation and conditions, so the clipping's 20% DEET = six hours is not a universal guarantee. Netting remains useful for daytime sleepers.
- **Correction:** the source's global claim of no chikungunya vaccine was already false: WHO's **8 August 2025** Q&A identified two vaccines authorised in some jurisdictions, with limited global availability and WHO policy review ongoing. This does not establish Indian approval or routine deployment. [Dated WHO Q&A](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/chikungunya).
