# GenomeIndia: reference genomes and precision medicine

## Programme and January 2025 milestone
- **DBT-funded multi-institution consortium**, initiated **2020**; large-scale sequencing to represent Indian genetic diversity. January 2025 data milestone builds on earlier sequencing announcements. [PMO address, 9 January 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2091506&lang=2&reg=48).
- Earlier supplied programme reporting describes **10,074 samples across 99 ethnic groups** and an analysis subset of **5,750**; the **8 April 2025 consortium paper** reports 10,074 individuals sequenced from **83 population groups**, with **9,772** retained after quality control. Keep these differently defined reports distinct; use the published paper for the April dataset.
- Data housed at **Indian Biological Data Centre, Faridabad, Haryana**; research access requires appropriate safeguards, not unrestricted release of named individuals' medical records.
## What the reference enables
| Objective | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Variation catalogue | Common, low-frequency and rare variants; **SNPs** and structural variants |
| Haplotype reference | Variants inherited together; supports statistical imputation of missing variants |
| Genome-wide arrays | Affordable research/diagnostic tools suited to represented populations |
| Biobank | Stored **DNA/plasma** for approved future research |
- **Sample → sequence → quality checks/joint genotyping → reference database → validated research/clinical applications**.
- Potential: disease-risk research, pharmacogenomics, precision medicine and host susceptibility to infections; association does not guarantee diagnosis or cure.
- Ethics: informed consent, privacy, secure access, representative sampling, benefit sharing and avoiding caste/ethnic determinism or genetic discrimination.
## 8 April 2025: published reference dataset
- The GenomeIndia consortium’s *Nature Genetics* paper distinguishes **10,074 individuals sequenced** from **9,772 genomes retained after quality control**, representing **83 population groups** (30 tribal, 53 non-tribal). The source’s 99-group description belongs to earlier programme reporting and should not replace this paper’s sample definition.
- Preliminary calling yielded approximately **180 million raw variants**; further checks yielded about **130 million autosomal variants**. These are different processing stages, not interchangeable totals. After excluding singletons/doubletons, about 65% of variants were ultra-rare (minor-allele frequency below 0.1%).
- The paper describes consented sampling, population representation, coordinated quality control, and data at IBDC, Faridabad. The supplied 5,750-sample analysis is an earlier subset, not the April paper’s final call-set.
- Revision implication: larger, representative reference panels improve imputation, rare-disease investigation and drug-response research, with clinical validation and safeguards still required. [GenomeIndia consortium paper, published 8 April 2025](https://home.iitm.ac.in/sinha/pdf/Bhattacharyya_2025_NatGenet.pdf).
