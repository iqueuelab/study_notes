# Mosura fentoni and Cambrian arthropod evolution

## Fossil and anatomy
- **Mosura fentoni**, radiodont described in **2025** from **Burgess Shale, British Columbia, Canada**; fossil age about **506 million years**, not automatically 540 million simply because it belongs to the Cambrian. Radiodonts are extinct early arthropod relatives, distantly related to modern insects, crustaceans and arachnids.
- Small, roughly **1.5–6 cm**, with **three eyes**, grasping/spiny jointed frontal appendages, toothed circular mouth and swimming flaps; source describes **26 trunk segments** differentiated into neck/mesotrunk/posterotrunk, six broad swimming-flap-bearing middle segments and up to **16 tightly packed gill-bearing posterior segments**.
- A **tagma** is a functionally specialised grouping of segments. Respiratory posterior region resembles solutions in horseshoe crabs/other arthropods through **evolutionary convergence**, not proof it is their direct ancestor or that horseshoe-crab tails themselves collect oxygen.
- Relationship near basal hurdiid radiodonts and specialised segmentation illustrate early diversity/flexibility in arthropod body organisation. Respiratory efficiency/habitat explanations remain hypotheses.
## Why Burgess Shale matters
- Exceptional soft-tissue preservation reveals nervous/digestive/circulatory anatomy; Mosura fossils preserve an **open circulation** with body cavities/lacunae. Collections comprised **61 specimens**, many collected decades before description. [Research museums’ announcement](https://www.rom.on.ca/news-releases/manitoba-museum-and-rom-palaeontologists-discover-506-million-year-old-predator), [primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.242122).
- Cambrian diversification around the early Cambrian produced many animal body plans; this later Cambrian animal helps investigate that evolutionary radiation, without moving all arthropod origins to one exact fossil date.
