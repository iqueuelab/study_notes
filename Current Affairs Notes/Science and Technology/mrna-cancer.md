# mRNA cancer vaccines and immunotherapy

## Mechanism
- Tumour profiling →identify relevant antigens/neoantigens →design mRNA →cells produce antigen →immune recognition targets cancer cells.
- **Therapeutic personalised vaccines** treat existing cancer; unlike preventive **HPV/HBV vaccination**, they do not automatically prevent all cancers.
- mRNA acts through cellular protein production; it does not need to alter the patient's DNA. Selection, immune response and benefit vary across tumours/patients.
## February 2025 status
- Russia/Gamaleya's **December 2024 announcement** described preclinical development and a promise of free availability in early 2025; a promise is **not evidence of routine licensed use, survival benefit or guaranteed fewer side effects**.
- UK **NHS Cancer Vaccine Launch Pad** facilitates clinical trials; it is not proof all experimental vaccines became routine NHS care.
- **CureVac is German**, correcting the PDF's US attribution; **CVGBM** brain-tumour vaccine early-phase results must not be equated with completed efficacy trials.
## Distinguish therapies
| Approach | Main principle |
| --- | --- |
| mRNA vaccine | Present tumour-related antigens to stimulate immunity |
| CAR-T | Engineer T cells to recognise targets |
| Checkpoint inhibitors | Release inhibitory immune signals |
| Monoclonal antibodies | Target specified molecules/cells |
- Risks/constraints: heterogeneous tumours, immune escape, delivery/manufacturing time, toxicity, cost and limited mature evidence. Source **120 trials** is a dated unverified count.
- Evaluate clinical endpoints, informed consent, comparative safety, regulatory approval and equitable access; novelty alone does not establish an effective treatment.
- [NHS programme description](https://www.england.nhs.uk/cancer/nhs-cancer-vaccine-launch-pad/): referral to eligible **clinical trials**, not routine efficacy confirmation.
