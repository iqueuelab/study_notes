# HIV: treatment cascade, prevention and rights

## Biology and prevention
- HIV is a **retrovirus** attacking CD4 immune cells; advanced untreated infection can progress to AIDS. Antiretroviral therapy suppresses replication and improves survival but is not a routine cure.
- Transmission: sexual exposure, infected blood/needles and mother-to-child; not ordinary touch, shared utensils or casual social contact. Safe blood, prevention services and sustained treatment reduce risk. [WHO](https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hiv-aids).
## 95–95–95 cascade
- By-2025 targets: **95% of all people living with HIV know status → 95% of diagnosed people receive ART → 95% of treated people achieve viral suppression**.
- Denominators change at each step; do not read this as 95% of all infected people at every stage. [UNAIDS](https://www.unaids.org/en/resources/documents/2024/progress-towards-95-95-95).
- NACP Phase V (2021–26): prevention/testing/treatment and reduction ambitions relative to 2010; the source's approximately 2.4 million burden and 44% incidence decline are dated estimates, not current counts.
- Treat All/free ART and Sampark reconnecting patients to care support retention. HIV/AIDS Act **2017**: non-discrimination, informed consent and confidentiality with statutory exceptions.
- Address stigma, migrant/key-population access, counselling, medicine continuity and resistance monitoring; registrations alone do not establish viral suppression.
