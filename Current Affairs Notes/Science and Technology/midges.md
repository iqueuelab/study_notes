# Culicoides midges and bluetongue disease

## Andaman–Nicobar survey

- ZSI survey **2022–23**, reported January 2025: **23 Culicoides species**, **13 first Indian records**; Indian inventory **93 valid species** in this account. New national records do not mean 13 species newly described to science.
- **Culicoides**, family **Ceratopogonidae**, order **Diptera**, tiny biting midges (“bhusi flies”). **Not all insects called midges belong to this genus or bite**; non-biting chironomid graphics are a different family.
- First records listed: **C. barnetti, gouldi, flaviscutellaris, flavipunctatus, hui, histrio, guttifer, perornatus, okinawensis, quatei, obscurus, coronalis, kusaiensis**.
- Cosmopolitan genus, source excludes **New Zealand/Antarctica**. Females of blood-feeding species bite livestock/wildlife; sexes/species also differ in nectar use. “Exclusively blood, never nectar” is misleading.

## One Health

- Source **17/23 known human-biters**, five bluetongue-vector species; no human disease transmission documented **in this survey**, not proof all midges globally lack human-health effects.
- **Bluetongue**, viral ruminant disease; fever, swelling, salivation and sometimes bluish tongue; sheep can suffer severe/fatal disease. Vector competence differs among species.
- Source family-level counts **~60 viruses / 40 protozoans / 24 filarial nematodes** are reported pathogen associations, not proof each surveyed species transmits every one.
- Identify species, monitor vectors/livestock, reduce suitable breeding exposure and coordinate animal-health surveillance.
