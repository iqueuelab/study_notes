# Cell2Sentence-Scale: AI and cancer immunotherapy research

## October 2025: an experimentally tested AI hypothesis
- **Cell2Sentence-Scale 27B**, developed by **Google with Yale** using Gemma, is a **27-billion-parameter** single-cell foundation model. It represents gene-expression profiles in textual cell sentences and integrates biological information; it does not literally listen to biochemical conversations. [Research overview](https://research.google/pubs/scaling-large-language-models-for-next-generation-single-cell-analysis/).
- A virtual screen of **over 4,000 drugs** compared patient tumour contexts with low-level interferon signalling and isolated cell-line contexts. It proposed that **silmitasertib (CX-4945), a CK2 inhibitor**, could conditionally increase antigen presentation, making tumour cells more visible to immune recognition. **Interferons** are immune-signalling proteins; antigen presentation includes cell-surface MHC display, rather than a general synonym for tumour detection.
- Human cell experiments supported the combination: drug alone little/no effect; low-dose interferon modest effect; together stronger antigen presentation. **Low interferon already present** differs from absent signalling. This is an **in-vitro lead**, not an approved cancer treatment, demonstrated cure, screening test or established patient-survival benefit. [Google announcement, 15 October](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/google-gemma-ai-cancer-therapy-discovery/).
- Source's **10–30% previously known hits** does not prove all remaining predictions are discoveries; hypotheses need experimental replication, mechanism, safety and clinical validation. A bioRxiv preprint is public research, not proof peer review is complete. Data quality, bias and reproducibility constrain AI drug discovery alongside its speed and scale advantages.
