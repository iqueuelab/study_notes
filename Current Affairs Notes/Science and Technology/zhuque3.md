# Zhuque-3: orbital launch and reusable booster testing

## 3 December 2025: orbit reached, booster recovery failed
- **LandSpace's Zhuque-3 maiden orbital flight** sent its second stage into the planned orbit, while the first-stage recovery failed after **anomalous combustion during recovery**. The PDF's ‘test failed’ headline must not imply failure to reach orbit. [Xinhua mission report](https://english.news.cn/20251203/81fb366b5e064a6b9217037188ebf5d5/c.html).
- Reusable first stages aim for controlled return, landing, inspection and reflight; **recovering a booster is distinct from reusing one**, and does not mean the upper stage is reusable. Guidance, engine restart/throttling, thermal protection and structural/landing control complicate recovery.
- Source design claims **at least 20 reuses and roughly 18-tonne payload** are prospective/configuration-dependent, not achieved performance from the maiden flight. The reported combustion anomaly does not establish whether overheating, mixture imbalance or vibration caused it; the PDF's list is speculation rather than investigation findings.
- Reusability can reduce costs and speed launch cadence when refurbishment and operations are reliable. Commercial launch competition and constellation deployment have communications, economic and security relevance; they do not establish Chinese parity with Falcon 9's operational record. **Falcon 9's first successful orbital-booster landing was 2015**; ‘only SpaceX has perfected reuse’ is too broad an absolute and ‘China's first domestically developed orbital rocket’ is plainly incorrect.
