Noble rot and a genome exception

  • Botrytis cinerea can cause noble rot in grapes: skin penetration/water loss concentrates sugars, enabling sweet wines such as Sauternes (France), Trockenbeerenauslese (Germany/Austria), Tokaji Aszú (Hungary). Selective hand-picking adds labour/cost; the fungus is also a plant pathogen.
  • May 2025 research in Science on B. cinerea and Sclerotinia sclerotiorum found haploid chromosomes distributed across multiple nuclei: each nucleus contains only a subset. This challenges “one nucleus, one complete genome”; it does not mean the fungi cannot reproduce or be propagated clonally by any method.
  • UV-mutagenesis observations prompted chromosome counting, FISH, DNA-content measurement and single-nucleus PCR. Ascomycetes form sexual ascospores within an ascus (plural asci). Primary study.
  • Revision: cnidarians (coral–algal partners), fungi (lichens/mycorrhizae) and protozoa (e.g. termite-gut mutualists) all include symbiotic species. Lichen = fungal partner + photosynthetic alga/cyanobacterium; fungal structure/moisture support plus photosynthesis enables pioneer colonisation of bare rock and soil formation.
  • Bacteria/fungi can grow in suitable artificial media; viruses require living hosts and are not independent decomposers. Fungi and bacteria recycle nutrients from dead matter.
  • Viruses lack independent energy-generating machinery; transmission is not restricted to biological vectors. Marine viruses exist, bacteriophages infect bacteria, and viruses can alter host transcription: all three negative assertions in the issue's 2025 question are false.
