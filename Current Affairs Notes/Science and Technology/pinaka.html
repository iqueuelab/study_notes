Pinaka rocket artillery and ammunition procurement

Editable notes

System and roles

  • Indigenous Multi-Barrel Rocket Launcher / MLRS, developed by DRDO–ARDE for Indian Army; Army is user, not sole designer.
  • Two pods × six rockets, mobile truck launcher with stabilising outriggers; loader/replenishment vehicles and command post. 214 mm basic rocket, source payload about 100 kg, differing warheads.
  • Area targets: exposed troops, vehicles, communications, airfield facilities and ammunition/fuel dumps; pre-fragmented high explosive and area-denial rounds have different effects. Artillery rocket system ≠ surface-to-air defence.
  • Basic Mk-I commonly cited around 38 km; enhanced/guided rounds extend range/accuracy. Source's inconsistent 48/60/90 km version table, 48-second salvo and exact 5,757.70 km/h Mk-3 should not be learned as universal specifications. Launcher commonly cited 12 rounds in ~44 seconds; launcher versus six-launcher battery differs.
  • January source describes enhanced HE 45 km, area-denial 37 km, guided developments about 75 km, future 120/300 km ambitions. Planned variants ≠ then-operational capability, and “air attacks” is misleading.

January approval and contract distinction

  • January CCS approval reported near ₹10,000 crore, source approximate splits ₹6,050 / ₹4,000 crore, signing expected by March. Approval ≠ contract signed or ammunition delivered.
  • Correct MIL = Munitions India Limited, not Mishra Dhatu Nigam (MIDHANI). Private supplier Economic Explosives Limited (EEL), Solar group.
  • 6 February 2025 clarification: MoD signed rocket contracts totalling ₹10,147 crore with EEL/MIL; BEL SHAKTI software upgrade also contracted. MoD release.
  • Domestic ammunition capacity, logistics/replenishment, credible delivery schedules and quality trials support readiness; higher volume/range alone does not prove precision or eliminate escalation concerns.

June 24 regiment expansion

  • Regiment: three batteries × six launchers = 18 launchers. Battery salvo of 72 rockets differs from a single launcher's 12 rounds; avoid mixing areas/timings.
  • August 2020 procurement, ₹2,580 crore: Tata/L&T launcher-command systems and BEML vehicles; package reported 114 launchers, 45 command posts and 330 vehicles, including support/training requirements.
  • Source retrospective: six operational regiments, two raised in 2024, two more to complete training by mid-2026; ten by 2026 and 22 eventually are plans/snapshots, not June 2025 operational strength. Grad BM-21 replacement requires ammunition, logistics and training; speculative Mk3 ranges are not fielded air defence.

Export interest reported in the July 8-labelled section

  • Saudi Arabia, Vietnam and Indonesia were reported as prospective buyers of Guided Pinaka. Interest is distinct from a signed contract, delivery or independently verified combat performance. Exports can deepen defence partnerships and support domestic ammunition manufacturing.

29 December 2025: LRGR 120 maiden flight test

  • DRDO tested Pinaka Long Range Guided Rocket at ITR Chandipur, Odisha, maximum 120 km, with planned manoeuvres and precise target impact reported. ARDE designed with HEMRL, supported by DRDL/RCI; trial coordinated by ITR and Proof & Experimental Establishment. Launched from an in-service Pinaka launcher, demonstrating compatibility across range variants. DRDO release.
  • Test success and the same-day procurement AoN are different milestones; neither proves mass production or field induction. Extended range/guidance improves stand-off artillery precision and logistics commonality.
  • Existing notes cover launcher/pods/support vehicles and earlier ammunition procurement. The worksheet's exact Mk III 5,757.70 km/h, projected 300 km variant and mixed Mk I/Mk II ranges are not all verified characteristics of the tested LRGR 120; omit them as established specifications.
