Titan and acrylonitrile: testing the azotosome hypothesis

Editable notes

March 2026: experiment challenges membrane hypothesis

  • Correct the coaching prose using its own newspaper clipping: the report is titled “Test disfavours acrylonitrile cells in Titan lakes”. In laboratory methane/ethane mixtures at Titan-like conditions, acrylonitrile formed a stable co-crystal with ethane, without evidence of the proposed membranes. This challenges one pathway; it does not prove all life on Titan impossible.
  • Earlier simulations proposed nitrogen-containing membrane analogues called azotosomes. A hypothesis/model is not an observation of a living cell. Vu and Hodyss, Science Advances, 11 March 2026, experimentally tested the idea. Research paper.
  • Titan, Saturn's largest moon: dense nitrogen-rich atmosphere, methane/ethane lakes and complex organic chemistry. Detected organic molecules are potential chemical ingredients, not evidence of life.
  • Acrylonitrile/vinyl cyanide (CH2=CH–CN): volatile, flammable organic liquid; industrial propylene ammoxidation uses ammonia/oxygen. Uses include acrylic fibres, nitrile rubber and ABS plastics. Exposure is toxic; cold extraterrestrial chemistry should not be confused with ordinary industrial handling conditions.
