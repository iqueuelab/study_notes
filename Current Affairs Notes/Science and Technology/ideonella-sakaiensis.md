# Ideonella sakaiensis and enzymatic PET recycling

## Organism → enzyme pathway
- **Ideonella sakaiensis**, genus Ideonella/family **Comamonadaceae**: Gram-negative, rod-shaped, motile with a single flagellum, non-spore-forming and non-pigmented; source dimensions **1.2–1.5 × 0.6–0.8 μm**. Identified from a PET-recycling-site microbial community in **Sakai, Japan**; reported in **2016**. Isolation near polluted material does not prove pollution newly created the species.
- **PET → PETase → chiefly MHET → MHETase → terephthalic acid + ethylene glycol → microbial assimilation/recovered feedstock.** Experiments establish the two enzymes' roles; performance depends on substrate crystallinity and conditions. [Gene-disruption study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8388835/).
- **PET = polyethylene terephthalate**, a thermoplastic polyester from ethylene glycol/terephthalic acid; strong, lightweight and transparent; bottles, food packaging and textile fibres. Degrading PET does not establish degradation of polyethylene, PVC or every mixed plastic.
## Recycling opportunity and limits
- Source describes moist, oxygenated contaminated soils/sludge; a laboratory recycling pathway is not permission to release organisms into ecosystems. Biological depolymerisation could recover monomers and complement collection/segregation, reuse and conventional recycling.
- Source calls the products “harmless”; **ethylene glycol is hazardous if ingested**, so avoid treating monomers or incompletely treated effluent as universally environmentally benign. Recovered chemicals need controlled handling, purification and process assessment. [CDC ethylene-glycol toxicology](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/ershdb/emergencyresponsecard_29750031.html).
- Practice: the Gram-positive/spore-forming/marine-origin statement is false. PDF selects **(b), 2 and 3**, but its blanket “benign compounds” wording needs the handling qualification above; this is source practice, not a dated CSE PYQ.
