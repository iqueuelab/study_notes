# Jarosite and luminescence dating on Mars

## 21 May 2025 research report
- **Jarosite**, yellow-brown hydrous iron sulphate, ideal formula **KFe₃(SO₄)₂(OH)₆**; forms in acidic oxidising aqueous environments. Opportunity detected it on Mars in **2004**: evidence relevant to past water chemistry, not proof of life.
- Six terrestrial samples from **Kachchh, Gujarat**, a dry/saline Martian analogue, were tested as potential luminescence dosimeters/chronometers. Radiation stores energy in mineral traps; heating or suitable light releases a measurable signal. **Age ≈ accumulated dose / dose rate**, provided initial resetting, stability and environmental dose are understood.
- Source laboratory procedure: weak-acid washing, controlled irradiation and repeated heating/cooling; thermoluminescence peaks near **100, 150, 300 and 350°C**, persisting after **450°C** annealing. This supports instrument-development potential, not a demonstrated operational Martian rover clock.
- Source gives saturation **590–2,600 Gy**, dose rate **65 mGy/year = 65 Gy/ka**, and a possible dating window of **~25,000 years**. The later published paper reports **700–2,600 Gy** for the relevant saturation measures and stresses different signal stability/fading. Do not derive a universal precise maximum age from one saturation value. [Primary study, published July 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/maps.70021).
- Proposed applications: recent sediment transport/dust storms, flood deposits and volcanic layers using compact heaters/LEDs. Actual Martian validation and dose/resetting calibration remain necessary; a ~25 ka method does not date all billion-year-old water events.
