# Patent landscape: universities and commercialisation

## Filing, grant and innovation are separate measures
- Universities' IP cells, legal assistance, incentives and technology-transfer offices can convert research into protectable inventions; patent counts alone do not measure scientific quality, successful products or public benefit.
- **WIPO 2024 report, calendar 2023:** resident applications were **55.2% of filings received by India's office**, not the PDF's 57%; Indian-origin applicants ranked **sixth globally** in filings. Source's claim India overtook the US as second-largest patent-grant recipient in 2021 is unsupported and should not be memorised. Resident share, applicant origin, receiving office and grant counts have different denominators. [WIPO resident-share report](https://www.wipo.int/web-publications/world-intellectual-property-indicators-2024-highlights/en/patents-highlights.html), [global origins](https://www.wipo.int/pressroom/en/articles/2024/article_0015.html).
- Source **2023 institution/company/individual shares 43%/17%/32%** and computer/electrical/biomedical/physics shifts **1.27→26.5% /8.27→16.4% /0.6→10% /2→4%** concern its reported dataset; the scope is insufficient to make them shares of every Indian-office patent. Likewise IIT Madras **156→300** and IIT Bombay **421 (2023–24)** are institutional examples, not a verified national league table.
## Enabling policy and bottlenecks
- **National IPR Policy 2016; Atal Innovation Mission 2016; KAPILA 2020** support IP awareness/innovation. Digital filing, eligible expedited examination and fee concessions support participation; fee reductions depend on applicant category/rules.
- Source reports grant times shortening from **8–10 to 2–3 years**, sometimes less; these are not a guaranteed statutory processing time. Its **80% awaiting decision** is an inadequately scoped backlog assertion, not a verified share of every application.
- **R&D ~0.67% GDP** is a dated expenditure estimate, not a 2025 measurement. Limited research resources, legal/administrative capacity and weak university–industry links impede commercialisation.
- Priorities: sustained R&D investment (source proposes **2% GDP**), examiner capacity, IP literacy, fair licensing, proof-of-concept support and research–industry collaboration; evaluate use, licensing revenue and societal outcomes alongside grant volume. Practice answer's categorical “universities largest share in India” needs its dataset denominator, so do not reproduce the supplied answer as a verified all-India fact.
