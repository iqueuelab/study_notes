# Small modular reactors and India's nuclear mission

## Reactor distinctions
| Concept | Key point |
|---|---|
| SMR | Usually up to 300 MWe per module; modular manufacture and deployment |
| Bharat Small Reactor (BSR) | Existing 220 MWe pressurised heavy-water reactor approach |
| BSMR-200 | Developing 200 MWe pressurised **light-water** reactor; not a PHWR |
- **3 April 2025 DAE answer:** concept design complete, detailed engineering underway; erection/start-up expected about **six years after financial approval**, regular operation by the end of the **seventh year**. These are approval-dependent estimates. BARC/NPCIL lead development; competitive private component/EPC supply does not imply unrestricted private reactor ownership. [DAE clarification](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2118377&lang=2&reg=48).
- Slightly enriched uranium is an option; do not assume every Indian reactor uses natural uranium. Passive/engineered safety systems require independent licensing; **nuclear is non-fossil, not renewable**.
## Policy and deployment
- **Budget 2025–26**, not Budget 2024: ₹20,000 crore Nuclear Energy Mission for SMR R&D and at least five indigenous SMRs targeted by **2033**. Funding is not solely for one reactor. **100 GW by 2047** is a capacity ambition, not achieved output.
- Potential uses: captive power for steel/cement/aluminium, smaller grids and suitable remote demand, and assessed reuse of coal-plant land/grid infrastructure. Cooling water, safety zones, security, waste, licensing and viable demand still matter.
- Factory fabrication, standardisation and staged investment may reduce construction/financing burdens; cheaper electricity, rapid delivery and accident immunity are **not guaranteed** before commercial replication.
- Fleet map: **Tarapur—Maharashtra; Rawatbhata—Rajasthan; Kakrapar—Gujarat; Narora—UP; Kaiga—Karnataka; Kalpakkam/Kudankulam—Tamil Nadu**. Indigenous 700 MWe PHWRs form the larger-reactor route alongside SMRs. Use the dated fleet snapshot in Nuclear power for capacity/counts, rather than the source's inconsistent totals.
## Fuel cycle and Mains assessment
- Three-stage strategy: natural-uranium PHWRs → plutonium fast breeders → thorium/U-233 systems. Reprocessing recovers usable uranium/plutonium but does not eliminate waste, cost, security or proliferation concerns.
- Assess fuel/enrichment supply, vendor capability, financing/offtake, independent oversight, liability, emergency planning and long-term spent-fuel management. Supplier participation and proposed legal amendments must be distinguished from operative ownership/licensing rules.
