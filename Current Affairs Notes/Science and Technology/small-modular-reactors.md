# Small modular reactors and India's nuclear mission

## Reactor distinctions
| Concept | Key point |
|---|---|
| SMR | Usually up to 300 MWe per module; modular manufacture and deployment |
| Bharat Small Reactor | Existing 220 MWe pressurised heavy-water reactor approach |
| BSMR-200 | Proposed 200 MWe pressurised **light-water** reactor, using slightly enriched uranium |
- BSMR-200 is not a PHWR. BARC/NPCIL development was at concept/design stage; an approval-dependent construction estimate is not an operational date. [DAE clarification](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2118377&lang=2&reg=48).
- Budget **2025–26** announced a ₹20,000 crore Nuclear Energy Mission, with at least five indigenous SMRs targeted by 2033. A 100 GW nuclear-capacity goal for 2047 is a target, not achieved capacity.
- Factory fabrication may reduce project size and financing exposure, but lower cost per kWh and absolute accident immunity are not guaranteed. Nuclear is non-fossil; it is not classified as renewable.
## Fuel cycle and policy
- India's three-stage strategy: natural-uranium PHWRs → plutonium-based fast breeders → thorium/U-233 systems. Not every Indian reactor uses natural uranium.
- Reprocessing recovers usable material but does not eliminate radioactive waste; safeguards, proliferation concerns and long-term disposal remain important.
- Evaluate independent safety oversight, siting, liability, financing, private participation under applicable law and reliable manufacturing capacity. Avoid memorising internally inconsistent reactor totals from the source.
## Industrial deployment and construction
- The April source repeats **BSMR-200 concept design completed** and an estimated **60–72 months after sanction** for establishment. A sanction-dependent estimate is not an operating deadline. The design is a **pressurised water reactor using slightly enriched uranium**, not a PHWR. [12 March 2025 DAE answer](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2118377&lang=2&reg=48).
- Potential uses: reliable captive power for steel, cement and aluminium; brownfield thermal-plant sites; suitably assessed remote applications. Existing land/grid infrastructure may help, but water, cooling, safety zones and licensing still require assessment.
- **Budget 2025–26**, not Budget 2024, announced ₹20,000 crore for the Nuclear Energy Mission's SMR R&D and at least five indigenous SMRs targeted by 2033. It is not ₹20,000 crore solely for one reactor. The 100 GW by 2047 goal is a capacity target. [DAE Budget account](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2099244&lang=2&reg=48).
- Nuclear-fleet map recall: **Tarapur—Maharashtra; Rawatbhata—Rajasthan; Kakrapar—Gujarat; Narora—Uttar Pradesh; Kaiga—Karnataka; Kalpakkam and Kudankulam—Tamil Nadu.** Kakrapar's indigenous 700 MWe PHWR units illustrate the large-reactor route alongside SMRs.
- Avoid the source's reactor breakdown: 19+2+2 totals 23, not its stated 24; capacity/counts need a dated official reference. Generation share, installed capacity and future targets measure different things.
## Mains argument
- Modularity can reduce project size and permit staged investment; passive systems and standardisation may help safety and construction. Cheap electricity or faster delivery is not guaranteed before commercial replication.
- Assess enrichment/fuel supply, financing, vendor capability, independent regulation, liability, emergency planning and spent-fuel management. Recycling usable uranium/plutonium reduces fresh-fuel demand but does not eliminate waste or safeguards requirements.
