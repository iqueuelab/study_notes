# Guillain–Barré syndrome and post-infection nerve injury

## Mechanism and symptoms
- Rare **immune-mediated peripheral nerve disorder**: immune attack damages myelin and/or nerve fibres → weakness, tingling/numbness, reduced reflexes and sometimes paralysis.
- **Not contagious**; an antecedent infection may be transmissible. **Peripheral nervous system ≠ brain/spinal cord**. All ages affected, more often adults/men; background incidence roughly **1–2 per 100,000/year**, population-dependent.
- Common progression: legs → arms/face, impaired walking/coordination; swallowing/respiratory muscles may be affected. **Autonomic dysfunction** can disturb heart rate/blood pressure; weakness need not remain mild/temporary.
## Triggers and Pune context
- Often follows gastrointestinal/respiratory infection, notably **Campylobacter jejuni**, and **EBV/CMV**, among others. **Mycoplasma pneumoniae** is another recognised association; not every urinary infection causes GBS.
- Surgery and rare associations with particular vaccines occur; **infections carry greater GBS risk than their relevant preventive vaccines**. Source's broad “trauma/stress” list is not evidence ordinary emotional stress is an established routine cause.
- **January 2025 Pune cluster**, central/State teams investigating; clipping reports **73 cases / 14 ventilated as of 25 January**, a dated snapshot, not final outbreak totals or proof a specific water source caused every case.
## Treatment and recovery
- **IVIG**, pooled donor immunoglobulins, or **plasma exchange** can modify the immune attack; respiratory/cardiac monitoring, supportive care and rehabilitation matter.
- Antivirals are not the main treatment for the immune disorder; management of an identified antecedent infection is a separate decision.
- Many recover over weeks/months, sometimes much longer, with persistent disability in some; no instant curative treatment. Rapidly progressive weakness/breathing difficulty requires urgent clinical assessment. [WHO explanation](https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/guillain-barr%C3%A9-syndrome).

## January 2026: Neemuch report and classification
- Compilation reports a **Guillain–Barré syndrome cluster in Neemuch district, Madhya Pradesh**, without case counts or an identified cause. This is separate from the January 2025 Pune cluster already covered.
- **AIDP is a demyelinating subtype of GBS, not a synonym for every GBS case**; axonal variants also occur. The source's equal male/female frequency conflicts with WHO: GBS affects all ages but is more common in adults and males. [WHO factsheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/guillain-barr%C3%A9-syndrome).
- Existing notes retain peripheral-nerve immune injury, ascending weakness, respiratory/autonomic danger, post-infection triggers, IVIG/plasma exchange and variable recovery. Fever/history of infection is not a diagnostic requirement, and recovery within six months is not guaranteed.
