# Quorum sensing: bacterial chemical communication

## Signals and coordinated behaviour
- **Quorum sensing**: bacteria produce/release/detect **autoinducers**; signal accumulation and environmental context regulate gene expression across a community. **Bonnie Bassler's** work explores within-species and between-species communication, hence the lecture's “multilingual” metaphor. It is chemical signalling, not conscious language or a single universal circuit shared by all bacteria. [Bassler laboratory](https://basslerlab.scholar.princeton.edu/).
- **Vibrio cholerae** uses signalling to regulate virulence/biofilms; **Aliivibrio (Vibrio) fischeri** supports bioluminescence in the Hawaiian bobtail squid. Pathogenic and beneficial collective behaviours coexist; reaching a threshold does not necessarily switch on virulence in every species.
- Research seeks communication inhibitors to alter pathogenic behaviour and useful industrial pathways. These are potential antimicrobial strategies, not established replacements for all antibiotics or guaranteed resistance-free treatment. Agricultural/environmental applications need organism-specific validation.
- **2013 virus PYQ(a), statement 1 only**: viruses lack independent energy-generation machinery, require living cells for propagation and use transmission routes beyond biological vectors. **2013 decomposers(b), fungi+bacteria**, already banked. **2025 virus statement set(d), none**: viruses occur in oceans, bacteriophages infect bacteria, and viruses can alter host transcription. Viral lysis does influence nutrient cycling even though viruses are not the conventional decomposer answer.
