# Green hydrogen and off-grid production

## Kutch pilot
- **ANIL**, Adani Enterprises subsidiary, announced **23 June 2025** commissioning of a **5 MW off-grid green hydrogen pilot, Kutch, Gujarat**: solar power + BESS and automated closed-loop electrolyser responsive to renewable variability. The company's first-of-kind claim concerns this category. [Company release](https://connect.adani.com/media/2025/jun25/anil/).
- Renewable-powered electrolysis splits water into hydrogen and oxygen; off-grid means independent of central grid supply. Direct fossil-carbon emissions can be avoided; lifecycle emissions still depend on equipment, power and water supply.
- Uses: replace grey hydrogen in ammonia/fertiliser and refining; hydrogen direct-reduced iron can replace carbon reductants in steel. Storage adds flexibility but entails conversion losses, cost, transport and safety issues.
- **National Green Hydrogen Mission 2023:** at least five million tonnes annual production by 2030; projected **over ₹8 lakh crore investment** differs from government budget outlay; supports industrial decarbonisation/2070 net zero.
- **2023 PYQs:** adapted internal-combustion engines, natural-gas blending for heat/power and fuel cells all use hydrogen; fertilisers, refineries and steel all offer applications. Permissible blending depends on infrastructure, not a universal 20% rule.
- **2024 PYQ:** fuel-cell vehicle exhaust is **water vapour**; 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O + electricity + heat. Clean tailpipe does not prove a zero-emission supply chain.

## Better green technology: August editorial
- **Laboratory cell efficiency, commercial module efficiency and field yield differ**; 15–18% is not a universal 2025 silicon ceiling. Higher efficiency can reduce land per output, but costs/materials/ecology matter.
- Roughly 47% laboratory result refers to **multijunction III–V cells under concentrated illumination**, not ordinary single-junction GaAs panels at everyday sunlight. [NREL](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy20osti/76570.pdf).
- **Artificial photosynthesis:** research using light, catalysts, water/CO₂ to produce fuels such as methanol; not already a scaled universal substitute for electrolysis.
- **RFNBO=renewable fuel of non-biological origin**, renewable non-biomass energy inputs; renewable electrolytic hydrogen itself qualifies under EU conditions. Hydrogen-derived fuels add conversion/lifecycle costs; RFNBO is not wholly separate from hydrogen. [European Commission](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/eus-energy-system/hydrogen/renewable-hydrogen_en).
- Source “85% energy import dependency” confuses oil with total energy. India does not import a majority of every fuel. Diversification, efficiency, R&D, domestic manufacturing and reliable grids improve security amid Ukraine/West Asia disruptions.
- Less efficient panels raise land/cost requirements, not automatically fossil-based hydrogen; evaluate full-chain emissions, water, storage/transport and energy losses.

## October 2025: electrolyser competition
- **Electrolyser manufacturing capacity, installed capacity and actual hydrogen output are different measures.** China benefits from scale, supply chains and lower equipment costs; dominance comparable to solar PV remains an editorial forecast, not an assured outcome.
| Technology | Materials and operating distinction |
|---|---|
| Alkaline | Mature, often lower capital cost; commonly nickel-based electrodes and alkaline electrolyte; system design affects flexibility |
| PEM | Proton-conducting membrane; responsive operation/high-purity output; platinum/iridium catalysts and titanium components raise cost and material concerns |
- The PDF's blanket “alkaline less efficient” comparison is too broad: evaluate electricity consumption, load range, durability, balance-of-plant and project costs, not technology labels alone.
- Source reports China **36.5 million tonnes hydrogen in 2024**, **120,000 tonnes green output**, **85% alkaline manufacturing share**, annual equipment-price falls of **20–30%** and up-to-**45%** cost advantage in Europe. These are source-reported, differently defined estimates; IEA’s 2025 review puts China near **60% of all electrolyser manufacturing capacity**, a different denominator from alkaline alone; manufacturing share is not green-hydrogen production share, and imported-system installation costs can erase factory-price advantages. [IEA 2025 review](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-hydrogen-review-2025).
- Buyers also need dependable renewable electricity, water, product purity, integration, storage and firm offtake. Critical-material dependencies, domestic industrial policy/trade scrutiny and local service requirements complicate a simple cheapest-stack strategy. India’s mission supports domestic capability, but an announced project is not achieved production.
