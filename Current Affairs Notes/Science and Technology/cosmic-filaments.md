# Cosmic filaments and large-scale rotation

## December 2025: a rotating cosmic filament
- Filaments are elongated structures of **dark matter, gas and galaxies**, linking clusters around cosmic voids. Gravity amplifies early density fluctuations into the cosmic web; gas/matter flows influence galaxy growth and angular momentum. Positions/redshifts and simulations reconstruct the three-dimensional pattern.
- **Correction:** Oxford's announcement is **4 December 2025**, not the source pointer's **December 2024**. **14 hydrogen-rich galaxies form a ~5.5-million-light-year string within a ~50-million-light-year filament containing over 280 other galaxies**, about **140 million light-years away**. The 14 galaxies are not the entire 50-million-light-year filament. [Oxford announcement](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2025-12-04-astronomers-spot-one-largest-spinning-structures-ever-found-universe-0).
- Opposite motions on either side of the spine and aligned spins suggest large-scale rotation; models infer about **110 km/s**. This is an observational/model-based inference, not proof that every filament rotates identically or that the filament is a rigid solid object.
- Existing **2023 objects-in-space PYQ** repeats: **Cepheids = pulsating variable stars; nebulae = dust/gas clouds; pulsars = rotating neutron stars**. Only supplied pair 3 is correct, **(a)**.
