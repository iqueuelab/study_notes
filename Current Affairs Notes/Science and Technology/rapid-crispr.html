RAPID-CRISPR and leukaemia diagnosis

Editable notes

Disease and Indian innovation

  • APL = acute promyelocytic leukaemia, an aggressive blood cancer usually associated with PML::RARA fusion. Dangerous bleeding makes rapid recognition/treatment critical; it is highly treatable with timely specialist care.
  • ACTREC, Tata Memorial Centre, under the Department of Atomic Energy, developed RAPID-CRISPR; the study appeared in Blood Advances, February 2025. CRISPR means Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats and derives from microbial defence systems.

Diagnostic pathway

Sample preparation → RT-LAMP amplification → Cas12a target recognition → reporter cleavage → visible lateral-flow signal.

  • LAMP is loop-mediated isothermal amplification; RNA is reverse-transcribed and amplified without PCR-style temperature cycling. Cas12a recognition of the fusion-associated target triggers a reporter signal.
  • The study tested conventional extracted-RNA workflows and a simplified extraction-free preparation coupled with RT-LAMP. The source's assertion of no genetic amplification is incorrect: amplification remains part of the assay.
  • Reported 100% sensitivity and specificity apply to the study samples, not every future patient/field setting. Blood or bone-marrow specimens can be used; speed and simpler readout support resource-limited diagnosis.
  • This is diagnostic detection, not gene-editing treatment. Wider use needs external validation, quality control, training and prompt referral/treatment access. Primary study.

Public-health relevance

  • Affordable near-patient testing can reduce diagnostic delays and geographical inequity. Procurement, laboratory networks and referral pathways matter alongside the invention.
  • Programme integration suggested by the coaching article is a policy proposal, not proof the test was already nationally rolled out or included in Ayushman Bharat.
