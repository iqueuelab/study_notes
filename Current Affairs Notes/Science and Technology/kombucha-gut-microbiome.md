# Kombucha and the gut microbiome

## Human study and limits
- Kombucha is fermented tea made with a **SCOBY (symbiotic culture of bacteria and yeast)**. Fermentation transforms sugars through microbial metabolism; live microbes in a drink do not by themselves establish proven probiotic clinical benefit.
- Brazilian **pre–post study**: 46 healthy adults initially (23 with obesity, 23 normal weight), **200 ml black-tea kombucha daily for eight weeks**; stool sequencing assessed bacteria/fungi. It was not a large randomised trial proving treatment of obesity. [Journal of Nutrition study](https://research.unipd.it/retrieve/318591d5-861e-40e5-9bf5-4dc18e4dcd2b/1-s2.0-S0022316624012392-main.pdf).
- Overall bacterial diversity changed little; selected taxa shifted, especially in obesity. Source reports increases in **Akkermansiaceae/Prevotellaceae** in obesity, **Bacteroidota** across groups and **Parabacteroides** in normal-weight participants; decreases included **Ruminococcus gnavus, Dorea** and fungi **Exophiala/Rhodotorula**. Associations with metabolism/inflammation do not make whole taxa universally “good” or “bad”.
- Source polyphenol breakdown: **81% flavonoids, 19% phenolic acids**. Microbial processing of tea compounds and short-chain-fatty-acid pathways are possible mechanisms; not all polyphenols bypass small-intestinal absorption.
- No significant improvement in glucose, insulin or inflammatory markers in this short study. Diet, region, baseline microbiota and preparation vary; Indian populations' microbial diversity cannot be reduced to a single profile. Larger controlled/longer trials are needed; do not present kombucha as a medical treatment. Source market-value figures are commercial estimates, not biological evidence.
