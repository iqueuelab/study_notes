Minuteman III: ICBMs, MIRVs and nuclear deterrence

Editable notes

March 2026: routine US ICBM test

  • 3 March, 11:01 pm Pacific time (reported 4 March): GT 255, unarmed Minuteman III from Vandenberg, California, carried two test re-entry vehicles. USAF states it was scheduled years earlier and was not a response to current events. Test payload differs from an operational nuclear deployment. Historical descriptions of single-warhead deployment should not imply an ongoing New START treaty constraint: the treaty expired on 5 February 2026. Contemporary CRS account. USAF release.
  • LGM-30G: L=silo-launched, G=surface attack, M=guided missile; deployed June 1970. Contractor Boeing, not the source's Douglas attribution. Three solid-propellant motor stages; official data 36,030 kg, range 6,000-plus miles, burnout speed ~15,000 mph. The source's universal 13,000 km and near 100% reliability overstate what this fact sheet establishes. USAF specifications.
  • Land ICBMs, submarine-launched missiles and strategic bombers form the nuclear triad; MIRV means multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles. The sheet lists 400 missiles as of February 2019; do not treat source 440 as a verified current deployment count.
  • Replacement LGM-35A Sentinel, formerly GBSD: February 2026 official target early 2030s initial capability, superseding the source's 2029 expectation. Contemporary programme update.
