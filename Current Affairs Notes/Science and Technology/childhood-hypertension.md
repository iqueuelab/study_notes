# Childhood hypertension and school food literacy

## May 2025 school-health argument
- Source cites **CNNS 2016–18 :7.3% adolescents with hypertensive readings, urban 9.1%**, and **NFHS 5 :12% aged 15–19 with elevated BP**. Different thresholds, age samples and measurement protocols mean these cannot alone prove a rising clinical-hypertension trend. Paediatric diagnosis requires age-appropriate assessment/repeated readings; one high reading is not definitive diagnosis.
- Risks: obesity, inactivity/screen-heavy routines and salty highly processed diets; underlying kidney/endocrine conditions also matter. Source **over 8 g salt/day** lacks a national survey baseline. WHO’s adult limit is less than 5 g/day; children’s allowance is lower and energy-adjusted.
- **PM POSHAN** can combine balanced locally appropriate meals with food literacy and safe student participation. Source scale~120 millionchildren/1.27 millionschools is an undated programme-era figure, not verified current coverage.
- Japan’s **Shokuiku** food education and Vietnam examples illustrate learning about food sources/meal preparation; source claims of reduced obesity do not establish the programme alone caused all changes.
- Reform proposals: fresh diverse menus, healthier food environments, activity, nutrition education, family engagement and BP/health follow-up. Do not assume school meals are every child’s sole nutrition source or that educating pupils alone treats clinical hypertension.
