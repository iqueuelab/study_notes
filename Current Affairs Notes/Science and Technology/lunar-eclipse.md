# Blood Moon, lunar eclipses and Rayleigh scattering

## Total lunar eclipse: 7–8 September 2025
- **Sun → Earth → Moon** alignment: the full Moon passes into Earth's umbra. Earth's atmosphere refracts some sunlight into the shadow; preferential scattering of shorter wavelengths leaves more red/orange light illuminating the Moon. [NASA explanation](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/eclipses/).
- “Blood Moon” is a popular description of a copper/red eclipsed Moon. Dust, aerosols/clouds and geometry change its appearance; it is not a separate lunar object or proof of a physical transformation.
- **Rayleigh scattering ∝ 1/λ⁴** for particles much smaller than the wavelength: blue/violet scatter more than red. Violet, not blue, is the shortest visible wavelength; atmospheric transmission, solar spectrum and eye sensitivity help explain the blue sky.
- Longer atmospheric paths at sunrise/sunset preferentially remove shorter wavelengths from the direct beam, leaving red/orange light. Eclipse redness requires atmospheric refraction as well as wavelength-dependent scattering. [NASA September event account](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/by-the-warm-light-of-the-moon-154814/).
