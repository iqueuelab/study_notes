HER2 breast cancer: subcutaneous targeted therapy

Editable notes

October 2025: shorter administration and unequal access

  • Trastuzumab and pertuzumab target HER2-positive breast cancer. PHESGO combines both antibodies with hyaluronidase, enabling subcutaneous administration by increasing tissue permeability. This is targeted therapy for indicated patients, not a universal breast-cancer cure or elimination of all chemotherapy.
  • Correct the issue's “2–5 minutes for all doses”: prescribing information specifies approximately 8 minutes loading dose and 5 minutes maintenance, plus required observation/clinical monitoring. Serious cardiac, administration-related and embryo-fetal risks remain; shorter injection time is not zero-risk treatment or a self-injection recommendation. Prescribing information.
  • FeDeriCa supports pharmacokinetic non-inferiority of the fixed-dose combination; HannaH concerned subcutaneous trastuzumab, not identical proof for every combination/regimen. Source's ADEPT/Indian time-saving and satisfaction findings need study-specific populations/endpoints; an approximately 80-minute saving is not guaranteed for every patient.
  • Potential advantages: less chair/infusion time and improved convenience. Equitable uptake needs trained oncology staff, reliable supply, affordability, insurance/public-programme access and referral support outside large cities. A home-based service, where suitable and authorised, still requires professional supervision and emergency capacity.
