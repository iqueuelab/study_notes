Science and Technology

Lunar surface temperatures and accessible ice

Editable notes

Measurement versus inference

  • Chandrayaan-3's Vikram lander carried ChaSTE, developed by PRL and VSSC, to probe approximately the upper 10 cm of lunar regolith near 69.37°S—not at the exact South Pole.
  • Measurements ran during the lunar day, 24 August–2 September 2023. Reported surface temperatures differed strongly between nearby sites, illustrating slope-related thermal variation.
  • A quoted night temperature near −181°C is a model estimate, not ChaSTE's direct overnight measurement.
  • Modelling indicates pole-facing slopes steeper than about 14° at suitable high latitudes can permit shallow stable ice. This is not direct proof of ice at every such site. ISRO research account.
  • Water could support life support and fuel/oxygen production through in-situ resource utilisation; abundance, extraction, energy and engineering feasibility still require investigation.
  • Chandrayaan-1's water-molecule evidence and Chandrayaan-3's thermal measurements answer different questions. India joined the Artemis Accords in 2023.

ChaSTE instrument and exam traps

  • Chandra's Surface Thermophysical Experiment on Vikram used a controlled probe with 10 temperature sensors, reaching roughly 10 cm into regolith; its deployment and thermal measurements inform instrument/material design.
  • ChaSTE at 69.37°S measured high southern-latitude temperatures; this is not the exact geographic pole. Slope/illumination and shallow insulating regolith cause strong local variation.
  • Temperature-based conditions for possible stable ice are not direct detection of water ice. Permanently shadowed cold traps and illuminated lander-site measurements must not be conflated. ISRO research account.
  • “First” applies to this regional/surface measurement achievement; Apollo 15 and 17 already deployed lunar heat-flow experiments. NASA records. ChaSTE is not the first successful thermal probe anywhere on the Moon.
  • Other worlds present different penetration challenges: InSight's HP3 mole lacked adequate regolith friction; Philae's limited hammer penetration on its comet does not establish one deployment method universally superior.
  • Thermal data support future habitat, power-system and resource-use planning; they do not themselves establish commercially extractable ice or a completed lunar base.
