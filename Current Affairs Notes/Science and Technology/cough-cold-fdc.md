# Paediatric cough–cold FDC restriction and drug safety

## 16 May 2025 : restriction and implementation
- **S.O.1717 (E),15 April 2025**, under Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 section 26 A: restricts manufacture/sale/distribution of **chlorpheniramine maleate + phenylephrine hydrochloride** FDC subject to a **“shall not be used in children below four years” warning** on labels/package insert/promotional literature. This is an age-related restriction, not a blanket prohibition on every cough syrup for every patient. [Gazette](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadGazette_NotificationsFiles/S.O.%201717%28E%29%20dt%2015.04.2025.pdf).
- Chlorpheniramine: first-generation antihistamine, sedation risk; phenylephrine: vasoconstricting decongestant, cardiovascular concerns. Regulatory concern is risk/benefit in young children, not proof every properly used medicine inevitably causes respiratory arrest.
- Source says Delhi HC applied the notification prospectively and companies must communicate the restriction; its **15 April 2024 stock cutoff is inconsistent with the 2025 notification**. Do not learn it as a verified rule permitting all old stock to be administered to under-fours.
- Most uncomplicated childhood colds are viral/self-limiting; indiscriminate FDC use may obscure diagnosis or expose children to unnecessary medicines. The source’s second-generation antihistamine examples are not substitutes appropriate for every age/illness.
- **Honey is unsuitable for infants under 12 months**; omit the source’s unqualified honey recommendation. Public-health priorities: responsible prescribing, label enforcement, pharmacist/parent information, clinician continuing education, adequate inspectors and clear stock-management instructions. A recommended recall is distinct from an actual mandatory recall order.

## October 2025: syrup quality investigation and paediatric advisory
- Issue reports **nine child deaths in Madhya Pradesh and two in Rajasthan** under investigation; these are the issue's provisional counts, not a final outbreak toll or proof of a common cause.
- Initial Centre-sampled syrups reportedly tested negative for **diethylene glycol (DEG)/ethylene glycol (EG)**, while Tamil Nadu reported **Coldrif batch SR-13** adulteration and action against **Sresan Pharmaceuticals, Kancheepuram**. Results for different samples/times cannot establish every bottle was safe; the initial negative statement must not be treated as final exoneration.
- **3 October DGHS advisory:** cough/cold medicines should **not be prescribed or dispensed below two years**, are generally not recommended below five, and require careful assessment/supervision above that. Correct the source's “below two unless prescribed” exception. [Official broadcaster, 3 October](https://newsonair.gov.in/centre-issues-an-advisory-states-on-use-of-cough-syrups-for-children/).
- DEG/EG contamination can cause severe kidney injury; toxic industrial chemicals can enter pharmaceutical excipients through adulteration or poor quality control. **Propylene glycol can be a source of contamination; it does not ordinarily just degrade into DEG/EG**, as the source suggests. DEG and EG have different metabolic pathways; avoid treating them as interchangeable chemicals.
- CDSCO coordinates central standards/regulation; State controllers license/inspect relevant manufacturing and sales establishments under the Drugs and Cosmetics framework. Batch traceability, verified excipient suppliers, GMP/Schedule M compliance, timely interstate alerts and rational prescribing are complementary safeguards. Earlier Gambia/Uzbekistan/Jammu incidents illustrate recurring risks, but the source's historical death totals are provisional reports rather than harmonised final counts.

## October 2025: children's health rights and medicine governance
- Editorial reports **25 child deaths in Madhya Pradesh** following contaminated cough syrup. This is the article's dated reported toll, not an independently finalised national outbreak count. Separate **batch contamination/quality failure** from **age-specific clinical restrictions**: the April 2025 notification concerned the chlorpheniramine–phenylephrine FDC warning below four, not a contamination-based ban on all syrups. Existing notes retain the exact notification and October DGHS advisory.
- Children differ in organ maturity, body weight, metabolism and drug response; adult-dose extrapolation alone is unsafe. **Paediatric pharmacovigilance** needs age-specific safety evidence, appropriate formulations/dosing, adverse-event reporting and follow-up. Harry Shirkey's **“therapeutic orphans”** describes inadequate research/protection.
- **Article 39(f)** directs healthy development with freedom/dignity; health protection also links to **Article 21**. Institutional division is by statutory functions and licensing categories, not simply **large manufacturers = Centre / small manufacturers = States**, as the source table suggests. Coordinate CDSCO/State controllers on inspections, batch tracing, interstate alerts and accountability.
- WHO's **Essential Medicines List for Children (EMLc)** guides priority age-appropriate medicines. Regular evidence-based updating, procurement and access to suitable formulations are proposals; do not infer that India's NLEM applies to adults only or that a separately named binding national EMLc already exists.
- International comparison: **EU PUMA** is a paediatric-use marketing-authorisation route. US **BPCA** provides incentives for studies; **PREA** creates relevant mandatory paediatric-study obligations—these are distinct instruments. [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/pediatric-drug-development-regulatory-considerations-complying-pediatric-research-equity-act-and).
- Editorial reforms: dedicated paediatric capacity, supplier/excipient quality checks, rational prescription/dispensing, pharmacist/parent information, indigenous research, professional accountability and affordable safe medicines. A new national paediatric authority is a recommendation, not an established body. The source's **39% child share** needs an age definition and census year; the **1974 children's policy** is historical and should not erase the later **2013 National Policy for Children**.
- The later policy was adopted on **26 April 2013**; it affirms a rights-based approach to survival, development, protection and participation. [National Policy for Children 2013](https://icds.gov.in/sites/default/files/policies-document/National_Policy_for_Children_2013.pdf).
