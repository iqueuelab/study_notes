# Perovskite photovoltaics and aqueous recycling

## Technology and study
- Perovskite names an **ABX₃ crystal structure**, not a substance made only of organic material. Halide perovskites can act as the light-absorbing layer in solar cells; silicon and perovskite cells/tandems are distinct designs.
- Advantages: high laboratory efficiency, potentially economical processing, thin/light devices and possible flexibility. Lead-containing formulations, moisture/heat stability and large-area durable manufacture remain challenges; not all perovskites contain lead.
- A **12 February 2025 Nature study** demonstrated aqueous recovery of degraded perovskite and recycling of other valuable cell components. It reported **99.0 ± 0.4% by mass recovery of perovskite** and comparable device performance after **five recycling rounds**. This is not a blanket 99% recovery claim for every layer. [Original study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08408-7).
## Recycling concept
**Used device → separate layers → recover/repair perovskite crystals and other components → fabricate new device.**
- Aqueous chemistry uses **sodium acetate**, **sodium iodide** and **hypophosphorous acid**; acetate helps dissolve lead iodide, while the optimised iodide/acid system supports recovery of suitable crystals. Other layers use solvents including ethanol/ethyl acetate.
- Treat this as controlled laboratory recycling, not a home procedure. Water-based processing reduces dependence on hazardous solvents; it does not make lead-containing effluent harmless or remove chemical handling requirements.
## Circular-economy assessment
- Recovering absorbers, glass, substrates, charge-transport layers and electrodes can conserve resources and reduce waste. Laboratory cell efficiency above 25% in the field does not establish commercial module efficiency or lifetime.
- Scale-up needs reliable collection, separation, lead containment, wastewater management, energy/solvent accounting and quality control. Use **life-cycle assessment** across raw materials, manufacture, use and end of life; commercial/environmental benefits depend on the complete system.
- Practice recall: high efficiency/potential low production cost explain interest; the study's three named aqueous additives are acetate, iodide and hypophosphorous acid.
