# Hanle Dark Sky Reserve: astronomy and community tourism

## October 2025: preserving darkness and livelihoods
- **Hanle, eastern Ladakh, Changthang Wildlife Sanctuary**, around the Indian Astronomical Observatory at roughly **4,250 m**, is India's first Dark Sky Reserve (2022). Partners include **IIA**, Ladakh administration and **LAHDC Leh**. Dry air, high altitude, clear nights and low artificial light support observations; dark-sky management protects astronomy, wildlife and cultural experience.
- **Himalayan Chandra Telescope:** optical/near-infrared; **GROWTH-India:** transient astronomy; **HAGAR** and **MACE:** high-energy gamma-ray observations. IIA/partners operate different instruments; MACE is associated with **BARC**.
- Community measures include shielding lamps, blackout curtains, warm light and visitor awareness. **Bortle class 1** describes very dark skies on a 1–9 scale; it is not proof of absolute absence of all artificial light or automatic international accreditation.
- Source reports **25 Astronomy Ambassadors, including 18 women**, conducting telescope tours and school outreach; around **10,000 visitors in 2024** is a dated local estimate. Homestays/guiding diversify livelihoods; carrying capacity, waste, scarce water and light control determine tourism sustainability.
- The third annual **Star Party, 18–23 September 2025**, brought amateur astronomers/photographers; annual events began in 2023. [IIA event](https://www.iiap.res.in/outreach/hdsr/hdsr-activities/upcoming-events/hsp2025/).
- **Zodiacal light:** sunlight scattered by interplanetary dust; **gegenschein:** enhanced glow opposite the Sun; **Belt of Venus:** pink twilight band above Earth's shadow. Altitude acclimatisation, cold, UV exposure and remote infrastructure require visitor planning; proposed planetarium/dome facilities remain plans.
