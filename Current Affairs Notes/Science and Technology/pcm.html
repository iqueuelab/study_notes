Science and Technology

Phase-change materials and thermal energy storage

Editable notes

Mechanism

  • PCMs absorb/release latent heat during a phase transition; near the transition temperature they can buffer temperature changes. They store energy, not generate it or automatically become renewable energy.
  • Charging: heat absorbed → melting. Discharging: solidification → heat released. Ice/water around 0°C is a familiar example; other materials select different operating temperatures.
TypeExamples / cautions
OrganicParaffins, fatty acids; often low conductivity/flammability concerns
InorganicHydrated salts; possible corrosion, phase separation or subcooling
EutecticMixtures formulated for a selected transition temperature
Bio-basedFeedstock-derived materials; performance varies by formulation

Applications and limits

  • Cold-chain food/pharmaceutical transport; temperature-buffering building walls/roofs; solar thermal storage; battery/electronics thermal management.
  • Each vaccine has its own validated storage requirements; not all vaccines require 2–8°C. PCM capacity is finite and requires proper charging/containment.
  • Check leakage, cycling durability, thermal conductivity, fire safety, cost and lifecycle benefits. Source percentage savings are case-specific, not universal guarantees.
  • Logistics, clean-energy and efficient-building policies may support these applications; do not infer every cited scheme mandates PCM use.
