# Nobel Chemistry 2025: metal–organic frameworks

## MOFs and the corrected prize year
- Susumu Kitagawa (Kyoto), Richard Robson (Melbourne) and Omar M. Yaghi (UC Berkeley) won the **2025** Chemistry Nobel for developing metal–organic frameworks. The PDF repeatedly prints 2024 incorrectly. Robson's foundational construction dates to 1989; the source's 1970s discovery timeline is misleading. [Nobel announcement](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2025/press-release/).
- Metal ions/clusters form nodes joined by organic ligands into crystalline networks; pores and very large internal surface areas permit molecular adsorption, storage and separation. Linker/node choice tunes pore size and chemistry; some frameworks flex or “breathe”, but not all MOFs have the same flexibility, stability or electrical conductivity.
- Research/application areas: CO₂ capture, hydrogen/methane storage, atmospheric-water harvesting, PFAS removal, catalysts/sensors and controlled drug delivery. Performance and commercial maturity vary by material and application; laboratory promise does not guarantee universal deployment.
- Zeolites are generally inorganic microporous frameworks; MOFs offer modular organic–inorganic design. MOFs do not universally replace zeolites or outperform every zeolite. Unyearred practice: both statements on coordinated metal–organic networks and high porosity are correct.
