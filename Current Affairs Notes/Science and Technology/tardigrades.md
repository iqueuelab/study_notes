# Tardigrades: cryptobiosis and extreme survival

## Identity and habitat
- **Water bears / moss piglets**, phylum **Tardigrada**: microscopic, segmented, free-living invertebrates, usually under 1 mm; roughly 1,300 described species in the source's estimate.
- Four pairs of short legs, commonly bearing claws (number/form varies); plump body, tough cuticle and fluid-pressure support. A buccopharyngeal apparatus with piercing stylets extracts food from plant cells or small prey.
- Marine, freshwater and terrestrial habitats include deep seas, mountains, glaciers and deserts. Terrestrial moss/lichen dwellers need a water film for active life. **Space exposure is an experiment, not a natural habitat.**
## Survival mechanism
Water loss / adverse conditions
↓
Legs retract; body contracts into a **tun**
↓
**Cryptobiosis**: metabolism becomes almost undetectable; desiccation-induced form is **anhydrobiosis**
↓
Rehydration permits renewed activity under suitable conditions.
- Tuns can persist for years; some long-duration revival reports extend to decades. Survival depends on species, exposure duration and preparation. Extreme temperature, pressure, radiation and vacuum tolerance does not mean normal growth in those conditions; **extremotolerant** is a useful distinction from organisms thriving at extremes.
- Source illustrations show very low/high temperature limits and near-total water loss: treat these as experimental tolerance bounds, not a universal operating range for every species.
- Astrobiology and stress-biology research examine membrane/DNA protection, desiccation resistance and biological preservation. In **September 2007**, ESA's Foton-M3 carried dehydrated tardigrades for space-exposure experiments; surviving vacuum does not establish extraterrestrial origins. [ESA experiment](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Research/Tiny_animals_survive_exposure_to_space), [NASA research](https://science.nasa.gov/biological-physical/investigations/cell-science-04/).
