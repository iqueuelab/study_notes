Hepatitis A: epidemiological transition and immunisation

Editable notes

November 2025: proposed UIP inclusion

  • Editorial argues for hepatitis A vaccination in the Universal Immunisation Programme, alongside discussion of typhoid conjugate vaccine. This is a policy recommendation, not evidence of nationwide UIP inclusion or that typhoid prevention should be abandoned.
  • HAV: faecal–oral transmission, contaminated food/water; typically acute infection and no chronic carrier state. Improved sanitation reduces childhood exposure and can leave more susceptible adolescents/adults, among whom symptomatic/severe disease is more common. Outbreak prevention needs safe water, sanitation, surveillance and vaccination. There is no specific antiviral treatment; supportive care remains important.
  • Source cites outbreaks in Kerala, Maharashtra, Delhi and Uttar Pradesh, and urban seroprevalence falling from over 90% to below 60%; these are study/location-dependent reported snapshots, not a uniform national immunity trend. Acute liver failure is a possible severe complication, not the usual outcome of every infection.
  • Inactivated and live attenuated vaccines are distinct products/schedules. Source recommends phased introduction in high-risk States, periodic serosurveys and coordination with existing booster visits; programme choice requires burden, safety, cost-effectiveness and delivery assessment. A universal claim that co-administration creates “no extra burden” is unjustified.
  • Correct the indigenous-vaccine claim: WHO records H2 live attenuated vaccines as developed/manufactured in China, with Indian registration/private-market use from 2005; Indian marketing by Biological E does not establish indigenous development/manufacture of Biovac-A. Protection can be long-lasting, but do not treat one dose or lifelong immunity as universal for every vaccine. WHO safety review.
  • 2013 tattooing PYQ: hepatitis B and HIV-AIDS, 2 and 3 (b); contaminated equipment can transmit blood-borne infection, whereas chikungunya is primarily Aedes-borne. 2019 hepatitis PYQ: incorrect statement (b)—HBV has a vaccine; HCV does not. Both can remain clinically silent and cause chronic liver disease; HAV should not be confused with these chronic infections. The PDF's global HBV/HCV/HIV counts mix reference years and are not current comparative totals.
