Deep Ocean Mission and Samudrayaan

Editable notes

Mission and components

  • Ministry of Earth Sciences, multi-institutional Central Sector mission, Cabinet-approved 2021, estimated ₹4,077 crore, two phases over initial 2021–2026 period.
  • Six components: deep-sea mining/submersible/robotics; ocean climate advisories; biodiversity exploration/conservation technology; deep-ocean survey; ocean energy/freshwater; advanced marine station for ocean biology.
  • Samudrayaan manned-submersible component: Matsya 6000, intended three people to 6,000 m, scientific sensors. Design/testing milestone ≠ completed crewed 6-km operational dive.
  • Supports blue economy knowledge/technology; living and non-living resource assessment must consider fragile deep-sea habitats, sediment plumes and international rules.
  • Graphic contrasts shallow human diving and deep engineering; pressure rises with depth, requiring a strong pressure sphere, life support, communications and recovery systems. Mission is wider than mineral extraction alone.

Carlsberg Ridge exploration contract: September 2025

  • 15 September 2025: India signed its third ISA exploration contract, for polymetallic sulphides in a 10,000 sq km Carlsberg Ridge area. India thereby holds two sulphide exploration contracts. An exploration contract is not permission/proof of commercial mining. Indian High Commission signing account.
  • Earlier contracts: Central Indian Ocean Basin nodules, 2002 (source extension to 2027), and Indian Ocean Ridge sulphides, 26 September 2016, to 2031. Afanasy Nikitin Seamount cobalt-rich-crust application remains distinct; Sri Lanka's continental-shelf submission complicates the legal setting.
  • Sulphides form around hydrothermal systems and may contain copper, zinc, gold and silver. Manganese nodules and cobalt-rich ferromanganese crusts are different deposit types; the source's “sulphide nodules” conflation is incorrect.
  • ISA, Kingston, Jamaica, administers mineral activities in the Area, the seabed beyond national jurisdiction, whose resources are common heritage of humankind. The overlying high-seas water column and seabed legal regimes should not be conflated.
  • EEZ up to 200 nautical miles concerns sovereign resource rights; continental shelf extension is governed by UNCLOS Article 76 and scientific/legal conditions. CLCS makes recommendations; coastal States establish outer limits on that basis. It does not adjudicate maritime boundary disputes. The source's blanket 500-nautical-mile Bay of Bengal entitlement is misleading; the special Statement of Understanding is not an automatic entitlement for all regional States.
  • Carlsberg is a spreading ridge between Indian and Somali/African plate domains, adjoining the Owen fracture system; the Indian–Arabian boundary claim and 300,000 sq km licence area conflate tectonic/region descriptions with the 10,000 sq km contract.
  • Critical-mineral security and Indian Ocean capability must be weighed against cost, immature extraction technology and deep-sea biodiversity/plume risks. 2021 PYQ: (b), statements 2 and 3 only—ISA, not Global Ocean Commission, grants mineral exploration contracts; India has rights and rare-earth minerals occur on the seafloor.

Syntactic foam and test stages

  • Syntactic foam = hollow microspheres embedded in a resin matrix: low-density, pressure-resistant buoyancy material. Pressure hulls protect occupants; foam provides buoyancy; ballast, drop weights, thrusters and recovery systems have different roles. A steel test sphere is not the final titanium pressure hull or proof of a 6,000-m crewed dive.
  • Source describes a French foam supply chain with testing in Norway and pressure testing in Russia. Its end-2024/April-2025 timetable is a recycled forecast, not a reliable November 2025 progress report; its illustrative 3.2-m sphere must not override the official 2.1-m crew sphere specification.
  • 17 February 2025 official account: integrated dry tests followed by harbour wet testing at Kattupalli during January–February; later parliamentary reporting confirms crewed/uncrewed harbour trials. These validate subsystems, flotation, control, communications and life support, not completed 500-m or 6-km open-ocean dives. MoES wet-test account, July parliamentary update.
  • Certification, shallow-water trials, pressure qualification and deep-water integration must precede scientific deployment. International test cooperation does not change NIOT/MoES programme leadership; ISRO supports the pressure-sphere work. Forecast dates should be labelled by their announcement period, not treated as achieved milestones.
