# NOTAMs: aviation hazards and operational notices

## Purpose and terminology
- **NOTAM:** aeronautical notice disseminating time-critical establishment, condition or changes in facilities/services/procedures or hazards essential to flight operations. Issued through authorised aeronautical-information systems; informs pilots, controllers and airport/other personnel.
- Source singular **“Notice to Air Mission”** is imprecise. The FAA used **Notice to Air Missions**, then reverted to **Notice to Airmen on 10 February 2025**; nomenclature is jurisdiction-specific, not proof of a universal ICAO rename. [FAA definition/history](https://www.faa.gov/about/initiatives/notam/what_is_a_notam).
- Typical triggers: restricted airspace/military exercises/VIP movements, runway/taxiway closures, failed navigation aids/lights, cranes/temporary obstacles, airshows/parachuting/glider operations and urgent hazards. Do not mistake every NOTAM for complete airspace closure.
## Format and use
- **NOTAMN new; NOTAMR replaces; NOTAMC cancels**. Encoded identifiers, location, validity times, vertical limits and explanatory text require trained interpretation; source graphic categories **aerodrome/en-route/navigation warning** describe operational grouping, not an exhaustive universal letter code. Printed sample is illustrative, not a live restriction.
- Distributed quickly via NOTAM databases and electronic flight-planning/briefing channels; safety preparation combines applicable notices with weather, route, airport and other information. Check start/end times and replacement/cancellation before flight; stale/incomplete review can cause delays, hazards or airspace violations.
