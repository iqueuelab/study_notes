# Measles and subacute sclerosing panencephalitis

## Delayed neurological complication
- **SSPE** is a rare, progressive, usually fatal brain disorder following persistent **wild-type measles virus** infection, typically appearing years after apparent recovery.
- Mainly affects children/adolescents; early behavioural/cognitive decline can progress to myoclonus, seizures, motor/vision loss, swallowing difficulty and severe neurological disability.
- Course varies; a quoted one-to-three-year terminal course is not a rigid rule for every patient. No reliably curative treatment is established; clinical management and experimental approaches are distinct.
- Prevent measles through high **two-dose measles-containing-vaccine** coverage. India uses MR in its public immunisation programme; MMR is another measles-containing vaccine.
- High population coverage reduces transmission; it does **not erase delayed SSPE risk in someone already infected years earlier**. Measles vaccine strains are not established causes of SSPE. [WHO](https://www.who.int/groups/global-advisory-committee-on-vaccine-safety/topics/measles-vaccines).
- Mission Indradhanush helps close immunisation gaps; surveillance and equitable access complement coverage targets.
## Measles recognition and prevention
- Measles virus: **Morbillivirus, Paramyxoviridae**; highly contagious **airborne** infection. Symptoms commonly begin 10–14 days after exposure: fever, cough, coryza, conjunctivitis; **Koplik spots**, followed by face-to-body rash.
- Complications include pneumonia, diarrhoea/dehydration, blindness, encephalitis and the delayed complication **SSPE**. Infants, unvaccinated people and those with impaired immunity/malnutrition are vulnerable.
- India's UIP schedule uses **MR (measles–rubella), not MMR**: first dose **9–12 months**, second **16–24 months**. [Health Ministry schedule](https://mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Final%20Printed%20English%20AR%202024-25.pdf).
- High two-dose coverage (about 95%) and surveillance prevent outbreaks; Mission Indradhanush addresses missed vaccinations. Supportive care and clinician-directed vitamin A matter; antibiotics address bacterial complications, not the measles virus itself.
- Elimination goals, verified elimination and eradication are different. A dated national/regional target is not evidence that measles has disappeared or that all regions share one deadline.

## Vaccination impact recalled in May 2025
- A **2024 modelling study for EPI's fiftieth anniversary** estimated measles vaccination saved **about 94 million lives (9.4 crore) during 1974–2024**, out of approximately **154 million** saved through vaccination against 14 diseases. These are counterfactual model estimates, not newly counted deaths prevented in May 2025. [WHO study account](https://www.who.int/news/item/24-04-2024-global-immunization-efforts-have-saved-at-least-154-million-lives-over-the-past-50-years).
- The source's regional **2.9 crore Africa/2 crore Southeast Asia** figures are reported model outputs and refer to WHO regions, not a uniform country-by-country effect. Its historical hospitalization/case-fatality percentages are setting-specific, not universal measles risks.
- **1963:** Enders and colleagues' live vaccine licensed in the US; **1968:** improved further-attenuated vaccine by Hilleman and colleagues. **WHO EPI: 1974; Gavi: 2000**. [CDC history](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/about/history.html).
- Hygiene/nutrition improve health but cannot substitute for immunity against highly contagious **airborne** measles. Infection can impair immune memory and increase later infections. Missed vaccination, pandemic service disruption, hesitancy, crowding and malnutrition sustain vulnerability.
- Retain existing symptom, complication and supportive-care notes. **India's UIP uses MR, not MMR**, at **9–12 and 16–24 months**; two-dose high coverage and surveillance matter. No measles-specific routine antiviral cure is established; clinician-directed vitamin A/supportive care and treatment of bacterial complications differ from vaccination prevention.
- A **2018 Mumbai MR-campaign clipping** embedded in the issue reports **6.46 lakh of 9.05 lakh** intended pupils covered and misinformation about pig-virus content; this is historical local campaign coverage, not the May 2025 national vaccination rate.
- India's **2025 elimination ambition** and broader regional goals are targets, not verified elimination; the PDF's blanket “WHO/India 2030 deadline” is not a substitute for a dated national target. Eradication is global cessation of transmission; elimination of transmission in a defined area differs from elimination as a public-health problem below a threshold.
- Practice recall: measles is not waterborne; Gavi supports vaccine access; EPI was launched by **WHO**. These undated practice questions are separate from PYQs.
