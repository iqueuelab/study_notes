# Measles and subacute sclerosing panencephalitis

## Delayed neurological complication
- **SSPE** is a rare, progressive, usually fatal brain disorder following persistent **wild-type measles virus** infection, typically appearing years after apparent recovery.
- Mainly affects children/adolescents; early behavioural/cognitive decline can progress to myoclonus, seizures, motor/vision loss, swallowing difficulty and severe neurological disability.
- Course varies; a quoted one-to-three-year terminal course is not a rigid rule for every patient. No reliably curative treatment is established; clinical management and experimental approaches are distinct.
- Prevent measles through high **two-dose measles-containing-vaccine** coverage. India uses MR in its public immunisation programme; MMR is another measles-containing vaccine.
- High population coverage reduces transmission; it does **not erase delayed SSPE risk in someone already infected years earlier**. Measles vaccine strains are not established causes of SSPE. [WHO](https://www.who.int/groups/global-advisory-committee-on-vaccine-safety/topics/measles-vaccines).
- Mission Indradhanush helps close immunisation gaps; surveillance and equitable access complement coverage targets.
