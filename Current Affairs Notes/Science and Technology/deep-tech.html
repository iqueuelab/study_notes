Science and Technology

Deep technology startups and commercialisation

Editable notes

Meaning and ecosystem

  • Deep tech: ventures built on scientific discoveries/advanced engineering and defensible intellectual property; long R&D/prototyping/validation cycles, high upfront capital, technical uncertainty and potentially large societal/industrial impact.
  • Domains: AI/ML, biotechnology/life sciences, quantum computing, advanced materials, photonics/electronics, robotics/drones, aerospace/defence, green energy and selected blockchain technologies. Using a fashionable technology alone does not make every app a deep-tech venture.
  • Applications include medical diagnostics, autonomous logistics, sustainable energy and industrial automation. Commercial success needs both scientific validation and demand.
  • Source's 2021 snapshot reports over 3,000 startups, US$2.7 billion funding, over 12% of startup funding, Bengaluru 25–30% share, followed by Delhi-NCR/Mumbai. These are historical ecosystem estimates, not April 2025 counts. The 53% growth and less than 20% substantially financed lack clear denominator/time definition; do not learn as exact national statistics. NASSCOM–Zinnov report.

Support, bottlenecks and strategy

  • Atal New India Challenge (Atal Innovation Mission/NITI Aayog) supports innovation addressing sectoral challenges. NASSCOM Deep Tech Club 2.0 provides mentoring/networks/exposure; source's 1,000-firm ambition is a support target, not evidence all firms scaled.
  • Obstacles: patient-capital gap, expensive specialised talent, retention, investor difficulty assessing scientific risk, weak lab-to-market mentorship, market access, regulatory delays and insufficient industry–academia links.
  • Priorities: public/private R&D and sectoral patient funds; shared testing/prototyping facilities; technology-transfer capacity; researcher–industry collaboration; proportionate regulation; public procurement/early customers to validate indigenous technologies. Distinguish announced support from commercial outcomes.
