# Synthetic aperture radar and NISAR

## How SAR forms an image
Microwave pulse from moving satellite/aircraft → surface backscatter → coherent measurements at successive positions → signal processing synthesises a large aperture → high-resolution image.
- Active microwave sensing works day/night and through cloud/smoke/haze; performance in rain depends on wavelength/intensity. It does not mean all radar penetrates every material.
- Repeated observations reveal floods, ground deformation, earthquakes, landslides, glaciers and crop/forest change; interpretation depends on geometry, wavelength and processing.
## NISAR: June 2025 context
- **NASA–ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar**, then a forthcoming mission planned from Sriharikota. NASA contributes **L-band SAR**, reflector/boom and key electronics; ISRO contributes **S-band SAR**, spacecraft and launch services (source diagram).
- Repeat mapping is designed on a **12-day cycle**, with ascending/descending observations; the source's “twice every 12 days” should not be read as a guaranteed uniform cadence for every surface point.

## July 21 launch announcement, reported July 22
- NISAR then scheduled for **30 July 2025,17:40 IST**, aboard **GSLV-F16** from Sriharikota; source prelaunch specification **2,392 kg,743 km sun-synchronous orbit,98.4° inclination**.
- **12 m deployable mesh reflector** serves L/S-band radar; source **242 km swath**, SweepSAR and modified **I3K bus**. These are planned mission/configuration figures, not proof launch or commissioning had occurred byJuly 22. [ISRO dated prelaunch release](https://new1.isro.gov.in/ISRO_HINDI/Mission_GSLVF16_NISAR.html).
- Applications include deformation/subsidence, shoreline, ecosystem/vegetation, ice and disaster monitoring; retain the core note's qualified 12 day repeat-cycle/rain-penetration explanation.
