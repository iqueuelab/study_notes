Chronic pulmonary aspergillosis after tuberculosis

Editable notes

Disease distinction

  • CPA, chronic lung disease caused by Aspergillus, often A. fumigatus, an environmental mould in soil/dust/decaying vegetation; spores inhaled, not ordinarily person-to-person transmission.
  • Pre-existing lung cavities/damage, especially post-TB, COPD or sarcoidosis, predispose. Severe immunosuppression is particularly important for invasive aspergillosis; CPA can occur without it. Do not collapse all Aspergillus syndromes into one.
  • Persistent three-month-plus respiratory symptoms, cough/haemoptysis, weight loss, fatigue/breathlessness; fungal ball aspergilloma or chronic cavitary changes. Post-TB symptoms ≠ automatically recurrent bacterial TB.
  • Diagnosis combines imaging and Aspergillus IgG/other microbiology with clinical context; antibody positivity alone is not the same as confirmed CPA. CDC overview.

Assam study

  • January 2025, Dibrugarh tea-community study: 128 tea gardens screened, selected symptomatic sample 177; clipping 17.8% CPA, 18.5% among TB patients, 48.9% among those who completed TB treatment. These are study/subgroup estimates, not Assam-wide population percentages. Original research.
  • Poverty, nutrition, occupational exposure and diagnostic access compound vulnerability. Clipping cites Assam TB 217 per lakh, national prevalence survey 2019–21, and CPA 60 per lakh estimate; keep disease/period/estimate distinct.
  • Source 1.2 million globally annually is not an adequately defined current all-CPA incidence estimate; post-TB estimates and total disease prevalence must not be conflated.

Care and policy

  • Specialist antifungals such as itraconazole/voriconazole, selected surgery for serious fungal-ball bleeding and nutritional/underlying-disease support; treatment depends on form, resistance and patient assessment.
  • Follow up TB survivors, train clinicians, improve affordable imaging/serology and antifungal access; better nutrition/living conditions support health, but ventilation alone cannot remove ubiquitous mould exposure.
