# Accelerator mass spectrometry and radiocarbon dating

## Method and use
- **Accelerator mass spectrometry (AMS)** directly counts rare isotopes such as **carbon-14**, rather than waiting to measure radioactive decays. Convert sample carbon to ions → accelerate → separate isotopes/interferences → measure isotope ratios → calibrate age.
- Useful for organic archaeological remains such as charcoal, wood and bone, geological/environmental reconstruction, and biomedical tracers. Small samples and high sensitivity preserve precious material; contamination, pretreatment, reservoir effects and calibration still matter.
- The source reports Tamil Nadu archaeology authorities sending **23 charcoal samples to Beta Analytic, USA**, to date archaeological layers. Sample submission is not a published age/result or proof every artefact in that layer is identical in age.
- Its **20 mg vs ≥10 g**, hours vs days comparison is illustrative rather than universal laboratory requirements; AMS counts atoms/isotopic ratios, not “extremely small radioactivity”. Material age must be interpreted with stratigraphy and archaeological context.
