# Kulasekarapattinam spaceport and SSLV

## August 2025 launch-pad milestone
- **Kulasekarapattinam, Thoothukudi/Tuticorin district, Tamil Nadu**: dedicated SSLV launch complex, also for non-government launch activities. ISRO laid the launch-pad foundation **27 August 2025**; major-facility construction began **5 March 2025**. End-2026 completion/first SSLV flight and 20–25 launches/year were plans, not achieved capacity. [ISRO milestone](https://www.isro.gov.in/Foundation_Stone_Laid_for_Launch_Pad_at_SSLV_Launch_Complex.html).
- Southward ocean access reduces the **dog-leg manoeuvre around Sri Lanka** required for relevant polar trajectories from Sriharikota → better payload/fuel efficiency. Range-safety procedures remain necessary; “no risk” is too absolute.
- The issue's **2,233 acres and ₹100 crore initial-phase work** must not be read as total project cost. Sriharikota's approved Third Launch Pad is a separate project.
## SSLV revision
- **Three solid propulsion stages + liquid Velocity Trimming Module**; about **500 kg to a 500 km planar orbit**. Low infrastructure needs, short turnaround and flexible dedicated smallsat missions support commercial/startup access.
- **SSLV-D1, August 2022** failed to inject EOS-02/AzaadiSAT into intended orbit; **D2, February 2023**, successfully launched three satellites. Do not omit the later D3 success when describing the completed development programme.
- PSLV commonly serves Earth observation/SSO; GSLV/LVM3 support heavier payloads and GTO missions. Orbit determines apparent sky motion: a geostationary satellite is fixed relative to an observer, not any satellite merely because of its launch vehicle. PSLV has also launched spacecraft to transfer trajectories; categorical “PSLV cannot launch geostationary satellites” is misleading.
- **2018 PYQ: only statement 1 (a)**; LVM3 has three propulsion stages, not the four alternating solid/liquid stages attributed in the distractor.
