# AstroSat: multiwavelength space astronomy

## A decade in orbit
- India's first dedicated multiwavelength astronomy observatory **AstroSat**, launched **28 September 2015 by PSLV-C30 from Sriharikota**, completed ten years in September 2025, beyond its five-year design life. Roughly 1.5 tonnes, near-equatorial orbit about 650 km; source 1,513 kg and some official summaries ~1,515 kg are rounding/specification variants.
- Five scientific instruments: **UVIT** ultraviolet imaging; **LAXPC** X-ray timing/spectroscopy over **3–80 keV** (not merely low-energy X-rays); **CZTI** hard X-rays, gamma-ray burst capability; **SXT** soft X-rays; **SSM** monitoring transient X-ray sources. [ISRO payloads](https://www.isro.gov.in/AstroSatPayloads.html).
- Simultaneous UV/X-ray observations help study black-hole/neutron-star accretion, stellar flares, galaxies and transient events. Source cites distant-galaxy ultraviolet detection (~9.3 billion light-years) and Proxima Centauri studies; these are particular research results, not proof it images all wavelengths like every space telescope.
- Collaboration includes ISRO, IUCAA, TIFR, IIA and RRI with international contributions. **2016 PYQ: neither statement (d)**: Japan/European missions predate AstroSat; 2,000 kg and 1,650 km figures are incorrect. Existing common bank retains full question/options.
