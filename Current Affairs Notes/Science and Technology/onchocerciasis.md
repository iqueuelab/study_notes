# Onchocerciasis: river blindness and transmission elimination

## December issue: Niger milestone and disease revision
- **Correction of event timing:** WHO announced Niger's verification on **30 January 2025**, making it the **first African country and fifth globally** verified for **interruption/elimination of onchocerciasis transmission**. The December issue revisits this achievement; it is not a newly dated December elimination or global eradication. [WHO announcement](https://www.who.int/news/item/30-01-2025-who-verifies-niger-as-the-first-country-in-the-african-region-to-eliminate-onchocerciasis).
- Parasitic nematode **Onchocerca volvulus**; infected **Simulium blackflies** breed near fast-flowing rivers/streams. Fly ingests microfilariae → development in fly → infective larvae enter another person during bites → adults produce microfilariae. **Not direct person-to-person transmission**.
- Microfilariae in skin/eyes cause itching, depigmentation, nodules, inflammation and potentially permanent blindness. Disease burden is concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa, with residual foci in Brazil/Venezuela; remote riverine communities face poverty and care-access barriers.
- Repeated community-directed **ivermectin** treatment reduces microfilariae and transmission; long-lived adults mean repeated campaigns are needed. Vector control, surveillance and long-term programme commitment complement treatment. **Doxycycline targets Wolbachia bacterial symbionts**, under appropriate clinical/programmatic use. These are population-health revision points, not self-treatment instructions.
- NTD control differs from elimination of transmission, elimination as a public-health problem and worldwide eradication. Existing **2014 smallpox** and **2001 Athlete's Foot** PYQs repeat: **smallpox only (b)**; **fungus (b)**. Chickenpox is caused by VZV and diphtheria by a bacterium; neither is eradicated in India.
