# Axiom-4, Gaganyaan and the International Space Station

## Mission and India’s role
- **Ax-4:** fourth private astronaut mission to the ISS, organised by **Axiom Space**, using SpaceX transport with NASA/partner coordination. Commercial low-Earth-orbit activity complements public space programmes.
- **2 August 2024:** ISRO announced **Shubhanshu Shukla—prime mission pilot; Prasanth Balakrishnan Nair—backup** from its Gaganyaan astronaut pool. Training covered spacecraft operations, emergencies, microgravity experiments and astronaut health. [ISRO selection](https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO%20Press%20Release-SFA%20Signing.html).
- **4 April 2025:** mission was still forthcoming; the PDF’s “launched in August 2024” is incorrect. August 2024 concerned selection/training, not launch.
- **Later development—25 June 2025:** launched aboard SpaceX Dragon/Falcon 9 from Kennedy Space Center. Shukla became the first Indian to visit the ISS; the completed stay was **18 days**, rather than the initial approximately fortnight-long plan. Returned **15 July 2025**. [ISRO mission outcome](https://www.isro.gov.in/Axiom04_mission_successfully_concluded_return_ISRO_Gaganyatri_ShubhanshuShukla.html).
## ISS quick recall
- International microgravity laboratory in **low Earth orbit, approximately 400 km high**; NASA, Roscosmos, ESA, JAXA and CSA partnership.
- First modules **1998**; continuous human presence since **2 November 2000**. Typical crew about seven, with temporary variation during handovers/visiting missions.
- Approximately **7.7 km/s**, one orbit about **90 minutes**: roughly 16 orbits/sunrise–sunset cycles daily. Research: biology, medicine, materials, fluid physics and technologies for longer missions. [NASA ISS facts](https://www.nasa.gov/international-space-station/space-station-facts-and-figures/).
## Link to Indian human spaceflight
- **Ax-4 experience → operational skills and microgravity research → Gaganyaan capability → longer-duration missions.** Participation does not itself constitute an independently launched Indian crewed mission.
- **Gaganyaan:** indigenous human-spaceflight programme; uncrewed tests, escape systems and crew safety must precede crewed flight. Successful independent crewed launch would place India alongside the Soviet/Russian, US and Chinese capabilities; distinguish ambition from achievement.
- **Bharatiya Antariksh Station by 2035** was a programme target, not an already operational station. Avoid treating an early 20-ton concept or old launch forecast as a final configuration/date.
- Benefits: space diplomacy, commercial participation, scientific collaboration and training; constraints: cost, life support, radiation, microgravity health effects and reliable emergency recovery.

## June 27 headline
- The issue reports Shubhanshu Shukla reached space/ISS and calls him the **634th person in space**. Such ordinal counts depend on the definition/boundary and tracking list; the durable milestone is **first Indian to visit ISS**, after launch on **25 June 2025**, not first Indian ever in space.

## In-flight biological experiments reported July 10
- Shubhanshu Shukla photographed **fenugreek and green-gram sprouts in petri dishes** and stored samples in an ISS freezer to study microgravity effects on germination/early growth. The sprouts experiment involves **University of Agricultural Sciences, Dharwad and IIT Dharwad**; multigeneration post-flight work will examine genetic, microbial and nutritional changes.
- Clipping also reports microalgae deployment/storage for potential food, oxygen and biofuel applications; imaging of six crop-seed varieties for later multigeneration studies; and stem-cell recovery/repair research. These are experiments/potential applications, not demonstrated space-farming self-sufficiency or proven clinical therapy.

## Crop seeds: post-flight research
- **IIST Space Biology Lab + Kerala Agricultural University** study dry seeds exposed aboard Ax-4: rice **Jyothi, Uma**; horse gram **Kanakamani**; tomato **Vellayani Vijay**; sesame **Thilakathara**; brinjal **Soorya**. Dry exposure differs from germinating sprouts on ISS.
- Compare **space-returned, Earth-stored control and fresh seeds** for germination, growth, morphology, flowering/fruiting, yield and genetic changes over subsequent generations. Space radiation/microgravity may alter biology; beneficial mutations or higher yields are hypotheses, not demonstrated outcomes.
- Collaboration involves ISRO/HSFC, IIST and KAU; source identifies project lead **K.G. Sreejalakshmi** and May 2025 MoU. The issue anticipates receipt of seeds after landing; completed mission return is recorded separately.

## Gaganyaan forecast reported August 2025
- Source plans **first uncrewed December 2025, two more 2026, crewed early 2027**: then-announced schedules, not completed flights/guarantees.
- Orbital-module development, crew-escape testing and launch-vehicle human-rating are separate requirements; one qualification claim does not establish total mission readiness.
