# Exoplanet biosignatures and the value of null results

## Asking testable questions
- A search should distinguish **habitability**, life's actual presence and remotely **detectable biosignatures**. No detected sign is not proof life is absent; weak biospheres, abiotic look-alikes and limited instruments produce false negatives/positives.
- **Bayesian analysis** updates prior beliefs with observations and detection-model assumptions. Carefully selected, well-characterised null results constrain occurrence rates; unrepresentative samples or uncertain detection sensitivity weaken inference.
- ETH-led study: idealised **40/80 no-detection observations** could yield **99.9% upper limits of ~20%/~10%** for the observed occurrence parameter. These are conditional statistical bounds for similar sampled planets, not an actual survey proving how rare all life is. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06779).
- **LIFE—Large Interferometer for Exoplanets** and **HWO—Habitable Worlds Observatory** are proposed future observing concepts; improved spectra could assess water/atmospheric chemistry, with theory and contextual validation required. Detecting water, oxygen or methane alone does not establish biological origin or confirm extraterrestrial life.
