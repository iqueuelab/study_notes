Vitamin D: forms, sources and deficiency

  • Fat-soluble vitamin: D2 (ergocalciferol) and D3 (cholecalciferol). UVB exposure enables skin synthesis of D3; oily fish, egg yolks, fortified foods and supplements supply dietary vitamin D. The liver forms 25-hydroxyvitamin D; the kidney produces active calcitriol.
  • Supports calcium/phosphate absorption and bone mineralisation; also has muscle and immune functions. Severe deficiency causes rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults. Osteoporosis is loss of bone mass, a distinct condition; inadequate vitamin D can contribute to poor bone health.
  • Risks include limited sunlight, older age, dark skin, covered clothing, malabsorption and poor dietary intake. UVB varies with season, latitude, pollution and skin exposure; no universal safe exposure duration or fixed 10 am–3 pm prescription follows. NIH fact sheet.

12 April 2025: interpreting the Indian deficiency report

  • The source's “one in five Indians” claim comes from a commercial laboratory sample, not a nationally representative population survey. Testing selection and the deficiency cut-off affect estimates; do not extrapolate to all Indians or assert a worsening national trend from this report alone.
  • Observational associations with cancer, cardiovascular/autoimmune disease and infections do not establish causation or justify blanket supplementation for prevention.
  • The 2024 Endocrine Society guideline advises against routine vitamin D testing in generally healthy people; clinical indications and population-specific needs differ. Universal screening under POSHAN, RBSK or Ayushman Bharat is the source's proposal, not an established policy. Clinical guideline.
  • FSSAI standards provide for vitamin A/D fortification of milk and edible oil; flour fortification uses other specified nutrients. Do not treat a proposal for mandatory vitamin D fortification of all milk/oil/flour as operative nationwide law. Public-health options include dietary diversity, quality-controlled fortification, awareness and evidence-based targeted care. FSSAI regulations.
