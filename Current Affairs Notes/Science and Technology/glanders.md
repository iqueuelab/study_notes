# Glanders: equine disease surveillance and eradication

## Revised national action plan, 2025
- DAHD's **National Action Plan for Control and Eradication of Glanders in India, Revised 2025** strengthens surveillance, testing, infected-equine elimination, movement control and owner/veterinary awareness. [DAHD plan](https://dahd.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-08/NationalActionPlanforControlandEradicationofGlandersinIndiaRevised-2025.pdf).
- **Burkholderia mallei**, a bacterium, mainly affects **horses, mules and donkeys**; zoonotic infection can affect handlers and veterinarians. Nasal/pulmonary disease causes discharge, nodules/ulceration and pneumonia; cutaneous form **farcy** causes nodules, ulcers and lymphatic lesions.
- Equine transmission includes contaminated feed/water and infected secretions/equipment; human exposure may occur through damaged skin/mucosa or inhalation. Animal eradication policy should not be confused with a claim that human glanders cannot be treated.
- **WOAH-listed** and nationally notifiable under the **Prevention and Control of Infectious and Contagious Diseases in Animals Act, 2009**; international reporting follows WOAH notification rules, not a separate global legal notification by every individual owner. Testing, biosecurity and regulated movement protect animal health, livelihoods and public health.
