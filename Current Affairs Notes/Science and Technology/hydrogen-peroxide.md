# Hydrogen peroxide: oxidising agent and applications

## Chemistry and uses
- **H₂O₂**; commonly encountered as a colourless aqueous liquid. Trace vapour occurs naturally; unstable decomposition releases heat: **2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂**.
- **Nonflammable but a strong oxidiser**: contact with organic material can intensify fire or cause spontaneous combustion. Decomposition to water/oxygen does not make concentrated peroxide harmless.
- Dilute household formulations (source **3–9%**) have medicinal/bleaching uses; higher concentrations serve textile/paper bleaching, chemical manufacture and rocket propulsion. Other oxidising applications include sterilisation, wastewater treatment and chemical synthesis. The clipping concerns alleged milk adulteration, not an approved food-preservation use. [ATSDR chemistry/use account](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/TSP/substances/ToxSubstance.aspx?toxid=55).
- The advertising inset's general wound-healing/safe-daily-use claims should not be memorised as medical advice; toxicity depends on concentration and exposure.
