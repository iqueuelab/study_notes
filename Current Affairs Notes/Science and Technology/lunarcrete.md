# Lunarcrete: regolith-based lunar construction

## December 2025: construction using lunar resources
- **Lunarcrete** is an umbrella concept for concrete-like construction material using **lunar regolith** (surface rock fragments and fine dust) as aggregate. **In-situ resource utilisation (ISRU)** reduces the material that must be launched from Earth; it is a research/development pathway, not an established lunar settlement technology.
- Requirements: structural strength, radiation shielding and thermal insulation under vacuum and extreme temperature cycling. Transport costs and scarce accessible water make ordinary water-intensive Portland-cement processes difficult.
- Approaches: small imported cement quantities with controlled water/curing; **molten sulphur binder + regolith**, solidified by cooling without water; or **microwave/concentrated-solar sintering**, fusing particles with no separate cement binder. Sulphur's high-temperature behaviour, vacuum exposure, sourcing and durability require engineering assessment.
- The issue reports an **LSU/Arup Bhattacharya dome-habitat simulation** under approximately **+120°C to −130°C**, with double walls/air gap and an internal **22°C** result. Treat this as a source-reported design/simulation outcome, not proof that an unpowered lunar building always stays at 22°C. Thermal properties, geometry, boundary conditions and life-support loads determine actual performance.
- LSU and NASA research independently confirms work on **sulphur–regolith waterless concrete** for robotic construction; scientific promise does not establish full habitat certification. [LSU research account](https://www.lsu.edu/eng/news/2023/12/lsu-cm-lunar-robotic-construction.php).
