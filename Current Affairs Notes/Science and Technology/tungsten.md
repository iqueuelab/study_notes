# Tungsten: properties, ores and uses

## Prelims facts

- Symbol **W**; melting point about **3,422°C**, highest among metals; used for hard, heat-resistant materials.
- Ores: **wolframite (Fe,Mn)WO4**, **scheelite CaWO4**, **ferberite FeWO4**; deposits often associated with hydrothermal processes.
- Applications: tungsten carbides/cutting tools, alloy/special steels, hard-facing materials, armour/ammunition components and historically bulb filaments; alloys may include chromium, nickel, molybdenum or titanium.

## Source resource figures

- Karnataka **42%**, Rajasthan **27%**, Andhra Pradesh **17%**, Maharashtra **9%**; remaining 5% across Haryana, Tamil Nadu, Uttarakhand and West Bengal. The resource inventory year is missing, so these are source figures pending verification.
- Source describes domestic dependence on imports, including trade partners Singapore/US/UK; export destinations Germany, US, Israel, UK, Japan, Sweden. They are dated/unspecified trade lists, not permanent rankings.

## Avoid unsupported absolutes

- Do not memorise 'resistant to all acids' or '95% used by steel' as universal facts. Distinguish mineral resources, economically recoverable reserves and actual production.
- Atomic number **74**, atomic mass about **183.84**, density about **19.3 g/cm³**; these appear in the source graphic.
