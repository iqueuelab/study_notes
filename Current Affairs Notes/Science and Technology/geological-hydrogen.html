Geological hydrogen and hydrogen colour labels

Editable notes

White hydrogen

  • Naturally occurring hydrogen produced by geological processes, including water–iron-rich-rock reactions and radiolysis; reservoirs, concentration and replenishment need investigation.
  • Lorraine/Moselle research had already reported potential in 2023. Approximately 46 million tonnes was an extrapolated possible resource, not a proved economically recoverable reserve newly certified in March 2025. CNRS.
  • The source's US$92 trillion valuation is unreliable. Arithmetic: 46 million tonnes = 46 billion kg; at an illustrative US$2/kg, gross value is US$92 billion, before extraction costs—not trillion or net profit.
  • Natural occurrence avoids manufacturing hydrogen by electrolysis/reforming, but extraction, separation, compression and transport still use energy. Purity, scale and lowest cost are not guaranteed.
  • Combustion contains no fuel carbon, but life-cycle emissions, leakage and possible NOx still matter; “zero environmental impact” is incorrect. Helium co-occurrence is site-dependent.

1 May 2025: prospectivity versus proved reserves

  • Serpentinisation involves water reacting with iron-bearing ultramafic rocks; radiolysis splits water under radiation from minerals. Other geological/organic processes can contribute, but source, migration, reservoir and seal conditions together determine accumulation.
  • Small molecules can leak and hydrogen can react or be consumed by microbes. Historically limited sampling and hydrocarbon-focused exploration help explain overlooked occurrences; lack of discovery does not prove universal scarcity. Exploration, gas separation, compression, storage and transport remain costly uncertainties.
  • Global resource models describe a potentially large subsurface endowment, not certified recoverable reserves or guaranteed centuries of supply. Most predicted hydrogen may be inaccessible or dispersed; model uncertainty is large. USGS research.
  • The issue identifies India's Andaman/Himalayan ophiolites and mafic/ultramafic formations, Deccan rocks and possible hydrothermal settings as exploration candidates. Geological plausibility is not a proved Indian commercial reserve; its Vindhyan/Chhattisgarh/Rajasthan references need site-specific assessment.
  • Natural replenishment may occur at some sites but does not establish extraction is renewable at commercial rates. Fuel cells produce water at use; life-cycle energy, hydrogen leakage and extraction impacts still matter. Existing colour/process distinctions are unchanged; claims that white hydrogen is always cheapest or green/pink universally zero-emission are unsupported.
