# Meningococcal infection: transmission and prevention

## Meghalaya advisory and disease basics
- The issue reports a Meghalaya health advisory following a meningococcal scare; an advisory alone does not establish a confirmed statewide epidemic. **Neisseria meningitidis** is a bacterium causing meningitis (inflamed brain/spinal-cord membranes) and meningococcaemia/sepsis.
- Spread is chiefly through respiratory/throat secretions during close or prolonged contact; asymptomatic carriage can occur. Crowding raises transmission risk. It does not usually spread as readily as a cold through casual contact.
- Fever, severe headache, stiff neck, confusion and sometimes a non-blanching rash are warning features; not every case has the complete symptom set. Invasive disease can progress rapidly to shock and organ damage. Prompt antibiotics and supportive care are central; public-health management includes contact assessment, preventive antibiotics for eligible close contacts and serogroup-appropriate vaccination. [CDC overview](https://www.cdc.gov/meningococcal/about/index.html), [WHO regional guidance](https://afro.who.int/health-topics/meningococcal-meningitis).
- **Meningitis is a syndrome with several causes** (bacterial, viral, fungal and others); meningococcal disease is one specific bacterial cause. Vaccination does not cover every possible cause of meningitis.
