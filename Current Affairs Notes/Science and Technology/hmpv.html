Human metapneumovirus (HMPV)

Editable notes

Virus and disease

  • Respiratory virus identified 2001, causing upper/lower respiratory infections; clinically resembles RSV and other respiratory illnesses.
  • Symptoms: cough, fever, nasal congestion, wheeze or shortness of breath; severe disease can include bronchiolitis/pneumonia. Higher risk: young children, older adults and immunocompromised people.
  • Spread: respiratory secretions, close contact and contaminated surfaces; usual incubation 3-6 days. Late-winter/spring circulation is documented in temperate US settings, not a universal calendar for every country.
  • No specific antiviral treatment or vaccine; care is supportive. Hand hygiene, covering coughs and staying away from others while ill help reduce spread. CDC overview.

January 2025 context

  • Source discusses increased respiratory infections in China; hospital videos/social-media claims do not establish a new pandemic. Distinguish surveillance evidence from rumours and HMPV from a newly discovered pathogen.
  • Co-circulating pathogens mentioned: influenza A, SARS-CoV-2 and Mycoplasma pneumoniae (a bacterium, not a virus).
  • Cover confirms 4 January 2025; the inner heading incorrectly says January 2024.

January 7 consolidation

  • The source again reports cough, fever, nasal congestion, wheezing and breathing difficulty; severe disease can involve bronchiolitis, bronchitis or pneumonia, especially children, older adults, immunocompromised people and people with chronic lung disease.
  • HMPV circulates seasonally, often winter/spring in temperate regions; outbreaks do not establish a novel virus or pandemic. Do not use social-media hospital/cemetery claims as epidemiological evidence.
  • Source '2–5 days of illness' is not a guaranteed duration; severity and underlying health vary. Mycoplasma pneumoniae is a bacterium, despite its placement in the PDF's 'other viruses' list.
