Lunar nuclear power: Russia–China plans and space governance

Editable notes

December 2025: Russian lunar power-station plan

  • Roscosmos announced a Lavochkin contract in December, work through 2036, to develop lunar power infrastructure for rovers, observatories and the International Lunar Research Station (ILRS) programme with China. The source calls it nuclear; 2036 is a plan/target, not an operating installation. Russia's official notice describes development, ground/flight tests and deployment. Russian federal agency report, 24 December.
  • A typical lunar night lasts roughly 14 Earth days; polar terrain/illumination varies. Continuous power supports life support, communications, instruments and prospective ISRU. Nuclear power can complement solar/storage, but requires heat rejection, shielding, reliability and safe autonomous operation.
  • Fission reactor generates energy through a controlled chain reaction; radioisotope power source converts decay heat and is not a reactor. The PDF's “SMR/radioisotope/fission” menu is speculative, not confirmed engineering configuration or evidence all are equivalent. Do not infer a specific reactor size/fuel from the announcement.
  • 1967 Outer Space Treaty bars nuclear/WMD placement in orbit or stationing in space and reserves Moon/celestial bodies for peaceful purposes; it does not ban peaceful nuclear power sources. Due regard, contamination avoidance, launch safety and responsibility/liability are relevant. UNOOSA treaty.
  • ILRS and US-led Artemis reflect cooperation/competition over scientific infrastructure; future mining, permanent habitation and Mars support are ambitions. NASA's fission-surface-power/Kilopower work is a comparison, not an already running lunar plant. Chandrayaan's achievements do not establish an Indian lunar nuclear plant; strategic capability does not confer lunar sovereignty.
