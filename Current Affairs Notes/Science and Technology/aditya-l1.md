# Aditya-L1 and Lagrange points

## Mission
- ISRO solar observatory: **PSLV-C57 launch 2 September 2023; halo-orbit insertion 6 January 2024**, near Sun–EarthL1~1.5 millionkm from Earth. It does not travel to the Sun's surface.
- Seven payloads: **VELC, SUIT, SoLEXS, HEL1OS** remote solar observations; **ASPEX, PAPA, magnetometer** in-situ particles/fields.
- Study corona, heating, flares, solar wind and space weather; complements other observatories rather than duplicating every instrument.
## Lagrange points
- Five points in the rotating two-body system; L1–L3 unstable and need station-keeping; L4/L5 can be stable under appropriate mass-ratio conditions.
- Balanced effective forces do not mean zero gravity or no fuel requirement. Halo orbit provides useful observing geometry.
- Source claims of ISRO's first-ever PSLV restart are incorrect; earlier restart demonstrations preceded this mission.

## November 2025: VELC near-Sun CME spectroscopy
- **IIA/NASA researchers** used **Visible Emission Line Coronagraph (VELC)** spectra in the **5303 Å Fe XIV line** to infer conditions in a CME near **1.06 solar radii**. VELC blocks bright disk light to study the corona; near-limb spectroscopy is the specific advance, not the first observation of any CME by any instrument.
- Source values: electron density **3.7 × 10⁸ cm⁻³**, **thermal energy 9.4 × 10²¹ J**, mass **2.7 × 10¹⁴ g =270 million metric tonnes**, initial speed **~264 km/s**, temperature **~1.8 million K**. These are event estimates, not constants for every CME; thermal energy is not interchangeable with kinetic energy. The later journal paper independently confirms density, thermal-energy and mass figures. [IIA research paper, published 25 November 2025](https://prints.iiap.res.in/bitstream/handle/2248/8861/Near-Sun%20Characteristics%20of%20a%20CME%20Inferred%20from%20Observations%20in%20the%205303%20%C3%85%20Coronal%20Emission%20Line.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1).
- **CME = plasma and magnetic-field ejection**, distinct from a solar flare's electromagnetic radiation burst. Direction and magnetic orientation govern Earth impacts: geomagnetic storms, auroras and risks for satellites/navigation/radio and power grids. Better measurements help modelling/forecasting; they do not guarantee precise warning of every storm.
- Existing mission dates/orbit/payloads remain above. **L1 is 1.5 million km from Earth, not close to the Sun’s surface**; close coronal observation describes telescope geometry, not spacecraft position. VELC includes specific visible/infrared spectral channels, not a generic claim that all seven payloads observe 400–700 nm. Reject the source's physically confused Titanic iceberg energy/mass comparison.
