Snakebite envenoming and neglected tropical diseases

Editable notes

Disease and inequality

  • Envenoming: toxin injury from venom injection; certain spitting snakes can also cause ocular exposure. Not every snakebite injects venom.
  • WHO neglected tropical disease (NTD) portfolio since 2017; rural/agricultural, poor and tribal communities face greater exposure and delayed care. Disability can include tissue loss/amputation, renal injury, visual impairment and psychological harm.
  • India's conventional “Big Four”: Indian cobra, common krait, Russell's viper, saw-scaled viper. Important causes, but not every serious bite or region is covered by this list; “90% of all bites” is not a universal verified share.

Prevention and response

  • WHO 2019 strategy: halve mortality and disability by 2030; community empowerment, safe/effective treatment, stronger health systems and partnerships. WHO strategy.
  • WHO plans include better antivenom availability, quality manufacturing and stockpiles; source 25% manufacturer increase is a strategy target, not achieved capacity.
  • India National Action Plan for Prevention and Control of Snakebite Envenoming (NAP-SE), 12 March 2024, One Health approach and target to halve deaths by 2030. Source's 2015 launch conflicts with the actual launch. Health Ministry.
  • Community education, early transport/referral, accessible quality antivenom, trained personnel and surveillance; antivenom choice/treatment requires clinical assessment.

NTD trap

  • NTDs disproportionately affect disadvantaged communities and receive insufficient research/service attention. Examples: scabies, yaws, trachoma, leishmaniasis, Chagas disease and snakebite envenoming.
  • Not all NTDs are infections: snakebite is toxin-mediated. Lack of clean water/sanitation matters for several NTDs but is not the cause of every NTD.
