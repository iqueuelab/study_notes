# Nuclear power: global role and constraints

## Global role: June 2025 revision
- Nuclear fission plants have generated electricity since the 1950s. Nuclear supplies roughly **9% of global electricity** and is an important low-carbon source, with the US, China and France major producers. The PDF's 2023 generation figures must not be treated as 2025 output.
- Advantages: low operational carbon emissions, firm power, long plant lives and diversification of energy supply. Plants still need outages, cooling water, fuel services and grid integration; reliability is not literal uninterrupted operation.
- Constraints: high initial capital/financing cost, long construction and decommissioning, accident prevention/emergency response, radioactive-waste management and public trust. **Chernobyl 1986** and **Fukushima 2011** are key safety case studies.
- Bangladesh's Rooppur and Türkiye's Akkuyu exemplify newcomer programmes under construction at this date. The source's table of supposed 2025 new operational reactors is not a reliable commissioning list; distinguish construction, first criticality and grid connection.
