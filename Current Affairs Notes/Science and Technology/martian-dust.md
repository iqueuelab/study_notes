# Martian dust: exposure hazards and mission design

## Why dust matters
- Fine, abrasive, charged regolith can enter seals/habitats and affect machinery, solar power and human exposure. Silica, perchlorates, iron compounds and potentially toxic metals raise health concerns.
- Inhalation may damage lungs; perchlorates can interfere with iodine uptake/thyroid function. Risk depends on dose, composition and duration: **no human has yet developed a clinically observed Martian dust disease**.
- Earth coal-dust “black lung” is an analogy, not evidence that Martian dust contains coal or causes the identical disease.
- Dust storms recur irregularly, not precisely every three Martian years; a Martian year is about **687 Earth days**. Opportunity's 2018 storm-related loss differs from InSight's gradual dust-driven power decline in 2022.
## Mitigation
- Suitports/airlocks, dust exclusion and cleaning, filtration, abrasion-resistant components, exposure sensors, robotic handling and protected power systems.
- Mission medicine must address delayed evacuation, monitoring and informed consent. Iodine/vitamin supplements are not established universal antidotes; treatment requires evidence.
- Balance redundancy, mass/power costs, crew safety and long-duration feasibility. The supplied essay prompt has no verified UPSC year and belongs to practice, not official PYQs.
