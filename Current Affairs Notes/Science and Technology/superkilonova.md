# Superkilonova candidates and multi-messenger astronomy

## December 2025: possible superkilonova AT2025ulz
- Study reported **15 December**, Caltech explanation **16 December**: **AT2025ulz/ZTF25abjmnps**, about **1.3 billion light-years** away, is a **candidate**, not a confirmed new class. Indian participation includes IIT Bombay and Indian Institute of Astrophysics.
- **18 August 2025 S250818k** gravitational-wave trigger and subsequent **Zwicky Transient Facility** optical detection: early red/fading emission resembled a kilonova, then brightened/blued with supernova-like spectra. Association with the lower-confidence gravitational-wave event remains uncertain. [Caltech research account](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/possible-superkilonova-exploded-not-once-but-twice).
- Hypothesis: rapidly rotating massive-star collapse/supernova → possible fission or fragmentation producing unusually small neutron-star remnants → merger/kilonova within supernova debris. A typical supernova does **not** routinely produce two neutron stars; this remains a model requiring further evidence.
- Kilonova light comes from radioactive decay in neutron-rich merger ejecta, linked to **r-process heavy elements** such as gold/platinum. **GW170817 (2017)** is the established comparison. Combining electromagnetic observations and gravitational waves is **multi-messenger astronomy**.
- Source’s fallback-heating account is an alternative energy-injection idea, not evidence for a proven taxonomy of “two superkilonova types”, and brighter/bluer/longer-lived behaviour is not a universal observational definition. Life-cycle graphics simplify outcomes; initial mass, rotation and binarity matter.
- **2023 PYQ revision:** Cepheids are periodically variable stars; nebulae are gas/dust clouds; pulsars are rotating neutron stars.
