# H125: private-sector helicopter assembly in India

## October 2025: Vemagal assembly plan
- **Tata Advanced Systems + Airbus** announced **Vemagal, Kolar district, Karnataka** as the site of India's first private-sector helicopter final assembly line. First Indian-made **H125** deliveries were **expected in early 2027**, with South Asian exports envisaged; this was an announced plan in this issue. [Airbus, 1 October 2025](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-10-airbus-tata-advanced-systems-limited-launch-a-new-era-made-in-india).
- **Single-engine light utility helicopter**, adaptable to high-altitude/high-temperature operations, rescue, medical transport, tourism, law enforcement and aerial work. A planned **H125M** military offering must be distinguished from an Indian armed-forces induction order.
- Local assembly, component supply, skills and maintenance can deepen the aerospace ecosystem alongside HAL's established helicopter programmes. Private industrial collaboration is not automatically a public–private partnership concession; Airbus is a European multinational, so “French-only company” is an oversimplification.

## 17 February 2026: assembly line inaugurated
- The **Tata Advanced Systems–Airbus H125 final assembly line at Vemagal, Kolar, Karnataka** was inaugurated virtually by the Indian and French leaders. This advances the October 2025 site announcement to an inaugurated facility; it does not mean every planned helicopter has been delivered.
- The private-sector facility covers manufacturing, integration, testing and maintenance/lifecycle support. **H125 is the civil platform; H125M production remains potential**, rather than an Indian military procurement order. First delivery was still expected in early 2027 in the issue. [Airbus inauguration announcement](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-tata-advanced-systems-inaugurates-airbus-h125-helicopter-production-line-to-boost-indias-vertical).
- Final assembly integrates components and systems, followed by testing and delivery. Assembly in India can build supplier and maintenance skills; it does not establish that every component or technology is indigenous.
