Trachoma elimination and avoidable blindness

Editable notes

WHO milestone and disease

  • WHO validated India's elimination of trachoma as a public-health problem on 8 October 2024, the third South-East Asia Region country after Nepal and Myanmar. The May 2025 issue revisits this achievement; its “2023 elimination” table should not replace the official validation date. WHO announcement.
  • Chlamydia trachomatis bacterial eye infection; spreads through infected eye/nasal discharge, hands, shared materials and eye-seeking flies. Repeated infection → eyelid scarring → inward-turning lashes (trichiasis) → corneal injury/blindness.
  • SAFE: Surgery for trichiasis; Antibiotics against infection; Facial cleanliness; Environmental improvement including water/sanitation. NPCBVI, a centrally sponsored programme, supports avoidable-blindness reduction through eye care/screening and surgery; cataract surgery itself is not treatment for trachoma infection.

Exact elimination criteria

  • WHO thresholds: trichiasis unknown to the health system <0.2% in adults ≥15 years, follicular inflammation <5% in children 1–9 years in every formerly endemic district, sustained for at least two years without ongoing antibiotic mass treatment, plus a funded system able to detect/manage incident trichiasis. Not merely “blindness below 1%”. WHO criteria.
  • Source historical blindness shares 5% in 1971 → below 1% describe a burden trend with unclear denominators, not the validation test. Maintain surveillance and accessible eye/WASH services after elimination.

PYQ disease distinctions

  • 2014: smallpox alone (b) among diphtheria, chickenpox and smallpox. Smallpox (Variola virus) globally eradicated in 1980; last naturally acquired case Somalia, 1977. Diphtheria is bacterial (Corynebacterium diphtheriae), controlled through vaccination including DPT; chickenpox is varicella-zoster viral infection, not eradicated. Varicella vaccine availability differs from inclusion in India's UIP.
  • 2001 athlete's foot: fungus (b); tinea pedis, commonly dermatophytes such as Trichophyton, causes itching/peeling/cracks and spreads through contaminated surfaces/materials. Moisture facilitates growth; hygiene/dry feet and appropriate antifungals matter.
  • Eradication means permanent worldwide reduction to zero incidence; elimination of transmission concerns a defined geographical area; elimination as a public-health problem meets disease-specific thresholds. Endemic means continuing occurrence, not necessarily controlled disease.
