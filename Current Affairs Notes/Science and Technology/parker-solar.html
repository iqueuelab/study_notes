Parker Solar Probe and solar storms

Editable notes

Mission and corrected timeline

  • NASA probe launched 12 August 2018, Delta IV Heavy; studies the corona and solar wind. Venus flybys reduce heliocentric orbital angular momentum, enabling close perihelia.
  • 24 December 2024 record approach: about 6.1 million km from the Sun's surface. 22 March 2025, 23rd close approach, matched this distance and roughly 692,000 km/h speed—not March 2024 as printed. NASA March update.
  • Carbon-composite heat shield about 4.5 inches thick; low-density coronal plasma has high temperature but transfers far less heat than dense matter at the same temperature.

Four instrument suites

SuiteMeasures
FIELDSElectric and magnetic fields
IS☉ISEnergetic particles
SWEAPSolar-wind electrons, alpha particles and protons
WISPRImages of corona/solar wind
  • Faraday/Solar Probe Cup is part of SWEAP, not a fifth suite. NASA instruments.
  • Research: coronal heating, Alfvén waves, magnetic switchbacks, wind acceleration and near-Sun dust depletion. These are active investigations, not proof every heating question is solved; dust sublimation is important near the Sun.

Space weather

  • Flare: electromagnetic radiation release; CME: large plasma/magnetic-field eruption. They can occur together but are not identical or inseparable.
  • Flare classes A, B, C, M, X classify soft-X-ray flux in decade bands; numeric suffixes specify strength within a class.
  • Solar storms can disrupt GPS/HF radio, affect satellite orbits, damage grids and cause widespread auroras; they do not cause equatorial tsunamis or global forest fires simply by arriving.
  • Aditya-L1 observes from around Sun–Earth L1 (~1.5 million km from Earth); complements close in-situ Parker observations.
