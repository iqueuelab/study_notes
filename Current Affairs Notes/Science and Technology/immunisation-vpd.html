Science and Technology

Immunisation gaps and vaccine-preventable disease resurgence

Editable notes

4 May 2025: warning and causes

  • WHO, UNICEF and Gavi, 24 April 2025: misinformation, population growth, conflict/migration, pandemic disruption and donor cuts threaten vaccination. WHO’s stocktake covered 108 country offices, mostly poorer countries; nearly half reported moderate/severe vaccination/supply disruptions and over half surveillance disruption. It was a rapid stocktake, not a census of every health system. WHO warning.
  • 2023: 14.5 million zero-dose children, versus 12.9 million in 2019. In monitoring, zero-dose is operationally measured through missing the first DTP-containing dose; do not interpret it as verified absence of every conceivable vaccine. Fragile/conflict-affected settings account for a large share of missed children.
  • Resurgent measles, meningitis and yellow fever illustrate immunity gaps. Source reports 935 US measles cases as of 1 May 2025; retain as its dated snapshot, not a current total. US measles elimination in 2000 meant interruption of endemic transmission, not impossibility of imported cases/outbreaks.

Value and India

  • Source cites 154 million lives saved over 50 years, about 40% of infant-survival improvement, and roughly 60% of saved lives attributable to measles vaccination. Its US$54 return per US$1 invested is an estimated economic benefit, not cash paid back to each person.
  • UIP is a major programme: source-era annual planning scale 26 million newborns, 34 million pregnant women, over 13 million sessions. Polio-free regional certification 2014; maternal/neonatal tetanus elimination 2015. Elimination is not eradication of the pathogen or a reason to discontinue vaccination.
  • Measles-rubella, pneumococcal and rotavirus vaccination expand protection. NFHS-5 (2019–21): 76.1% full immunisation among children aged 12–23 months under that survey definition; do not apply it to all ages or equate it with current administrative coverage.
  • Rural/urban, income, migration and last-mile access gaps coexist with achievements; supply, cold chain, trained staff and trust all matter.

Response and planned financing

  • Immunisation Agenda 2030, catch-up/Big Catch-Up campaigns, resilient supply/surveillance, sustained budgets and community engagement; rebut misinformation with trusted local health workers and clear communication.
  • Gavi’s 2025 replenishment ambition: at least US$9 billion, to vaccinate 500 million additional children and avert over eight million future deaths in 2026–30. These are financing/programme targets, not lives already saved. Gavi investment plan.
  • U-WIN, rather than CoWIN as the routine-vaccination programme platform, digitises UIP records for pregnant women and children. Digital reminders/records complement outreach; avoid excluding people without devices. Government U-WIN account.
  • Prioritise catch-up for missed children, maternal health coordination, secure vaccine supply and outbreak detection. Not every vaccine-preventable outbreak becomes a pandemic.
