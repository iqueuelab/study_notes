Science and Technology

Foot-and-mouth disease

Editable notes

January 2025 outbreak

  • Water buffalo near Berlin, Brandenburg, Germany; first German occurrence since 1988, officially notified 10 January 2025, not an unspecified exactly 40-year interval. WOAH statement.

Pathogen, hosts and spread

  • Highly contagious Aphthovirus, Picornaviridae, livestock disease; cattle, pigs, sheep, goats and other susceptible cloven-hoofed animals. Horses/dogs/cats are not usual susceptible hosts.
  • Seven serotypes: O, A, C, SAT1, SAT2, SAT3, Asia1; immunity/vaccines are not universally interchangeable across serotypes or all strains.
  • Animal secretions/excretions, aerosols, direct contact and contaminated equipment/clothing; people can mechanically carry virus without having the livestock disease.
  • Fever, mouth/tongue/lip/teat/interdigital blisters → lameness, salivation, poor intake; weight loss, growth retardation and persistent milk-production loss. Adult deaths generally low; young animals may die from severe disease/myocarditis.
  • Transboundary disease: trade restrictions and major production losses. Source 77% of world livestock refers to population in areas where disease circulates, not a measured 77% simultaneously infected.

Prevention and traps

  • Matched vaccines, surveillance/rapid notification, biosecurity, movement controls, outbreak response and appropriate official disease-status procedures; first disease for which WOAH established official status recognition.
  • Livestock FMD ≠ human hand-foot-and-mouth disease; not ordinarily a human-health/food-safety threat. WOAH disease information.
  • Intensive husbandry can amplify spread; traditional breeds are not automatically immune. Graphic Futvac is a vaccine example, not a universal vaccine name for all serotypes.

Precision trap

  • Seven serotypes does not mean only seven viral strains: multiple strains/subtypes occur within serotypes.
