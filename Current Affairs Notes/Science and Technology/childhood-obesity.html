Childhood obesity: World Obesity Atlas and prevention

Editable notes

World Obesity Atlas 2026

  • World Obesity Federation, ahead of World Obesity Day, 4 March: modelled 2025 estimates place India second by absolute number, after China, with roughly 41 million aged 5–19 living with high BMI, including 14 million with obesity. This is not the world's second-highest prevalence rate.
  • The source's ~15 million aged 5–9 plus ~26 million aged 10–19 concern overweight or obesity/high BMI. Calling the entire younger group obese contradicts the smaller all-age obesity total. Source comparison: China 62m high BMI/33m obesity, US 27m/13m; obesity is a subset, not an additional group.
  • Global high-BMI estimate 419m/20.7% in 2025, compared with source 14.6% in 2010, projected 507m/26.4% in 2040; projections depend on assumptions. Reported India high-BMI-associated disease indicators 2025→2040: high BP 2.99→4.21m, high triglycerides 4.39→6.07m, MASLD 8.39→11.88m. They are not total lifetime disease incidence. Federation Atlas. The article's 74% insufficient adolescent activity, 32.6% suboptimal breastfeeding and 35.5% school-meal coverage concern different age groups/baselines; they are not obesity-incidence rates.
  • Prevention: healthier school meals, accessible activity, marketing safeguards, sugary-drink taxes and non-stigmatising care. The source's halving childhood obesity by 2030 misstates the international goal to halt the rise, not halve prevalence. WHO NCD targets.
