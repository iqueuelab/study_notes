# Rocket propellants and ISRO’s semi-cryogenic engine

## Three propulsion categories
| Type | Typical propellant anchor | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Earth-storable liquid | Vikas: UH25 fuel + nitrogen tetroxide oxidiser | Easier long storage, but toxic/corrosive handling |
| Semi-cryogenic | Liquid oxygen (LOX) + kerosene | Dense fuel and high thrust; LOX still needs cryogenic handling |
| Cryogenic | LOX + liquid hydrogen (LH₂) | High specific impulse; very cold storage and bulky low-density hydrogen |
- Approximate normal boiling points: **LOX −183°C; LH₂ −253°C**. Fuel and oxidiser are different; rocket engines carry oxidiser because space provides no atmospheric oxygen.
- LOX–kerosene need not have higher specific impulse than LOX–hydrogen. Density, thrust, tank size, handling and stage role create engineering trade-offs.
## March 28, 2025 milestone
- LPSC's **SE2000, 2,000 kN-class** engine will power proposed **SC120**, replacing LVM3's **L110 liquid core**; LVM3's solid S200 boosters and cryogenic upper stage are different components.
- **Mahendragiri**: Power Head Test Article hot-fired for **2.5 seconds**, validating ignition/bootstrap operation and integrated pre-burner, turbopump, start/control subsystems. This was an intermediate test, **not a complete engine's flight qualification**. [ISRO](https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO_achieves_breakthrough_in_Semicryo_engine_development_Mar_2025_Final.html).
- Repeated tests, full engine/stage qualification and flight integration remain distinct milestones. Payload enhancement is an intended result; a development announcement does not mean the upgraded stage already flew.
- Importance: heavy payloads, deep-space/human-spaceflight capacity, domestic propulsion and launch competitiveness; challenges include combustion stability, turbopumps and thermal control.

## August 2025 launch target
- ISRO chairman's source-period target: first LVM3 flight with **semi-cryogenic core in Q1 2027**; planned cryogenic upper-stage propellant increase **28→32 tonnes**. These are development/integration targets, not an already-flown configuration.
- Existing SE2000/SC120 notes establish **2,000 kN (~200-tonne-force)**, LOX–kerosene and replacement of **L110**; replacing a stage is not simply swapping one 80-tonne Vikas for a completed flight-qualified engine. Higher thrust/density and cost are design trade-offs, not proof higher specific impulse than LOX–hydrogen.
- Embedded cryogenic graphic's caption **'73.55 kN vacuum thrust'** conflicts with modern LVM3 CE20's roughly 200-kN class; do not transfer older cryogenic-engine specifications to CE20. **December 2014 experimental suborbital flight** differs from first orbital developmental flight in **2017**.
- Verified anchors: [ISRO LPSC propulsion FAQ](https://www.lpsc.gov.in/faq1.html), [ISRO launch history](https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO_EN/LVM3_Launchers.html).

## November 2025: CE20 bootstrap start
- **7 November 2025**, HAT facility, ISRO Propulsion Complex, Mahendragiri: **10-second vacuum test** demonstrated bootstrap build-up and steady operation. Multi-element igniters in thrust chamber and gas generator allowed turbopumps to start without the auxiliary stored-gas starting system. CE20 already had single-start qualification at **19–22 tonnes thrust** and Gaganyaan qualification.
- Avoiding a new start-up gas bottle for each restart can preserve payload and enable future multi-orbit flexibility. This ground demonstration is a milestone toward multiple in-flight restarts, not proof that a restartable configuration has already flown. [ISRO, 19 November](https://new1.isro.gov.in/Boot_Strap_Mode_CE20_Cryogenic_Engine.html).
- CE20 uses **LH₂ fuel + LOX oxidiser** in LVM3's cryogenic upper stage. Do not confuse this with SE2000/SC120 semi-cryogenic core development. Vacuum testing simulates low ambient pressure; it is not evidence the facility reproduces every thermal condition of space.
- Practice revision: bootstrap = build-up without external start-up assistance; CE20 launch vehicle = **LVM3**. The two MCQs have no CSE year and are not PYQs.
