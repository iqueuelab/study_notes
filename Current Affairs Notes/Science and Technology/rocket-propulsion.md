# Rocket propellants and ISRO’s semi-cryogenic engine

## Three propulsion categories
| Type | Typical propellant anchor | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Earth-storable liquid | Vikas: UH25 fuel + nitrogen tetroxide oxidiser | Easier long storage, but toxic/corrosive handling |
| Semi-cryogenic | Liquid oxygen (LOX) + kerosene | Dense fuel and high thrust; LOX still needs cryogenic handling |
| Cryogenic | LOX + liquid hydrogen (LH₂) | High specific impulse; very cold storage and bulky low-density hydrogen |
- Approximate normal boiling points: **LOX −183°C; LH₂ −253°C**. Fuel and oxidiser are different; rocket engines carry oxidiser because space provides no atmospheric oxygen.
- LOX–kerosene need not have higher specific impulse than LOX–hydrogen. Density, thrust, tank size, handling and stage role create engineering trade-offs.
## March 28, 2025 milestone
- LPSC's **SE2000, 2,000 kN-class** engine will power proposed **SC120**, replacing LVM3's **L110 liquid core**; LVM3's solid S200 boosters and cryogenic upper stage are different components.
- **Mahendragiri**: Power Head Test Article hot-fired for **2.5 seconds**, validating ignition/bootstrap operation and integrated pre-burner, turbopump, start/control subsystems. This was an intermediate test, **not a complete engine's flight qualification**. [ISRO](https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO_achieves_breakthrough_in_Semicryo_engine_development_Mar_2025_Final.html).
- Repeated tests, full engine/stage qualification and flight integration remain distinct milestones. Payload enhancement is an intended result; a development announcement does not mean the upgraded stage already flew.
- Importance: heavy payloads, deep-space/human-spaceflight capacity, domestic propulsion and launch competitiveness; challenges include combustion stability, turbopumps and thermal control.
