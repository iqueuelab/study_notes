# Spinal muscular atrophy and prenatal research

## Mechanism and care
- Common 5qSMA: **autosomal recessive SMN1 defect on chromosome 5**→low survival motor neuron protein→motor-neuron loss→progressive weakness, often proximal, with respiratory/nutritional complications.
- SMN2 copy number can modify severity; types 0–4 reflect age/severity patterns rather than completely separate diseases.
- Disease-modifying treatments include nusinersen, risdiplam and onasemnogene abeparvovec; physiotherapy is not the only treatment. Respiratory, nutritional and orthopaedic support remain important.
- February 2025 prenatal risdiplam report concerned **one case**, not an established universal prenatal cure. [NEJM report](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMc2300802).
- Research requires informed consent, maternal/fetal safety monitoring and long-term follow-up; access and costs raise equity concerns.

## June 20 revision
- Source estimates SMA at roughly **1 in 10,000 births**. Its prenatal-treatment story repeats the single-case research report; it does not establish a prenatal cure.
- **PYQ 2021:** mitochondrial replacement can be performed before fertilisation (maternal spindle transfer) or after fertilisation (pronuclear transfer); mitochondrial inheritance is maternal. Both numbered statements are correct. This mitochondrial mechanism differs from SMN1-related SMA.
