# ExoMiner++ and AI-assisted exoplanet classification

## January 2026 NASA update
- **ExoMiner++**, an open-source deep-learning system from **NASA Ames**, classifies possible planetary **transits** in Kepler/TESS observations. A planet crossing its star can cause a small brightness dip, but eclipsing binaries/background sources can imitate that signal.
- It combines compatible **Kepler and TESS** data despite different observing strategies; interpretable diagnostics and prediction scores help researchers examine classifications. **AI confidence is not physical confirmation**.
- Correct the version/timeline: original **ExoMiner** work validated **370 Kepler planets** across its earlier work; the updated system's initial TESS run flagged **about 7,000 candidate targets**, requiring follow-up. These are not 7,000 confirmed planets or all new validations by ExoMiner++ itself. [NASA, 22 January](https://science.nasa.gov/open-science/deep-learning-exoplanets-tess/).
- Open code/data permit replication and catalogue vetting. Future improvements include finding transit signals directly in raw data and supporting later surveys such as **Roman**; these remain development prospects. The image-only **SSC GD** definition question is outside CSE PYQs.
