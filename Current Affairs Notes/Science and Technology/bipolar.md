# Bipolar disorder: episodes, recognition and inclusion

## Distinct episodes
- Bipolar disorder involves episodes of marked mood/energy/activity change, beyond ordinary moodiness. **Mania** may involve high/irritable mood, reduced sleep, racing thoughts and risky behaviour; depression involves low mood, loss of interest and impaired functioning.
- **Bipolar I**: at least one manic episode; depression often occurs. **Bipolar II**: hypomania plus major depression, with no manic episode. Cyclothymia involves less severe recurring symptoms. [NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/bipolar-disorder).
- Genetics are polygenic and interact with environment; a heritability estimate describes population variation, not a percentage of an individual's illness “caused by genes”.
- Stress/HPA-axis and circadian disruption are associated with illness; direction of causation can be uncertain, not one proven universal mechanism.
## Public-health relevance
- Initial depression and overlooked hypomania can delay diagnosis. Clinical assessment considers lifetime episodes, family history, substances and medical alternatives; family history alone does not diagnose the condition.
- Appropriate ongoing treatment, psychosocial support, sleep/routine management and reduced stigma can support productive lives. This is revision content, not a self-diagnosis checklist.
- **World Bipolar Day: 30 March**. Awareness and accessible mental healthcare matter; an ongoing survey is not already a published national prevalence result.
