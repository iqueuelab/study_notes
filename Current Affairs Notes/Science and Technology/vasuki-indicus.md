# Vasuki indicus: giant Eocene snake

## Fossil and significance
- IIT Roorkee researchers described **Vasuki indicus** in **Scientific Reports, April 2024**; the April 2025 issue revisits that discovery. Fossils were collected in **2005** from the Panandhro lignite mine, Kutch, Gujarat.
- **27 vertebrae**, middle Eocene, approximately **47 million years old**; belongs to extinct **Madtsoiidae**. Vertebral measurements give estimated length **10.9–15.2 m**, with uncertainty across estimation methods; comparable to Colombia's Titanoboa rather than a conclusively measured longest snake.
- Robust vertebrae suggest a large, slow terrestrial ambush predator, probably using constriction. Turtles, crocodilians, catfish and primitive whales occurred in associated deposits; actual prey is uncertain.
- Warm Eocene environments and ectothermy help interpret reptile gigantism. Fossils also illuminate snake evolution, Indian-plate biogeography and dispersal; they do not predict imminent giant snakes under modern warming.
- Name honours the mythological serpent Vasuki; cultural naming is separate from scientific identification. Among living snakes, reticulated pythons are longest; green anacondas are especially heavy.
