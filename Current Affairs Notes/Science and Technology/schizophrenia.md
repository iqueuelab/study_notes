# Schizophrenia: symptoms, recovery and physical health

## January 2026: brain–bone genetic overlap
- The clipping reports a large **genome-wide association analysis** of schizophrenia and bone-health traits in over half a million people. The research identified shared genetic signals, including **195 loci mapped to 1,376 protein-coding genes**. Shared associations suggest overlapping biology; they do not show every patient develops osteoporosis or that schizophrenia directly causes bone loss. [Researchers' January announcement](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1111350).
- Medication effects, nutrition, vitamin-D status, physical activity, hormonal changes and falls can also affect fracture risk. Integrated care should assess physical as well as mental health; genetic findings do not justify stopping prescribed treatment or supply a ready diagnostic test.
## Clinical and social recall
- A **psychotic disorder** affecting perception, thought, emotion, behaviour and functioning; **not split personality**. Symptoms may include hallucinations, delusions, disorganised speech/thought, reduced motivation/emotional expression and cognitive difficulties. Onset often occurs in late adolescence/early adulthood; genes, environment and psychosocial factors interact.
- Effective support combines medicines when indicated, psychological/family interventions, rehabilitation, education/employment support and community care. Course and recovery vary: reject the source's universal claims that no recovery is possible and everyone necessarily requires the same lifelong regimen. Stigma and an assumption of inevitable violence impede care. [WHO clinical overview](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/schizophrenia).
- The supplied **MP exam** question classifies schizophrenia as psychotic; it is outside the UPSC CSE bank. The illness has biological and physical-health dimensions, so “not a physical disorder” should not be used to deny medical care.
