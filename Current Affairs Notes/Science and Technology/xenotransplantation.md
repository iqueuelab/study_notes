# Xenotransplantation: pig organs, safety and ethics

## Definition and milestones
- Transplantation/implantation/infusion of living animal cells, tissues or organs into humans; also includes relevant ex-vivo exposure procedures. Distinct from human-to-human allotransplantation.
- **2021** pig-kidney work in a brain-dead recipient; **2022** gene-edited pig-heart transplant in a living patient; **16 March 2024, Massachusetts General Hospital**: first genetically edited pig kidney transplanted into a living human. It was not a new first in March 2025. [Hospital](https://www.massgeneral.org/news/kidney-xenotransplant-faqs?cid=sur4433i).
- Baby Fae's **1984 baboon-heart** case was an earlier landmark, not the first-ever xenotransplant attempt in history.
- Pigs offer comparatively suitable organ size/physiology and breeding scale; genetic changes aim to reduce immune incompatibility. A transplant's technical completion differs from long-term success.
## Benefits and safeguards
- Potential to relieve donor shortages, but rejection, immunosuppression effects, cross-species infection (including concern over porcine endogenous retroviruses) and uncertain durability require follow-up.
- Informed consent, transparent trials, biosafety surveillance, animal welfare, affordability and fair allocation are central.
- Religious/cultural preferences differ among individuals; a dietary pork prohibition does not establish universal rejection of a medically necessary transplant.
- Waiting-list numbers from one country cannot be relabelled as the global total. Experimental promise does not mean routine organs are universally available.
