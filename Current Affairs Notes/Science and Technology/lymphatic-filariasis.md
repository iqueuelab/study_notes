# Lymphatic filariasis: transmission and elimination

## February 2026 mass drug administration
- **10 February 2026:** Health Minister J. P. Nadda launched annual MDA across **124 districts/719 blocks in 12 endemic States**, with **2027 elimination as a public-health problem** the national goal. Starting 2026, the programme shifted to a **unified annual campaign**, replacing earlier February/August rounds. Targets and provisionally identified areas are not proof elimination has been achieved. [Health Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2225910&lang=2&reg=48).
- A neglected tropical disease caused by nematodes **Wuchereria bancrofti (~90% of global cases), Brugia malayi and Brugia timori**. Adult worms inhabit lymph vessels; asymptomatic infection can damage them, while lymphoedema/elephantiasis and hydrocele can produce disability, stigma and economic loss.
- Mosquitoes transmit infective larvae. **Culex** is important, but global vectors also include **Anopheles/Aedes**, with **Mansonia** relevant to some Brugia transmission. It is not exclusively Culex worldwide. [WHO factsheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/lymphatic-filariasis).
- MDA treats **eligible at-risk populations**, including people without symptoms, to reduce microfilariae and interrupt transmission. **DEC + albendazole**, with **ivermectin** in IDA settings, follow programme eligibility/exclusions; “whole community” does not mean every age, pregnancy or clinical group takes identical drugs. Vector control, hygiene/limb care, hydrocele surgery and surveillance complement drug coverage.
- **2001 PYQ (b), fungus** causes Athlete's Foot (tinea pedis), distinct from filarial roundworms; already banked.
