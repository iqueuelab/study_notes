Nickel ores and hydrogen-plasma extraction

Editable notes

8 June 2025 research development

  • Max Planck Institute for Sustainable Materials, Germany: hydrogen-plasma reactor combines smelting, reduction and refining of low-grade nickel ore; electrically excited hydrogen removes oxygen, producing water rather than carbon-reduction CO₂.
  • Correction: product is a refined ferronickel alloy, not necessarily pure nickel. Direct reactor chemistry avoids carbon reduction, while modelled life-cycle benefit ~84% less CO₂/up to 18% less energy depends on green hydrogen and renewable electricity; not zero supply-chain emissions. Research institution.
  • Conventional laterite routes are energy-intensive; source ~20 tonnes CO₂/tonne nickel, projected >6 million tonnes demand by 2040 depend on process/scenario. Laboratory demonstration ≠ commercial plant: scale-up, melt mixing/interface access, current/arc design, hydrogen cost/safety, power and capital are unresolved.

Nickel, ores and use

  • Ni, atomic number 28; silvery metal, ductile/malleable and useful corrosion-resistant alloying material. Sulphide ores and weathered laterites require different processing; “never native in nature” is too absolute given meteoritic/native occurrences.
  • Garnierite is a green nickel-rich assemblage of hydrous silicates, including serpentine/talc-like minerals, not one fixed mineral. New Caledonia, Indonesia and Philippines laterites are important. Generic source 1–4% Ni is an ore-grade guide, not a universal garnierite composition.
  • Stainless steel is the largest use; also selected EV cathodes, NiMH batteries, aerospace/turbine alloys, plating, electronics and magnets. Not all lithium-ion batteries contain nickel; LFP is a notable exception.
  • Sukinda, Odisha: nickel in chromite-overburden/lateritic limonite; Jharkhand and other occurrences need deposit-specific evidence. Source 92% Odisha concerns resource concentration, not proof 92% of mined production. Resources, reserves, output and refining shares differ. IBM nickel inventory.
  • Source 2022 Indonesia/Philippines/Russia production list, 58.2% combined share, China 50.4% consumption/75% Indonesian refining control need dated denominators; not permanent rankings. Chinese-linked investment/oversupply can affect prices and ownership; assess supply concentration alongside domestic processing, recycling and environmental safeguards.
  • Industrial dust/emissions, mining and waste exposure can cause dermatitis and respiratory disease; certain nickel compounds are human carcinogens, not identical risks for every form/dose. Protect workers and control air/water/soil releases.

PYQ corrections

  • 2025 Botswana–diamond, Chile–lithium, Indonesia–nickel: all three (c). Lithium Triangle = Chile/Argentina/Bolivia; resource abundance is not guaranteed extractable reserves or world-market dominance.
  • 2010 mineral pairs: nickel–Orissa alone (b). The question claims 90% natural sources, so simply finding some copper in Jharkhand does not make that pair true; source's answer (a) is wrong.
  • 2025 battery cathodes: cobalt, lithium and nickel (c), only three; graphite generally anode. These are component possibilities across usual chemistries, not proof every EV cathode uses all three.
