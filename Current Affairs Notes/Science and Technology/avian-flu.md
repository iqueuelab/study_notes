# Avian influenza H5N1 and One Health surveillance

## Virus and spread
- **Influenza A**, family **Orthomyxoviridae**; H and N refer to **haemagglutinin / neuraminidase**. Influenza A has **18 H /11 N** identified subtypes; **H5N1** is a subtype, not the only avian influenza virus.
- H5N1 lineage first identified in **Guangdong, 1996**; highly pathogenic **clade 2.3.4.4b** spread widely among wild birds/poultry and reached mammals. Europe 2020, North America 2021, South America 2022 and Antarctic-region detections in 2024 illustrate dated spread.
- Human infections generally follow exposure to infected animals/contaminated environments; 2024 US dairy-cattle outbreaks underline the need for cross-species monitoring.
- **No sustained human-to-human transmission reported** in the relevant period; adaptation requires surveillance rather than assuming an inevitable pandemic. [WHO risk assessment](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/influenza/avian-and-other-zoonotic-influenza/2024_07_18_fao-woah-who_h5n1_assessment.pdf?sfvrsn=a3a2b2b_4).
## Exam traps and response
- Source **~60% fatality** describes a selected historical set of confirmed cases; not fatality among every infection or a prediction for a future pandemic.
- Wild-bird migration, poultry production/trade and exposure interact; climate change can alter ecology but is not a demonstrated sole cause of every outbreak.
- **One Health:** animal/wildlife surveillance + human clinical monitoring + environmental information; biosecurity, laboratory sequencing, timely reporting, worker protection and safe outbreak control.
- Coordinate public-health/veterinary agencies without unsupported blanket claims about all poultry products or ordinary person-to-person contagion.
