# K. Kasturirangan: space science and education policy

## April 2025: scientist and institution builder
- **K. Kasturirangan (1940–2025)** died **25 April 2025**, aged 84; the PDF heading's **1940–2024 is incorrect**. [ICTS remembrance](https://icts.res.in/news/k-kasturirangan-1940-2025).
- ISRO Chairman **1994–2003**, following U.R. Rao and preceding G. Madhavan Nair; also chaired the Space Commission and served as Department of Space Secretary.
- Project Director, **Bhaskara I (1979) and II (1981)** experimental Earth-observation satellites; overall direction of **IRS-1A (1988)**, India's first operational remote-sensing satellite. These missions supported resource mapping, agriculture, water management and urban planning.
- His tenure saw PSLV operationalisation and GSLV's first successful flight testing; do not assume GSLV development began only during his chairmanship. Degrees in physics from Bombay University; doctorate in experimental high-energy astronomy in **1971**, working at PRL, Ahmedabad. Padma Shri, Padma Bhushan and Padma Vibhushan recipient. [ISRO biography](https://www.isro.gov.in/kasthurirangan.html).
- Nominated Rajya Sabha member and Planning Commission member; chaired the **2017–2019 draft education-policy committee**, contributing to **NEP 2020**. Recall multidisciplinary education, research support and home/mother/local-language instruction wherever possible through at least Grade 5; these are policy directions, not proof of universal implementation.
## Launch-vehicle comparison in the page graphic
| Vehicle | Main design and purpose |
|---|---|
| PSLV — Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle | Four stages: solid/liquid/solid/liquid; widely used for Earth-observation and other missions, including Chandrayaan-1 and Mars Orbiter Mission launches |
| GSLV — Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle | Three-stage vehicle with cryogenic upper stage; used for geosynchronous transfer missions; distinguish GSLV Mk II from LVM3 |
| SSLV — Small Satellite Launch Vehicle | Three solid propulsion stages plus liquid Velocity Trimming Module; small-satellite launch on demand |
- Rockets place payloads in orbits; **GSLV is not itself a “fixed-orbit” satellite**, and geosynchronous orbital altitude is not its universal launch altitude. Payload capacity depends on variant and target orbit. Do not learn the graphic's GSLV “5,000 kg LEO” or SSLV “500–700 kg” as universal capacities.
- ISRO specifies SSLV capability of **up to 500 kg to a 500-km planar orbit**; **EOS-07**, not “CMS-01”, was carried on successful SSLV-D2. [ISRO SSLV-D2](https://www.isro.gov.in/SSLV_D2_Mission_successful.html).
