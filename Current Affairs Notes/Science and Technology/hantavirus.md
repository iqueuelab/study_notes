# Hantavirus: transmission and disease forms

## Revision facts
- Current family: **Hantaviridae**, order Bunyavirales; Bunyaviridae is an older classification.
- Infection commonly follows inhalation of material contaminated by rodent urine, droppings or saliva; contact and bites can also transmit infection.
- **HPS** predominates in the Americas and affects the lungs; **HFRS** predominates in Asia/Europe and involves haemorrhagic/renal illness. Fatality differs substantially by virus and setting.
- Person-to-person spread is uncommon, but **Andes virus is an exception**; “never transmitted between humans” is incorrect.
- HPS may progress from fever, muscle aches and gastrointestinal symptoms to severe respiratory illness. Management is clinical supportive care; severe cases may need respiratory or renal support.
- There is no US-licensed HPS vaccine or specific curative treatment. This narrower statement must not be converted into “no vaccine for any hantavirus disease anywhere”. [CDC overview](https://www.cdc.gov/hantavirus/hcp/clinical-overview/hps.html).
- Prevention centres on rodent exclusion and safe cleanup; avoid dry sweeping contaminated material. Risk follows exposure, not a universal age-based rule.
