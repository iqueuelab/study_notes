# Salar de Pajonales: gypsum and biosignatures

## Mars analogue and microbial refuge
- **Salar de Pajonales**, northern Chile at the Atacama–Altiplano boundary, is a high-altitude salt-flat system around **3.5 km above sea level**. Desiccation, strong ultraviolet radiation, salinity and episodic hydration make it a terrestrial analogue for some Martian environments; it does not replicate all Martian conditions.
- A **5 February 2026** study examined gypsum stromatolites/crusts near Flamencos Lagoon. Translucent gypsum can admit photosynthetically useful light while sheltering endolithic microbes from radiation and retaining moisture. Mineral structures preserve molecular/morphological traces of earlier life alongside living communities. **Stromatolites** are layered structures associated with microbial activity.
- This informs where Mars missions might search for **biosignatures** in sulfate deposits; it is evidence of life on Earth, not discovery of Martian life. [Original study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2025.1693302/full).
## Gypsum revision
- **Calcium sulfate dihydrate, CaSO₄·2H₂O**; anhydrite is CaSO₄. Common in evaporitic sedimentary deposits with halite/anhydrite; precipitation from evaporating saline waters is a principal process. Occurrence is not restricted to Permian/Triassic rocks.
- Uses include cement set control, gypsum plaster/plaster of Paris and soil amendment. In **sodic soils**, calcium helps replace exchangeable sodium; drainage/leaching remain necessary, so gypsum does not universally remove all soil salinity. Marine gypsum is recovered from salt pans including Gujarat/Tamil Nadu. Gypsum is not itself a source of lime in the same chemical sense as limestone.
