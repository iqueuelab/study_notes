# Remote sensing: vegetation, water and resource exploration

## Observation methods and what they infer
| Method | What it measures / key qualification |
|---|---|
| Optical multispectral/hyperspectral | Reflected energy in spectral bands; material signatures depend on surface conditions, sensor resolution and atmosphere |
| Active SAR | Emits microwaves and measures backscatter; useful day/night and through clouds; calm open water often dark, but wind, urban flooding and vegetation complicate classification |
| Satellite gravimetry | Changes in mass/gravity; separate from an optical spectral-signature image |
- **NDVI = (NIR − red)/(NIR + red)**. Chlorophyll absorbs red; **leaf internal structure strongly reflects NIR**, rather than chlorophyll deliberately reflecting it “to avoid overheating”. NDVI tracks greenness/density/stress proxies, not a unique diagnosis of disease or a direct universal carbon-stock measurement. [NASA explanation](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/Features/MeasuringVegetation/measuring_vegetation_2.php).
- Water mapping: common open-water **NDWI uses green/NIR**, **MNDWI green/SWIR**, helping distinguish water from some built surfaces; threshold signs are useful tendencies, not universal water/land rules. Sunlight-dependent optical methods face cloud/night limits; radar provides complementary flood observations. Spectral water-colour changes can indicate sediment/algae, requiring validation for pollution attribution.
- Hyperspectral narrow bands help map **exposed minerals, rock types and alteration zones**. Geological structures (anticlines/sedimentary basins) and possible hydrocarbon micro-seepage effects guide exploration; satellites do **not see buried gold/copper/lithium or oil reservoirs directly**, and vegetation stress has many causes. Confirm prospects with geology, geophysics, sampling/drilling and environmental assessment.
- **GRACE, 2002–2017**, inferred regional water-storage changes from inter-satellite distance/gravity variations. Groundwater estimates need soil-moisture, snow and surface-water information, not direct imaging of each aquifer. [NASA method](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-gravity-of-water/). Its **2009 northern-India depletion finding** linked substantial losses to groundwater consumption, especially irrigation; this is historical research, not a new January satellite measurement. [NASA 2009 account](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3623/).
- Applications: crop/forest monitoring, flood response, water management, exploration and climate/carbon estimation. Combine remote observations with field validation, uncertainty estimates and appropriate spatial/time scales.
