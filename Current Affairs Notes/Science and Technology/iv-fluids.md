# Intravenous fluids and patient safety

## What they are
- Sterile fluids delivered **into a vein** for clinically assessed resuscitation, replacement or maintenance; not automatically needed for every sick/dehydrated person.
| Type | Contents / examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Crystalloid** | Small dissolved solutes; **normal saline, D5W (5% dextrose), lactated Ringer's** |
| **Colloid** | Larger molecules, e.g. **albumin**, synthetic starch products such as hetastarch |
- Fluid composition varies: water, sodium/chloride and sometimes glucose, potassium, calcium/lactate; not every fluid contains every electrolyte. Colloids' distribution concerns **capillary permeability**, not simply inability to cross cell membranes.
- D5W and saline have different physiological effects; colloids are not universally superior. Synthetic starch fluids have important safety restrictions; no treatment/dose recommendation follows from this recall table.
## Safety and January 2025 incident
- Source reports **12 doctors suspended in West Bengal**, Medinipur maternal/newborn deaths and alleged faulty/expired IV fluids. Suspension/allegation does not itself establish final causation or responsibility.
- Source blanket “simple, safe, low risk” is incomplete: wrong volume/composition, contamination, line infection and fluid/electrolyte overload can cause harm. Multiple fluids at one site require compatibility/clinical checks.
- Prescription by trained personnel, sterile supply-chain quality, expiry/batch traceability, assessment/reassessment and monitoring; transparent adverse-event investigation. [NICE hospital guidance](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg174/chapter/recommendations).
