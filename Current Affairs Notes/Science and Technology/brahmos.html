BrahMos: supersonic cruise missile

Editable notes

System and distinctions

  • Brahmaputra + Moskva gives the name; DRDO–Russia's NPOM joint venture. Supersonic cruise missile, not a ballistic missile or hypersonic BrahMos-II.
  • Two stages: solid booster → liquid-fuel ramjet sustained flight; approximately Mach 2.8. Original ~290-km range differs from extended-range tested variants; specify variant instead of treating one range as universal.
  • Land/ship/air versions; air launch from modified Su-30MKI. Submarine version was tested from a submerged platform on 20 March 2013; demonstration is not proof operational fit on every Indian submarine. BrahMos test.
  • All-weather/day-night and fire-and-forget guidance; no continuous operator command does not mean no onboard guidance. Mission planning, sensors, target profile and platform configuration affect precision; avoid absolute “fastest in world” claims.
  • Tactical strike/deterrence, indigenous capability with international collaboration and defence-export diplomacy. The clipping's “missile triad” means land/sea/air launch modes, not India's nuclear triad.
  • Philippines first delivery, 19 April 2024, already preceded this issue: source incorrectly groups it with merely prospective exports. Vietnam discussions do not establish a signed/delivered sale. BrahMos delivery.

16 May 2025 : export interest

  • Source reports heightened interest following Operation Sindoor. Philippines: confirmed US$375 million contract for three coastal-defence batteries, with deliveries beginning in 2024. Indonesia/Vietnam/Malaysia and other listed Asian, Latin American, Gulf, African and European markets are reported discussions/interest, not signed/delivered sales.
  • DRDO–NPOM ownership 50.5%–49.5%; first test 12 June 2001. Source warhead 200–300 kg, speed Mach 2.8–3 and extended 500–800 km ranges are variant/configuration-dependent, not specifications of every exported missile. Submerged-platform tests do not establish operational submarine deployment.
  • Brah Mos-II is a hypersonic development ambition, distinct from existing supersonic Brah Mos. Export opportunities depend on approvals, financing, integration, training and support; commercial interest alone is not export revenue.

Practice wording correction: May 2025

  • Worksheet statement 1 is false: supersonic DRDO–Russia NPOM collaboration, not subsonic India–Israel. Statement 2, land/air/sea launches, is correct.
  • Statement 3's unqualified “range limited to 290 km due to MTCR” is ambiguous/stale: original variant about 290 km; extended-range variants existed before this issue. Source answer (b) 2 and 3 works only if explicitly referring to the original configuration; (a) 2 only fits an assertion that every present variant is still capped. Do not silently endorse the latter claim. Manufacturer extended-range test.

22 May 2025: export graphic caution

  • Repeated post-Sindoor export-interest table does not establish new contracts: source Indonesia $200–350 million, Vietnam $700 million, and Malaysia/Thailand/Singapore/Brunei, Brazil/Chile/Argentina/Venezuela, Egypt/Saudi Arabia/UAE/Qatar/Oman, South Africa/Bulgaria are reported prospects, not verified completed sales. Philippines contract/deliveries are separately established in existing notes.
  • Source height 15 km/10 m and warhead/range figures describe configurations/flight profiles, not universal parameters of every missile. Operational performance cannot be inferred solely from promotional specifications.
  • Graphic wrongly implies MTCR membership permits development above thresholds but categorically forbids exports. MTCR is an export-control arrangement, with a strong presumption of denial for qualifying Category I systems capable of delivering ≥500 kg to ≥300 km; it is not a universal domestic-development ban or automatic ban on every missile exceeding 300 km. Hypersonic Mach ≥5 is distinct from BrahMos's supersonic speed; BrahMos-II remains a development ambition.
  • Export-control details: US State Department MTCR FAQ.
