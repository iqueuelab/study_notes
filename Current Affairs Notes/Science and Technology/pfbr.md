# Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor and the three-stage nuclear programme

## PFBR at Kalpakkam
| Feature | Revision fact |
|---|---|
| Developer | BHAVINI, a DAE public-sector enterprise |
| Design output | 500 MWe; Tamil Nadu |
| Fuel | Uranium–plutonium mixed oxide (MOX) |
| Neutrons/coolant | Fast neutrons; liquid sodium; no neutron moderator |
| Breeding | Fertile U-238 blanket → fissile Pu-239 |
- **4 March 2024:** commencement of core loading witnessed by the Prime Minister. Core loading, first criticality, grid connection and commercial operation are separate milestones. The April 2025 article forecasts readiness in 2026; this is a forecast, not proof of commissioning. [PMO milestone](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2011347&lang=2&reg=48).
## Three-stage pathway
**Natural-uranium PHWR → recovered plutonium → fast breeders → eventual thorium/U-233 systems.**
1. PHWRs use heavy water as moderator/coolant and produce electricity and plutonium in spent fuel.
2. Fast breeders use plutonium-bearing fuel and breed further fissile material from U-238. Reprocessing/recycling links the stages.
3. Thorium-232 can breed U-233 for future thorium-based systems. AHWR is a development option; India does not already operate a commercial AHWR fleet. PFBR's present uranium blanket is not evidence of established commercial thorium operation.
## Significance and constraints
- Breeding improves utilisation of uranium resources and builds fissile inventory for long-term energy security. Indigenous design/manufacture supports technological capability and low-carbon electricity.
- Sodium transfers heat effectively without slowing neutrons substantially, but reacts with air/water: leak detection, separation of circuits and engineered safety matter.
- Delays, cost escalation, regulatory demonstration, fuel-cycle infrastructure, waste management and public confidence constrain deployment. Reprocessing recovers uranium/plutonium; radioactive waste still needs secure management. Avoid treating any design as accident-proof.

## July forecast and fuel-cycle distinctions
- July7 article again expects **PFBR commissioning in2026**; a revised forecast, not achieved commercial operation. It repeats **500MWe,Kalpakkam,BHAVINI,MOX,liquid sodium** and the three-stage pathway already corrected in April.
- Spent PHWR fuel must be **reprocessed** to recover plutonium for fast-reactor fuel; the PFBR does not simply burn intact PHWR spent-fuel bundles. Its initial uranium blanket breeds plutonium; future thorium/U-233 use is a pathway, not present commercial thorium operation.
- Sodium heat transfer and engineered/passive systems support safety, but sodium-air/water reactions, cooling and containment need controls. A single negative-temperature-coefficient slogan cannot establish every reactivity feedback is negative or every loss-of-power event safe.
- Closed-cycle recovery can improve fuel utilisation and alter waste inventories; **it does not eliminate radioactive waste or safeguards/security obligations**. Source's~25%world thorium share depends on resource estimates, not assured economic reserves.
