# Rowmari–Donduwa wetlands and Ramsar proposal

## November 2025: proposed designation in Assam
- Experts, forest officials and civil society sought Ramsar recognition for **Rowmari–Donduwa wetland complex**, **Laokhowa Wildlife Sanctuary, Nagaon, Assam**; proposal submitted to the Assam Forest Department for onward consideration. **Proposal is not designation**.
- Issue describes about **3 km²** of wetlands within **70.13 km² Laokhowa**, connected to the **Kaziranga–Orang–Burhachapori** landscape on the Brahmaputra floodplain. Flood storage, aquatic habitat, fisheries/reeds/grazing and wildlife connectivity underpin wise use; encroachment, polluted runoff, siltation, invasive water hyacinth and disrupted hydrology threaten it.
- Issue-reported census: **Rowmari 20,653 birds/75 species; Donduwa 26,480/88 species**, combined annual diversity around **120 species**. Its attribution to the **sixth census in 2024** is not independently resolved here; retain counts as source-reported observations, not a validated comparable ranking against all northeastern wetlands.
- Knob-billed duck, black-necked stork and ferruginous pochard are examples, but the source's blanket “all globally threatened” label is unreliable. Bird abundance/species diversity alone does not automatically grant Ramsar status; formal criteria, nomination and management apply.
- The issue's **82 Indian Ramsar sites** is stale against its own Gogabil report; **94** is the late-October 2025 snapshot. Its “only two northeastern sites” misses **Khecheopalri, Sikkim**. Existing Bihar wetland notes retain Convention dates/wise-use obligations. Ramsar listing does not guarantee tourism revenue or automatic international funding, and protected-area wetlands have overlapping legal regimes.
