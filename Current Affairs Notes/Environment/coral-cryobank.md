# Coral larvae cryobanking and the Coral Triangle

## October 2025: Philippines coral conservation
- The issue reports a Philippine coral-larvae cryobank, described as Southeast Asia's first. UP Marine Science Institute's project announcement confirms development of the country's first coral cryobank facility with Taiwanese expertise; retain the wider regional-first label as source-reported. [UP MSI](https://msi.upd.edu.ph/up-msi-to-establish-phs-first-coral-cryobank-facility-for-coral-restoration/).
- Cryoprotectants plus rapid cooling in liquid nitrogen (about −196°C) can vitrify larvae, avoiding damaging ice formation; rapid laser warming and recovery protocols seek viable larvae for research and reef restoration. Successful freezing is species/protocol dependent, not a guarantee every frozen larva survives or rebuilds a reef.
- Coral Triangle: Indonesia, Malaysia, Papua New Guinea, Philippines, Solomon Islands and Timor-Leste; exceptional coral/reef-fish diversity and coastal livelihoods. Warming/bleaching, acidification, destructive fishing, pollution and sedimentation threaten reefs. The source's 14% loss refers to 2009–2018; do not conflate it with the separate 70–90% projected decline at 1.5°C or assert an unsupported universal 2050 deadline.
- Genetic backup complements emissions reduction and habitat protection. Constraints: larvae collection/captive husbandry, lipid-rich cold-sensitive cells, symbionts, species-specific protocols, finance and restoration-site survival. Regional research collaboration includes Philippines, Taiwan, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand; these partners are not the same list as Coral Triangle countries.
