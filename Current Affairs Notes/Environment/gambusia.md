# Gambusia: mosquito biocontrol and invasive-species risks

## Larvivorous fish and mosquito control
- **Gambusia affinis** (western mosquitofish) and **G. holbrooki** (eastern mosquitofish) originate in North America; introduced elsewhere for mosquito-larva control. **Guppies (Poecilia reticulata; older name Lebistes)** are another larvivorous example.
- **2008 PYQ: (c) Gambusia fish**, among crab, dogfish, Gambusia and snail. This historical exam answer concerns biological control; larva consumption does not establish a universal daily rate or guaranteed reduction of every mosquito-borne disease.
- Introduced Gambusia can prey on/compete with native aquatic fauna. Avoid the source's blanket “eco-friendly/relatively safe” conclusion: control benefit depends on habitat and species, and non-native releases can harm biodiversity. [USGS species profile](https://nas.er.usgs.gov/queries/FactSheet.aspx?SPeciesID=849).
- The issue's **250–300 larvae/day** and “first documented in Italy, 1931” are not retained as established universal/historical facts without supporting evidence. Integrate vector-habitat management with public-health monitoring rather than treating fish release as a complete control programme.
