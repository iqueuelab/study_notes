# Avalanches: triggers and risk reduction

## Process
- Snow mass descends a slope when gravity exceeds stability; slab failure often combines a weak layer and loading from snowfall/wind, warming or rain.
- Types: slab, loose-snow, wet-snow and powder-snow avalanches; ice/rock avalanches are related mass movements, not all identical snow processes.
- Many snow avalanches occur on~30–45° slopes; terrain, snowpack and weather interact, so angle alone is not a prediction.
## Reduce exposure
- February 2025 Mana-region disaster highlights risks to mountain workers and routes. Map runout zones, monitor weather/snowpack, issue actionable warnings and close exposed work/routes when necessary.
- DGRE expertise, Army/ITBP/NDRF coordination, trained rescue and appropriate shelters/equipment support preparedness; remote sensing cannot guarantee exact event prediction.
- Combine disaster planning with changing mountain climate and infrastructure siting; do not treat all snowfall as equally dangerous.
