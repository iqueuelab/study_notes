# Biochar: carbon storage and productive uses

## Process → uses
- **Biomass → pyrolysis (limited/absent oxygen; source diagram 300–700°C) → carbon-rich biochar + bio-oil + combustible syngas.** Feedstock and process determine safety, yield and properties; mixed municipal waste requires segregation/contaminant control.
- Stable carbon can persist for centuries; porous biochar improves soil structure, water/nutrient retention and habitats for beneficial microbes. It can reduce some soil greenhouse-gas emissions, but effects vary with soil, crop, dose and char quality.
- Other uses: modified char for industrial CO₂ adsorption; wastewater pollutant adsorption; additives in construction materials; syngas for electricity and upgraded bio-oil for fuel substitution. These are potential applications, not universal performance guarantees.
## India: source scenario, not achieved output
- PDF estimates annual feedstock **600 Mt farm residues +60 Mt municipal waste**. Using **30–50%** could yield **15–26 Mt biochar**, roughly **0.1 Gt emission-removal potential**, and **8–13 TWh electricity (~0.5–0.7%)**; bio-oil could substitute some imports (source projects ~2% fossil-emission reduction). The emission quantity's carbon/CO₂ basis is unspecified; do not convert it into a verified national removal inventory.
- Illustrative source ranges: **10–25% crop-yield gains;10–20% less fertiliser;30–50% less N₂O**; concrete **2–5% char /115 kg CO₂ stored per m³**; **200–500 L wastewater/kg char**, **2.5–6.3 Mt char demand**, against **70 billion L/day wastewater**. All require study/process conditions, not guaranteed field/design rules. **N₂O GWP100≈273** is a mass-based climate metric, not general toxicity.
## Adoption priorities
- Obstacles: feedstock collection and quality, upfront cost, awareness, uncertain demand/business models, evolving technology, weak agriculture–energy–climate coordination and credible measurement/reporting/verification (MRV).
- Establish regional feedstock/process standards, field trials, residue/bioenergy and State climate-plan links, local production, farmer training and verified carbon finance. Source projects **5.2 lakh rural jobs**; this is an opportunity estimate.
- Source's blanket “no carbon-credit systems” claim is too broad: crediting depends on the scheme, approved methodology, additionality, permanence and lifecycle accounting. Avoid claiming every farmer automatically earns credits.
- **2020 PYQ: (d), all three**—vertical growing media, nitrogen-fixing microbial habitat and longer water retention. [USDA biochar/soil-carbon overview](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/conservation-basics/soil/soil-health/soil-carbon-amendments).
