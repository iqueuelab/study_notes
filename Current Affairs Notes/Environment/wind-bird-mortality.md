# Wind farms: bird mortality and biodiversity safeguards

## Thar evidence and a headline correction
- WII-led **2020–21 Jaisalmer study**, published June 2025: **3,000 km²**, about **900 turbines**, sample **90 turbines**, seven seasonal searches; **124 carcasses**, none in a single concurrent survey at **28 control sites**.
- Bias-adjusted estimate **1.24 ±0.18 birds/turbine/month**, about **14.9/year** or **4,464 birds/year/1,000 km²**. These are estimated rates, not 124 deaths every month.
- **Not the world's highest:** original paper cites **Belgium 19.1–34.3/turbine/year**, and calls Thar near the upper global range. Search-radius deaths combine turbines and nearby associated power lines; control comparison does not prove every death was blade collision. **No bustard–turbine collision was observed**; power lines are an established bustard threat. Raptors are especially vulnerable after adjusting for crossings. [Original study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-03407-8).
## Planning response
- Thar is a **Central Asian Flyway** wintering/conservation landscape; renewable projects can cause collisions, habitat loss/displacement and cumulative infrastructure pressures.
- Avoid sensitive sites; test painting part of one blade black, seasonal/detection-triggered shutdowns, and robust monitoring. **BirdLife International's AVISTEP** maps avian sensitivity; ground surveys remain necessary. [BirdLife tool](https://datazone.birdlife.org/tools/avistep).
- Issue advocates mandatory environmental assessment for onshore wind and stronger ecological surveys; do not infer exemption removes every forest/wildlife/coastal permission. Offshore expansion is not risk-free: construction noise, marine fauna, migration routes and submarine cabling require assessment. Source's **30-GW by 2030/4-GW bidding** are plans, not operating capacity.
