# Project Cheetah, Kuno and Gandhi Sagar

## Species and reintroduction

- **Acinonyx jubatus**: fastest land mammal, slender/spotted open-habitat predator; scrub, grassland and savanna, not only treeless desert.
- Cheetah declared extinct in India **1952**. African animals brought to **Kuno, MP: eight Namibia September 2022 +12 South Africa February 2023**; translocation of 20 founders is not evidence all survived.
- February source proposed more animals at **Gandhi Sagar Wildlife Sanctuary, MP**, around **368 km²**, with grassland/dry deciduous/riverine habitats. Prey availability requires assessment, not an automatic guarantee of viability.
- Global species **IUCN Vulnerable**; Asiatic population in Iran **Critically Endangered**; **CITES Appendix I /Indian Schedule I**, correcting source Schedule II. [Official schedule](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/schedulefile?aid=AC_CEN_16_18_00007_197253_1517807324579&rid=759).

## Risks and evaluation

- Habitat/prey limitations, disease, conflict, dispersal and adaptation; mortality causes need veterinary evidence rather than attributing every death to collars or starvation.
- Source **December 2024: 24 animals, 12 adults/12 cubs** is a dated snapshot, not the February/current total. Founder subspecies and historical Asiatic population differ.
- **Cheetah Conservation Fund, 1990, Namibia**; NTCA/WII monitoring and habitat/community work support India's programme. [Action-plan overview](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1850587&lang=2&reg=48).
- Judge success by long-term survival, breeding, genetic diversity, suitable habitat, community benefits and a self-sustaining population—not arrival ceremonies or short-term cub counts alone.
