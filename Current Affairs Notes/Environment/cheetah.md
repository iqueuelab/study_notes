# Project Cheetah, Kuno and Gandhi Sagar

## Species and reintroduction
- **Acinonyx jubatus**: fastest land mammal, slender/spotted open-habitat predator; scrub, grassland and savanna, not only treeless desert.
- Cheetah declared extinct in India **1952**. African animals brought to **Kuno, MP: eight Namibia September 2022 +12 South Africa February 2023**; translocation of 20 founders is not evidence all survived.
- February source proposed more animals at **Gandhi Sagar Wildlife Sanctuary, MP**, around **368 km²**, with grassland/dry deciduous/riverine habitats. Prey availability requires assessment, not an automatic guarantee of viability.
- Global species **IUCN Vulnerable**; Asiatic population in Iran **Critically Endangered**; **CITES Appendix I /Indian Schedule I**, correcting source Schedule II. [Official schedule](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/schedulefile?aid=AC_CEN_16_18_00007_197253_1517807324579&rid=759).
## Risks and evaluation
- Habitat/prey limitations, disease, conflict, dispersal and adaptation; mortality causes need veterinary evidence rather than attributing every death to collars or starvation.
- Source **December 2024: 24 animals, 12 adults/12 cubs** is a dated snapshot, not the February/current total. Founder subspecies and historical Asiatic population differ.
- **Cheetah Conservation Fund, 1990, Namibia**; NTCA/WII monitoring and habitat/community work support India's programme. [Action-plan overview](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1850587&lang=2&reg=48).
- Judge success by long-term survival, breeding, genetic diversity, suitable habitat, community benefits and a self-sustaining population—not arrival ceremonies or short-term cub counts alone.
## Gandhi Sagar development
- **20 April 2025:** Gandhi Sagar Sanctuary, Mandsaur district, Madhya Pradesh, received the two cheetahs **Prabhas and Pavak** transferred from Kuno; it became the programme's second Indian site. This transfer is distinct from proposed additional imports from Africa. [Akashvani announcement](https://newsonair.gov.in/two-cheetahs-to-be-released-in-mps-gandhisagar-sanctuary-today/).
- Grassland, dry deciduous forest and riverine patches offer a varied landscape. Assess prey density, enclosure/free-ranging transitions, veterinary capacity, connectivity and community impacts; listing chital, chinkara, nilgai or wild pig does not establish adequate prey quantitatively.
- Source repeat **Schedule II** is incorrect: cheetah is in Indian **Schedule I**. December 2024 survival counts remain dated snapshots; do not use them as April 2025 totals. Imported founders/cubs and future planned animals are separate categories.

## October 2025: Nauradehi announced as another site
- Madhya Pradesh's CM announced **Nauradehi** as the State's proposed third cheetah home after Kuno and Gandhi Sagar, with further animals expected from Namibia. This is an announcement, not evidence arrivals or a self-sustaining population had already occurred.
- Nauradehi's roughly **1,200 km²** dry-deciduous/grassland/scrub landscape in central MP is the source's sanctuary-scale description, not a measurement of the entire wider tiger-reserve complex. Prey assessment, veterinary capacity, dispersal monitoring and community participation remain essential.
- A managed **metapopulation** links breeding populations and genetic management across sites; multiple enclosures alone do not guarantee genetic viability. The issue's mortality count is an older, incompletely defined snapshot.
- Associated report: six crocodiles (four females, two males) from **Van Vihar, Bhopal**, released in **Indira Sagar backwaters, Narmada**. The typed explanation calls them “likely mugger”; do not convert that uncertain identification into a confirmed species record.

## 9 March announcement, reported 10 March 2026
- **Jwala, a Namibian female at Kuno**, gave birth to **five cubs**, taking the official source-period Indian total to **53**, including **33 thriving Indian-born cubs**. This was the **10th successful litter on Indian soil**, and Jwala's third. These are dated living-population/cub counts, not the cumulative number of all births or imported founders. [MoEFCC, 9 March](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2236900&lang=2&reg=48).
- The same release records **nine Botswana cheetahs (six females/three males)** placed in Kuno quarantine on **28 February**, undergoing acclimatisation/health monitoring before wider release. Thus the practice question's Namibia/South Africa pair describes the initial imports, not every supplying country by March2026.
- An embedded clipping reports NTCA viewing movements from Kuno toward Rajasthan as natural territorial dispersal. Dispersal requires landscape monitoring and inter-State coordination; it is not by itself proof of failure or proof of a self-sustaining wild population.
- Existing species/status notes apply. The source's African-versus-Asiatic table is simplified: **A. j. jubatus is a subspecies, not the name for every African cheetah**, and cheetahs do not occur throughout all Africa. Its **“12 Asiatic cheetahs left”** lacks a dated survey and should not replace a population assessment. Survival, reproduction, habitat/prey, genetics and community coexistence matter beyond the birth milestone.
