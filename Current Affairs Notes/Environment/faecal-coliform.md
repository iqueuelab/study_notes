# Faecal coliform and river-water quality

## Indicator, not a universal pathogen count

- Thermotolerant/faecal coliforms are used as indicators of possible faecal pollution; **E. coli** is an important subset. Not every detected organism is itself pathogenic, and a single indicator does not identify every virus/parasite.
- Sources: untreated sewage, animal waste, runoff and inadequate sanitation. Exposure can signal risk of enteric disease; organism concentration, exposure and other water parameters must be assessed together.
- Organic matter raises oxygen demand; nutrients can cause eutrophication. **A coliform count alone does not quantify BOD, dissolved-oxygen depletion or nutrient loading**.

## Standards and methods

- CPCB organised-bathing guidance: **500 desirable /2,500 maximum permissible faecal-coliform MPN per 100 mL**. PDF's unqualified 500 maximum and universal WHO 200 recreational limit are misleading. [CPCB restoration guidance](https://cpcb.nic.in/wqm/Ind-Guidelines-RestWaterBodies.pdf).
- Drinking-water **E. coli should not be detectable in 100 mL**; do not replace this with a vague standard for every total-coliform group in every context.
- **MPN:** statistical estimate from dilution/fermentation; **CFU:** cultured colony count; not interchangeable numerical units without the relevant method.
- Multiple-tube fermentation, membrane filtration and enzyme-substrate tests; use sampling protocols, repeat measurements and transparent laboratories.

## Control

- Sewage interception/STPs, reliable operation, safe sanitation, runoff management, riparian buffers and enforcement; publish accessible findings and address sources upstream.
- River-bathing management must assess multiple parameters and maintain infrastructure beyond festivals. Clear communication should avoid declaring water safe from one favourable metric alone.
