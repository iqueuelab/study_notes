# Aravalli safari proposal and ecosystem protection

## Proposal and ecology
- Proposed **10,000-acre** safari park in **Gurugram/Nuh, Haryana**; February source reports objections by retired forest officers. “World's largest” is a proposal claim, not completed achievement.
- Aravallis support groundwater recharge, habitat connectivity and ecological buffering in a heavily urbanising/water-stressed region. Traffic, construction and water-intensive attractions may undermine these functions.
- Captive-animal exhibits/tourism are not automatically equivalent to protecting native wildlife and functioning habitat.
## Geography and source corrections
- Ancient **Proterozoic fold belt/residual hills**, extending through **Gujarat, Rajasthan, Haryana and Delhi**; not formed by the recent India–Eurasia collision that built the Himalaya.
- Broadly hundreds of kilometres long; mountain range, geologically buried extension and visible hill belt are different measures. Do not treat source average-height figures as the maximum elevation.
- **Carbon dating measures suitable organic material**, not the age of copper metal itself; archaeological context can date mining activity.
## Legal and policy assessment
- Forest classification, diversion permissions, protected-area notifications, ESZ rules and applicable court orders require project-specific examination; neither every activity everywhere nor all areas within a universal 10 km strip share one blanket ban.
- **ESZ extent is site-specific** and can vary; protection extends to sensitive corridors where notified. Conservation/restoration priority needs environmental assessment and legal compliance.
- Compare low-impact community tourism with large built attractions; assess water budgets, cumulative impacts, native restoration, wildlife welfare and transparent local consultation.
- Employment benefits do not automatically outweigh ecological damage. Track actual forest condition, hydrology and livelihoods rather than an attraction's advertised scale.
## Aravalli geography and drainage additions
- NE–SW residual ancient fold belt across **Delhi, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat**, source length roughly **670 km**; **Guru Shikhar, Mount Abu, Rajasthan, 1,722 m**. Source divides Sambhar–Sirohi and Sambhar–Khetri belts; discontinuous weathered ridges reflect long erosion.
- Aravalli–Delhi orogenic belt belongs to the Indian Shield; formation spans multiple Precambrian episodes. **“Over 2.5 billion years” is not one precise age for every exposed mountain/ridge**. Old geology does not mean zero present earthquake risk.
- **Banas → Chambal → Yamuna → Ganga**, correcting the source’s direct Yamuna-tributary label. Sahibi historically drains toward Yamuna; modern modified channels differ. **Luni → Rann of Kachchh**, an inland/saline terminal drainage context, not a simple open-mouth river into the Arabian Sea.
- Copper, lead, zinc and marble support mining but increase habitat/water pressure. Dry deciduous habitats, groundwater recharge and buffering against desertification matter; forests/ridges are not an impermeable wall against every dust event.
- Fold mountains arise through compressive deformation; Himalaya is a younger active orogen. Avoid simplistic exact age rankings of “Western Ghats 150 million years” versus every Aravalli rock: landform formation and underlying rock age differ.
- Practice answer **B** is the source’s watershed interpretation; retain drainage qualifications rather than equating an endorheic basin with direct seaward flow.

## July 2025 safari proposal
- Source links Haryana's proposed roughly **10,000-acre** safari with Great Nicobar compensatory-afforestation planning and cites preference for land below 40% forest density. Sparse canopy does not imply ecological emptiness; water use, native habitat and connectivity need assessment.
- **2023 forest-law amendment** includes qualifying government/authority-owned zoos/safaris outside protected areas in specified forest-management activities; this is not blanket permission for every safari on any forest land or waiver of other applicable approvals/court directions.
- Captive tourism and compensatory planting do not recreate an island's original ecosystem. Evaluate transparent site-specific hydrology, biodiversity and community impacts.

## December 2025: Aravalli definition and sustainable-mining plan
- **20 November 2025 Supreme Court order**, not the PDF's “October final decision”, accepted the committee's mining-related definition and directed a **Management Plan for Sustainable Mining (MPSM)** through **MoEFCC/ICFRE**. No new mining leases until the plan; existing operations subject to strict conditions. [Court order](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/1995/2997/2997_1995_1_1502_66178_Order_20-Nov-2025.pdf).
- **100 m means height above local relief**, not altitude above mean sea level. Supporting slopes within the enclosing contour and specified intervening land between qualifying hills matter; “every hill below 100 m is open to mining” is false. The narrower threshold/ecological-continuity critique and government's inclusiveness defence remain contested assessments; neither unverified percentage nor rhetoric replaces mapped safeguards.
- Committee/CEC themes: scientific mapping across **Delhi, Haryana, Rajasthan and Gujarat**, cumulative mining assessment, sensitive habitats/corridors, aquifer recharge and restoration. A recommendation, court direction, completed management plan and actual enforcement are distinct stages. **May 2024** lease restrictions and **2009** Haryana mining restrictions are background, not new December bans.
- Range already covered: ancient NE–SW fold/residual belt; **Guru Shikhar 1,722 m, Mount Abu, Rajasthan**. **Correction: Chambal originates in Madhya Pradesh's Vindhyan region, not the Aravallis; Banas joins Chambal before Yamuna; Sabarmati/Luni have Aravalli headwaters**. Ancient rocks do not establish zero present seismic risk.
- Mining yields minerals/building stone but creates dust, habitat fragmentation, water-table and recharge risks. Ridges/vegetation buffer desertification without being an impermeable wind barrier. **UNCCD** is a relevant conservation framework, not an automatic special legal designation for every Aravalli parcel.
- **Aravalli Green Wall** seeks native restoration around the hill belt; the **26-million-ha-by-2030 pledge is India's national degraded-land restoration ambition**, not simply the project area. The project was launched in **2023**, with later 2025 implementation activity; PDF's June-2025 first-launch label conflates stages. [MoEFCC action plan](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/pdf-uploads/pdf_682f0a92983b08Wall.pdf).
- Embedded safari clipping proposes first-phase work; a minister's expectation does not prove judicial/environmental approvals or completed construction. **2007 Guru Shikhar PYQ—Rajasthan (a)**.
