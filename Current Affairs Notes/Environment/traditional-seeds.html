Conserving traditional seed varieties

Editable notes

Agrobiodiversity and food security

  • Traditional varieties/landraces are locally maintained crop populations shaped by farmer selection and adaptation. Their genetic diversity, associated knowledge and food cultures are resources for breeding, nutrition and climate adaptation.
  • Loss of varieties narrows options against drought, floods, pests and disease. Traits differ: not every landrace is high yielding, stress resistant, nutrient rich or low input, and improved/hybrid varieties are not inherently unsustainable. Evaluate genotype, environment and management together.

  • On-farm conservation maintains evolving crops and farmer knowledge; ex-situ gene banks, including ICAR-NBPGR collections, provide backups. Seed viability, regeneration, documentation and access matter; storage alone does not secure continued cultivation.
  • Recognise farmers' knowledge and legal rights. PPV&FR Act, 2001, section 39 protects saving, using, sowing, resowing, exchanging, sharing and selling farm produce including protected-variety seed, but excludes sale of branded seed of a protected variety. Breeders' rights and conservation incentives coexist; it is incorrect to describe every IPR regime as prohibiting farmer seed exchange. Authority brochure.
  • MSP already covers crops including jowar, bajra, ragi and several pulses. The issue is effective procurement, access and incentives for neglected crops/varieties, not first-ever MSP for all millets/pulses. MSP is not a separate guaranteed price for every indigenous rice variety. Official MSP table.
  • Suitable traditional crops can enter school meals, anganwadi nutrition, hospitals and PDS where procurement/processing allow. Avoid replacing dietary diversity with another uniform monoculture.
  • Source example: MSSRF's Tribal Agrobiodiversity Centre, Jeypore, Odisha links farmer conservation, local knowledge and stakeholder consultations; Odisha Millet Mission illustrates combining cultivation and market support. Conservation must deliver viable livelihoods as well as maintain genetic heritage.
  • Sustainable farming can reduce waste, pollution and input dependence and support biodiversity/health; these are management-dependent outcomes, not automatic properties of every traditional seed.

Mains practice framework

Causes → genetic/nutritional/climate risks → community banks + on-farm/gene-bank conservation + participatory breeding + farmer rights + procurement/value chains + consumer demand. The source's 250-word, 15-mark question is undated practice, not a verified UPSC PYQ.

Keonjhar Kalachampa: farmer innovation and benefit sharing

  • April 2025 case: Jayant Kumar Jena sought a share of commercial benefits from Keonjhar Kalachampa, a paddy variety developed by his father. A claim is not an awarded payment or final legal precedent. The case connects farmer breeding/conservation with the formal seed chain.
  • Source-reported traits: resistance to disease/pests, flexible sowing periods, non-lodging plants and yield around 7,000 kg/ha under reported conditions; do not generalise these into guaranteed yields or resistance to every climatic stress.
  • Source chronology: formal seed-chain integration 2013, central notification 2015, over 87,000 quintals of certified seed distributed 2018–2023. OUAT, Odisha State Seeds Corporation (OSSC) and the State Seed Testing Laboratory (SSTL) helped evaluation/testing and scaling. These figures are the supplied report's case details, not independently verified experimental performance.

  • Benefit sharing for genetic-resource contribution differs from section 39(2) compensation for failure of a registered variety to perform as represented under stated conditions. Do not collapse all payments into one automatic royalty. PPV&FR Authority rights brochure, Act.
  • Plant-variety protection under this sui generis, TRIPS-related regime is different from a patent, GI, central seed notification, certified-seed quality assurance and a conservation award; each has a separate purpose and procedure. Registration duration also varies by category, so the source image's simple 15/18-year statement should not replace category-specific law.
  • Revision chain: farmer selection → testing/documentation → notification/seed multiplication → commercial use → evidence-based benefit-sharing claim. Preserve farmer identity and genetic-resource records; build participatory research and transparent benefit sharing alongside seed access.

Beej Utsav

  • Four-day festival in tribal areas of Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat, covering 60+ village panchayats; supported by Vaagdhara, Banswara and Gram Swaraj Samooh, Krishi Evam Adivasi Swaraj Sangathan, Saksham Samooh and Bal Swaraj Samooh.
  • Beej Samvad, biodiversity fairs, seed balls/plantation and Beej Mata/Beej Mitra honours link seed access, local knowledge and climate resilience. Avoid the source's universal claim that indigenous seeds outperform hybrids or need no external inputs.
  • 2015 Seed Village PYQ: train farmers in quality-seed production and supply others at the right time and affordable cost; not simply discourage all seed purchases.
