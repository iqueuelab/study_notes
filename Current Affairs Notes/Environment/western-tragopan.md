# Western tragopan: breeding and habitat conservation

## December 2025: Sarahan captive-breeding account
- **Tragopan melanocephalus**, western Himalayan pheasant, **Himachal Pradesh State bird**; fragmented forest distribution in India/northern Pakistan. Correct the source's ‘Endangered’: **IUCN 2025 assessment is Vulnerable**, estimated **3,200–9,500 mature individuals**; this is a range estimate, not a precise census. [IUCN assessment](https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2025-2.RLTS.T22679147A177694929.en).
- **Blyth's tragopan**, shown in the side graphic, is a different species and **Nagaland's State bird**; do not transfer its range or image label to western tragopan.
- **Sarahan Pheasantry, Himachal Pradesh**: source milestones **1993 early captive births**, **2005 four chicks**, **43 captive-born birds during 2007–15**; **46 birds** and annual hatch/survival counts are the report's dated facility snapshot. Its simultaneous ‘first births 1993/world first 2005’ requires distinction between early births and a sustained successful programme, not two interchangeable first dates.
- Improved vegetation/nesting cover, seasonal feeding and reduced stress/disease aided husbandry. **2020–21 experimental releases**, one bird reportedly surviving nearly a year, show promise but do not establish a self-sustaining wild population; reintroductions stalled after **2023** amid funding/research needs.
- **Ex situ** breeding supports an insurance population; **in situ** habitat protection remains essential. Roads/grazing/fuelwood pressure, disturbance and climate-related breeding/food mismatch threaten survival. Release-site assessment, genetic management, predator/behavioural monitoring and local stewardship must accompany release. ‘87% genetic diversity preserved’ lacks a defined baseline here and is held for review.
- PYQ recall: **Ceylon frogmouth, Coppersmith barbet, Gray-chinned minivet, White-throated redstart—birds (2020, a)**; **cicada, froghopper, pond skater—insects (2024, c)**. These are already banked. The insect examples are Hemiptera; water-surface skating uses surface tension.
