# Gorillas: taxonomy, One Health and conservation

## Species, range and conservation
| Species | Subspecies | Main range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Western gorilla, *Gorilla gorilla* | Western lowland; Cross River | Western equatorial Africa; Cross River specifically Nigeria–Cameroon border |
| Eastern gorilla, *Gorilla beringei* | Mountain; eastern lowland/Grauer's | Mountain: Virunga Massif and Bwindi–Sarambwe (DR Congo, Rwanda, Uganda); Grauer's: eastern DR Congo |
- **Mountain gorilla, G. beringei beringei: IUCN Endangered**, improved from Critically Endangered in **2018**; eastern gorilla at species level remains **Critically Endangered**. The other three subspecies are Critically Endangered. The approximately **1,000** figure refers to survey-era mountain gorillas, not all gorillas or a new 2026 census. [IUCN recovery assessment](https://iucn.org/news/species/201811/fin-whale-mountain-gorilla-recovering-thanks-conservation-action-iucn-red-list).
- Gorillas are large, tailless apes, mostly herbivorous and terrestrial knuckle-walkers; troops commonly have a dominant mature male **silverback**. Slow reproduction, roughly one birth every **4–6 years**, slows recovery. Individual nose patterns help identification.
- **CITES Appendix I** sharply restricts international trade; commercial international trade in wild specimens is generally prohibited. Habitat conversion/logging/mining, bushmeat/infant capture, civil conflict and human-transmitted infections threaten populations.
- Map recall: **Virunga NP (DR Congo), Volcanoes NP (Rwanda), Mgahinga and Bwindi (Uganda)**; Cross River belongs on the Nigeria–Cameroon border, not East Africa's Kenya/Tanzania mountains.
## One Health and governance
- Gladys Kalema-Zikusoka's work illustrates links between wildlife veterinary care, human health, sanitation and community livelihoods. Disease prevention, anti-poaching, local benefit sharing and stable institutions complement protected areas.
- Armed conflict and institutional collapse can increase hunting/forest encroachment and endanger rangers. Gorilla tourism needs visitor limits and disease-distance safeguards; revenue alone does not guarantee conservation.
- **2008 CSE PYQ:** langur is a monkey; gibbon, gorilla and orangutan are apes (**c**). Apes lack external tails; the source's tail-only mnemonic is an oversimplification for all monkeys. **CDS-I 2025 Virunga question is excluded from CSE banks.**
