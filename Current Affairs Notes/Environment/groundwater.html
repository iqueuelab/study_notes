January 2025 report: retain scope

  • PDF reports nitrate exceedance in 440 districts in 2023, compared with 359 in 2017, and about 56% of districts affected. District occurrence is not the percentage of unsafe wells.
  • Source hotspot figures: Rajasthan 49%, Karnataka 48%, Tamil Nadu 37%; 30.77% pre-monsoon → 32.66% post-monsoon exceedance. The supplied summary does not reliably define every denominator/sample universe; treat these as report-specific figures, not universal national rates.
  • Fluoride hotspots listed: Rajasthan, Haryana, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana; uranium: Rajasthan, Punjab, Haryana, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka.
  • Source extraction stage about 60.4%, and 'safe' assessment units about 73%, compared with 67.4% in 2022. Safe extraction status does not certify safe drinking-water quality. CGWB 2024 reports.

Threshold trap

  • BIS drinking-water nitrate limit: 45 mg/L. WHO guideline: 50 mg/L as nitrate ion; specify the standard and chemical reporting basis.
  • The PDF's practice answer wrongly treats the WHO 50 mg/L statement as false by substituting India's threshold. WHO nitrate fact sheet.

Institutions and programmes

  • CGWB: scientific assessment, exploration, aquifer mapping, level/quality monitoring, recharge studies, reports, yearbooks, atlases and training; Ministry of Jal Shakti. Established in 1970 by renaming the Exploratory Tube Wells Organisation; merger with GSI's groundwater wing in 1972.
  • CGWA: groundwater extraction regulation under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986; distinguish its regulatory role from CGWB's scientific work.
  • NAQUIM maps and manages aquifers; Atal Bhujal Yojana promotes community groundwater management; Jal Shakti Abhiyan / Catch the Rain encourage conservation and recharge.
  • CPCB/SPCB pollution control under the Water Act, 1974; sewage/effluent treatment prevents contamination at source.

Mains response

  • Monitor hotspots and disclose water quality → reduce fertiliser losses and repair sanitation → enforce industrial waste controls → provide safe alternative drinking water → recharge with quality safeguards.
  • Combine metering/water budgets, efficient irrigation, community participation and demand management; recharge alone cannot offset unlimited extraction.
  • Benefits: health protection, reliable food production, lower treatment costs and reduced ecological damage. Avoid recharge structures that inject contaminated runoff.

Water conservation and community action

  • March 30, 2025 Mann Ki Baat reporting cited over 11 billion cubic metres saved in seven–eight years; this is a reported cumulative figure, not direct proof every aquifer recovered by that amount.
  • Rainwater harvesting, wastewater reuse, demand management, watershed restoration and traditional johads (Rajasthan) can improve local water security. Maharashtra's Jalyukt Shivar is a water-conservation initiative.
  • Jal Shakti Abhiyan/Catch the Rain, Atal Bhujal Yojana groundwater management and Namami Gange water-quality work have different scopes; do not equate recharge, storage and pollution reduction.
  • Monitor water balance and quality, maintain recharge structures, consult communities and adapt crops/industrial demand; capture alone cannot offset unlimited extraction.
