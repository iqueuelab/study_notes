Climate Risk Index: extreme-weather losses and resilience

Editable notes

CRI 2026, released during COP30

  • Germanwatch, independent NGO based in Bonn, published Climate Risk Index 2026 during COP30, Belém. The title year is 2026, but its long-term observation window is 1995–2024. India ranked ninth over this window; that is not its rank for every individual year. Germanwatch report.
  • Global reported toll over the window: over 832,000 deaths, more than 9,700 extreme-weather events and nearly US$4.5 trillion inflation-adjusted direct economic losses. The issue cites 5.7 billion affected; cumulative affected counts may include repeated exposure and must not be read as unique individuals.
  • It is a retrospective impact index, not a prediction of 2026 weather, an IPCC report or a complete measure of all climate vulnerability. Event databases, reporting gaps and monetary valuation limit comparability; recorded weather losses do not alone attribute every event wholly to anthropogenic warming.
  • India faces recurrent floods, cyclones, heatwaves and droughts; large exposed populations, monsoon agriculture, coasts, Himalayan hazards, poverty and weak housing/services interact. The report's recurrent-shock argument is that some places cannot recover before the next disaster.
  • Responses: early warning and last-mile action, heat plans, resilient infrastructure, ecosystem restoration, water security, social protection and recovery finance. Adaptation complements mitigation; fair climate finance/loss-and-damage support matter. Practice publisher key Germanwatch (c), unyearred coaching practice.
