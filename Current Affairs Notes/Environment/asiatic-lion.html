Environment

Asiatic lions and conservation translocation

Editable notes

Range, status and habitat

  • India's surviving wild Asiatic lions occupy the Gir/Saurashtra landscape, Gujarat, including areas outside Gir's protected boundaries; historically extended across West Asia. Panthera leo persica is the traditional name; modern taxonomy includes Indian lions within P. l. leo, with P. l. melanochaita covering southern/eastern African populations. “Asiatic vs all African” is not an exact two-subspecies division.
  • 2020 population estimate: 674, source landscape ~30,000 km², nine districts/53 talukas; these are 2020 figures, not a May 2025 count. Growth does not eliminate genetic/disease risks. Gujarat Forest Department.
  • Correction: lion species P. leo is Vulnerable, but species-wide status must not be passed off as proof that Asiatic lions were downgraded from Endangered to Vulnerable after 2008. Historical Asiatic assessment was Endangered; taxon/assessment dates differ. Indian protection Schedule I, Indian population CITES Appendix I; other lion populations generally Appendix II. IUCN Cat Specialist Group.
  • Gir National Park/Wildlife Sanctuary established 1965, source combined protected area ~1,412 km²; separately cited buffer 470 km² should not be treated as the national park's own area. Dry deciduous/thorn/scrub habitats; Girnar Sanctuary, 2008, Junagadh, extends protection in the hills, with teak/dhak/acacia and leopards, chinkara/chousingha and birds. Source bird count >300 needs defined geographical scope.
  • Typical Asiatic identification includes a less-developed mane and abdominal skin fold; body size/social systems vary, so source's male-pride contrast is not an absolute rule.

Threats and translocation

  • Restricted range and isolation → disease catastrophe, inbreeding and environmental-disaster risk; habitat/prey loss, roads/open wells/electric fencing and human-lion conflict require landscape protection, veterinary surveillance and community participation.
  • 2017 PYQ: Kuno-Palpur Wildlife Sanctuary, MP (b) proposed second lion population. This is the historic site name/status; proposal/court direction does not establish completed translocation. Geographic separation spreads risk but does not automatically increase genetic diversity without managed founders/exchange.
  • Do not retain source's unsupported claims that Sariska lost lions through recent poaching or that Kuno's African-cheetah project was an earlier completed Asiatic-cheetah reintroduction.
  • 2019 PYQ: Asiatic lion alone (a) exclusively naturally found in India today. Greater one-horned rhino India/Nepal, not a confirmed current wild population in Bhutan/Bangladesh. Bactrian camels occur beyond India; distinguish domesticated Ladakh camels from the separate wild camel Camelus ferus of China/Mongolia. A Rajasthan dromedary is not proof of a natural wild population.
