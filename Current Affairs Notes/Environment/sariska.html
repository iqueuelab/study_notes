Sariska: tiger habitat and mining boundaries

Editable notes

Boundary proposal and ecology

  • The issue reports a proposal to rationalise Critical Tiger Habitat (CTH) boundaries at Sariska, potentially affecting 50+ marble/dolomite mines shut following court action. Treat this as a contested proposal, not reopening approval; balance livelihoods against fragmentation, pollution, disturbance and ecological integrity.
  • Alwar, Rajasthan; Aravalli landscape of hills, valleys, cliffs and plateaus; tropical dry-deciduous/thorn forest dominated by dhok (Anogeissus pendula), with ber, gugal, kadaya, bamboo and kair.
  • Tiger, leopard, caracal, sambar, chital, nilgai, wild boar and four-horned antelope; peafowl, partridges and monitor lizards. Kankwari Fort is inside; Pandupol, Siliserh Lake and Bhangarh are geographic/heritage recall points.
  • Project Tiger inclusion 1978–79; Ranthambore entered in 1973–74, so Sariska was not Rajasthan's first tiger reserve. NTCA records sanctuary upgrading in 1958 and preliminary national-park notification in 1982, contradicting the issue's 1979 park date. NTCA profile.
  • CTH is the core area identified scientifically under Wild Life (Protection) Act 1972; legal safeguards and community rights cannot be replaced by a blanket claim that every human activity is prohibited.
  • PYQ recall: Dampa—Mizoram; Gumti—Tripura (not Sikkim); Saramati—Nagaland. Pakhui/Pakke—Arunachal Pradesh. Among Corbett/Ranthambore/Nagarjunasagar–Srisailam/Sundarbans, Nagarjunasagar–Srisailam has the largest CTH (2,595.72 km²); its current Andhra Pradesh reserve should not be conflated with Telangana's separately notified Amrabad.
