# Black seadevil: deep-sea adaptations

## Identification and habitat
- **Melanocetus johnsonii**, humpback anglerfish/black seadevil; known deep-sea species, **not a newly discovered species**.
- February 2025 Tenerife footage concerned an unusual daylight surface sighting; it did not establish surface water as normal habitat or the fish's first-ever scientific observation.
- Lives at mesopelagic/bathypelagic depths; source “only deeper than 2,000 m” is too restrictive. Dark colour, large mouth/teeth and expandable body aid opportunistic feeding.
## Adaptations and conservation
- Modified dorsal spine bears a **bioluminescent lure**, associated with symbiotic bacteria; attracts prey in darkness. Females are much larger than males.
- Low light, pressure and limited food shape deep-sea ecology; an anatomical adaptation is not proof the fish occurs only in the abyssal zone.
- Source **IUCN Least Concern** classification does not by itself demonstrate a measured stable population; status and population trend are different.
- Ocean layers: **epipelagic 0–200 m →mesopelagic 200–1,000 m →bathypelagic 1,000–4,000 m →abyssopelagic 4,000–6,000 m**; approximate depth conventions.
- Research and habitat protection matter amid deep-sea pollution, warming and extraction pressure; rare footage alone does not establish the sighting's cause.
- [FishBase species listing](https://fishbase.org/identification/SpeciesList.php?genus=Melanocetus) identifies *M. johnsonii* as an already described species (**1864**).
