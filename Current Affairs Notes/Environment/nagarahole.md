# Nagarahole, chital and prey–predator balance

## Reserve map

- **Mysuru and Kodagu, Karnataka**; landscape connectivity with **Wayanad Sanctuary, Kerala**, south, and **Bandipur**, southeast; Western Ghats/Nilgiri forest landscape.
- Scrub and deciduous forests with other moist habitats. Calling the Western Ghats a UNESCO site does not mean every site/entire reserve is automatically a listed component.
- Fauna: tiger, leopard, dhole, sloth bear; **chital, sambar, gaur, four-horned antelope, elephant**; southwestern langur.

## Dated population interpretation

- January 2025 report: **28 chital/km²**, source **142 tigers**, described as third-highest reserve population in a 2023 release. Keep survey area/method/year distinct; these are not today's counts.
- Adequate prey supports predators, but density alone does not establish a perfectly balanced/healthy ecosystem. Food/water, habitat, disease, grazing pressure, seasonal change and survey uncertainty matter.
- Habitat diversity/grass availability, controlled livestock grazing and protection can support prey; camera-trap and other systematic monitoring inform management. Tigers outside core areas may imply landscape use/conflict risk, not automatically beneficial overflow.

## Chital recall

- **Axis axis**, spotted deer, native to the Indian subcontinent, **Least Concern** in source. Reddish-brown coat/white underparts, persistent white spots; social herds, source typical **10–50**.
- Males larger; males carry **three-tined antlers**, shed annually; antlers differ from permanent horns.
- Source approximate size **35 inches/89 cm**, **187 lb/85 kg**, antlers near **3 ft/91 cm**; dimensions vary with sex/age/population. The printed **20–30-year lifespan** should not be treated as a verified typical wild lifespan.
- Distribution graphic lists India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Bhutan and small Pakistan occurrence; distinguish native range, introduced populations and locally extinct occurrences.
