# Agarwood: resin formation, trade and Northeast livelihoods

## Species and resin
- **Agar/oud/gaharu** is fragrant resin-impregnated wood, notably from **Aquilaria malaccensis, Thymelaeaceae**. Northeast India, particularly **Assam and Tripura**, is important for cultivation; the wider agarwood-producing group occurs across South/Southeast Asia.
- Valuable dark aromatic resin develops as a **defence response to injury and microbial interaction**; healthy trees do not uniformly contain commercial fragrant resin. Natural damage/borers can introduce fungi/bacteria, and plantations use induction techniques. The source's single-fungus-only account is too narrow: BSI documents several associated microbes and wounding pathways. [Botanical Survey of India](https://bsi.gov.in/uploads/documents/Non-Detriment%20Findings%20%28NDFs%29/NDF%20Study%20Report%20of%20Aquilaria%20malaccensis%20%28Agarwood%29%20in%20India.pdf).
- Uses: incense, distilled **agar oil** in luxury perfumery and traditional medicines; customary medicinal use does not establish clinical efficacy. Resin quality, induction success, maturity and legal traceability influence value; high prices do not mean every tree is equally valuable.
## January 2026 value-chain initiative
- **24 January**, DoNER Minister laid the foundation stone of an **₹80-crore Agarwood Value Chain Development Scheme at North Fulkabari, Tripura**. Cultivation, processing, value addition and market links seek better farmer returns; **up to 50% higher Tripura production capacity** is a scheme aim, not achieved output. [DoNER announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2218232&lang=1&reg=6).
- The embedded clipping highlights **CITES-linked export facilitation**. Aquilaria international trade requires applicable CITES/Indian permits and legal-origin/non-detriment conditions; easing procedures is not a blanket removal of species safeguards.
- Contrast the accompanying **2016 Red Sanders PYQ: (a), statement 1 only**. Red Sanders belongs to southern dry deciduous forests, not tropical rain forests; it is a different tree/product. Agarwood practice key **(b), statement 2 only** rejects the suggestion all healthy heartwood is inherently fragrant.
