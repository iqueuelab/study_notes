# Blue carbon and coastal wetland protection

## Carbon storage
- Carbon captured/stored by ocean/coastal ecosystems; key **coastal vegetated sinks: mangroves, salt marshes, seagrass meadows**. Carbon stored in vegetation and especially sediments. [NOAA](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/bluecarbon.html).
- Source's **10-times terrestrial-forest rate** describes a comparison of relevant coastal habitats, not a universal rate for all oceans/reefs or equal carbon stocks everywhere.
- Coral reefs are biodiversity-rich but **not included among the standard three coastal blue-carbon habitats** in the practice question; carbon sequestration and carbonate formation differ.
## Services and threats
- Climate mitigation + coastal protection + nurseries/fisheries/food security + adaptation.
- Conversion for aquaculture/agriculture, destructive harvesting, pollution and urban/industrial reclamation threaten stored carbon and livelihoods; degradation may release carbon.
- Protect existing habitats, restore suitable hydrology, prevent pollution and measure long-term carbon/benefits; poorly placed plantations do not replace functioning seagrass or marsh ecosystems.

## Carbon accounting and PYQ recall
- Coastal plants fix CO₂; roots trap sediment and waterlogged, oxygen-poor soils slow decomposition, allowing long storage. Preservation supports mitigation, storm/erosion protection and fish/bird habitat.
- Correct the source's “2% of ocean area absorbs 50% of all ocean carbon” shortcut: a widely cited roughly half figure concerns **carbon burial in coastal/ocean sediments**, not all oceanic CO₂ uptake. Stocks, annual burial and gross photosynthesis have different denominators. [NOAA explanation](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/understanding-blue-carbon).
- **2021 PYQ: blue carbon = carbon captured by oceans and coastal ecosystems (a)**. **2023 CCS PYQ: all three**—enhanced basalt weathering, ocean alkalinity enhancement and underground injection of carbonated water. They differ in mechanism; feasibility, permanence and ecological monitoring remain relevant.
- Green carbon refers to terrestrial biomass/soils; black carbon is soot from incomplete combustion; brown carbon is light-absorbing organic aerosol. None is a colour classification of seawater.
