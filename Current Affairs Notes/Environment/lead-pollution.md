# Lead pollution and public health

## Exposure and effects
- Lead is a toxic metal; exposure comes from unsafe battery recycling, paints, contaminated products, occupational dust and legacy pollution. Geological weathering is a natural source.
- Children are especially vulnerable: impaired brain development, learning and behaviour; other effects include anaemia, kidney damage, hypertension and reproductive harm.
- **No known safe blood lead level.** A 5 micrograms/dL action threshold is not a safety boundary. [WHO guidance](https://www.who.int/news/item/27-10-2021-who-guidance-to-reduce-illness-due-to-lead-exposure).
- India phased out leaded petrol by 2000, but contamination can persist.
## Regulation and response
- **Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022** replaced the **Batteries (Management and Handling) Rules, 2001**. They cover multiple battery categories and use EPR for collection/recycling/refurbishment. The source's title 'Lead Battery Waste Management Rules (2016)' is incorrect. [MoEFCC/PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1854433&lang=2&reg=48).
- Priorities: safer products, formal recycling, occupational protection, child/pregnancy screening, pollution monitoring/remediation and reliable exposure data.
## Source figures requiring baseline checks
- January 2025 PDFs cite 275 million exposed children, 4.6 million DALYs and 165,000 annual deaths, but omit the underlying study years/thresholds.
- They identify Bihar, UP, MP, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh and Andhra Pradesh as high-burden states and cite a 40% share with unclear denominator. Keep these as unverified source estimates, not a definitive state ranking.
