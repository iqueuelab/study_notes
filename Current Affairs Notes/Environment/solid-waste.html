Source segregation and the municipal waste chain

Editable notes

Segregation and treatment

  • Source segregation: separate wet/biodegradable, dry/recyclable and domestic hazardous waste; special biomedical/e-waste streams follow appropriate rules.
  • Keep streams separate in collection/transport; segregating at home achieves little if mixed again in the truck.
StreamAppropriate route
Suitable wet organicsComposting/biomethanation with contamination controls
Dry recyclablesRecovery/recycling through authorised systems
Non-recyclable high-calorific residuesSuitable controlled energy recovery, not indiscriminate burning
Hazardous/inert/process residuesAppropriate authorised treatment/engineered disposal
  • Benefits: less landfill methane/leachate, better recycling, safer workers and resource recovery; treatment may still generate residues/emissions.

Rules and programmes

  • Source-era SWM Rules2016, Plastic Waste Rules2016/EPR, SBM-U2.0 and SATAT compressed-biogas support. 2022 SUP ban targets listed products, not every single-use plastic object.
  • Producers' responsibility and municipal obligations differ; blanket plastic/e-waste EPR cannot be attributed solely to SWM Rules.
  • Indore collection/biogas, Pune SWaCH waste-picker integration, Alappuzha decentralised composting illustrate approaches; source's30,000-Pune-worker and100%-zero-waste claims require verification.

Data and governance

  • Source ~160,000 tonnes/day generated /79,000 processed /3,159 dumpsites /14 WTE plants, composition and city segregation shares are mixed dated estimates; not a harmonised current national inventory.
  • Sweden's “99% recycling” often conflates material recycling with energy recovery; international segregation, treatment and landfill percentages are different metrics.
  • Awareness plus reliable separate collection, ULB capacity, user fees/enforcement, EPR accountability, decentralised organics treatment and dignified waste-worker inclusion.
  • WTE is not an automatic answer for high-moisture mixed waste; technology choices need feedstock, pollution control, costs and maintained operation.
