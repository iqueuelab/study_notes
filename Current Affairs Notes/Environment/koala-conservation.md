# Koalas: disease, genetic bottlenecks and conservation

## Species and disease
- **Phascolarctos cinereus**, family Phascolarctidae: Australian **arboreal, folivorous marsupial**, closely related to wombats; specialises on eucalyptus leaves, using a digestive system adapted to toxic, low-nutrient foliage. Forepaws have two opposing digits for climbing.
- Eastern/southeastern range includes Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria and southeast South Australia; open forest/woodland habitat faces clearing, fires, heat/drought, vehicles and disease. Global **IUCN Vulnerable** differs from Australia's legal **Endangered** listing for Queensland/NSW/ACT populations.
- Chlamydial disease, especially **Chlamydia pecorum**, can cause eye/urinary/reproductive disease, blindness and infertility. Infection rates reported up to 70% apply to some populations, not every wild koala. Transmission includes mating and mother-to-young exposure; do not treat the worksheet's every listed route as equally established.
- **Vaccine approval was 2025 background, not a new March 2026 event.** UniSC developed the vaccine; veterinary-regulator authorisation supports controlled rollout, alongside habitat conservation. Vaccination does not remove other threats. [Australian environment department,23 September 2025](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/about/news/world-first-vaccine-new-hope-koalas).
## March 2026 genomic finding
- A study analysed **418 whole genomes**. Some Victorian populations that experienced severe historical bottlenecks subsequently expanded, showing early genetic recovery/new variation and fewer harmful variants; northern populations with higher diversity nevertheless carried greater harmful-mutation burdens and declining effective size. [University of Sydney,11March 2026](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2026/03/11/genome-study-koalas-genetic-risk-endangered-species.html).
- **Bottleneck:** sharp reduction in breeding population can lose genetic diversity and raise inbreeding risk. Rapid growth can alter the outcome, but this does not prove all bottlenecks are harmless or Victoria's conservation problems solved. Assess population trajectory, harmful variants, adaptive capacity, habitat and disease together, rather than diversity alone.
