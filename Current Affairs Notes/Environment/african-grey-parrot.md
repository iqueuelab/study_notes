# African grey parrot: trafficking and trade documentation

## November 2025: exotic-bird trade records
- The embedded report says RTI replies reveal missing African grey parrot trade records across State forest departments. Absence of an agency record is a documentation/enforcement gap, not proof that no birds are traded or that every captive bird was illegally acquired.
- **Grey parrot, Psittacus erithacus**: African forest species, grey plumage, red tail and pale facial area; about **33 cm**, roughly **400–450 g**. Strong vocal mimicry, learning and social cognition make it attractive to the pet trade. The PDF’s “smartest/largest” rankings and a universal “five-year-old child” equivalence are not reliable diagnostic facts.
- **Endangered** in the source’s IUCN classification; trapping for pet markets and forest loss are major threats. Long captive lifespan (source roughly **40–60 years**) does not demonstrate secure wild populations. Modern taxonomy distinguishes the **Timneh parrot**, so broad older West–Central African range descriptions should not be applied indiscriminately to both species.
- **CITES Appendix I**, effective **2 January 2017** after CoP17 (2016). International commercial trade in wild specimens is generally prohibited; tightly regulated exceptions and captive-bred rules mean this is not an absolute ban on every permitted movement. [CITES record](https://cites.org/sites/default/files/eng/com/ac/29/E-AC29-30.pdf).
- Conservation needs traceable acquisition/import documentation, species identification, coordinated enforcement and habitat protection; intelligence and longevity do not justify capture from the wild.
