Environment

Ganga streamflow: long-term drying and basin management

Editable notes

September 2025 research

  • IIT Gandhinagar/University of Arizona researchers reconstructed Ganga streamflow using tree-ring drought information and hydrological modelling over roughly 1,300 years, back to 700 CE. Paper published 22 September 2025, not September 2024 as printed. Original paper.
  • Recent multi-decadal drying was unusually severe in the reconstruction; source compares 1991–2020 drying intensity at 76% above a severe sixteenth-century analogue and highlights the 2004–2010 drought. The 76% is not a 76% fall in annual river discharge or a claim the whole river has ceased flowing.
  • Weaker monsoon precipitation, Indian Ocean warming/aerosols, groundwater depletion reducing baseflow, and land-use/irrigation pressures interact. Tree-ring reconstruction and model comparisons involve uncertainty; observed historical drying and future projections are different questions.
  • Basin-wide surface/groundwater management, environmental flows, demand-efficient irrigation, recharge, pollution control and drought preparedness protect food, drinking-water and energy security. Source 600-million population/40%-GDP figures need basin/year definitions rather than treatment as exact current totals.

River-system recall

  • Bhagirathi from Gangotri/Gaumukh joins Alaknanda at Devprayag; Ganga enters plains at Haridwar. Vishnuprayag is Dhauliganga + Alaknanda, correcting the source's “Vishnu Ganga”. Karnaprayag Pindar–Alaknanda; Rudraprayag Mandakini–Alaknanda; Nandprayag Nandakini–Alaknanda.
  • Left-bank: Ramganga, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Mahananda. Right-bank: Yamuna (Chambal/Sind/Betwa/Ken system), Son; Damodar drains to Hooghly. Padma and Bhagirathi–Hooghly are distinct downstream channels; Sagar Island is associated with the Hooghly outlet, not every Ganga distributary's mouth.
  • Source roughly 2,525 km length and 8.6 lakh km² Indian basin are rounded geographic figures. Kosi's floodplain migration/braiding and Chambal ravines are useful contrasts; DVC does not eliminate all Damodar flood risk.
