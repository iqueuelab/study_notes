Himachal Pradesh: ecological risk and development governance

Editable notes

October 2025 discussion of July judicial intervention

  • Pristine Hotels and Resorts v State of Himachal Pradesh, SLP(C) 19426/2025: on 28 July 2025, Justices J.B. Pardiwala and R. Mahadevan dismissed the company's challenge but directed registration of a public-interest writ concerning State ecology and a response/action plan. The dispute concerned June green-area restrictions around Shri Tara Mata Hill, Shimla; it is not a fresh October judgment. Court order.
  • The order highlights mountain cutting, hydropower, deforestation and unplanned construction. Judicial concern about ecological collapse is not a literal scientific prediction that the State will disappear. Revenue priorities must account for ecological risks and communities' long-term safety.
  • The issue's analysis links young, steep, seismic terrain with slope destabilisation, blasting/tunnelling, debris disposal in rivers, floodplain encroachment and drainage obstruction. Hydropower can alter sediment, environmental flows and fish passage; small or run-of-river projects are not automatically impact-free.
  • Roads, tunnels and retaining walls require site-specific geology/design: labelling a tunnel “green infrastructure” does not prove safety. Project-by-project EIA should be complemented by cumulative basin/catchment impacts, carrying capacity and hazard mapping rather than segmentation to avoid appraisal.
  • Implement land-use/floodplain zoning, native vegetation and bioengineering, safe debris disposal, minimum ecological flows, scientific monitoring, community participation and contractor/department accountability. Use GSI/NDMA expertise and satellite/GIS evidence; laws/policies without enforcement do not reduce exposure.
  • Extreme rainfall, flash flood and cloudburst are different observations. Existing cloudburst notes retain the measured IMD rainfall criterion; destruction alone does not establish it. Source 170+ hydropower projects is a dated reported scale figure, while its blanket 5.5-m hill-road rule needs the particular circular and exceptions before application to every highway.
  • Mains approach: fragile geography → exposure created by development → enforcement failures → cumulative, risk-informed planning and measurable accountability. The printed 250-word/15-mark item is coaching practice, not a verified PYQ.
