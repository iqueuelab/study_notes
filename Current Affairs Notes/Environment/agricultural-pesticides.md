# Agricultural pesticides: toxicity and regulatory distinctions

## PYQ-linked classification
- **Carbofuran, methyl parathion, phorate and triazophos are agricultural pesticides**: the 2019 PYQ answer is **(a)**. Carbofuran is a carbamate; the other three are organophosphates. Their toxicity underlies concern for farm workers, non-target organisms, soil and water.
- The source supplies a Kerala list of insecticides, fungicides and herbicides; historical use, State restrictions and current national approvals are different. Do not treat this clipping as a verified September 2025 all-India ban register or assign identical persistence/carcinogenicity to every compound. Exposure protection, integrated pest management and enforcement reduce harm.

## January 2026: draft Pesticides Management Bill, 2025
- Agriculture Ministry opened public consultation **7 January**, with comments due **4 February 2026**. This was a **draft**, intended to replace the **Insecticides Act 1968 /Rules 1971**; it had not become operative merely through consultation. Official priorities include digital traceability, stronger action on spurious products, compounding/decriminalisation of petty offences and accredited testing laboratories. [7 January announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2212144&lang=1&reg=1).
- Proposed lifecycle controls span import/manufacture, storage/transport, packaging/labelling, advertising, sale, use and disposal. Definition covers specified chemical/biological pest-control substances, plant-growth regulators, defoliants, desiccants, fruit-thinning agents, sprouting inhibitors and relevant pre/post-harvest protection.
- **Central Pesticides Board:** scientific/technical advice; **Registration Committee:** registration, review/amendment/cancellation and a digital national register. State licensing/enforcement complements central registration and standards. Safe handling and container disposal protect workers, consumers and non-target organisms.
- Draft Schedule II proposes **up to five years' imprisonment or ₹10–50 lakh fine or both** where specified health/safety or composition contraventions cause death/grievous hurt; this is not the penalty for every minor breach or every adverse event irrespective of statutory conditions. [Draft text](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jan/doc202617752901.pdf).
- Safer inputs, integrated pest management, biological alternatives, quality testing and residue compliance can support farmer/public health and exports. The 1968 law already covered notified insecticides/fungicides/herbicides; its title does not mean it regulated only insect-killing substances.
