# Rhesus macaque: protection and human–wildlife conflict

## November 2025: proposed restoration of protection
- Source reports **Standing Committee of the National Board for Wildlife** recommended restoring **Macaca mulatta** to **Schedule II** after omission during the 2022 schedule revision. **Recommendation is not an enacted schedule notification**; the source's status table incorrectly presents the proposal as already operative. Omission from protection is also not automatically the same as a time/area-specific declaration of vermin.
- **Cercopithecidae**, Old World monkey, **IUCN Least Concern**, **CITES Appendix II**. These international categories differ from Indian statutory scheduling. Widely distributed across **South/Southeast Asia**, including Indian plains, Himalayan foothills, forests, farms and urban/temple landscapes.
- Brown/grey coat, hairless pink-red face/rear, **non-prehensile tail**; source approximate body **45–65 cm**, tail **~25 cm**, habitat elevation up to **~3,000 m**. Omnivorous/opportunistic: fruit, seeds, roots, grain, insects and occasional animal foods; seed dispersal contributes to forest ecology.
- **Diurnal, terrestrial and arboreal**, social troops with dominance hierarchies, vocal/facial/postural communication; females often remain in natal groups and males disperse. A single alpha-male description understates complex female and male social relationships. Used in biomedical/behavioural research; lawful research and illicit capture are distinct categories.
- Conflict drivers: feeding/accessible food waste, crop attraction, habitat fragmentation and human encroachment; feeding can alter dependence and behaviour. Policy should combine secure waste, visitor education, crop/building protection, habitat measures and lawful veterinary management, while tackling trafficking and exploitation. Local abundance and cultural reverence do not eliminate welfare or conservation concerns.
