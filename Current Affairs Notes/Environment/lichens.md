# Lichens: symbiosis, succession and Western Ghats diversity

## Symbiotic biology and ecological roles
- Lichen thallus is an association of a **fungal mycobiont** with photosynthetic **algal/cyanobacterial photobiont**. Fungus supplies structure/protection and helps retain water/minerals; photobiont supplies fixed carbon. Examples include **Nostoc, Chlorella and Trentepohlia**; fungi are mainly ascomycetes. This revision model does not imply every association contains exactly two species.
- **Crustose, foliose and fruticose** growth forms; no true roots/stems/leaves. Rhizines anchor many foliose forms, not every lichen. Occur on bark, rocks and soil from tropics to polar regions; tolerance depends on species.
- Pioneer colonisation, rock weathering, dust trapping and soil formation aid **primary succession**; food/nesting microhabitats and soil-crust stability support ecosystems. Neither “always the first organism” nor “all lichens are keystone species” is universally valid. Sensitivity to **SO₂** makes communities useful bioindicators, but absence alone does not prove pollution without substrate/climate context.
- Applications: dyes/perfumes, bioactive-compound research and **lichenometry** estimating exposed-surface age with species/site growth calibration. Laboratory antimicrobial activity is not proof of an approved human treatment.
## Western Ghats research reported in November 2025
- Source team from **Maharaja’s College, Kochi** reports **Parmotrema sahyadricum (Wayanad)**, **Solenopsora rhizomorpha (Eravikulam/Mathikettanshola)**, **Buelloa ghattensis** and **Pyxine janakiae (Mathikettanshola)**. Preserve these as source-reported spellings/localities pending the individual taxonomic papers; publication versus a new locality record must be checked separately.
- Research began in **2022**; the issue's blanket **“new species, 2024”** is not a verified common publication year. The **IPNI author record lists P. sahyadricum in 2022**, demonstrating that November news is not necessarily November discovery. [Taxonomic author record](https://www.ipni.org/a/37828-1). The source's “Janaki Ammal likely” dedication is speculation and should not be learned as established etymology.
