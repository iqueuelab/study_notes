Smew: migratory diving duck and Assam wetlands

Editable notes

February 2026: first Kaziranga-landscape record

  • Source reports Smew (Mergellus albellus) recorded at Rowmari–Donduwa beels, Laokhowa buffer, during the seventh Kaziranga waterbird census,14–20January 2026. The February report describes a January observation; it is the first reported record for this landscape, not the first Indian record.
  • Compact migratory diving duck, family Anatidae; northern Eurasian/taiga breeding range. Nests in mature-tree cavities; winters on freshwater lakes/reservoirs, ice-free rivers and sheltered brackish/coastal waters. Fish dominate winter diet, while breeding diet includes aquatic invertebrates. BirdLife species assessment.
  • IUCN Least Concern globally; threats include habitat loss, loss of nesting trees, pollution and hunting. Local rarity and national winter-population status are distinct from global Red List category.
  • Census clipping reports 105,540 waterbirds/107 species across the wider reserve landscape; observation effort and habitat conditions matter when interpreting year-to-year counts. Wetland connectivity and protection from encroachment aid migrants.
  • A new sighting is a monitoring signal, not by itself proof climate change caused a migration shift. Repeated distribution, abundance and environmental data are needed to separate climate effects, ordinary vagrancy and observation changes.
