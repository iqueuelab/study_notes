Wild boar, vermin declarations and conflict management

Editable notes

Kerala request and legal distinctions

  • The May issue reports Kerala's request for wild-boar vermin notification and greater decentralisation of wildlife-control authority to Chief Conservators of Forests. A State request is not a Union notification or completed statutory amendment.
  • Wild Life (Protection) Act, section 62: the Union may notify a Schedule II wild animal as vermin for a specified area and period under the revised schedules; relevant protection changes only within that notification's scope. The PDF's Schedule III wild-pig reference uses the older schedule arrangement. Current Schedule II.
  • Do not conflate section 62 with section 11 authorisation for a specific dangerous/diseased animal, or read either as unlimited permission to kill any wildlife. The Chief Wildlife Warden/authorised officers must follow statutory conditions, reasons and safeguards; mere apprehension is insufficient.
  • Wild boar Sus scrofa, family Suidae: adaptable omnivore, native to Eurasia/North Africa; rooting, crop-raiding and potential attacks create forest-fringe conflict. Widely introduced/feral populations can damage ecosystems outside the native range; activity can be nocturnal or vary with disturbance. IUCN global status Least Concern does not remove Indian statutory protection.
  • Sus scrofa cristatus is the Indian wild-boar form; the source incorrectly equates it with both the Andamanese pig and Moupin pig. Do not treat those names as interchangeable. Sariska, Bandhavgarh, Corbett and Ranthambore are useful occurrence examples, not an exhaustive distribution.
  • The map distinguishes historical notifications in parts of Uttarakhand/Bihar from requests in Gujarat/Maharashtra/Himachal Pradesh; these are not proof every old authorisation remains operative in 2025. Check species, place and expiry of each notification.
  • Pair evidence-based population/conflict assessment with fencing, crop protection, compensation, habitat/waste management, community reporting and humane legally authorised intervention. Broad culling needs ecological and effectiveness scrutiny.

PYQ revision

  • 2022 wildlife-law question: (b), 2 only, correcting the source's (a). Protection follows a protected animal outside a sanctuary; section 39, not section 3, deals with specified government-property circumstances, so “all wild animals are sole government property” is overbroad. Mere apprehension of danger does not justify capture/killing.
  • 2020 musk deer: (a), Askot and Gangotri in the Uttarakhand Himalaya. Kishanpur is Uttar Pradesh's Terai and Manas Assam's foothills; neither is the best natural-habitat match for this question. Musk deer are Moschus species, distinct from wild boar.
