# Alpine musk deer and conservation-breeding identification

## Species and identification
- **Moschus chrysogaster**, family **Moschidae**, distinct from true deer (Cervidae); solitary, largely crepuscular herbivore of high-altitude Himalayan/Asian forest–scrub, approximately **3,000–5,000 m**. Browses grasses, forbs, lichens/mosses and twigs.
- **Endangered**; threats: musk poaching and habitat disturbance. Indian protection **Schedule I**. **CITES population qualification:** Moschus populations of Afghanistan, Bhutan, India, Myanmar, Nepal, Pakistan are Appendix I; other populations Appendix II. Appendix I restricts international commercial trade; it is not an absolute ban on every permitted movement. [CITES taxonomic/trade assessment](https://cites.org/sites/default/files/eng/com/ac/16/16-7-4.pdf).
- Distinguish **Alpine M.chrysogaster** and **Himalayan M.leucogaster**. CZA review found taxonomic confusion and **no currently reported captive stock of Alpine musk deer in recognised Indian zoos**, suggesting its breeding programme was never initiated. Historic Chopta/Darjeeling stock was probably Alpine; Himachal stock probably Himalayan. PDF's assertion of a definitively started programme “for the wrong species” overstates this qualified assessment. [CZA planned-breeding review, p22](https://cza.nic.in/uploads/documents/publications/english/review.pdf).
- Correct species identity, genetic/studbook records and appropriate founder populations are prerequisites for ex-situ conservation; a photograph labelled Himalayan cannot establish Alpine identity.
- Source range countries: **India, Nepal, Bhutan and China**; species-level identification matters because Himalayan musk deer has overlapping distribution.
