# Whale pump: nutrients, productivity and marine food webs

## October 2025: baleen-whale nutrient recycling
- Baleen-whale faeces/urine return **nitrogen and phosphorus** to surface waters, where nutrient availability can limit phytoplankton. Feeding at depth and excretion near the surface, together with migrations, redistribute nutrients: **whales → nutrients → phytoplankton → zooplankton/fish → marine food webs**.
- Research combines measured nutrient concentrations with estimates of release and ecosystem modelling in the **Nordic and Barents Seas**. The issue reports productivity increases **up to 10%**, especially offshore in nutrient-poor summer conditions; this is a spatial/seasonal study estimate, not a global uniform productivity rise. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2505563122).
- Whales remain consumers, even when facilitating primary production; calling them photosynthetic 'producers' is misleading. Nutrient cycling can support biological carbon uptake, but extra plankton growth is not automatically equivalent to a measured permanent carbon-removal quantity.
- **Mysticeti:** baleen filter feeders (blue, fin, humpback, minke, sei); **Odontoceti:** toothed whales (sperm whale, orca, dolphins), commonly using echolocation. Blue whale is largest overall; **sperm whale is largest toothed whale**. The supplied question is **UPPSC 2023**, outside CSE banks.
- Whale conservation restores ecological functions alongside species populations; commercial whaling, food-web changes and nutrient redistribution connect biodiversity with ocean resilience.
