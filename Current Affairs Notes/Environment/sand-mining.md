# Sand mining: river ecology and coastal impacts

## 13 March 2026: National Chambal Sanctuary
- Supreme Court took **suo motu cognizance** of illegal sand mining threatening the **National Chambal Sanctuary**, spanning **Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh**. The clipping names Justices **Vikram Nath and Sandeep Mehta** and reports habitat damage, organised extraction, unregistered vehicles and difficulty enforcing earlier NGT monitoring directions.
- Chambal is a **lotic (flowing-water)** ecosystem important for **critically endangered gharial**, a fish-eating crocodilian. Mining alters bed morphology, sandbanks/nesting habitat and river–groundwater interaction; relocating animals cannot solve destruction at the receiving site. Later court directions must not be backdated to this initial intervention.
## Sources, pressure and response
- **Sustainable Sand Mining Management Guidelines 2016:** riverbeds/floodplains, lakes/reservoirs, agricultural deposits, palaeochannels and coastal/marine sources; **M-sand** is manufactured by crushing suitable rock, not naturally deposited river sand. Sand for construction differs from extraction targeting heavy-mineral beach deposits.
- Demand, weak monitoring/EIA compliance and criminal–official collusion encourage over-extraction. Effects include channel/bank erosion, local water-table decline, disturbed sediment budgets, turbidity and aquatic/riparian habitat loss; changed flooding/sedimentation depends on the site rather than always increasing everywhere.
- Coastal extraction can remove beach/dune storm buffers, accelerate erosion, damage turtle nesting and estuarine habitats and promote saline intrusion, harming fishing/farming settlements. The source cites Kerala's Alappuzha/Kollam, Tamil Nadu's southern coast and Goa estuaries; its individual causal examples need site-specific evidence rather than assuming every eroding shore is caused by mining.
- Use replenishment-based limits, district surveys, credible clearance/monitoring, transport traceability and independent enforcement; protect no-go sensitive areas. Quality-controlled **M-sand and recycled construction material** can substitute where suitable, with quarrying/energy/dust impacts assessed. [MoEFCC sand-mining guidelines](https://environmentclearance.nic.in/writereaddata/SandMiningManagementGuidelines2016.pdf).
- **2019 GS-III revision:** connect extraction → sediment/coastal-buffer loss → habitat/water/livelihood effects → concrete examples → regulation and substitutes. **2018 prelim** riverbed impacts question is already banked; correct answer includes groundwater pollution and lower water table, not decreased salinity.
