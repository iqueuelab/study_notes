# Battery waste: EPR pricing and critical-mineral recovery

## Battery Waste Management Rules 2022
- Growing EV/storage deployment creates spent batteries needing safe collection, recycling/refurbishment and recovery of lithium, nickel/cobalt where present. Battery chemistry differs: not every lithium-ion battery contains cobalt/nickel.
- **BWMR 2022** places extended producer responsibility on producers; registered recyclers/refurbishers and **EPR certificates** underpin compliance. Battery waste has its own rules: do not automatically count it within the e-waste inventory or transfer e-waste certificate price bands unchanged.
## Editorial: adequate EPR floor pricing
- Genuine recycling requires machinery, collection/transport, skilled labour and safe processes. Too-low certificate remuneration can undermine viable recovery and reward paper compliance, unsafe informal processing or fake certificates.
- Reform proposals: cost-based predictable remuneration, audited recovery/traceability, genuine enforcement against fraud, producer compliance and domestic recovery capacity. Critical-mineral recovery can reduce import/foreign-exchange exposure; it does not remove all virgin-mineral demand.
- Editorial forecasts **139 GWh demand by 2035 from 4 GWh in 2023** and **over US$1 billion forex loss by 2030** depend on scope/assumptions; keep as attributed projections. The printed **700,000 tonnes lithium batteries within 1.6-million-tonne 2022 e-waste** is unverified and mixes legal/statistical categories; do not learn it as established inventory.
- Lower global metal prices may give producers room to absorb compliance costs, but **no increase in consumer prices** is not guaranteed; assess cost pass-through and fair competition. Better EPR helps circularity, energy security and India's **2070 net-zero** ambition alongside repair/reuse.
