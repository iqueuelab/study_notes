Green cardamom: taxonomy and wild relatives

Editable notes

Botanical recall

  • Green/small cardamom Elettaria cardamomum, Zingiberaceae, humid shaded tropical hills; distinguish large/black cardamom (*Amomum subulatum*).
  • 2024 Taxon study recircumscribes Elettaria as seven species: cultivated *E. cardamomum*, four taxa reassigned from *Alpinia* (ensal, floribunda, involucrata, rufescens) and two newly described species.
  • E. facifera—Periyar/Idukki; E. tulipifera—Agasthyamalai and Munnar, Kerala source localities. New description ≠ newly evolved plant. Kew accepted name, original taxonomy paper.
  • Name traced to old Malayalam “elletari”, recorded in Hortus Malabaricus, seventeenth-century botanical work.

Agriculture and conservation

  • Wild relatives provide potential genetic resources for resistance/climate adaptation; discovery does not prove commercial edibility, superior flavour or ready-to-use cultivars.
  • Kerala Idukki/Wayanad, Karnataka Kodagu/Chikkamagaluru, Tamil Nadu Nilgiris: major growing landscapes; rain/heat extremes, pest pressure, cost and volatile prices affect returns.
  • Source India “world's largest producer/exporter” claim is wrong: Guatemala leads global cardamom production, India is an important producer with strong domestic demand. National Horticulture Board.
  • Malabar/Mysore are recognised cultivar types; source labels them GI tags without registration evidence. Do not equate a cultivar name with GI status; ₹1,000 crore/100-country export figures lack specified period.
  • Spices Board promotes sector/exports; ICAR–IISR research. Protect habitats, conserve germplasm, support sustainable shade cultivation/quality branding; SDGs 8,12,15.
