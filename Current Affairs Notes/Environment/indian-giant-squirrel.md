# Indian giant squirrel (Shekru)

## January 2026 sighting and identification
- The issue reports a sighting during **All India Tiger Estimation 2026** in the **Atwan region, Pune forest division**. An incidental observation is evidence of local presence, not a population estimate or proof that the entire forest is undisturbed.
- **Indian/Malabar giant squirrel, Ratufa indica**, a large, colourful **arboreal rodent endemic to India**; **Shekru**, Maharashtra's State animal. Mainly associated with forested central/peninsular India, including the **Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats and Satpura** landscapes. [Mammal Diversity Database](https://www.mammaldiversity.org/taxon/1001608/).
- **Diurnal**, usually solitary, with a long bushy tail, strong climbing limbs and multicoloured maroon/brown/black/cream fur. Canopy continuity and mature trees support movement and nesting. Tree nests/dreys are the key recall; avoid treating ground burrows as its normal nesting strategy.
- **IUCN Least Concern** does not mean absence of local threats: fragmentation, forest loss and infrastructure can isolate populations. Protect canopy connections and feeding/nesting trees; a sighting alone is not a validated universal indicator of forest quality.
