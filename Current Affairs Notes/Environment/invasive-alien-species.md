# Invasive alien species: trade pathways and biosecurity

## Concepts and pathways
- **Alien species:** introduced outside its natural range through human activity; **naturalised:** self-sustaining population; **invasive alien:** establishes/spreads and causes ecological, economic or health harm. Alien/naturalised does not automatically mean invasive; boundaries need not be national borders.
- Deliberate introductions: biocontrol (Gambusia/Poecilia), food (tilapia), ornamental/aquarium/pet trade. Accidental introductions: seeds/pests in crops, timber and cargo; ship ballast water and associated organisms.
- Changing trade partners can connect previously unlinked ecosystems while quarantine infrastructure lags. Climate change, disturbed habitats and pollution can increase establishment risks.
## Illustrative cases
| Species | Recall |
| --- | --- |
| Giant African snail | Introduced to India in the nineteenth century (source says around 1847); crop/garden damage, native-snail competition, potential rat-lungworm host |
| Parthenium | Associated with contaminated wheat imports in the 1950s/PL-480 era; competes with vegetation and causes allergies |
| Tilapia and Gambusia | Food/biocontrol introductions can harm native freshwater communities |
| Red-eared slider | Released pets compete with native turtles |
| Cotton mealybug | Crop losses, including cotton in the Deccan |
| African catfish | Source cites Keoladeo, Rajasthan: predation can disrupt native fish and waterbirds |
- Ballast-water example in source: Asian paddle crab introduction to New Zealand; distinguish an introduction pathway from proof that every introduced individual carries white-spot virus.
## Costs and response
- Original study estimated **US$127.3 billion observed, high-reliability costs, 1960–2020 (~₹8.3 trillion)**; the PDF's **₹830 crore** conversion is wrong. Total costs under the study's wider dataset were US$182.6 billion. Sparse coverage means recorded costs underestimate impacts. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10530-022-02780-z).
- Source's >2,000 count concerns alien species, not proof all are invasive; its 3% economic-study coverage and aquatic-cost comparisons depend on dataset/scope.
- Strengthen port/airport quarantine, import risk assessments, surveillance/early warning, real-time tracking and rapid control; combine climate/trade data under cross-sector **One Biosecurity** cooperation. Assess consequences after introduction as well as before imports.
- CBD obligations and **Kunming–Montreal Target 6** address pathways, priority species and sites. Target: **reduce introduction/establishment rates of other known/potential IAS by at least 50% by 2030**, not a generic 50% reduction in every impact. [CBD Target 6](https://www.cbd.int/gbf/targets/6).
- Plant Quarantine Order 2003 and biodiversity planning are relevant Indian tools. Source's claimed operational **NAPINVAS** and Indian **National Invasive Species Information Centre (NISIC)** are unverified; do not memorise as confirmed national institutions.
- **PYQ 2023:** Invasive Species Specialist Group belongs to **IUCN**, under its Species Survival Commission; supports the Global Invasive Species Database and management/policy knowledge.
