Nilgiri wood pigeon: forest ecology and climate risk

Editable notes

Identity and conservation

  • Columba elphinstonii, endemic to India's Western Ghats; a large pigeon with a dark chestnut/grey body and distinctive black-and-white patterned hindneck. Mainly fruit-eating, it disperses seeds in moist evergreen/semi-evergreen forest and shola-associated wooded valleys.
  • Core ranges include Nilgiri and Anamalai hills; populations also occur in northern Western Ghats and isolated hill forests such as Biligirirangan/Nandi. Endemism concerns the species' natural range, not confinement to one named hill.
  • IUCN Least Concern, 2025 assessment publication; older sources showing Vulnerable are superseded. A global category does not imply absence of habitat/climate threats. IUCN assessment.
  • The issue reports modelling of late-century habitat contraction/fragmentation under climate change. These are scenario-based projections, not observed extinction by a fixed date. Hunting, forest conversion, plantations and infrastructure already reduce habitat; conserve connected native forest and microclimatic refuges. Wildlife (Protection) Act Schedule I protection is separate from the IUCN risk category. Goa Forest Department management plan.
