# SAIME: mangrove restoration with sustainable aquaculture

## October 2025: mangrove-linked aquaculture recognised
- **SAIME—Sustainable Aquaculture in Mangrove Ecosystems**, developed by Kolkata-based **Nature Environment and Wildlife Society (NEWS)**, combines brackish-water shrimp farming with mangrove restoration in the Indian Sundarbans. NEWS announced FAO **Global Technical Recognition** in Sustainable Aquatic Food Systems for **15 October 2025**, during FAO's 80th anniversary/World Food Forum in Rome. [NEWS announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/newsfornature_fao-worldfoodforum-sustainability-activity-7384203711939751936-TUxf), [FAO ceremony listing](https://www.fao.org/forestry/all-events/5/en).
- Source sites: **Chaital, North 24 Parganas; Madhabpur, South 24 Parganas, West Bengal**; **42 farmers/29.84 ha**, with **5–30% mangrove cover** integrated into farming areas and pond embankments. Mangrove litter supports pond nutrient/food webs; the model features **black tiger shrimp, Penaeus monodon**. This does not mean shrimp universally need only leaf litter and no managed nutrition.
- Source reports **over 100% increase in annual average net profit**, associated with reduced inputs; this is project experience, not guaranteed income for every adopting farmer. Mangrove habitat, nursery functions, carbon storage, nutrient cycling and coastal buffering can align restoration with livelihoods, rather than clearing habitat for intensive monoculture.
- Sundarbans occupy the **Ganga–Brahmaputra–Meghna delta across India/Bangladesh**. The Indian **Sundarbans National Park** entered UNESCO's list in **1987**; Bangladesh's Sundarbans property was inscribed separately in **1997**, so 1987 is not the designation date of the whole transboundary delta.
- Replication requires suitable tidal hydrology/native species, farmer training, secure tenure, ecological monitoring, fisheries/coastal-policy coordination and credible markets/certification. Buffering does not guarantee safety from every cyclone or sea-level rise. Links: SDGs **1,2,13,14,15** and FAO's Blue Transformation. Source's “National Fisheries Policy 2020” was a draft, and generic “Blue Economy Mission” should not be assumed a specific notified scheme.
