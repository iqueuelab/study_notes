# Coal-power pollution and rice–wheat yields

## Study and estimated agricultural cost
- Stanford-led study used electricity generation/wind data for **144 Indian power stations** and satellite NO₂ observations to estimate coal-related pollution over cropland; effects detected up to **100 km** away.
- Eliminating the modelled coal emissions during important **January–February and September–October** growing periods could increase annual rice output value by **US$420 million** and wheat by **US$400 million**. These are counterfactual estimates, not realised compensation or measured nationwide uniform losses.
- Some exposed areas show yield effects approaching **10%**; Chhattisgarh has particularly high exposure, with substantial estimated gains in parts of West Bengal and Madhya Pradesh. [Stanford study explanation](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2025/02/coal-emissions-cost-india-millions-crop-damages).
## Pollutants and appropriate controls
| Pollutant | Impact/control distinction |
|---|---|
| NOₓ, including NO₂ | Plant stress; ozone/secondary-particle formation; combustion controls and suitable denitrification technologies |
| SO₂ | Acidification/secondary particles; **flue-gas desulfurisation (FGD)** uses sorbents to remove sulphur compounds |
| Particulates/fly ash | Health, light/soil/water effects; **electrostatic precipitators** remove particles, not gaseous NO₂ |
| CO₂ | Climate forcing; efficiency and cleaner energy reduce emissions |
- Supercritical/ultra-supercritical plants improve thermal efficiency; they do not eliminate all pollutants or substitute for pollutant-specific controls.
## Policy application
- Prioritise high-exposure plants near cropland; strengthen emission monitoring/enforcement, cleaner power, ash management and agriculture–environment coordination.
- Include food/feed and farmer-income losses alongside health/climate costs. Agroforestry and resilient farming complement emissions reduction rather than neutralise the source.
- Source’s 2024 Hunger Index rank **105/127** is contextual, not evidence that coal caused that ranking; a Ministry of Coal budget increase likewise does not directly measure electricity output or plant pollution.

## Combustion and pollution-control recall
- Flue/stack gas is combustion exhaust. **Nitrogen (N₂), not nitrogen oxides, forms its major fraction** in typical air-fired combustion; water vapour, CO₂, oxygen and smaller pollutant components depend on fuel/process. The PDF's “mostly nitrogen oxides” is incorrect.
- FGD commonly uses calcium/sodium alkaline reagents to remove SO₂; suitable systems can remove about **95%**. **FGD gypsum** is a by-product of relevant wet limestone/oxidation systems; an unsupported worldwide claim of drastic production decline should not be memorised.
- SO₂-derived acid deposition harms vegetation, aquatic ecosystems and soils and accelerates corrosion/stone weathering. Particulate controls and gaseous NOₓ controls differ, as explained above.
- PYQs: **2011 CO₂ + nitrogen oxides + sulphur oxides from coal combustion → (d)**; **2020 coal ash heavy metals, SO₂/NOₓ emissions and high Indian coal ash → all correct (d)**; **2021 furnace oil is refinery-derived, used for power and produces sulphur emissions → all correct (d)**. Ash content varies by coal grade; 35–45% is not universal.
