# Kanpur heavy-metal contamination and environmental health

## November 2025: NGT seeks exposure mapping
- The issue reports **NGT directions to Uttar Pradesh** after chromium/mercury were found in residents' samples in **Kanpur Nagar, Kanpur Dehat and Fatehpur**, linked to contaminated industrial areas including **Rania and Rakhi Mandi**.
- Reported chromium exceedances: **95.7% of 514 tested in Kanpur Nagar; 71.9% of 214 in Kanpur Dehat; 85.96% of 171 in Fatehpur**. These are proportions of tested samples, not district-wide prevalence, and a blood test alone does not prove every pathway/source of exposure.
- The State was asked to map affected **villages/ULBs, people and exposure routes**, and submit a timeline/action plan within **two weeks**, including medical facilities, staffing, laboratories and testing capacity. Assessment spans **food chain, groundwater, surface water and air**.
- An implementation schedule was sought for **22 earlier recommendations of 1 July 2025**. The PDF expressly says their detailed list is unavailable and guesses its contents; do not represent those guesses as actual tribunal directions. Safe drinking water, screening, source control, remediation and compensation assessment are sensible policy responses requiring the actual order for legal attribution.
- Metals persist as elements, although **speciation, mobility, bioavailability and toxicity change**; “all remain in every body for decades” is too broad. **Hexavalent chromium** is carcinogenic; mercury is neurotoxic, particularly concerning for child development and pregnancy. Total chromium is not automatically all Cr(VI).
- **NGT Act 2010**, precaution and polluter-pays principles link industrial-waste compliance with public health. Coordinate pollution boards, health departments and local bodies; track exposure reduction and accountability. Practice answer: Chromium-6 **carcinogenic properties (b)**; unyearred practice, not PYQ.
