# Ranthambore Tiger Reserve

## Location and ecology
- Eastern **Rajasthan**, Sawai Madhopur–Karauli landscape, at the meeting of **Aravalli and Vindhyan** terrain; **Banas** to the north, **Chambal** to the south.
- Among the original nine Project Tiger reserves in **1973**; Ranthambore National Park declared **1980**. Reserve landscape includes **Kailadevi** and **Sawai Mansingh** sanctuaries; the National Chambal Sanctuary is a distinct protected area, not automatically a reserve component. [NTCA assessment](https://ntca.gov.in/assets/uploads/Reports/MEE/Evaluation_reports_TRs.pdf).
- Dry deciduous woodland and grasslands; **Dhok (Anogeissus pendula)** dominant. Tigers, leopards, sloth bears, striped hyenas and jackals; prey include sambar, chital, nilgai and wild boar; diverse terrestrial/water birds.
- Ranthambore Fort is part of UNESCO's **Hill Forts of Rajasthan**, within the park landscape.
## Coexistence
- The issue reports a fatal tiger attack on a child: proximity to wildlife requires rapid local response and conflict prevention.
- Livestock grazing, settlements, habitat fragmentation, poaching and excessive tourism can increase pressure. Combine corridor protection, managed visitor access, community eco-development, early warning and timely locally applicable compensation.
