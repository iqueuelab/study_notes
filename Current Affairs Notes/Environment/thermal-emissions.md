# Thermal power emissions: FGD and regulatory accountability

## Pollutants and technology

- Coal/lignite stations emit **PM, SO₂, NOx and CO₂**. **7 December 2015** standards introduced/revised pollutant limits; original compliance target **2017** later extended.
- **FGD = flue-gas desulphurisation**, removes SO₂, not CO₂; auxiliary power, water, reagents/by-products and plant retrofits add costs. Lower-sulphur Indian coal does not imply zero SO₂ or uniform exposure.
- SO₂ contributes to respiratory harm and secondary aerosols; climate mitigation and local air-pollution control overlap but are distinct objectives.

## February 2025 regulatory snapshot

- **30 December 2024 notification** extended differentiated deadlines: **A to 2027, B to 2028, C to 2029**; source's single “all until 2027” is incomplete. Category A proximity to NCR/million-plus cities; B critically polluted/non-attainment locations; C others. [Original notification](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_CEN_16_18_00011_198629_1517807327582&filename=amendment_on_tpp_for_extension_dated_30.12.2024.pdf&type=notification).
- **Later change, 11 July 2025:** A compliance by 2027; B applicability case-by-case on expert appraisal; C exemption subject to stack-height conditions by 2029. Keep this separate from February source analysis. [MoEF parliamentary reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2152127&lang=2&reg=48).

## Governance issues

- Retrofit lead times, technology/supply constraints, tariff pass-through and disagreement over uniform versus exposure-based requirements must be assessed against public-health gains.
- Installing equipment ≠ operating it. Require verified continuous-emissions monitoring, public compliance data, independent audits and penalties; protect consumers from paying for unused assets.
- **Polluter pays** assigns environmental prevention/remediation costs to the responsible entity; final consumer price incidence can still arise through lawful tariff mechanisms.
- SO₂ cap-and-trade is an air-pollution instrument; **carbon pricing does not directly replace SO₂ controls**. Risk-based local standards, enforceable milestones and cleaner generation/storage should complement each other.
- Source statements about IIT/NEERI studies and blanket absence of data need study/scope verification; FGD energy use does not by itself make every pollution-control installation incompatible with climate goals.

## Practice recall

- 2015 standards true; high-sulphur Indian-coal generalisation and claim of uniform CEA support false. Questions supplied here are undated practice, not PYQs.

## Crop damage and pollutant-specific controls

- Stanford-linked modelling estimated coal-plant NO₂ impacts on nearby rice/wheat yields, potentially up to 10%; counterfactual output gains valued aroundUS$420 million rice/US$400 million wheat are estimates, not realised revenue. [Study summary](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2025/02/coal-emissions-cost-india-millions-crop-damages).
- **ESP→particulates; FGD→SO₂; low-NOx burners/SCR/SNCR→NOx.** An ESP does not remove NO₂ by itself.
- Calibrated continuous monitoring, independent checks and published data support health/crop protection. Budget growth does not directly measure generation or pollution growth.
