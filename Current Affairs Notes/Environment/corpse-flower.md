# Titan arum: corpse-flower pollination

## Identity and life cycle

- **Amorphophallus titanum**, correcting PDF **“titanium”**; Titan arum / **Bunga bangkai**, native to **Sumatra, Indonesia**, IUCN **Endangered**. January Sydney flowering specimen **Putricia** drew large crowds.
- **Inflorescence**, not one giant individual flower: **spadix** bears many small flowers, enclosed by a purple **spathe**. May reach about **3 m**; bloom brief, roughly **24–48 hours**, interval variable and sometimes many years.
- Underground **corm** stores energy built through leaf cycles; bloom needs adequate reserves, not a fixed once-yearly/once-decade clock.
- Female flowers receptive **before male pollen release**, reducing self-pollination; carrion smell/heat attracts **flies and beetles**, encouraging pollen transfer. [US Botanic Garden](https://www.usbg.gov/gardens-plants/corpse-flowers).
- Source volatile list: **dimethyl disulfide/trisulfide, methanethiol, methyl thioacetate, 3-methylbutanal, isovaleric acid**; odour is a mixture, not a single compound.
- Botanical propagation/pollen exchange helps conserve genetic diversity; rare garden bloom does not imply natural population recovery.
