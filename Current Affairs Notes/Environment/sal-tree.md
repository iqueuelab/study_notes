# Sal tree: ecology and pollution tolerance

## November 2025: air-pollution study discussion
- The issue attributes to **Himachal Pradesh University, Shimla** a study highlighting **Sal, Shorea robusta, family Dipterocarpaceae**, for pollution tolerance and leaf-surface dust retention. The supplied extract gives no original study citation or comparative protocol; “best natural air purifier” is a reported claim, not an established universal ranking.
- Sal is a large, seasonally leaf-shedding tree of tropical/subtropical moist and dry deciduous forests. Source distribution: **Uttarakhand, UP Terai, Jharkhand, Odisha, MP, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal**, and **Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh**. Broad growth figures **30–35 m, 1,000–3,000 mm rainfall, up to ~1,500 m** are indicative site ranges rather than rigid species limits.
- **Leaf surfaces capture particles → canopy alters local microclimate; roots stabilise soil; biomass stores carbon; forest structure supports fauna.** Pollutant deposition/tolerance differs from destroying pollutants. Chlorophyll's photosynthetic role does not prove direct universal removal of CO/SO₂, or a measured citywide AQI improvement.
- Economic uses: durable hardwood for construction/railway sleepers and **Sal dammar resin** for incense/varnish; leaf-based livelihoods and habitat value complement timber. Source cultural association: Sal trees in Buddhist accounts of Queen Māyā/Buddha's birth, and sacred traditions.
- Choose urban planting by native suitability, available rooting space, water, biodiversity and ventilation needs. Planting complements emission control; source claims of minimal care, pollution resistance and century lifespan depend on setting, not guarantees for every planted tree.
