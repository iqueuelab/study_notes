# Water hyacinth: invasion, livelihoods and collective control

## Kerala backwaters and livelihood impacts
- **Water hyacinth, Eichhornia crassipes /Pontederia crassipes**, South American floating invasive with lilac flowers, introduced ornamentally during the colonial period. Avoid the source practice's unsupported named-introducer claim; it is not native to India.
- Dense mats in **Vembanad–Kuttanad, Kerala** obstruct irrigation, boats/nets, fish habitat and tourism; shade vegetation and impede surface gas exchange. Decomposition consumes oxygen and can emit methane; oxygen is not simply unable to “reach” water by a single mechanism.
- Source infestation estimate **over2 lakh hectares nationally** lacks survey year/method; a source-period estimate, not a current measured inventory. Its **methane25×CO₂** figure uses an older100-year warming metric and should not be treated as timeless or a toxicity comparison.
- Odisha SHGs use dried biomass for baskets/furniture; source Assam/West Bengal examples include paper/biogas. Compost/textile/biofuel uses require contamination checks and viable processing/markets; biomass enterprise must not create incentives to spread the weed.
- Combine nutrient-load reduction, early detection, locally suitable removal, safe biomass handling, monitoring and tested biological approaches; short clearance drives allow regrowth. Coordinate agriculture/fisheries/irrigation/environment agencies with clear accountability, mechanisation, local participation and shared research.
- Source JainUniversity, Kochi workshop proposed outreach/discussion paper and science–local-knowledge integration; these were proposed actions. National policy/innovation incentives/private investment are editorial recommendations, not an enacted new scheme.
- **2018 PYQ Prosopis juliflora:(b)**, reduces biodiversity; arid/semi-arid invasive thickets can affect pastoral livelihoods. This is a separate invasive plant, not water hyacinth. Water-hyacinth practice intended **2 only(b)**; its fourth option contains a stray statement3.
