# Oarfish, folklore and earthquake evidence

## Species
- Giant oarfish **Regalecus glesne**, family **Regalecidae**, order **Lampriformes**; long, ribbon-shaped silvery body, red dorsal fin; among the longest bony fishes, reported lengths about **11 m**.
- Tropical/temperate oceans, commonly deep midwater; source **200–1,000 m** is typical rather than an absolute range. Feeds on plankton/small animals; dorsal-fin undulation and near-vertical posture are distinctive.
- Rare beach/surface sightings may involve illness or ocean conditions; an unusual sighting does not by itself identify the cause.
## Myth versus testable claim
- Japanese **Ryūgū no tsukai**, “messenger from the sea god's palace”; folklore links appearances to earthquakes/tsunamis.
- The fact sightings preceded a famous earthquake does **not establish predictive power**; need systematic records including sightings without earthquakes and earthquakes without sightings.
- Source refers to **2019 Japanese analysis** finding no significant relationship; no validated oarfish-based earthquake warning system follows from this material.
- Climate change, pollution, gas release and mining are possible environmental concerns, not proved causes of the specific Mexican sighting.
- Use authoritative seismic monitoring and scientifically supported early warning; warning detects an event already underway, **not exact advance prediction from animal omens**.
- Respect cultural narratives while explaining evidence; source's “not endangered because rare interactions” is not a valid reasoning method for assessing conservation status.
