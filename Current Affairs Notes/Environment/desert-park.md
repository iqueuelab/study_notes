# Desert National Park and Great Indian Bustard

## Location and ecosystem

- **Thar, Rajasthan, Jaisalmer and Barmer districts**; **about 3,162 km²**, source rounds to over 3,160. [Rajasthan protected-area record](https://forest.rajasthan.gov.in/content/raj/forest/en/aboutus/departmental-wings/wildlife/details-of-protected-area-.html).
- Arid landscape: rocky outcrops, salt-lake bottoms, interdunal areas, fixed dunes and open desert grass/scrub; **dunes about 20%**, not 50%. Source **under 100-mm annual rainfall** is not a universal park-wide climatic threshold.
- **Human settlements occur within the landscape**; conservation must account for pastoral/local livelihoods.
- **Great Indian Bustard**, critically endangered; source sighting of **12** is a local observation, not a national population estimate or by itself proof of population recovery.
- Other source fauna: **chinkara, desert/Bengal fox, Indian wolf, desert cat, hare, spiny-tailed/monitor lizards, saw-scaled viper**. Broad reptile lists do not prove every species equally abundant at every site.
- **Khejri, Prosopis cineraria**, ecological/cultural importance, including Bishnoi conservation traditions; regional fossil-rich geological heritage, Jurassic-era deposits cited.

## Correct misplaced park details

- **Rajbagh, Malik Talao and Padam Talao** are associated with **Ranthambore**, not lakes to memorise for Desert National Park. Source's **Dhok/Ronj/Salai/palm** list also mixes generic/other-park flora; prioritise desert vegetation and verified local records. [Ranthambore management plan](https://forest.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/raj/forest/ForestDepartment/PDFs/Department%20Wing/Wildlife/tigerconservationplan/TCP%20RTR.pdf).
- GIB conservation: habitat connectivity, low-disturbance breeding areas, safe power-line planning and community partnership; balance infrastructure with bird survival.
