# Bihar Ramsar sites and wetland wise use

## Gokul Jalashay and Udaipur Jheel
- Ramsar Secretariat announced **26 September 2025** that these Bihar oxbow lakes became India's **92nd/93rd Ramsar sites**. **93** is the September snapshot, not a permanent present total; source's “third globally” ranking is unsupported.
- **Gokul Jalashay, Buxar, site 2576:** southern Ganga edge; flood pulses expose marsh/agricultural land in dry months and inundate it in monsoon. Flood buffer; **50+ bird species** in lake/surroundings, pre-monsoon feeding/breeding areas; fishing, farming, irrigation and community weed/catchment cleaning.
- **Udaipur Jheel, West Champaran, site 2577:** bordered north/west by **Udaipur Wildlife Sanctuary**, surrounds a village; **280+ plant species**, including endemic Indian perennial **Alysicarpus roxburghianus**. Winters **~35 migratory bird species**, including **Vulnerable common pochard, Aythya ferina**. Illegal fishing, intensive farming and agrochemicals threaten it. [Ramsar announcement](https://www.ramsar.org/news/india-names-its-92nd-93rd-wetlands-international-importance).
## Convention and PYQ corrections
- **Adopted 1971 at Ramsar, Iran; effective 1975; India party 1982.** Wise use maintains ecological character through ecosystem approaches within sustainable development: conservation with sustainable use, not exclusion of all people, tourism alone or delayed exploitation (**2010 PYQ d**).
- Ramsar is a **legally binding framework treaty**; it commits parties to plan wise use of **all wetlands**, conserve/manage designated sites and cooperate internationally. Lack of a coercive sanctions system does not mean no treaty obligations. Correct PDF's “not legally binding/only Ramsar sites matter” explanation. [UNESCO treaty explanation](https://whc.unesco.org/en/faq/221).
- **2019 PYQ: 3 only (c)** in the supplied key. Read its precise wording: it does not justify the source's broad claim that nonlisted wetlands lack protection. **2010 Wetlands Rules**, issued under Environment (Protection) Act 1986, included authority-determined drainage/catchment areas; **2017 Rules** superseded them. Statutory notification is distinct from a Ramsar recommendation and present rule coverage.
- Embedded comparative graphics recall **Khichan/Menar (Rajasthan), Sakkarakottai/Therthangal (Tamil Nadu), Khecheopalri (Sikkim), Udhwa (Jharkhand)**; these are earlier additions, not six additional October designations. Oxbow formation: meander neck cut-off → abandoned curved lake.
