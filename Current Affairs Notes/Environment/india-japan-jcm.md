# India–Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism and Article 6

## August 2025 cooperation agreement
- India and Japan **signed the JCM MoC in New Delhi on 7 August 2025**, publicly announced **29 August** alongside the PM's visit; distinguish signing from announcement. [Japan foreign ministry](https://www.mofa.go.jp/press/release/pressite_000001_01619.html).
- Low-carbon technology, investment, capacity building and local manufacturing partnerships support verified emission reductions/removals. Japan and host-country benefits/credits must follow agreed rules rather than both claiming the same reduction.
- **Paris Agreement Article 6.2** supports cooperative approaches involving internationally transferred mitigation outcomes; transparent accounting, authorisation and corresponding adjustments avoid double counting and protect NDC integrity.
- India's **National Designated Authority** approves/evaluates relevant projects; ministerial authority to negotiate other Article 6 agreements is distinct from every project already receiving credits. [MoEFCC announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2161892&lang=2&reg=3).
- India's updated 2022 NDC: **45% emissions-intensity reduction from 2005 by 2030**, about **50% cumulative installed electric-power capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030**, additional **2.5–3 billion tonnes CO₂-equivalent sink** through forest/tree cover. Capacity differs from electricity generation; an intensity target is not a 45% absolute emissions cut.
- INDCs were pre-Paris intended contributions; NDC submission/updating and domestic measures are treaty obligations, while achievement of each nationally set target is not an internationally enforced emissions quota. **2016 PYQ answer (b): national climate action plans**.

## January 2026: Article 6 opportunities and implementation
| Provision | Revision distinction |
|---|---|
| **6.2** | Decentralised cooperative approaches/ITMOs, with authorisation, reporting and corresponding adjustments as applicable |
| **6.4** | UN-supervised **Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism**, methodologies, verification, registration/issuance and sustainable-development safeguards |
| **6.8** | **Non-market cooperation**; Article 6 is not solely a trading mechanism |
- **COP29, 2024**, completed important guidance/standards, enabling implementation; “fully operationalised” does not mean every registry, project approval and credit issuance was instantly completed. [UNFCCC explanation](https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement/the-paris-agreement/cooperative-implementation/article-6).
- Existing **India–Japan JCM August 2025** agreement illustrates cooperation; signing does not authorise every project. Private finance/technology can support renewable/storage, green hydrogen, offshore wind, biogas/CBG, fuel cells/e-mobility, SAF and CCUS, subject to India's eligible-activity rules and project additionality.
- Editorial priorities: clarify authorisation/adjustments, coordinate approvals, strengthen credible MRV, build removal projects such as **biochar/enhanced rock weathering**, and share capacity with developing countries. Source compares **~1,600-day Indian approvals with <400 elsewhere in Asia**, but supplies no comparable project cohort/time definition; retain as an attributed bottleneck claim, not a universal regulatory deadline.
- Avoid simplistic CDM-versus-Paris table: both can involve projects; Kyoto CDM did have methodologies/verification, and Paris safeguards still require implementation. Credit transfers must preserve NDC integrity; markets supplement domestic cuts, not guarantee profitable exports, growth without tradeoffs or automatic technology transfer.
