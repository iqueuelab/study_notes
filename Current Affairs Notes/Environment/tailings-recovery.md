# Tailings: mineral recovery and storage safety

## January 2026: recovering minerals from mine waste
- The issue reports the first national tailings framework. Official title: **Policy for Exploration of Critical Minerals in New Projects and Recovery of Critical Minerals from Overburden, Dumps and Tailings of Existing Mines, 2025**; the **2025 title** should be distinguished from its **January 2026 publication/reporting**. It covers new exploration and secondary sources, not fresh ore alone. [Ministry of Mines policy](https://mines.gov.in/admin/download/696db55d0647f1768797533.pdf).
- **Ore → crushing/grinding → beneficiation/extraction → tailings**: finely divided residual rock, unrecovered minerals and process residues. Often conveyed/stored as a water-based slurry; paste, thickened and filtered forms also exist. Tailings are distinct from unprocessed overburden and from smelting **slag/anode slimes**, although all may offer secondary recovery opportunities.
- Companion elements can accompany the principal metal: copper-processing streams may contain **selenium, tellurium, molybdenum, cobalt, rhenium, gold/silver**; zinc streams **germanium, indium, cadmium, silver**. Occurrence is deposit/process-specific; not every copper or zinc tailings pile contains all these at recoverable grades.
- Mapping inventories, sampling/assaying, recovery technology, costs and environmental safeguards determine viable supply. Critical-mineral recovery can reduce imports and virgin extraction while serving circular-economy goals; an announced policy is not proof commercial extraction has begun everywhere.
## Tailings safety and PYQ linkage
- Facility design must consider **topography, rainfall/water balance, seismicity, mineral chemistry and nearby communities**. Dams/embankments and surface impoundments need monitoring, sound drainage, stability checks and safe closure; failures can cause inundation and contamination. Residual chemicals/metals and acid generation vary by material.
- **2018 sand-mining PYQ: (b), groundwater pollution and lowering of water table**; lowering coastal riverbeds can facilitate saline intrusion, not necessarily reduce salinity. This concerns river-sediment removal, distinct from mineral recovery from tailings. Practice tailings key **(a), statement 1 only**: the policy includes secondary sources.
