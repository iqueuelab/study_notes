# Nilgiri wood pigeon: forest ecology and climate risk

## Identity and conservation
- **Columba elphinstonii**, endemic to India's **Western Ghats**; a large pigeon with a dark chestnut/grey body and distinctive black-and-white patterned hindneck. Mainly fruit-eating, it disperses seeds in moist evergreen/semi-evergreen forest and shola-associated wooded valleys.
- Core ranges include **Nilgiri and Anamalai hills**; populations also occur in northern Western Ghats and isolated hill forests such as Biligirirangan/Nandi. Endemism concerns the species' natural range, not confinement to one named hill.
- **IUCN Least Concern, 2025 assessment publication**; older sources showing Vulnerable are superseded. A global category does not imply absence of habitat/climate threats. [IUCN assessment](https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2025-2.RLTS.T22690173A250437157.en).
- The issue reports modelling of late-century habitat contraction/fragmentation under climate change. These are scenario-based projections, not observed extinction by a fixed date. Hunting, forest conversion, plantations and infrastructure already reduce habitat; conserve connected native forest and microclimatic refuges. Wildlife (Protection) Act **Schedule I** protection is separate from the IUCN risk category. [Goa Forest Department management plan](https://forest.goa.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/Management-Plan-of-Bhagwan-Mahavir-WIldlife-Sanctuary-and-National-Park.pdf).
