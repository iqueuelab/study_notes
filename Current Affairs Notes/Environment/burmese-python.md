# Burmese python: invasion biology and robotic decoys

## Florida control experiment, August 2025
- **Python bivittatus**, a large non-venomous constrictor native to South/Southeast Asia, including eastern/northeastern India; formerly treated as a subspecies of the Indian python, now recognised separately. Mainly solitary/nocturnal, strong swimmer; inhabits grasslands, wetlands, forests, mangroves and river valleys. Females are generally larger; source maxima around **20 feet/115 kg** are size illustrations, not typical adults.
- Introduced through the exotic-pet pathway in **Florida/Everglades**, where predation disrupts native mammal/bird/reptile communities. Few effective population controls does not mean literally no animal ever preys on a python.
- University of Florida and South Florida Water Management District researchers are testing **robotic marsh-rabbit decoys**: motors/heat/solar power mimic prey to attract elusive snakes for detection and removal. It is an experimental aid to population control, not demonstrated ecosystem-wide eradication. [University of Florida](https://biology.ufl.edu/featured/2025/dr-chris-dutton-and-his-robo-bunnies-tackle-the-burmese-python-problem-in-the-everglades/).
- Source lists **IUCN Vulnerable** in its native range, threatened by habitat loss/hunting and trade. Native-range conservation and invasive-population control can apply simultaneously; prevent releases and monitor pathways.
