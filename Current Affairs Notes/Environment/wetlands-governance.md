# Wetlands: governance, Ramsar sites and traditional knowledge

## Wetlands as public goods: 3 February 2026
- Wetlands support **water storage, flood moderation, biodiversity, fisheries/livelihoods, climate resilience and cultural practices**. Treat them as living ecosystems and shared resilience infrastructure rather than vacant development land or ornamental ponds.
- **World Wetlands Day: 2 February**; 2026 theme **Wetlands and traditional knowledge: Celebrating cultural heritage**. Examples in the issue: Tamil Nadu's cascading **kulam** tanks, Wayanad's **kenis** and Srikakulam fishing practices; traditional knowledge can complement hydrology and ecological science. [Convention campaign](https://www.ramsar.org/our-work/activities/world-wetlands-day).
- Main pressures: encroachment/obsolete land records; dams, road embankments, channel blockage, sand mining and groundwater extraction; untreated sewage/industrial effluent/agricultural runoff; coastal development, cyclones and sea-level rise; weak staffing, enforcement and interdepartmental coordination.
- Response: public maps and participatory boundary notification; restore feeder channels and catchments; treat wastewater **before** entry; regulate extraction/development; protect mangrove/floodplain buffers; train wetland authorities in hydrology, GIS, ecology, law and community engagement. Constructed treatment wetlands can supplement engineered treatment; natural wetlands should not become sewage sinks.
- **Wetlands Rules 2017** under the Environment (Protection) Act 1986, **NPCA** conservation support, coastal regulation and the Ramsar framework have different scopes. State/UT wetland authorities need capacity and measurable ecological outcomes. Rule coverage has specific exclusions/overlapping legal regimes; Ramsar's broad wetland definition does not mean every river channel or irrigation tank falls under the 2017 Rules. [MoEFCC implementation guidance](https://www.moef.gov.in/uploads/2020/01/final-version-and-printed-wetland-guidelines-rules-2017-03.01.20.pdf).
- The editorial's **40% lost/50% degraded** figures lack a specified reproducible national baseline here; do not present them as official census results. Monitor area, water quality, hydroperiod, biodiversity and livelihood outcomes using satellite time series and community evidence. The supplied 250-word/15-mark question is **practice**, not a UPSC PYQ.
