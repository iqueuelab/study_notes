# Contaminated sites: remediation rules and pollution boards

## Rules notified 24 July 2025
- **Environment Protection (Management of Contaminated Sites) Rules, 2025**, under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**. A contaminated site is formally declared by **SPCB/PCC** after detailed assessment finds prescribed contaminants above thresholds; suspected site is an earlier screening category. Legacy industrial dumping, chemical spills and waste handling/storage can leave contamination. [Gazette record](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/en/node/7336), [ministry clarification of July rules](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2157518&lang=2&reg=48).
- Local bodies/district administrations identify and list suspected sites, including public complaints; preliminary and detailed scientific assessment precedes a remediation design and follow-up monitoring. Issue describes **half-yearly reporting**, preliminary assessment within **90 days** and detailed assessment within **three months of probable-site listing**; these are specific procedural stages, not a universal three-month cleanup promise.
- **Polluter pays:** responsible persons bear remediation cost; orphan/untraceable/defunct polluters require public funding arrangements and later recovery where possible. Death/injury can attract the **BNS**, alongside environmental liability; every contaminated site is not automatically a proven criminal case.
- Issue inventory: **103 identified sites; remediation initiated at seven**. 'Initiated' does not mean cleaned; inventory is dated and differs from newly confirmed sites under the rules.
## Boards and implementation
- **CPCB:** statutory body constituted in **September 1974 under Water Act 1974**; Air Act 1981 and EPA functions supplement its mandate. Coordinates State boards, sets/supports standards, compiles pollution data, trains and advises.
- **SPCBs:** State statutory boards; inspect, monitor water/air, administer industrial **Consent to Establish/Operate**, and enforce waste rules. **PCCs** perform corresponding UT functions. Their mandates differ from local waste collection duties.
- Capacity gaps: scientific/legal vacancies, expanding mandates, weak coordination, inconsistent enforcement. Priorities: transparent inventories, site-risk ranking, competent laboratories, cost recovery, community health protection and post-remediation monitoring.

## Scope caution
- Issue distinguishes hazardous industrial chemical spills and polluted groundwater from streams addressed under separate regimes (radioactive waste, mining operations, marine oil and solid-waste processing sites). Apply the exact notified exclusions rather than assuming every landfill or every polluted site follows the same rule.
- Existing environmental duties preceded 2025; the source's “no legal process until now” overstates the novelty. These rules provide a specific contaminated-site identification/remediation framework, not the first environmental liability in India.

## NGT versus pollution-control boards: PYQ recall
- **2018 question: (b), statement 2 only.** Both are statutory: **NGT Act 2010** and **CPCB under Water Act 1974**, supplemented by Air Act 1981. NGT adjudicates environmental disputes; CPCB's pollution-control/coordination role differs. The PDF places this question after immigration, but its content belongs to environment.
