# Madras hedgehog and dryland habitat conservation

## Species and habitat
- **Madras/bare-bellied hedgehog, Paraechinus nudiventris**, locally **Mulleli**: small nocturnal mammal endemic to southern India. Spines are modified hairs; the belly lacks the protective spiny covering. Curling exposes the spines and shields vulnerable parts.
- Uses dry scrub, thorny vegetation, grasslands and agricultural margins; eats insects and other small invertebrates, with some plant food. Solitary behaviour and nocturnality make it difficult to observe. **Least Concern** is the global Red List category, not evidence every local population is secure. [Mammal Diversity Database](https://www.mammaldiversity.org/taxon/1003821/).
- The issue reports a **Tamil Nadu Forest Department scientific study in Theri red-sand-dune forests, Thoothukudi**. Avoid calling this the first scientific work ever: the Department's **2024 research proceedings** already document Theri surveys and 2023 sampling. Protect dryland habitat and investigate hunting linked to unproven medicinal beliefs. [Forest Department research proceedings](https://www.aiwc.res.in/admin-assets/uploads/handbook/57070.pdf).
## Behaviour and source correction
- **2021 PYQ: hedgehog and pangolin—1 and 3 (d)** roll up to protect vulnerable parts; marmots rely on burrows/vigilance. Pangolin protection uses **keratin scales**, whereas hedgehogs have spines; both are mammals, not reptiles.
- Source practice statement 2 says Least Concern **“due to rapid population decline”**. The supplied **both-correct** key overlooks this false causal clause; do not memorise it. Least Concern means the taxon does not meet higher-risk criteria at assessment, while local threats may still exist. The source's global/Indian hedgehog species totals are taxonomy-dependent and are not a secure identifying feature.
