# Stubble burning, market failure and governance

## Why burning persists
- Rice harvest followed quickly by wheat sowing, combine-harvester residue, labour/machinery costs and weak straw markets make burning an inexpensive private choice despite high social costs.
- Procurement/MSP incentives, water-intensive cropping, delayed paddy calendars and credit dependence interact with the short sowing window. Source interviews describe farmer perceptions of unequal enforcement and middlemen/debt constraints; these qualitative findings are not proof that every farmer faces identical conditions or that MSP never rises.
- Burning adds particulates and gases to regional pollution. Transported smoke combines with local emissions and poor dispersion/temperature inversion. Source **20–40% PM2.5 contribution** is episode/model dependent, not a uniform whole-winter or year-round Delhi share.
## Governmentality and market failure
- **Michel Foucault's governmentality** concerns governing conduct through institutions, knowledge, norms and incentives, including self-regulation; it is broader than simply “non-coercive government.”
- **Externality:** farmer's avoided clearance cost excludes downstream health/environmental damage. **Coordination/market failure:** straw collection, storage, transport and assured demand may be missing even where equipment exists.
- Punishment alone cannot repair absent affordable alternatives; incentives alone require monitoring and reliable service delivery.
## Response chain
- **In situ:** Happy Seeder for sowing into residue, mulching/decomposition and Super Straw Management Systems where appropriate.
- **Ex situ:** balers/collection → storage/transport → biomass pellets, energy, packaging and other viable uses. Paddy straw is not universally suitable animal fodder without treatment/assessment.
- Custom-hiring centres, timely machine availability, direct farmer support, accessible credit, viable diversification/procurement alternatives and stable biomass offtake reduce the private cost of compliance.
- Pair targeted enforcement with farmer participation, local crop/weather calendars and verified outcomes; address urban/industrial/transport emissions concurrently. Assess adoption, fires, farmer costs and ambient pollution rather than subsidies distributed alone.
## 9 May 2025 directive: Parali Protection Force
- CAQM's **Direction 90, dated 9 May 2025**, addressed the 2025 action plan targeting elimination of paddy stubble burning. The 11 May issue reports instructions to **Punjab, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh** to establish district/block **Parali Protection Forces** combining police, agriculture and other officials. This is an instruction to organise enforcement, not proof all forces were already staffed or burning eliminated. [CAQM directions register](https://caqm.nic.in/landingpage/pages/home).
- Farm-level mapping should select feasible in-situ treatment, diversification or ex-situ use. Monitoring includes intensified **late-evening/night patrols**, because burning can evade satellite observation, and penalties/environmental compensation with **red entries in farm records**.
- CAQM began under a **2020 ordinance**; its present statutory basis is the **CAQM Act, 2021**. It coordinates NCR/adjoining-area air-quality management across States and agencies; it is not simply a newly created police force.
- Pair enforcement with timely affordable custom-hiring equipment, extension support, viable diversification, straw collection/storage/markets and farmer participation. Paddy-straw fodder use requires suitability/treatment assessment rather than assuming universal feed quality.
- The source incorrectly describes **PM-KUSUM as a bioenergy programme**: it concerns decentralised renewable power and solar agricultural pumps/pump solarisation. Biomass utilisation belongs with appropriate bioenergy/crop-residue policies. [MNRE scheme](https://mnre.gov.in/en/pradhan-mantri-kisan-urja-suraksha-evam-utthaan-mahabhiyaan-pm-kusum/).
- Assess actual fires, adoption, farmer costs and ambient pollution; avoid claiming a dedicated force alone resolves economic or technical barriers.
