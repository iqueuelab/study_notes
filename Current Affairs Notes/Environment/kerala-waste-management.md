# Kerala waste management and the Vruthi campaign

## Campaign and changing waste
- **Malinya Muktham Nava Keralam**, Waste-Free New Kerala: State/local-government mobilisation for source segregation, treatment and responsible disposal. **Vruthi 2025 Clean Kerala Conclave**, **9–13 April, Kanakakkunnu, Thiruvananthapuram**, showcased waste-management practices; it is associated with the campaign rather than the campaign's formal name. [Kerala LSGD](https://lsgd.kerala.gov.in/en/latest_news/vruthi-2025-the-clean-kerala-conclave/).
- Consumption/urbanisation increased plastic and mixed/non-biodegradable waste beyond older household composting/manure practices. Source agriculture share “<10% GDP” is a broad State-economy context, not a measured waste-generation share.
- **Haritha Karmasena**, local self-governments, schools/colleges/youth, residents/business/worker groups; culture/arts and local competition seek sustained **“My Waste, My Responsibility”** behaviour. Reported sanitation-worker canal death in Thiruvananthapuram, dog bites/zoonotic risks and **Brahmapuram, Kochi, 2023 fire** illustrate health/occupational/ecological stakes; sanitation failure is not the sole proven cause of every disease.
## Centralised/decentralised: choose by waste stream
- Central facilities can deliver scale/professional treatment; **Guruvayur** is source's positive example. Brahmapuram failure shows governance/operation risks, not proof every central facility fails. Local processing supports participation but needs expertise, space and funding; use appropriate hybrid systems with collection/transport links.
- The source caricatures **Swachh Bharat** as only top-down toilet building: **SBM-U 2.0**, launched 2021, explicitly targets **garbage-free cities**, scientific solid-waste processing and sustained sanitation. Kerala's emphasis on local mobilisation can complement national policy. [SBM-U 2.0](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1780913&lang=2&reg=48).
- Priorities: segregation and reliable collection, safe wet-waste processing, material recovery/recycling and hazardous-stream separation; avoid transferring dumping burdens to marginalised communities.
- Strengthen **Extended Producer Responsibility**, verified recycling/end-market links, worker safety/tools/training, professional local capacity and transparent monitoring. State-driven campaign momentum/garbage-free declarations need sustained service-quality evidence; a 100% aspiration is not proof all litter/dumping ended.
- Kerala Urban Policy Commission's professionalisation recommendation and participatory awareness require implementation, finance and accountability to survive beyond a publicity campaign.
