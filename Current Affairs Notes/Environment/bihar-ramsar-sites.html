Bihar Ramsar sites and wetland wise use

Editable notes

Gokul Jalashay and Udaipur Jheel

  • Ramsar Secretariat announced 26 September 2025 that these Bihar oxbow lakes became India's 92nd/93rd Ramsar sites. 93 is the September snapshot, not a permanent present total; source's “third globally” ranking is unsupported.
  • Gokul Jalashay, Buxar, site 2576: southern Ganga edge; flood pulses expose marsh/agricultural land in dry months and inundate it in monsoon. Flood buffer; 50+ bird species in lake/surroundings, pre-monsoon feeding/breeding areas; fishing, farming, irrigation and community weed/catchment cleaning.
  • Udaipur Jheel, West Champaran, site 2577: bordered north/west by Udaipur Wildlife Sanctuary, surrounds a village; 280+ plant species, including endemic Indian perennial Alysicarpus roxburghianus. Winters ~35 migratory bird species, including Vulnerable common pochard, Aythya ferina. Illegal fishing, intensive farming and agrochemicals threaten it. Ramsar announcement.

Convention and PYQ corrections

  • Adopted 1971 at Ramsar, Iran; effective 1975; India party 1982. Wise use maintains ecological character through ecosystem approaches within sustainable development: conservation with sustainable use, not exclusion of all people, tourism alone or delayed exploitation (2010 PYQ d).
  • Ramsar is a legally binding framework treaty; it commits parties to plan wise use of all wetlands, conserve/manage designated sites and cooperate internationally. Lack of a coercive sanctions system does not mean no treaty obligations. Correct PDF's “not legally binding/only Ramsar sites matter” explanation. UNESCO treaty explanation.
  • 2019 PYQ: 3 only (c) in the supplied key. Read its precise wording: it does not justify the source's broad claim that nonlisted wetlands lack protection. 2010 Wetlands Rules, issued under Environment (Protection) Act 1986, included authority-determined drainage/catchment areas; 2017 Rules superseded them. Statutory notification is distinct from a Ramsar recommendation and present rule coverage.
  • Embedded comparative graphics recall Khichan/Menar (Rajasthan), Sakkarakottai/Therthangal (Tamil Nadu), Khecheopalri (Sikkim), Udhwa (Jharkhand); these are earlier additions, not six additional October designations. Oxbow formation: meander neck cut-off → abandoned curved lake.

Gogabil: 94th Indian site, announced 30 October 2025

  • Gogabil Lake (also written Gogabeel), Katihar, Bihar, Ramsar site 2579: Secretariat announcement 30 October 2025, correcting the issue's November declaration label. An oxbow wetland between Ganga and Mahananda that connects to both in floods; its community-managed lake is Bihar's first Community Reserve. Ramsar Secretariat.
  • Wintering birds on the Central Asian Flyway, including Vulnerable common pochard; Vulnerable Wallago attu catfish breeds here. Fishing, cattle use and irrigation coexist with conservation; catchment fertilisers/pesticides threaten ecological character. Community management requires sustainable harvesting, pollution control and hydrological protection.
  • Correct the issue's Bihar list: Kabar Tal/Kanwar Lake are the same site, not two. Six sites at this snapshot: Kabar Tal, Nagi, Nakti, Gokul Jalashay, Udaipur Jheel and Gogabil. Baraila is not one of these six; Gokul Jalashay is in Buxar, not East Champaran. Do not reproduce stale Tamil Nadu totals or unsupported “India third globally” ranking. 94 is an issue-era count, not a timeless present total.
