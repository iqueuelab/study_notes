# Glacier mass loss and cascading impacts

## March 2025 global assessment
- WMO/WGMS: **2022–24 largest three-year glacier loss on record**; **2024 hydrological-year loss about 450 billion tonnes**, the fourth most negative annual result—not the single worst year.
- Cumulative loss since **1975 about 9,000 billion tonnes**; all 19 glacier regions lost mass in 2024 for the third year running. Keep mountain/peripheral glaciers separate from complete Greenland/Antarctic ice sheets. [WMO](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/glacier-melt-will-unleash-avalanche-of-cascading-impacts).
- Glaciers lost roughly 5% of remaining ice during 2000–23 and contributed about **18 mm** sea-level rise; 18 mm over 23 years averages around 0.8 mm/year, not a fixed universal annual rate.
- **Mass balance = accumulation − ablation**. Cryosphere includes frozen water/ground; reduced snow/ice albedo increases absorbed energy and can reinforce warming.
- Glacier loss can initially increase runoff, followed by declining meltwater as ice shrinks. Seasonal water reliability, hydropower, irrigation, hazards and downstream livelihoods change differently by basin.
- Bakonzo communities' sacred associations in the Rwenzori illustrate cultural loss alongside ecological/economic harm.
- Priorities: emissions reduction, glacier/lake monitoring, GLOF warnings, water adaptation and careful mountain infrastructure. Paris temperature goals are collective commitments, not a mechanism that instantly refreezes lost glaciers.
