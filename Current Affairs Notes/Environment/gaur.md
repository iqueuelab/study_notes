# Gaur and Palamau prey-base conservation

## Palamau concern and species recall
- Source reports declining **gaur in Palamau Tiger Reserve, Jharkhand**, described as the State's last stronghold; it supplies no population time series, so do not infer a precise decline percentage.
- **Bos gaurus**, family **Bovidae**, commonly called Indian bison but a wild cattle species, among the largest living bovines. Native to South/Southeast Asia; Indian distribution includes Western Ghats, central India and Northeast.
- Evergreen, semi-evergreen/moist deciduous forests with grassy openings and dependable water; source preference below **1,500–1,800 m** is a habitat generalisation, not an absolute altitudinal boundary. Not a Thar-desert scrub specialist.
- **IUCN Vulnerable; CITES Appendix I; Wild Life (Protection) Act Schedule I**. International trade regulation, domestic legal protection and extinction-risk assessment are different systems. [Amended statute](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1726/1/aA1972-53.pdf).
## Threat → response
- Habitat fragmentation, mining/agriculture, disturbance, hunting and domestic-cattle disease exposure reduce viable herds. Gaur are important large prey for tigers; herbivory and seed movement influence vegetation.
- Source names rinderpest and foot-and-mouth disease; **rinderpest is a historical threat, globally eradicated in 2011**, not an established active 2025 outbreak. [WOAH eradication history](https://www.woah.org/en/disease/rinderpest/). Maintain livestock disease surveillance/immunisation, grazing safeguards, water/forage habitat and connected undisturbed forest.
- Practice: **1, 2 and 4 only (a)**; habitat restricted to Thar/Deccan dry scrub is false. Disease susceptibility does not establish that disease caused every observed decline.
