# Greater one-horned rhinoceros and habitat protection

## Species and distribution
- Issue reports **suspected poaching in Kaziranga, Assam**; suspicion is not confirmation of cause or a dated official national trend.
- **Rhinoceros unicornis**, family Rhinocerotidae; one black horn (~20–60 cm), folded grey-brown skin; chiefly grazes grasses/aquatic vegetation, also browses leaves/fruit. Floodplain grasslands and adjoining riverine woodland in India/Nepal.
- India: **Assam—Kaziranga, Manas, Orang NPs/Pobitora WLS; West Bengal—Jaldapara/Gorumara NPs; UP—Dudhwa**. Kaziranga is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and largest population stronghold.
- **2022 survey global total 4,014**; Kaziranga **2,613**, not the source's outdated ~2,400. Source's West Bengal “<100” is incorrect: published counts list **Jaldapara 287/Gorumara 52**. Kaziranga represents roughly **65%** of 4,014, so do not combine modern counts with older “over 70%” wording. [International Rhino Foundation country programme](https://rhinos.org/our-work/where-we-work/india/).
## Protection and conservation
- **IUCN Vulnerable; CITES Appendix I; Wildlife (Protection) Act Schedule I**. Five living species: white (Africa, Near Threatened), black (Africa, Critically Endangered), greater one-horned (South Asia, Vulnerable), Javan/Sumatran (Southeast Asia, Critically Endangered). Inset claim “largest of five” is incorrect: white rhinoceros is largest.
- Risks: horn poaching, habitat fragmentation/encroachment, conflict, flood mortality, invasive plants (including Mimosa), and concentration in few populations. Floods also sustain floodplain ecosystems; protection requires high-ground access/corridors, not treating every flood as ecologically harmful.
- **Indian Rhino Vision 2020 (launched 2005): target 3,000 Assam rhinos across seven protected areas by 2020**; Assam Forest Department, WWF-India, IRF and Bodoland partners. A target is not automatic achievement. Translocation from Kaziranga/Pobitora to Manas rebuilds/disperses populations; source's suggestion of transfer *to* Pobitora should not be generalised.
- Habitat management, anti-poaching camps/intelligence, surveillance, community partnership, genetic diversity and safe translocations complement protection. Inset cites **National Rhino Conservation Strategy** and **2019 New Delhi Declaration on Asian Rhinos** (India, Bhutan, Nepal, Indonesia, Malaysia); declarations should be distinguished from completed measures. “Project Rhino” is a loose source label, not proof of a distinct verified national scheme.
