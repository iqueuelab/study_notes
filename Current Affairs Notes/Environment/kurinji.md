# Kurinji: mass flowering and Western Ghats grasslands

## October 2025: Gudalur flowering
- The issue reports violet mass flowering after eight years in Gudalur Reserve Forest, Nilgiris, Tamil Nadu, identifying the plant as **Strobilanthes sessilis**. Kurinji is a common name covering several Strobilanthes species; the familiar **S. kunthiana (Neelakurinji)** has a twelve-year cycle. Do not assign one cycle, altitude or conservation status to every species.
- Gregarious flowering provides a large nectar pulse to pollinators and subsequent seeds support regeneration. Many periodic-flowering species are monocarpic: an individual flowers, seeds and dies, while the population persists through the next generation.
- Shola–grassland mosaics require grassland conservation as well as forest protection. Invasive black wattle, conversion, tourism pressure and changing climate can disrupt habitat and regeneration; protect native habitat and monitor flowering/seedling recruitment with local communities.
- Flowering alone does not prove a stable climate or successful conservation, and failed flowering alone does not diagnose climate change. The source's “all Strobilanthes endemic to the Western Ghats” wording is too broad: species-level range matters; the genus occurs beyond India.
