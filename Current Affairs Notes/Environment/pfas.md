# PFAS: persistence and bioaccumulation

## 2024 PYQ revision
- **Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS)** are a large group of synthetic chemicals used in many products/processes, valued for resistance to water/oil/heat. Carbon–fluorine bonds make many highly persistent; “forever chemicals” is shorthand for slow environmental breakdown, not literal infinite lifetime.
- PFAS can contaminate drinking water, food and food packaging; continued exposure can cause accumulation of certain PFAS in people/animals. Persistence, mobility, bioaccumulation and toxicity vary across compounds; do not attribute identical risk to every member. **Phosphoric acid is not PFAS**—the source places an unrelated chemistry PYQ after its section. [EPA explanation](https://www.epa.gov/pfas/pfas-explained).
- **PYQ 2024 answer (d) 1, 2 and 3:** widespread occurrence, not easily degraded, and persistent exposure can lead to animal bioaccumulation. Consider exposure controls, source substitution, monitoring and suitable water/waste treatment.
