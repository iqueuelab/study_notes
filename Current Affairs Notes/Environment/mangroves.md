# Mangrove ecosystems and restoration

## Ecology and distribution
- Salt-tolerant intertidal forests of tropical/subtropical coasts, estuaries and deltas. Pneumatophores, stilt roots, salt filtering/excretion and vivipary vary among species; not every mangrove has every adaptation.
- Indian examples: Sundarbans, Gujarat's coast/Kutch, Bhitarkanika, Pichavaram and Andaman–Nicobar. West Bengal has the largest State extent.
- ISFR 2021 reported about **4,992 sq km**, roughly 0.15% of India's geographical area; 2023's 4,991.68 sq km is essentially the same rounded scale, not a large national doubling.
- Functions: fish nurseries, shoreline stabilisation, carbon storage in biomass/soil and wave-energy reduction. They cannot guarantee complete cyclone protection.
## Restoration and interpreting studies
- Restore tidal hydrology and suitable native communities, not merely plant saplings anywhere. Control invasive vegetation while avoiding damage to existing habitat.
- Tamil Nadu study estimates of 4,500 ha in 2021 and 9,039 ha in 2024 use their own survey/mapping scope; do not equate them automatically with an official FSI series.
- **Carbon stock is not annual sequestration**, and tonnes of carbon differ from tonnes of CO₂. Compare methods, pools, area and dates.
- CRZ regulation and community stewardship support protection. Mangrove Alliance for Climate is a voluntary initiative, not a binding treaty.
- Carbon credits need credible baselines, additionality, permanence and safeguards for local rights.
## PYQ recall
- The combination of mangrove, evergreen and deciduous forests points to **Andaman and Nicobar Islands**; distinguish biome combinations from a single species' range.

## Swaminathan and restoration
- **M. S. Swaminathan**, founder-president of **International Society for Mangrove Ecosystems (ISME), established 1990 in Okinawa**, promoted mangroves for coastal protection, salinity-related food security and research on salt tolerance. ISME developed knowledge networks/manuals and **GLOMIS** information sharing.
- **Charter for Mangroves** was submitted to UNCED in **1992** as an annex to the World Charter for Nature; do not misdate the original World Charter for Nature itself to 1992. [ISME history](https://mangrove.or.jp/img/newsletter/ISMENews 33.pdf).
- MSSRF's **fishbone canal** approach restores tidal flushing/hydrology in degraded mangroves, tested along Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha and West Bengal; complemented by **Joint Mangrove Management** and community participation. Clear-felling can alter drainage/salinity, so degradation must not automatically be attributed to local people.
- Source recalls coastal buffering in the **1999 Odisha supercyclone** and **2004 tsunami**; protection varies with forest width, health, terrain and event intensity. Invasive **Prosopis juliflora**, aquaculture, construction and pollution remain pressures.
- **ISFR 2023:4,991.68 km²,0.15%** of geographical area. The PDF's “+16.68 km² since 2019” does not establish the latest two-year trend; keep comparable assessment baselines distinct. [Mo EFCC statement](https://www.pib.gov.in/Press Release Iframe Page.aspx?PRID=2115836&lang=2&reg=48).
- Graphic additions: **26 July**, International Day for Conservation of the Mangrove Ecosystem; **MISHTI**, Mangrove Initiative for Shoreline Habitats & Tangible Incomes, Budget 2023–24. **Mangrove Alliance for Climate**, launched by UAE/Indonesia at COP 27 in 2022, includes India; voluntary conservation cooperation, not an enforceable treaty.
