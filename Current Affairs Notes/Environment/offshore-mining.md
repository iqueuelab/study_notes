# Offshore mineral auctions: safeguards and fishing livelihoods

## 10 December 2025: parliamentary clarification
- First auction tranche launched **28 November 2024**, covering **13 offshore blocks**, including **three Kerala construction-sand blocks beyond territorial waters**; other reported materials include Gujarat lime mud and deposits near Great Nicobar. Source says no company had yet been selected at the time of the report; this is not a permanent auction-status claim.
- Government says blocks exclude **130 notified Marine Protected Areas** and **106 identified Important Coastal and Marine Biodiversity Areas**. An identified biodiversity area and a legally notified protected area are different categories. Excluding mapped areas does not demonstrate zero ecosystem/fisheries impact outside their boundaries.
- **Offshore Areas Operating Right Rules 2024**, stakeholder-ministry consultation; **Auction Rules 2024**, required legal/environmental approvals; **Conservation and Development Rules 2024**, production plan including baseline, impact assessment and mitigation through an environmental-management plan. Approval processes and an actual finding of safe operation remain different.
- **Offshore Areas Mineral Trust**, statutory provision under **Section 16A, 2002 Act**, established **9 August 2024**: coastal States represented; funds support research, mitigation, disaster relief and affected persons. [MoES parliamentary reply](https://www.moes.gov.in/sites/default/files/PIB2201539.pdf).
- Kerala protests raise seabed disturbance, sediment/ecosystem effects, coastal erosion risks, fish catch and food/livelihood security. Priorities: transparent baseline/EIA, fisher consultation, independent monitoring and enforceable safeguards; the government's adequacy assurance should be distinguished from demonstrated ecological outcomes.
