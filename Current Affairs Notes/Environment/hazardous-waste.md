# Hazardous waste and the Bhopal legacy

## Bhopal: hazard, accountability and remediation

- **Night of 2–3 December 1984**: methyl isocyanate (**MIC**) release from Union Carbide India Ltd's pesticide factory, Bhopal; maintenance/process-safety failures and inadequate safeguards are central lessons.
- Acute respiratory/eye injury and enduring health/environmental burdens; source cites **over 5,000 deaths, about half a million affected and at least 30 tonnes released**. Counts depend on period/definition and should not be treated as one universally settled toll.
- Source legal timeline: **1989 $470-million settlement; 2010 convictions of seven UCIL officials for negligence**. Cleanup/compensation did not automatically resolve long-term health or contamination.
- Source **January 2025 waste-removal** discussion is a dated step, not proof that the whole site was fully remediated.

## Waste characteristics and methods

- Hazardous properties include **toxicity, ignitability, corrosivity and reactivity**; industrial processes, metal treatment and pollution-control residues are important sources.
- Source **7.66 million tonnes/year**, **83% from Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, West Bengal and Chhattisgarh**, and **25 cement plants** co-processing: inventory year is unspecified, so do not cite as current totals.

| Route | Key distinction / safeguard |
| --- | --- |
| Co-processing | Substitute eligible raw material/fuel in industrial processes, e.g. cement kilns; controlled quality/emissions |
| Material recovery | Copper from suitable cable residues; lead from batteries; prevent worker/community exposure |
| Energy recovery | Eligible oils/solvents; pollutant control and authorisation |
| Incineration | Controlled combustion; ash and flue gas still require treatment/monitoring |
| Pyrolysis | Thermal decomposition without oxygen / limited oxygen; oil, gas and char depend on feedstock |

- Syngas mainly **CO + H₂**, with other gases depending on process; hazardous-feedstock char is **not automatically safe soil amendment**.

## Rules and international movement

- **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986; Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016**, as amended. The PDF's 1989 rules are historical, not the sole operative framework. Separate e-waste/battery rules also matter. [Gazette](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/hazardous-waste/rules/HWMRules2016_English.pdf).
- **Basel Convention**: transboundary movement and environmentally sound disposal, including prior-informed-consent controls; India a party since **1992**. [CPCB](https://cpcb.nic.in/NGTMC/HW/Final_Report_OA_804_2017.pdf).
- Source reduction, segregated authorised storage/transport, tracking, safe recovery and final disposal; cleaner technology, enforcement, emergency preparedness and health surveillance.

## MIC trap

- Colourless, volatile toxic chemical used in pesticide manufacture; water exposure can trigger a vigorous **exothermic reaction**.
- The PDF's claims 'no longer produced' and 'only one storage site worldwide' are unsupported/outdated; do not memorise them.

## Pithampur disposal: January 2025

- Waste moved from Bhopal to **TSDF, Pithampur, Dhar district, Madhya Pradesh**, around **2 January**; source gives inconsistent totals **358 / 377 tonnes**, so neither is a reconciled measurement. Do not describe UCIL's pesticide residues as simply untreated fertiliser.
- Source residues: **naphthol, carbaryl and reactor residues**; naphthol is a carbaryl precursor. Sealed/labeled transport differs from completed disposal.
- Local protests raise emission, water-quality and long-term landfill concerns. Background contamination or disease surveys do not alone prove the facility caused harm or that future operations are risk-free.
- Proposed **1,200°C trial incineration** and **90 / 270 kg** figures appear without clear time basis; preserve as reported trial/batch figures, not verified total trial mass or throughput. Source three/nine-month timetables were conditional plans.
- Transparent trial-emission results, independent water/health monitoring, ash characterisation, protected landfill and public communication; liners require long-term maintenance. Incineration destroys suitable organics but leaves **ash/residues**, not zero waste.
- Court dates, six-week allowance and **18 February hearing** are January procedural context, not proof disposal completed within that period.
