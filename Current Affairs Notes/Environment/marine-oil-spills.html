Marine oil spills: Kerala response and pollution control

Editable notes

25–27 May 2025: MSC ELSA 3

  • Liberian-flagged MSC ELSA 3, Vizhinjam → Kochi, sank 25 May off Kerala. Source reports oil/chemical risks, nine containers reaching Kollam/Alappuzha beaches, 643 containers including 13 calcium-carbide containers, and local fishing/shoreline restrictions. Calcium carbide reacts with water to release flammable acetylene; do not handle washed-up cargo.
  • Different location descriptions occur: PDF 38 nautical miles off coast; 26 May Coast Guard release about 15 nautical miles southwest of Alappuzha. Retain attribution rather than invent one reconciled position. Oil sheen and hazardous cargo risk do not imply every stored tonne/chemical had leaked. ICG response.

Effects and institutions

  • Oil pollution: uncontrolled petroleum release from ships, drilling, pipelines/operational discharges or disasters. Deepwater Horizon 2010 illustrates offshore failure; source cites Uran pipeline spill 2013 without detailed verification.
  • Oil coats/smothers organisms, affects light/photosynthesis, habitats and reproduction; birds/mammals lose insulation, fish suffer toxic/gill effects. Seafood/surface-contact risks, fisheries/tourism losses, cleanup costs and port/shipping exposure matter particularly for India’s long coastline and coastal livelihoods.
  • MARPOL 1973/1978 annexes: I oil; II noxious liquids in bulk; III harmful packaged substances; IV sewage; V garbage; VI air pollution. OPRC 1990 concerns preparedness/cooperation; CLC 1992 concerns qualifying tanker persistent-oil damage, not every container-ship spill. Bunkers Convention 2001 concerns ship-fuel liability; source's “India ratified 2015” is unverified and must not be learned as fact. IMO treaty information.
  • NOS-DCP 1996, updated including 2015: ICG national coordination; ports/oil facilities and States have defined local/shoreline roles. Merchant Shipping Act 1958, environmental law and port rules underpin relevant duties. Pollution-response resources at Mumbai/Chennai/Port Blair/Vadinar, vessels/aircraft, INCOIS trajectory/online oil-spill advisories and drills support response. Response arrangements.
  • NATPOLREX-IX 2023, Vadinar/Gulf of Kutch: contingency-plan validation, inter-agency capability and environmental protection. Source's “annually since 2009” should not be assumed without event-series verification. SACEP regional cooperation applies to South Asian seas; Nairobi Convention is Western Indian Ocean regional framework, not automatically India's regional treaty.

Control: distinguish recovery from degradation

MethodFunction/constraint
BoomsContain floating oil; waves/currents limit effectiveness
Skimmers/sorbentsMechanically recover or absorb oil; disposal/handling required
DispersantsBreak slick into droplets; transfer exposure into water column and require case-specific assessment
BioremediationMicrobes degrade hydrocarbons; temperature/oxygen/nutrients affect performance
Shoreline cleanup/surveillanceProtect responders; monitor trajectories and avoid damaging sensitive habitats
  • TERI Oilzapper is a bacterial consortium; claimed 2017 Chennai deployment is source-reported, not proof of universal success. IIT Guwahati superhydrophobic cotton/MOF composites are oil–water separation/sorption, not themselves biological degradation. IIT's related 2023 silica-coated cotton work illustrates the mechanism. Cold-adapted bacteria such as Cycloclasticus/Paraperlucidibaca illustrate research possibilities, not a ready treatment for every spill. IIT separation research.
  • PYQ 2023: define oil pollution → ecological mechanisms → India's livelihood/coastal-economy vulnerability → prevention, rapid response, liability and restoration.
