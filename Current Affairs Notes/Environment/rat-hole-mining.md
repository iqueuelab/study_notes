# Rat-hole mining: safety, environment and livelihoods

## Method and impacts
- Narrow coal seams/tunnels in **Meghalaya/Northeast**; small horizontal workings or pits with vertical access then narrow tunnels. Not every tunnel is simply a narrow vertical shaft.
- Rudimentary equipment, flooding, collapse, poor ventilation and hazardous/child labour; low wages and livelihood dependence compound risk.
- **East Jaintia Hills, December 2018**: **15 miners trapped after flooding**; source calls it collapse, but water ingress is central to the incident.
- Deforestation, soil erosion, acid mine drainage/coal slurry and water pollution; coal combustion adds particulates/gases but is a separate stage from extraction.
## Legal correction
- **NGT, 17 April 2014**, ordered stoppage of rat-hole/illegal mining in Meghalaya; source wrongly attributes the 2014 order to Supreme Court.
- **Supreme Court, 3 July 2019**: addressed mining/land rights and statutory compliance. Community/private mineral ownership does **not** exempt mining from **MMDR Act, 1957**, environmental approvals or safety rules. [Judgment](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2018/39439/39439_2018_11_1501_14662_Judgement_03-Jul-2019.pdf).
- **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**, applicable forest-conservation law, **Meghalaya Mines and Minerals Policy, 2012**; enforcement failures do not make laws legally inapplicable. Scientific permitted mining differs from continuing banned unsafe workings.
## Mains: enforce and rehabilitate
- Mine mapping/closure, transport monitoring, worker protection and responsibility; terrain, weak administration and political/economic incentives impede enforcement.
- Alternative agriculture/tourism and lawful safe livelihoods, skills/social protection, restoration and community participation; mechanisation alone does not guarantee sustainability.
- Source makes unspecific Central rehabilitation-aid claims without scheme/date: do not memorise an invented fund or entitlement.
