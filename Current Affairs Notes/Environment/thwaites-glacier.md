# Thwaites Glacier and marine ice-sheet instability

## February 2026: why Thwaites matters
- **West Antarctica**, draining toward the **Amundsen Sea**; enormous outlet glacier of the West Antarctic Ice Sheet, roughly the area of Great Britain/Florida. “Doomsday Glacier” is a popular nickname, not a prediction of an immediate catastrophe.
- **Warm ocean water → basal melting of floating shelf → weaker buttressing → faster grounded-ice flow and grounding-line retreat**. Much bedrock lies below sea level and deepens inland, favouring **marine ice-sheet instability**; grounding line is where grounded ice begins floating.
- Complete Thwaites loss corresponds to roughly **65 cm global sea-level equivalent**, over an uncertain long timescale. Destabilisation of neighbouring ice could add more; the source's **~3 m regional scenario** is distinct from Thwaites alone and is not an imminent forecast. [BAS 2024 findings](https://www.bas.ac.uk/news/grim-outlook-for-antarcticas-thwaites-glacier/).
- Older studies cited **~4% of annual global sea-level rise** from Thwaites; **Amundsen Sea Embayment's ~8%** in the 2024 briefing covers a wider region and different baseline. Floating-shelf loss mainly accelerates grounded ice discharge; avoid adding the shelf's whole volume directly as new sea-level rise.
- Embedded **2020 Siachen PYQ:** glacier lies **north of Nubra Valley—(d)**; Siachen is in the eastern Karakoram, distinct from Antarctic ice.
