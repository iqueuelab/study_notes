Himalayan permafrost thaw and infrastructure risk

Editable notes

Ground, not only surface ice

  • Permafrost is ground remaining at or below 0°C for at least two consecutive years; it can include soil, sediment or rock and need not contain visible ice. The active layer above can thaw seasonally.
  • Kashmir/Ladakh mapping cited remote-sensing/model estimates: 64.8% potentially covered, comprising continuous 26.7%, discontinuous 23.8% and sporadic 14.3%; Ladakh plateau estimate 87%. These are study estimates, not direct borehole confirmation everywhere.
  • Continuous/discontinuous/sporadic describe spatial coverage, not merely what fraction of one soil sample is frozen. Study-area boundaries must be checked before generalising to administrative geography.
  • Thaw can destabilise roads, buildings, hydropower sites and slopes; alters groundwater/base flow and may interact with glacier/lake hazards.
  • Carbon-rich permafrost can release CO₂/methane, reinforcing warming; emissions vary with carbon stocks and moisture—not every Himalayan rock slope releases large methane volumes.
  • Chamoli 2021 was a rock–ice avalanche/debris-flow disaster, not a straightforward glacial-lake outburst. South Lhonak, Sikkim, 2023 was a GLOF; do not interchange mechanisms.
  • Combine ground-temperature loggers/boreholes, satellite mapping and lake/slope monitoring; calibrate models and plan resilient siting/drainage rather than assuming every mapped lake will burst.
