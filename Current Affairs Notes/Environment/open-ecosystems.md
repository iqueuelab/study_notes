# Open ecosystems: grasslands, savannas, scrublands and deserts

## Ecological and livelihood value
- **Grasslands, savannas, scrublands and deserts are functioning ecosystems**. Treating every treeless tract as degraded forest or wasteland can drive inappropriate plantations, fencing, infrastructure and industrial conversion.
- Habitat for **Great Indian Bustard, Indian wolf and caracal**; soil and roots store carbon, alongside ecosystem services and climate resilience. Forests also store below-ground carbon; the distinction is not an absolute forest-above/grassland-below divide.
- **Dhangar, Rabari and Kuruba** pastoral communities depend on access and seasonal grazing mobility. Their knowledge and stewardship should inform restoration; enclosure/afforestation may damage livelihoods and biodiversity.
- Drylands supported Mesopotamian, Egyptian and Indus civilisations and adaptive water-management traditions. A naturally arid desert is distinct from **land degradation/desertification**.
## Restoration suited to place
- Assess baseline ecology before tree planting; use water harvesting, appropriate rotational grazing, natural regeneration and local knowledge. Conserve open habitat and reward sustainable land use/soil-carbon maintenance.
- Replacing the blanket “wasteland” label and renaming the desertification observance are **editorial proposals**, not enacted classifications or a new UN day. Avoid assuming every grazing regime is sustainable without local assessment.

## December 2025: soil carbon and grassland restoration
- Solapur, Maharashtra case describes **CAMPA-supported native-grass restoration**, using **Dichanthium annulatum, Chrysopogon fulvus and Cenchrus setigerus**. Source ATREE/Grasslands Trust comparison reports **21% more soil organic carbon after two years; 50% after three** versus untreated sites. These are site/study results, not universal restoration rates or proof that every CAMPA plantation is appropriate.
- **Banni, Kutch**: invasive **Prosopis juliflora** displaced open habitats and pastoral access. Community-managed **wadas**, rotational grazing, controlled biomass harvest and regeneration illustrate **Maldhari** stewardship. Source reports restored patches had higher SOC than several comparison habitats.
- **Numerical caution:** PDF's **“27 metric tonnes” total Banni carbon** lacks scale and conflicts with its **120 tC/ha** figure; do not use as a reliable aggregate. Its global “soil stores twice atmosphere plus all biomass” lacks a consistent depth/stock definition. Retain the robust point that soils are a major carbon reservoir.
- Roots, organic matter and microbes stabilise soil, improve infiltration and nutrient cycling; below-ground storage can be less exposed to immediate combustion than above-ground biomass, but fire/drought/degradation can still affect soil carbon. “Not lost in fires” and “grasslands always outperform forests” are too absolute.
- Colonial/postcolonial **wasteland** classifications and the source's **1985 National Wasteland Development Board** reference frame policy bias; naturally open ecosystems should be restored according to ecological baseline, not automatically planted with trees. Source Maharashtra livestock value **₹59,000 crore, 2018–19** is a dated livelihood illustration.
- PYQ revision: **2019 CAMPA**—National and State authorities, source key **1 only**; this does not erase applicable Gram Sabha/forest-rights obligations in implementation. **2018 Prosopis**—reduces local biodiversity, **(b)**; that question already exists in the common bank.
