# Nubian ibex: desert-cliff adaptation and conservation

## December 2025: Nubian ibex revision
- **Capra nubiana**, family **Bovidae**, is a wild goat of **arid rocky mountains, desert cliffs and steep escape terrain in northeastern Africa and the Middle East**. Do not confuse it with Himalayan/Siberian ibex populations or Nilgiri tahr.
- Hard outer hoof rims and softer pads provide rock grip; strong legs and agile movement aid climbing and predator avoidance. Males have prominent backward-curving horns and a dark beard; females are smaller with shorter horns. The issue calls it the smallest ibex.
- Source status snapshot: **Vulnerable**, decreasing, **fewer than 10,000 mature individuals**. Treat the estimate as the source's conservation baseline, not a census on the issue date; do not treat its broad country list or Himalayan comparison as a settled taxonomy/range assessment.
- Threats: hunting/poaching, habitat fragmentation, infrastructure, livestock competition and declining forage/water under aridity. Fragmented populations can lose genetic exchange; habitat connectivity, locally appropriate protection and population/genetic monitoring matter. Taxonomic differences require evidence rather than assuming all regional groups are separate species.
- Taxonomic reference: [American Society of Mammalogists species account](https://www.mammaldiversity.org/taxon/1006186/).
