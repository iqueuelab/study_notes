# Marine oil spills: Kerala response and pollution control

## 25–27 May 2025: MSC ELSA 3
- Liberian-flagged **MSC ELSA 3**, Vizhinjam → Kochi, sank **25 May** off Kerala. Source reports oil/chemical risks, nine containers reaching Kollam/Alappuzha beaches, **643 containers including 13 calcium-carbide containers**, and local fishing/shoreline restrictions. Calcium carbide reacts with water to release flammable acetylene; do not handle washed-up cargo.
- Different location descriptions occur: PDF **38 nautical miles off coast**; **26 May Coast Guard release about 15 nautical miles southwest of Alappuzha**. Retain attribution rather than invent one reconciled position. Oil sheen and hazardous cargo risk do not imply every stored tonne/chemical had leaked. [ICG response](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2131395&lang=2&reg=48).
## Effects and institutions
- Oil pollution: uncontrolled petroleum release from ships, drilling, pipelines/operational discharges or disasters. **Deepwater Horizon 2010** illustrates offshore failure; source cites Uran pipeline spill 2013 without detailed verification.
- Oil coats/smothers organisms, affects light/photosynthesis, habitats and reproduction; birds/mammals lose insulation, fish suffer toxic/gill effects. Seafood/surface-contact risks, fisheries/tourism losses, cleanup costs and port/shipping exposure matter particularly for India’s long coastline and coastal livelihoods.
- **MARPOL 1973/1978 annexes:** I oil; II noxious liquids in bulk; III harmful packaged substances; IV sewage; V garbage; VI air pollution. **OPRC 1990** concerns preparedness/cooperation; **CLC 1992** concerns qualifying tanker persistent-oil damage, not every container-ship spill. **Bunkers Convention 2001** concerns ship-fuel liability; source's “India ratified 2015” is unverified and must not be learned as fact. [IMO treaty information](https://www.imo.org/en/KnowledgeCentre/ConferencesMeetings/Pages/MARPOL.aspx).
- **NOS-DCP 1996**, updated including 2015: ICG national coordination; ports/oil facilities and States have defined local/shoreline roles. Merchant Shipping Act 1958, environmental law and port rules underpin relevant duties. Pollution-response resources at Mumbai/Chennai/Port Blair/Vadinar, vessels/aircraft, **INCOIS trajectory/online oil-spill advisories** and drills support response. [Response arrangements](https://www.itopf.org/knowledge-resources/countries-territories-regions/india/).
- **NATPOLREX-IX 2023, Vadinar/Gulf of Kutch:** contingency-plan validation, inter-agency capability and environmental protection. Source's “annually since 2009” should not be assumed without event-series verification. **SACEP** regional cooperation applies to South Asian seas; **Nairobi Convention** is Western Indian Ocean regional framework, not automatically India's regional treaty.
## Control: distinguish recovery from degradation
| Method | Function/constraint |
|---|---|
| Booms | Contain floating oil; waves/currents limit effectiveness |
| Skimmers/sorbents | Mechanically recover or absorb oil; disposal/handling required |
| Dispersants | Break slick into droplets; transfer exposure into water column and require case-specific assessment |
| Bioremediation | Microbes degrade hydrocarbons; temperature/oxygen/nutrients affect performance |
| Shoreline cleanup/surveillance | Protect responders; monitor trajectories and avoid damaging sensitive habitats |
- **TERI Oilzapper** is a bacterial consortium; claimed 2017 Chennai deployment is source-reported, not proof of universal success. **IIT Guwahati superhydrophobic cotton/MOF composites** are **oil–water separation/sorption**, not themselves biological degradation. IIT's related 2023 silica-coated cotton work illustrates the mechanism. Cold-adapted bacteria such as **Cycloclasticus/Paraperlucidibaca** illustrate research possibilities, not a ready treatment for every spill. [IIT separation research](https://www.iitg.ac.in/iitg_press_details.php?p=68%2Fiit-guwahati-develops-an-eco-friendly-method-to-mitigate-dual-environmental-hazards-biomass-burning-and-marine-oil-spills).
- **PYQ 2023:** define oil pollution → ecological mechanisms → India's livelihood/coastal-economy vulnerability → prevention, rapid response, liability and restoration.

## 9–11 June: MV Wan Hai 503 fire
- Separate from MSC ELSA 3: **Singapore-flagged Wan Hai 503** suffered explosions/fire on **9 June 2025**, about **88 nautical miles off Beypore, Kozhikode**; drifting vessel, hazardous cargo and floating containers created fire, navigation and marine-pollution risks.
- June 11 PDF reports **157 hazardous-cargo containers**, including resin solution, benzophenone, alcohol-wetted nitrocellulose and printing ink. Its approximate **2,000 tonnes marine oil + 240 tonnes diesel** should not be confused with a verified quantity released; official June 11 response reports **2,128 metric tonnes of fuel** onboard.
- **11 June:** Coast Guard winched five salvage personnel and an aircrew diver onto the burning vessel to prepare towing. Salvage, firefighting, search/rescue and pollution prevention are distinct but coordinated tasks. [Coast Guard update](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2135826&lang=2&reg=48).
- IMDG recall: **Class 3 flammable liquids; 4.1 flammable solids; 4.2 substances liable to spontaneous combustion; 6.1 toxic substances**. Radioactive material is Class 7. Source's drift speeds are transient incident estimates, not fixed properties of the vessel.
- MARPOL diagram dates: Annexes I/II entered into force **2 October 1983**, III **1 July 1992**, IV **27 September 2003**, V **31 December 1988**, VI **19 May 2005**; adoption of a convention differs from entry into force.

## September 2025: judicial security after MSC ELSA 3
- The embedded clipping reports the **Kerala High Court reduced security for release of sister vessel MSC AKITETA II to ₹1,227.62 crore**, from **₹9,531 crore**, in litigation over the May **MSC ELSA 3** sinking. This is security in an admiralty claim, **not a final determination that all claimed ecological/economic damage equals that amount**, and does not mean the sunken ship was released.
- The case connects polluter liability, environmental assessment, fishers' losses and effective compensation. Scientific monitoring and evidence of damage matter alongside rapid response; an order to provide security is distinct from a confirmed final payment to affected people.
- Repeated response/cleanup sections add no new incident. Existing notes distinguish sorption from bioremediation, CLC from bunker-fuel liability and disputed convention-ratification claims. **2011 Oilzapper PYQ: (a)**—bacterial bioremediation of oily sludge/oil spills, already banked; do not infer a universal guarantee of zero residues under all conditions.
