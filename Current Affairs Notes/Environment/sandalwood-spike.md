# Sandalwood, red sanders and spike disease

## Species and disease distinctions
- **Indian sandalwood (*Santalum album*)** has fragrant heartwood/oil used in perfumery, incense and ritual; it is a root hemiparasitic tree, unlike **red sanders (*Pterocarpus santalinus*)**, prized principally for red timber. They belong to different genera/families.
- Red sanders is endemic to southern Eastern Ghats, especially Andhra Pradesh; dry deciduous, rocky habitats face illegal felling/trade, fire and degradation. Source lists sandalwood as **Vulnerable**, red sanders **Endangered**, and red sanders **CITES Appendix II**; international-trade listing differs from domestic wildlife schedules. **Correction: red sanders is in WPA Schedule IV**, reflecting CITES-linked controls, not the sheet's Schedule II. **Schedule II of the export policy** is a separate classification. [DRI, 4 February 2026](https://dri.nic.in/writereaddata/4695606_2026-02-04.pdf).
- **Sandal spike disease:** phytoplasma-associated disease, spread through insect vectors, producing small crowded leaves/short internodes and eventual decline. Phytoplasmas are wall-less bacteria, not fungi; disease management requires detection, vector/host management and removal of infected trees rather than claiming an available curative treatment. [ICFRE disease resource](https://iwst.icfre.gov.in/database/SIS/silviculture/Pest%20%26%20Disease.html).
- Source historical account: reported in Kodagu in **1899**, with extensive tree removal **1903–1916**. Illegal harvest, disease and habitat/management constraints together affect supply; its historic 85%-of-global-trade claim is not a verified current market share.
