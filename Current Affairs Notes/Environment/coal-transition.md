# Coal transition: Chile lessons and Indian constraints

## December 2025: comparative coal-transition debate
- The editorial balances climate/air-quality damage against employment, affordability, reliability and revenues in coal-dependent districts, especially **Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha and West Bengal**. Abrupt closure without alternatives can impose concentrated local costs; orderly transition requires an explicit social and power-system plan.
- **Capacity ≠ generation; renewable ≠ non-fossil.** At **30 June 2025**, India had **50.08% non-fossil installed electricity capacity**, including **nuclear**, while renewables including large hydro were **48.27%**. This corrects the PDF's unqualified “50% renewable” claim. Its roughly **75% coal generation/20% renewable generation** figures describe a different dated output measure, not capacity shares. [MNRE June capacity table](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2144627&lang=1&reg=3).
- **CCPI edition correction:** India's **23rd rank, down 13 places**, belongs to **CCPI 2026**, released in 2025; the PDF labels it CCPI 2025. The publisher flags continued fossil dependence despite renewable progress. [CCPI India](https://ccpi.org/country/ind/).
- **Chile comparison:** source reports coal's power share **43.6% in 2016 → 17.5% in 2024**, alongside renewable expansion. Policy levers discussed: carbon pricing, emission standards, competitive renewable auctions, storage, planned plant retirement and economic diversification. These reported percentages do not alone identify each policy's causal contribution.
- Chile's **2019 plan** sought closure/reconversion of coal plants by **2040**, negotiated with operators. IEA records blended-finance support for early retirement. A target is not completed phase-out; India's scale, growing demand, domestic coal employment and district dependence differ. [IEA Chile policy](https://www.iea.org/policies/12978-decarbonization-plan-for-the-electrical-system), [IEA financing example](https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-outlook-2021/phasing-out-coal).
- Roadmap proposed: retire inefficient plants, scrutinise new coal commitments, expand **firm renewables + batteries/pumped hydro + grids**, electrify end uses, improve dispatch/PPAs and carbon-price incentives, and build alternative district industries. **Public finance** for reskilling, welfare and local infrastructure; **private finance** for clean generation/storage; suitable **DMF** support for affected areas.
- The **Green Energy Transition India Fund**, **2050 coal phase-out** and sectoral policy changes are editorial/study proposals, not announced operative national commitments; distinguish India's **2070 net-zero target**. The source's **3–10% GDP loss by 2100**, **14% infant-mortality association per GW** and **30% compliance-cost increase** lack sufficient study/method details here and should not be memorised as universal causal estimates. Health/climate co-benefits strengthen transition arguments without erasing distributional or reliability costs.
