# Red panda: Eastern Himalayan forest conservation

## Sikkim conservation breeding
- Red panda cubs were born at **Himalayan Zoological Park near Gangtok** after a reported seven-year interval; embedded clipping recalls earlier losses to **canine distemper**. Captive breeding supports conservation but does not itself prove wild populations recovered.
- **Ailurus fulgens**, lesser/red panda: small, largely solitary, arboreal mammal; order **Carnivora**, but bamboo dominates its diet, with fruit/insects/eggs and occasional small animals. Long tail helps balance and warmth; Sikkim's state animal.
- Mountain forests with bamboo understorey in the **Eastern Himalayas and adjoining southwest China**: India (Sikkim, northern West Bengal, Arunachal), Nepal, Bhutan, Myanmar and China—not Western Ghats/Terai plains. Himalayan/Chinese red-panda taxonomic treatments differ; do not read the graphic's subspecies map as a settled universal classification.
- **IUCN Endangered; CITES Appendix I; Wildlife (Protection) Act Schedule I**. Habitat/bamboo loss and fragmentation are major threats. Decline can signal forest degradation; indicator species is an ecological use, not a separate legal protection category.
