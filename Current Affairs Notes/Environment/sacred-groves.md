# Sacred groves: community conservation and cultural landscapes

## Core idea
- Community-protected forest/tree patches linked to deities, spirits and ancestral customs; **in-situ biodiversity refugia**, cultural heritage and ecological knowledge.
- Benefits: rare/endemic species and medicinal plants, soil/water conservation, microclimate and landscape connectivity. A small grove is not a guarantee against floods or cyclones, nor necessarily untouched by humans.

## Threats and response
- Land conversion, fragmented customary authority, commercial extraction and unregulated tourism erode protection.
- Recognise community rights, document ecology/oral traditions with consent, support Gram Sabha participation and restore surrounding corridors without replacing diverse old forests with plantations.

## Rajasthan community-restoration case
- **Chudasidh Adavad, Sirawas, Rajasthan:** source credits the **Meo community** and **KRAPAVIS, Krishi Avam Paristhitiki Vikas Sansthan**, with reviving a degraded sacred grove over about **15 years** (embedded article). Illustrates customary stewardship, biodiversity/water recovery and livelihood support.
- “Saved 2,000 animals” lacks a specified census; treat as a source claim, not a verified count of rescued wildlife. Community legitimacy and rights matter alongside restoration.
- Source recalls **2024 Supreme Court sacred-grove mapping/protection directions**; a direction to identify/protect groves does not automatically notify every grove as a statutory protected area. Additional examples: **Sarna—Munda/Oraon, Jharkhand; Kavu—Kerala; Devrai—Maharashtra; Law Kyntang—Khasi, Meghalaya**.
