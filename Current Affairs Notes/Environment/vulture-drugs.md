# Vultures and toxic veterinary drugs

## Nimesulide: correct regulatory scope
- **NSAID**, pain/inflammation medicine. Veterinary exposure can damage vulture kidneys; **visceral gout** indicates uric-acid deposition associated with renal failure.
- **Medicated livestock → contaminated carcass → scavenging vulture → kidney injury → gout/death**; highlights indirect ecological effects of pharmaceuticals.
- **S.O. 5633(E), 30 December 2024**, section **26A, Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940**: **nimesulide/formulations for animal use** prohibition. **20 February 2025** CDSCO circular sought enforcement. [CDSCO record](https://www.cdsco.gov.in/opencms/opencms/en/Notifications/Gazette-Notifications/), [enforcement circular](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadPublic_NoticesFiles/Nimesulide%20and%20its%20formulatin.pdf).
- January source's blanket assertion that this makes all human administration banned/unsuitable is unsupported; human-use safety restrictions are a separate issue. No medical-use recommendation follows from this note.
- Source lists earlier veterinary use in cattle/pigs/horses and human pain/fever indications; these describe usage, not endorsed prescribing.
- Conservation: enforce veterinary-drug restrictions, monitor carcass exposure, safe alternatives under professional guidance and vulture-population recovery; scavengers provide ecosystem sanitation services.
## Long-billed vulture and habitat
- Indian/long-billed vulture: **Gyps indicus**, Critically Endangered; cliff-nesting across much of central and peninsular India.
- Ramadevarabetta near Ramanagara, Karnataka, established in 2012, is an important vulture sanctuary; “first” should not become “India's only sanctuary forever”.
- Protect nesting cliffs, safe food and habitat connectivity; address quarrying, disturbance, electrocution and collision risks.
- Veterinary diclofenac harms vultures through renal failure. The 2006 veterinary prohibition differs from 2015 restrictions on large multidose human formulations; all human medicinal use was not banned.
- Vulture Safe Zones, captive breeding and monitored reintroduction complement drug regulation. Scavenging reduces carcass accumulation and associated disease risks; it does not sterilise every pathogen.

## September 2025: conservation network and breeding
- **We Foundation India**, collaborating with **Gauhati University Zoology Department**, launched a vulture conservation portal in Assam ahead of International Vulture Awareness Day (first Saturday of September). Source calls it India's first; purpose is networking researchers, conservationists and citizens, with local-language information beginning in **Assamese**.
- Citizen observations, awareness and coordination complement safe carcass supply, drug-law enforcement and protected nesting habitat. The issue also illustrates successful **Indian long-billed vulture breeding at Ramadevarabetta**, Karnataka; existing Gyps indicus/habitat and diclofenac notes apply.

## December 2025: planned Assam release
- **BNHS** planned to release **six captive-bred vultures, three males and three females, aged around 2–3 years**, in **January 2026** in Assam. Species: **slender-billed, Gyps tenuirostris**, and **white-rumped, Gyps bengalensis**, both **Critically Endangered**. This is a plan reported in December, not a completed release.
- Bred at **Rani Vulture Conservation Breeding Centre, Kamrup**; source reports **over 800** bred across BNHS centres. **Kamrup and Biswanath** were chosen within natural range and near existing flocks; Biswanath is near Kaziranga. These are source-era programme figures.
- **Soft release:** roughly **three months in acclimatisation enclosures → release → BNHS/Forest Department monitoring**. Exposure to other scavengers and local conditions helps captive-bred birds adapt; safe carcasses, community cooperation and drug-law enforcement remain essential.
- Existing notes cover diclofenac-mediated kidney failure, the **2006 veterinary prohibition**, separate **2015 multidose human-formulation restriction**, safe zones, captive breeding and species statuses. Do not copy the PDF's misplaced Himalayan griffon under an “Endangered” heading: existing corrected snapshot is **Near Threatened**. Nor treat “~20,000 vultures” as a secure all-species census or apply one decline percentage to every species.
- The **Assam portal** section repeats the earlier We Foundation India/Gauhati University launch and Assamese-language network; no new launch is established by republication. Additional cultural recall: vultures figure in Parsi funerary practice at Towers of Silence; scavenging reduces carcass-management costs and some disease risks, not every pathogen.
