# Vultures and toxic veterinary drugs

## Nimesulide: correct regulatory scope
- **NSAID**, pain/inflammation medicine. Veterinary exposure can damage vulture kidneys; **visceral gout** indicates uric-acid deposition associated with renal failure.
- **Medicated livestock → contaminated carcass → scavenging vulture → kidney injury → gout/death**; highlights indirect ecological effects of pharmaceuticals.
- **S.O. 5633(E), 30 December 2024**, section **26A, Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940**: **nimesulide/formulations for animal use** prohibition. **20 February 2025** CDSCO circular sought enforcement. [CDSCO record](https://www.cdsco.gov.in/opencms/opencms/en/Notifications/Gazette-Notifications/), [enforcement circular](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadPublic_NoticesFiles/Nimesulide%20and%20its%20formulatin.pdf).
- January source's blanket assertion that this makes all human administration banned/unsuitable is unsupported; human-use safety restrictions are a separate issue. No medical-use recommendation follows from this note.
- Source lists earlier veterinary use in cattle/pigs/horses and human pain/fever indications; these describe usage, not endorsed prescribing.
- Conservation: enforce veterinary-drug restrictions, monitor carcass exposure, safe alternatives under professional guidance and vulture-population recovery; scavengers provide ecosystem sanitation services.
## Long-billed vulture and habitat
- Indian/long-billed vulture: **Gyps indicus**, Critically Endangered; cliff-nesting across much of central and peninsular India.
- Ramadevarabetta near Ramanagara, Karnataka, established in 2012, is an important vulture sanctuary; “first” should not become “India's only sanctuary forever”.
- Protect nesting cliffs, safe food and habitat connectivity; address quarrying, disturbance, electrocution and collision risks.
- Veterinary diclofenac harms vultures through renal failure. The 2006 veterinary prohibition differs from 2015 restrictions on large multidose human formulations; all human medicinal use was not banned.
- Vulture Safe Zones, captive breeding and monitored reintroduction complement drug regulation. Scavenging reduces carcass accumulation and associated disease risks; it does not sterilise every pathogen.
