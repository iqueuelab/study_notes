# Reticulated python: record length and ecology

## February 2026: longest reliably measured wild snake
- **Ibu Baron (“The Baroness”)**, female **Malayopython reticulatus**, measured **7.22 m on 18 January 2026**, Maros, **Sulawesi, Indonesia**. Guinness recognises the **wild-snake** record; the PDF's unqualified “longest measured snake” confuses wild and captive categories. Discovered in late 2025, she is now protected by local conservationist Budi Purwanto. [Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2026/2/indonesian-python-makes-gwr-hissstory-as-the-longest-measured-wild-snake).
- Net-like markings explain “reticulated”; a giant **non-venomous constrictor**, preying on birds and mammals. Constriction stops circulation; it is not venom injection. Length and mass records are different; avoid the source's unsupported fixed “third heaviest” rank.
- South/Southeast Asian tropical forests, wetlands and riverine landscapes; India's documented native population includes the **Nicobar Islands**, rather than the source's unsupported emphasis on Northeast India. [Nicobar field study](https://journals.ku.edu/reptilesandamphibians/article/download/15368/13879/35305). Source lists global **Least Concern**; local habitat loss, skin/pet trade and human–snake conflict remain threats.
- **2010 King Cobra PYQ:** nest is for eggs/guarding, **oviparity—(c)**. **2019 animal-diet/reproduction PYQ:** some turtles, fish and marine mammals are herbivorous; some snakes are viviparous—**all four (d)**. These facts do not make pythons viviparous.
