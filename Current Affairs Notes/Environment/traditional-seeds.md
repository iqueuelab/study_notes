# Conserving traditional seed varieties

## Agrobiodiversity and food security
- **Traditional varieties/landraces** are locally maintained crop populations shaped by farmer selection and adaptation. Their genetic diversity, associated knowledge and food cultures are resources for breeding, nutrition and climate adaptation.
- Loss of varieties narrows options against drought, floods, pests and disease. Traits differ: **not every landrace is high yielding, stress resistant, nutrient rich or low input**, and improved/hybrid varieties are not inherently unsustainable. Evaluate genotype, environment and management together.
| Why diversity erodes | Response |
|---|---|
| Procurement, retail demand and consumer preferences favour a few uniform cereals | Diversify demand and suitable public food procurement; value regional food cultures |
| Low commercial demand; poor processing, storage, branding and value chains | Processing support, farmers' markets, traceable branding and farm-to-kitchen links |
| Community seed conservation lacks reliable funding/access | Local seed banks, regional conservation centres, farmer networks and backup collections |
| Research rewards short-term output of a few crops | ICAR/State Agricultural Universities and farmers collaborate in participatory breeding and stress testing |
| Input costs and climate shocks threaten livelihoods | Assess locally adapted varieties and diverse cropping systems alongside suitable improved varieties |
- **On-farm conservation** maintains evolving crops and farmer knowledge; **ex-situ gene banks**, including ICAR-NBPGR collections, provide backups. Seed viability, regeneration, documentation and access matter; storage alone does not secure continued cultivation.
- Recognise farmers' knowledge and legal rights. **PPV&FR Act, 2001, section 39** protects saving, using, sowing, resowing, exchanging, sharing and selling farm produce including protected-variety seed, but excludes sale of **branded seed of a protected variety**. Breeders' rights and conservation incentives coexist; it is incorrect to describe every IPR regime as prohibiting farmer seed exchange. [Authority brochure](https://plantauthority.gov.in/sites/default/files/fbrochure-eng.pdf).
- **MSP already covers crops including jowar, bajra, ragi and several pulses**. The issue is effective procurement, access and incentives for neglected crops/varieties, not first-ever MSP for all millets/pulses. MSP is not a separate guaranteed price for every indigenous rice variety. [Official MSP table](https://desagri.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/MSP_for-website_English-version.pdf).
- Suitable traditional crops can enter school meals, anganwadi nutrition, hospitals and PDS where procurement/processing allow. Avoid replacing dietary diversity with another uniform monoculture.
- Source example: **MSSRF's Tribal Agrobiodiversity Centre, Jeypore, Odisha** links farmer conservation, local knowledge and stakeholder consultations; **Odisha Millet Mission** illustrates combining cultivation and market support. Conservation must deliver viable livelihoods as well as maintain genetic heritage.
- Sustainable farming can reduce waste, pollution and input dependence and support biodiversity/health; these are management-dependent outcomes, not automatic properties of every traditional seed.

## Mains practice framework
**Causes → genetic/nutritional/climate risks → community banks + on-farm/gene-bank conservation + participatory breeding + farmer rights + procurement/value chains + consumer demand.** The source's 250-word, 15-mark question is undated practice, not a verified UPSC PYQ.
