# Climate change and grazing suitability

## Projected contraction by 2100
- A 2026 study applies a **safe climatic space** framework using temperature, precipitation, humidity and wind to grassland-based grazing. It projects **36–50% net decline in climatically suitable grazing areas by 2100**, with shifts within/between continents. These are scenario-dependent suitability projections, not land already lost. [Research abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41662520/).
- Estimated exposure: **110–140 million pastoralists and 1.4–1.6 billion livestock**, especially in Africa. Some inland Asian suitability may expand, including Kazakhstan/Iran in the source, without offsetting global contraction.
- Heat, drought and rainfall variability can reduce forage and livestock productivity; threats overlap poverty, hunger, gender inequality and political fragility. Grazing systems include rangelands, savannas and pastures; pastoral livelihoods depend on mobile access to forage and water.
- Indian application: protect common grazing lands, mobility corridors and water access; use fodder reserves, climate information and locally adapted stocking practices. Raikas/Maldharis and Gujjars/Bakarwals illustrate pastoral livelihoods; this global study does not supply a separate numerical Indian loss estimate.
