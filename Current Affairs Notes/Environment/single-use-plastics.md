# Single-use plastics, microbeads and circularity

## Problem and policy
- SUP products are intended for one/few uses before disposal: bags/films, cutlery, straws and other packaging; medical disposables also require safe segregated handling. Persistence, fragmentation into microplastics, blocked drains and wildlife exposure make brief use costly environmentally.
- India's **2021 rule amendment** prohibited identified low-utility/high-litter SUP items **from 1 July 2022**. Packaging outside that list is governed through plastic-waste rules and **2022 EPR guidelines**; this is not a ban on all plastics. Multilayer packaging poses recycling challenges, but not every multilayer structure has identical recyclability. [MoEFCC account](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1882855&lang=2&reg=48).
- Barriers: cheap incumbent materials, costly/inaccessible substitutes, livelihood dependence, poor segregation/collection and uneven enforcement. Responses: transparent compliance data and inspections, reuse/refill systems, support for alternatives and reliable collection/recycling. A ban on bags of every thickness is a **proposal in the source**, not the prevailing national rule.
- International examples include Bangladesh's 2002 thin-bag ban and EU restrictions taking effect in 2021. **UNEA resolution 5/14, 2 March 2022**, launched negotiations for a legally binding plastics instrument; it was not itself a completed treaty signed by 124 countries. [UNEP resolution](https://wedocs.unep.org/items/2d9aca95-4286-4b5e-a7db-984df28553ea).
## Microbeads and PYQ
- Manufactured tiny plastic particles, commonly PE/PP/polystyrene, used as scrubbers in some personal-care/cleaning products; often <5 mm. Escape through wastewater depends on treatment, rather than all plants filtering none.
- Ingestion can expose aquatic organisms and food webs to persistent plastics and associated contaminants. **2019 Prelims: (a), harmful to marine ecosystems**.

## Industry influence and inclusive recycling
- July editorial compares plastics lobbying with tobacco-industry tactics: shifting responsibility to consumers, sponsoring favourable science/PR, misleading green claims and resisting production controls. These are analytical allegations, not proof every producer or recycling technology behaves identically.
- “Biodegradable/compostable” claims require specified conditions and actual collection/treatment systems; recycling claims should state material, technical feasibility and economic limits. Transparent conflicts of interest, producer accountability, reuse and reliable collection complement informed consumers.
- Informal collectors/sorters face toxic exposures, insecure work and poor social protection. PDF's **70% recycling contribution**, **36% lobbyist increase at INC-3** and Asia-triples-by-2060 claim lack full denominator/baseline here; do not treat as reconciled current national totals.

## INC-5.2: negotiations in progress
- **Geneva, opened 5 August 2025**, originally scheduled through 14 August; resumed fifth session follows Busan 2024. On this issue date negotiations were ongoing, not an adopted global plastics treaty. [UNEP opening account](https://www.unep.org/en/news-and-stories/press-release/resumed-fifth-session-negotiations-global-plastic-pollution-treaty).
- Full lifecycle means **production/raw polymers → product design → use → disposal/waste management**. Plenary, contact/technical groups and stakeholder input negotiate articles; bracketed text marks unresolved language.
- Fault lines: production limits/chemicals, binding versus voluntary measures, finance, compliance, equity and national capabilities. A legally binding instrument can contain both binding and voluntary approaches; the resolution does not guarantee every clause is mandatory.
- Source describes India opposing production caps/fixed phase-out dates, defending development needs and supporting waste management/EPR and just transition. Separate this negotiating position from an agreed treaty obligation; the source's “like-minded” country list is a contemporaneous alignment, not formal treaty membership.
- Practice: INC **C (2,3)**; UNEA 5/14 description **B**. March 2022 is the mandate date; INC work began later in 2022.
