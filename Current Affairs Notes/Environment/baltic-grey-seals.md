# Baltic grey seals, declining ice and marine protection

## Ice loss and breeding
- **Grey seal (*Halichoerus grypus*)** breeds on Baltic drift ice and coastal islets; globally listed Least Concern in the source period. Reduced ice can concentrate land breeding, disturb colonies and lower pup survival. A suggested reassessment is **not an accomplished IUCN status change**; the sheet's ~3,000 annual pups lacks a clear year/geographic denominator.
- Grey seals are **facultative ice breeders**; distinguish more ice-dependent ringed seals. HELCOM finds reduced ice can affect breeding and pups born on land can fare worse; threats also include disturbance, bycatch, pollution and food-web changes. [HELCOM reproduction assessment](https://indicators.helcom.fi/indicator/seal-reproduction/).
- **Baltic Sea:** low-salinity, semi-enclosed northern European sea bordered by Denmark, Germany, Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, Russia, Finland and Sweden. “Mediterranean-type sea” describes enclosure; **it is not the Mediterranean Sea**. Eutrophication, pollution and climate change interact.
- **1992 Helsinki Convention:** Convention on the Protection of the Marine Environment of the Baltic Sea Area; cooperation through **HELCOM**. Protect breeding habitat, reduce disturbance/bycatch/pollution and mitigate warming.
## Treaty-term revision
- Annex-I countries originate in **UNFCCC**; Kyoto sets differentiated commitments. **CERs** and **Clean Development Mechanism** belong to Kyoto, not Nagoya. **Cartagena** concerns biosafety/GMOs; **Nagoya** access and benefit sharing under CBD.
