# Hilsa: migration, conservation and fish diplomacy

## September 2025: fish diplomacy before Durga Puja
- Embedded report: Bangladesh's interim government announced **1,200 tonnes of hilsa exports to India**, with applications sought by **11 September** ahead of Durga Puja. This is an announced export allocation, not proof the full quantity had arrived; the clipping compares **3,000 tonnes in 2024**. Food trade can preserve people-to-people links despite political friction.
## Ecology, livelihood and culture
- **Tenualosa ilisha**, herring family **Clupeidae**; anadromous migration from coastal/marine waters into rivers for spawning. Bay of Bengal and river/estuary systems including **Ganga–Hooghly, Brahmaputra–Meghna, Mahanadi and Godavari** matter; migration season varies by river and conditions.
- Bangladesh's national fish; important in Bengali/Odia food culture, gifts, festivals and fisheries livelihoods. The issue's **about 70% of world production** is a source-period estimate, not a timeless fixed share.
- Threats: juvenile **jatka** fishing, overfishing, water pollution, altered flows and barrages obstructing migration. Seasonal closures, sanctuaries, enforcement and fisher compensation must be locally specified; do not apply the source's June–August closure to every Indian State.
- Source lists **Least Concern** globally; this does not rule out local stock decline or remove the need for migration corridors, basin cooperation and sustainable catches.
