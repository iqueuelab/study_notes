# Great Nicobar: development, ecology and tribal rights

## Project and map recall
- **Great Nicobar**, southern Nicobar Islands, Andaman and Nicobar UT; strategic proximity to Indian Ocean sea lanes and Southeast Asia.
- Core integrated project: **container transshipment terminal at Galathea Bay, international airport, township and power plant**. [Environmental-clearance project record](https://environmentclearance.nic.in/auth/FORM_A_PDF.aspx?cat_id=IA%2FAN%2FNCP%2F201159%2F2021).
- The January 2025 PDF reports a **₹72,000-crore** overall proposal and an additional **100-acre seafront** request for cruise/tourism infrastructure and possible ship breaking. Do not replace the main container terminal with a cruise terminal or assume every additional proposal is approved.
## Ecological and social stakes
- Rainforests, mangroves, reefs and seagrass provide habitat/coastal protection; construction, dredging, pollution and noise may disrupt these systems.
- **Leatherback turtle nesting; Nicobar megapode; dugong/seagrass habitat** are key examples. Source also lists wild pig, treeshrew, crested serpent eagle and Nicobar paradise flycatcher.
- Embedded graphic reports **over 8.5 lakh trees / 130 km²**, and **648 flora / 330 fauna species** for the cited forest context. These are source-reported proposal/biodiversity figures, not independently harmonised counts.
- Graphic contrasts a ZSI report of **117 scleractinian coral species** with an EIA claim about the Galathea Bay site; retain as a **disputed site/assessment claim**, not a resolved finding.
- **Shompen** hunter-gatherer foraging grounds and **Nicobarese** livelihoods/land rights; migration can pressure water, housing, health services and cultural continuity.
## Development and security
- Potential benefits: transshipment, trade connectivity, jobs and strategic maritime presence; actual gains depend on viability, skills and equitable benefit sharing.
- Dual-use infrastructure needs access control, surveillance, resilience and coordination of civilian/military activities; hypothetical risks do not establish that sabotage or interference has occurred.
- Tourism and ship recycling, if pursued, need carrying-capacity assessment and robust waste/hazardous-material controls.
## Transparency and decision framework
- Source criticises security-based RTI restrictions and public participation; treat claims about particular denials as its reported dispute, not proof that no clearance or consultation exists.
- **Alternatives → cumulative EIA → rights/community consultation → security-sensitive disclosure rules → enforceable safeguards → independent monitoring**.
- Balance strategic value with irreversible habitat loss, tribal welfare and disaster vulnerability; scrutinise both costs and claimed mitigation effectiveness.

## July2025 seismic-risk debate
- Embedded reporting contrasts the project's EIA treatment of a2004-scale earthquake with an **IITKanpur assessment stressing uncertainty in predicting extreme seismic events**. Low estimated recurrence/probability is not zero risk; cumulative earthquake,tsunami,coastal-subsidence and evacuation/resilience analysis matters for port,airport,township and power infrastructure.
- Source quotes **~15ft2004subsidence**, a locality-dependent estimate, not uniform sinking of the entire island. Safety concerns do not themselves constitute a final engineering verdict that no infrastructure can be built.
- **Campbell Bay and Galathea national parks**, GreatNicobarBiosphereReserve; **TenDegreeChannel separates the Andaman and Nicobar groups**, not a narrow channel immediately north of GreatNicobar alone. **IndiraPoint** is India's southernmost land point.
- Source project footprint **16,610ha**, tribal reserve **751km²/84km²proposeddenotification**, and power unit **450MVA** are source descriptions requiring document-specific scope; MVA(apparent power) is not interchangeable with MW(real power). Shompen PVTG resource access and Nicobarese2004relocation experience require meaningful consultation/rights assessment.
- November2022TribalCouncil withdrawal ofNOC and April2023NGThigh-powered review are reported legal/administrative history; a committee review is not unconditional validation of all claims. Do not infer commercial-port Navy control solely from strategic rationale; official operating arrangements require separate evidence.

## August 2025: disputed forest-rights certification
- Embedded article reports the **Great and Little Nicobar Tribal Council** complained that administration had represented FRA rights as settled although the council disputed settlement of individual/community rights. It describes an **August 2022 official certificate** and a **July 2025 representation/meeting**; these are reported claims, not a final court finding of fraud or resolved title.
- The council also disputed coverage of the **2006 Forest Rights Act** in the islands and stressed Nicobarese displacement after the 2004 tsunami. Statutory recognition/settlement of applicable rights and informed consultation need to be examined separately from environmental clearance and infrastructure strategy.
- Existing notes already retain the four project components, **₹72,000 crore** proposal, **16,610 ha** footprint, seismic/tsunami uncertainty, Shompen PVTG, leatherback nesting and National Parks. Source's **450 MVA** is apparent power, not MW; strategic rationale does not establish that the Navy will commercially control the port.
- Practice **1, 2 and 3 only (c)**; Great Nicobar is **south** of the Ten Degree Channel. **2009 Shompen: Nicobar Islands (b)**; **2014 channel: Andaman and Nicobar (a)**.

## October 2025: port economics and the maritime-arc debate
- Editorial questions the economic case for the **Galathea Bay transshipment terminal** in the integrated Great Nicobar proposal. Existing notes retain the four components, ANIIDCO, approximately **₹72,000-crore** source-era overall proposal and ecological/tribal/seismic risks.
- **Capacity does not guarantee throughput**: shipping-line commitments, feeder networks, reliable handling, competitive charges, fuel/services and route integration determine hub success. A **transshipment hub** transfers containers between vessels and need not have a large local hinterland; absence of local cargo increases reliance on network economics but does not alone prove impossibility.
- Source compares **Colombo ~8 million TEU annual throughput** with a **Great Nicobar 16 million TEU capacity ambition**. An existing year's actual throughput and a future design target are not like-for-like measures. **TEU = twenty-foot equivalent unit**. Avoid assuming announced capacity is achieved traffic.
- **Vizhinjam, Kerala**, serves east–west shipping; **Vadhavan, Maharashtra**, links to a substantial industrial hinterland. Great Nicobar has a different cargo/connectivity/risk model; a “maritime triangle” label cannot substitute for project-specific viability and alternatives assessment. Vallarpadam/Krishnapatnam examples raise utilisation questions but do not establish that every function of those ports has failed.
- **INS Baaz**, commissioned **31 July 2012**, is a naval air station at Campbell Bay, supporting surveillance near strategic sea lanes; it is distinct from a container terminal. Existing military capacity neither proves the commercial project viable nor by itself settles all future security requirements. [Commissioning record](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=85620).
- Reject the issue's **“2010 tsunami”** shorthand: the major disaster discussed in the existing Nicobar notes is the **2004 Indian Ocean tsunami**. Its **one million versus one lakh trees** statements are internally inconsistent; retain earlier document-qualified forest figures rather than invent a reconciled total.
- Evaluate commercial risk separately from defence aims, alongside cumulative EIA, disaster resilience, Shompen/Nicobarese rights, turtle nesting and biodiversity. The editorial's claims of hasty clearance or unavoidable ecological collapse are arguments requiring evidence, not final judicial or engineering findings. **Articles 48A/51A(g)**, environmental protection and applicable forest-rights duties inform the assessment; a blue-economy vision is not a substitute for statutory appraisal.

## October 2025: Centre's mitigation case before NGT
- The Centre defended the integrated project before NGT, claiming awareness of ecological impacts, no Nicobarese/Shompen displacement and conservation/monitoring until **2052**. These are government submissions, not a final judicial finding that no rights or biodiversity harm will occur.
- Source now reports a **₹92,000-crore** project estimate; earlier ₹72,000 crore figures remain dated estimates rather than silently overwritten. It describes roughly **160 km²**, including **130 km² forest**, and claims **18% of Great Nicobar/1.82% of the archipelago**. Geographic percentage does not measure ecological or cultural impact proportionately.
- Proposed mitigation: relocation of approximately **16,000 of 20,668 coral colonies**, monitoring of others, protection of turtle nesting and megapode habitat. Source reports approximately **30 of 51 active megapode nests affected**. These are submitted counts/plans, not demonstrated survival or successful replacement of habitat.
- Petitioners contest surveys, tribal impacts, ICRZ/EIA compliance and disaster assessment. Clearance conditions, cumulative impacts, enforceable monitoring and tribal rights require scrutiny separately; conserve–monitor–translocate promises cannot alone prove that old-growth forest or endemic ecology is replaceable.
