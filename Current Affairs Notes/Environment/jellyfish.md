# Jellyfish blooms and coastal infrastructure risk

## Ecology meets power-plant resilience
- Embedded report describes an **August 2025 jellyfish swarm blocking cooling-water intakes at a French nuclear plant**, prompting shutdown. Marine organisms can disrupt critical infrastructure without damaging the reactor itself; intake monitoring, screening and safe cooling resilience matter.
- True jellyfish belong to **Cnidaria**, like corals and sea anemones; gelatinous, radially symmetrical zooplankton. **Epidermis → mesoglea → gastrodermis**; mesoglea is jelly-like material, not a third embryonic germ layer. They lack a central brain/heart/blood system, possess a nerve net and tentacular stinging structures (**nematocysts**), and pulse their bell while drifting with currents.
- Blooms can be favoured by warm water, removal of predators/competitors, eutrophication/hypoxia and artificial surfaces supporting life stages; species/local conditions determine outcomes. Pollution does not invariably cause a bloom, and no single bloom proves climate change as its sole cause.
- Large blooms can consume fish eggs/larvae, compete for plankton, foul fishing nets, sting tourists and obstruct power-plant intakes. Some jellyfish tolerate low oxygen better than competitors; avoid saying all survive every anoxic condition.
- **2021 detritivores PYQ: C (earthworms, millipedes, woodlice)**. Jellyfish and seahorses are predators; detritivores ingest dead organic matter, unlike microbial extracellular decomposition. Unyearred bloom practice: **D (all four)** within the source's broad framing.
