# Loggerhead turtle: climate stress and marine conservation

## Identity and ecology
- **Loggerhead turtle—Caretta caretta**, family **Cheloniidae**; a hard-shelled marine reptile named for its large head and powerful jaws. It consumes hard-shelled prey such as molluscs and crustaceans; “omnivorous” should not obscure its predominantly animal prey.
- Distributed across the **Atlantic, Indian and Pacific Oceans and Mediterranean**; different life stages use offshore and nearshore habitats. Geomagnetic navigation helps long-distance movements and return towards natal nesting regions.
- **IUCN global status: Vulnerable**; regional assessments differ. Do not confuse this with the different US Endangered Species Act population categories. [IUCN assessment](https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2017-2.RLTS.T3897A119333622.en).
## Climate change and conservation
- The issue discusses warming/food constraints affecting migration, nesting/reproduction and body size. Such regional observations should not be converted into a uniform measured global trend without the underlying study.
- **Temperature-dependent sex determination:** warmer nesting sand generally favours female hatchlings; excessive heat can kill embryos. Sea-level rise/storm erosion threatens beaches; warmer water can change prey distribution, foraging ranges and nesting seasons.
- **Bycatch** in trawls, longlines and gillnets is a major threat, alongside egg/turtle harvesting, debris/pollution, artificial beach lighting, coastal development and vessel strikes. Protect nesting sites and migratory habitat; use suitable turtle-excluder/modified fishing gear and monitoring. [NOAA conservation overview](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/loggerhead-turtle/conservation-management).
- Avoid treating “largest hard-shelled turtle” as its diagnostic feature: **large head, strong jaws, shell features and ecology** are more useful than an unqualified size ranking.
