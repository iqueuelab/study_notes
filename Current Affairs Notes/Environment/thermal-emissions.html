Environment

Thermal power emissions: FGD and regulatory accountability

Editable notes

Pollutants and technology

  • Coal/lignite stations emit PM, SO₂, NOx and CO₂. 7 December 2015 standards introduced/revised pollutant limits; original compliance target 2017 later extended.
  • FGD = flue-gas desulphurisation, removes SO₂, not CO₂; auxiliary power, water, reagents/by-products and plant retrofits add costs. Lower-sulphur Indian coal does not imply zero SO₂ or uniform exposure.
  • SO₂ contributes to respiratory harm and secondary aerosols; climate mitigation and local air-pollution control overlap but are distinct objectives.

February 2025 regulatory snapshot

  • 30 December 2024 notification extended differentiated deadlines: A to 2027, B to 2028, C to 2029; source's single “all until 2027” is incomplete. Category A proximity to NCR/million-plus cities; B critically polluted/non-attainment locations; C others. Original notification.
  • Later change, 11 July 2025: A compliance by 2027; B applicability case-by-case on expert appraisal; C exemption subject to stack-height conditions by 2029. Keep this separate from February source analysis. MoEF parliamentary reply.

Governance issues

  • Retrofit lead times, technology/supply constraints, tariff pass-through and disagreement over uniform versus exposure-based requirements must be assessed against public-health gains.
  • Installing equipment ≠ operating it. Require verified continuous-emissions monitoring, public compliance data, independent audits and penalties; protect consumers from paying for unused assets.
  • Polluter pays assigns environmental prevention/remediation costs to the responsible entity; final consumer price incidence can still arise through lawful tariff mechanisms.
  • SO₂ cap-and-trade is an air-pollution instrument; carbon pricing does not directly replace SO₂ controls. Risk-based local standards, enforceable milestones and cleaner generation/storage should complement each other.
  • Source statements about IIT/NEERI studies and blanket absence of data need study/scope verification; FGD energy use does not by itself make every pollution-control installation incompatible with climate goals.

Practice recall

  • 2015 standards true; high-sulphur Indian-coal generalisation and claim of uniform CEA support false. Questions supplied here are undated practice, not PYQs.

Crop damage and pollutant-specific controls

  • Stanford-linked modelling estimated coal-plant NO₂ impacts on nearby rice/wheat yields, potentially up to 10%; counterfactual output gains valued aroundUS$420 million rice/US$400 million wheat are estimates, not realised revenue. Study summary.
  • ESP→particulates; FGD→SO₂; low-NOx burners/SCR/SNCR→NOx. An ESP does not remove NO₂ by itself.
  • Calibrated continuous monitoring, independent checks and published data support health/crop protection. Budget growth does not directly measure generation or pollution growth.

July 14 editorial: exposure and equality

  • The editorial challenges unequal protection after the July SO₂ relaxation: lower average ambient SO₂ does not measure every exposed community's risk, and sulphate-aerosol cooling does not justify continued health damage. Aerosol cooling and greenhouse warming are distinct effects.
  • Cost, vendor constraints and low coal sulphur are government arguments; weigh them against secondary particles, acid deposition and public-health impacts, with transparent consultation and local monitoring. Existing category-specific rules remain the operative distinction.

July 15: government rationale and category distinction

  • Embedded graphic separates A ~11%, B ~11%, C ~78% of units: A mandatory SO₂ compliance; B case-by-case expert-appraisal recommendation; C exemption subject to stack-height compliance. Thus “22% all must install FGD” conflates mandatory and conditional categories, units and stations.
  • Government cites low domestic coal sulphur (~0.5%), generally compliant ambient SO₂ readings, retrofit costs (~₹1.2 crore/MW; source universal estimate ₹2.54 lakh crore) and limited measured urban benefit. These are dated policy arguments, not proof zero secondary-particle or agricultural harm.
  • Source reports only Dehradun/Kolar among 492 monitored cities in 2023 exceeded the relevant SO₂ benchmark; monitoring geography and averaging period matter. Do not compare international standards without matching averaging periods and legal criteria.
  • FGD installation/compliance shares vary by whether counting capacity, units or stations. Existing July 11 category rules take precedence over simplified infographic language. The 250-word/15-mark daily question is coaching practice.
