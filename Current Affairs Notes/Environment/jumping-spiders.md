# Jumping spiders and taxonomic discoveries

## June 2025 discovery
- **Spartaeus karigiri**, family **Salticidae**, subfamily **Spartaeinae**: reported from **Devarayanadurga, Karnataka**, and **Viluppuram, Tamil Nadu**, named for Karigiri/Elephant Hill. The species is catalogued in 2025. [World Spider Catalog](https://wsc.nmbe.ch/spec-data/71949).
- First Indian records reported for genera **Spartaeus and Sonoita**; do not extend this automatically to the whole subfamily. Sharp vision and predation involving invasion of other spiders' webs/aggressive mimicry differ from building orb webs for prey capture.
- **Sonoita cf. lightfooti** from Karnataka raises unresolved range-extension versus introduction questions; cf. signals provisional comparison, not certain species identification or proof of invasion.
- Source reports **Marpissa gangasagarensis (2005)** synonymised with **Phaeacius fimbriatus (1900)**: taxonomic revision avoids counting a known species twice.
- Reject the practice explanation that both discovery sites lie in the Western Ghats; Viluppuram is in eastern Tamil Nadu. Spider versus insect: **8 legs/two main body regions/no antennae** versus **6 legs/three regions/one antennal pair**.

## Pilia malenadu: October 2025 taxonomic discovery
- **Pilia malenadu**, jumping-spider family **Salticidae**, described by **John T. D. Caleb, Ajith Padiyar, A. P. C. Abhijith and Jithesh Pai** in **Zootaxa 5716(2), 279–288**, 30 October 2025. The study reports rediscovery of the enigmatic genus and the first female description; a new species is not the rediscovery of a previously named species. [Original study record](https://tb.plazi.org/GgServer/summary/6752685AFFD69147FF9BD739FFC6FFB6).
- Source locality: **Madhugundi, Mudigere taluk, Chikkamagaluru, Karnataka**, Western Ghats; *malenadu* refers to the hilly region. Source sample: **24 individuals—17 males, three females, four juveniles**, observed on **Memecylon umbellatum and M. malabaricum**.
- Observations suggest a specific plant microhabitat; they do not prove survival is biologically impossible on every other plant or that the surrounding ecosystem is uniformly healthy. Protecting native vegetation conserves dependent fauna. The source's 123-year interval refers to the 1902 genus history; avoid treating it as a demonstrated global absence of all Pilia records.
- The naturalist's earlier **Saffron Reedtail damselfly** observation illustrates regional biodiversity; spiders and damselflies belong to different arthropod groups. Practice intends **1 and 3 only (B)**, with statement 2's wide-habitat claim rejected; this unyearred question is not a PYQ.
