# Biohappiness, agrobiodiversity and opportunity crops

## Biodiversity → nutrition → livelihoods
- **M.S. Swaminathan’s “biohappiness”**: wellbeing from sustainable, equitable use of biological resources; **Evergreen Revolution** seeks enduring productivity without ecological harm. Agrobiodiversity includes crop/livestock/microbial diversity and associated knowledge.
- **Neglected and underutilised species (NUS), orphan/opportunity crops**: traditional foods with low research/market support. **Millets, jackfruit, amaranth, yams and tubers** offer dietary/resource-use opportunities, but resilience/nutrition vary by species and preparation.
- Source describes food/knowledge loss among **Nyishi/Apatani communities, Arunachal Pradesh**, and cash-crop shifts in **Kolli Hills, Tamil Nadu**. Crop concentration in rice/wheat/maize can reduce dietary and climatic resilience; it does not alone establish the cause of every NCD or unequal food distribution.
- India is one of **17 megadiverse countries**; portions of four hotspots: **Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats–Sri Lanka, Sundaland (Nicobar)**. Source rounded **2% land/8% biodiversity** uses species-based measures; “₹130 trillion/year forest services” lacks valuation baseline and should not be memorised as a realised annual income.
- **International Year of Millets 2023**, millet value chains/branding and **Odisha Millet Mission, including Koraput**, support producer-to-consumer links. “Shree Anna” is the policy label for millets; source “Shree Anna Yojana” should not imply one verified standalone national scheme with automatic benefits.
- Priorities: community seed systems, conservation/research of local varieties, women’s knowledge/participation, fair benefit sharing, appropriate inclusion of minor millets in PDS and nutrition programmes, processing/markets and soil health. Biodiversity science connects agriculture, ecology, nutrition and bioeconomy; potential jobs/health gains are not completed outcomes.
