# Source segregation and the municipal waste chain

## Segregation and treatment
- **Source segregation:** separate wet/biodegradable, dry/recyclable and **domestic hazardous** waste; special biomedical/e-waste streams follow appropriate rules.
- Keep streams separate in collection/transport; segregating at home achieves little if mixed again in the truck.
| Stream | Appropriate route |
| --- | --- |
| Suitable wet organics | Composting/biomethanation with contamination controls |
| Dry recyclables | Recovery/recycling through authorised systems |
| Non-recyclable high-calorific residues | Suitable controlled energy recovery, not indiscriminate burning |
| Hazardous/inert/process residues | Appropriate authorised treatment/engineered disposal |
- Benefits: less landfill methane/leachate, better recycling, safer workers and resource recovery; treatment may still generate residues/emissions.
## Rules and programmes
- Source-era **SWM Rules2016**, **Plastic Waste Rules2016/EPR**, **SBM-U2.0** and **SATAT** compressed-biogas support. **2022 SUP ban targets listed products**, not every single-use plastic object.
- Producers' responsibility and municipal obligations differ; blanket plastic/e-waste EPR cannot be attributed solely to SWM Rules.
- **Indore** collection/biogas, **Pune SWaCH** waste-picker integration, **Alappuzha** decentralised composting illustrate approaches; source's30,000-Pune-worker and100%-zero-waste claims require verification.
## Data and governance
- Source **~160,000 tonnes/day generated /79,000 processed /3,159 dumpsites /14 WTE plants**, composition and city segregation shares are mixed dated estimates; not a harmonised current national inventory.
- Sweden's “99% recycling” often conflates material recycling with energy recovery; international segregation, treatment and landfill percentages are different metrics.
- Awareness plus reliable separate collection, ULB capacity, user fees/enforcement, EPR accountability, decentralised organics treatment and dignified waste-worker inclusion.
- WTE is not an automatic answer for high-moisture mixed waste; technology choices need feedstock, pollution control, costs and maintained operation.

## January 2026 editorial: circular urban systems
- Extend existing segregated-treatment chain with **construction/demolition material recovery**, recycled-water/fecal-sludge management under **AMRUT/SBM**, credible markets for recycled aggregates/compost/RDF and waste-picker inclusion. Wet organic waste suits controlled composting/biomethanation; contaminated mixed waste undermines recycling and energy recovery.
- **Environment (Construction and Demolition) Waste Management Rules 2025**, notified in 2025, were scheduled to commence **1 April 2026**, replacing 2016 rules. The issue date therefore precedes commencement. EPR/central monitoring and collection/utilisation obligations require compliance systems, testing and integration with building approvals. [Official confirmation of commencement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2230897&lang=2&reg=48) (February 2026 verification of the pre-existing schedule).
- Source projections **165 Mt/year waste by 2030, 436 Mt by 2050, ~814m urban population by 2050**, and **41 Mt waste GHG emissions/12 Mt C&D** lack sufficiently aligned time/unit/inventory detail to serve as a harmonised current national dataset. Similarly **>50% organics/>one-third dry** vary by city/sample.
- The article recycles pre-COP30 wording: **Belém COP30 occurred in November 2025**, so “to be held” is stale in January 2026. **LiFE concept announced at COP26 in 2021; Mission LiFE formally launched October 2022**. Swachh Bharat's ODF declarations are not proof all open defecation has permanently disappeared.
- **Cities Coalition for Circularity (C-3)** offers a cooperation/knowledge-sharing example; implementation depends on ULB skills, finance, interdepartmental coordination, user incentives, enforcement, private participation and transparent performance monitoring. Resource reduction/reuse remain preferable where feasible; recycling is not the only valid circular-economy route.
- Practice Mains (250 words): connect waste with methane, resource efficiency and urban health; assess segregation/infrastructure, viable product demand, EPR enforcement and municipal capacity; propose measured outcomes and worker protection. Undated practice excluded from PYQs.
