# Great Barrier Reef and coral bleaching

## Map and conservation
- World's largest **coral reef system**, **Coral Sea, northeast Australia/Queensland**, Pacific; roughly **2,300 km**, **~3,000 reefs**. Marine Park **344,400 sq km**; source rounded **350,000** and World Heritage area measurements use differing boundaries. **Not every square kilometre is living coral**. [Reef Authority facts](https://www.gbrmpa.gov.au/learn/fascinating-facts-about-great-barrier-reef).
- **UNESCO World Heritage, 1981**, managed largely by **Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority/Reef Authority**. Source around **400 coral types, 1,500 fishes, 4,000 molluscs**; dugongs and green turtles are notable fauna. Source **10% global coral ecosystems** needs denominator/date.
- Platform/patch reefs, elongated reefs/walls and **fringing reefs**; classic evolution **fringing → barrier → atoll** with subsidence and continued growth, not a universal history for every reef.
## Coral recall
- **Coral polyps are animals**, commonly colonial; reef-building **hard corals deposit calcium carbonate**, unlike soft corals' usual structure. Many tropical corals associate with **zooxanthellae**.
- Heat/other stress → loss of symbiotic algae/pigment → **bleaching**. Bleached coral is **not necessarily dead**, but prolonged stress causes starvation/disease and mortality.
- Threats: warming, acidification, sediment/pollution, salinity changes, destructive fishing/coastal development and tourism pressure; protect water quality and habitats alongside emissions reduction.
- Benefits: biodiversity, coast protection, fisheries/jobs/tourism and research. Global reef biodiversity claims concern all coral reefs, not GBR alone.
- Indian examples: **Gulf of Kachchh, Gulf of Mannar, Andaman–Nicobar, Lakshadweep**; source also mentions Malvan. Conservation examples: **International Coral Reef Initiative**, **Global Coral Reef R&D Accelerator Platform**, India's **National Coastal Mission**; cryopreservation/larval restoration and mineral-accretion **Biorock** are tools, not substitutes for controlling stress.
## 24 April 2025: fourth global bleaching event
- **23 April ICRI update:** bleaching-level heat stress affected **84% of global coral reef area**, across Atlantic, Pacific and Indian basins, between January 2023 and March 2025; mass bleaching documented in **at least 82 countries/territories**. This measures exposure to stress, not the percentage of coral already dead. Source’s “54 countries” is an older snapshot. [ICRI update](https://icriforum.org/4gbe-2025/).
- Fourth global event was confirmed in **April 2024**; earlier events **1998, 2010 and 2014–17**, with bleaching-level heat-stress exposure of **21%, 37% and 68%** respectively in the ICRI comparison. Avoid the source’s unsupported “35% destruction in 2010” and “56% affected in 2014–17”: extent, bleaching and mortality have different denominators.
- Source’s SST **21.07°C** is an undated older monthly statistic, not a verified April 2025 global-ocean temperature.
## Ecology and impacts: additions
- Cnidarian polyps use tentacles to capture food. In symbiosis, coral provides shelter/nutrients and algae supplies photosynthetic products/pigments. **Hard corals build reef frameworks; soft corals inhabit reefs but do not build rigid reef-forming skeletons**, contrary to the source. [NOAA](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/education/tutorial_corals/coral01_intro.html).
- Source additionally lists **Palk Bay, Goa and Kerala coasts**; distinguish scattered coral communities from the principal extensive Indian reef systems recorded above.
- Reefs support roughly a quarter of marine species despite occupying a small fraction of the ocean; Great Barrier Reef turtle/biodiversity counts are site examples rather than global counts. Source’s **US$375 billion annual services/500 million dependent people** are older estimates without baseline here, not current measured totals.
- Heat/light stress, changed salinity, pollution and disease can damage corals. Acidification reduces calcification and recovery; nutrient enrichment promotes competing algae. The named *Vibrio shiloi* example is not a universal explanation of global bleaching.
- Mortality reduces habitat/genetic diversity and alters fish food webs/catches, food security and tourism income. Reef wave attenuation provides coastal protection; “up to 97%” is a study/context-dependent potential, not identical protection at every coast.
- Priorities: emissions reduction; control runoff/sediment and coastal development; sustainable fishing, regulated ecotourism and recovery monitoring. Local restoration cannot compensate for persistent ocean warming; bleaching can reverse if stress subsides, whereas prolonged stress can cause death.

- Newspaper clipping reports **~84% of global coral-reef area exposed to bleaching-level heat stress** by the April2025 update; this is not 84% of coral dead. It also refers to a **24-year Lakshadweep survey finding ~50% coral-cover reduction**; distinguish survey-site coral cover from national/global reef loss.
