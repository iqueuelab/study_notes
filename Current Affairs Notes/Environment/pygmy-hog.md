# Pygmy hog: alluvial grasslands and species recovery

## Species and conservation
- **Porcula salvania**, world's smallest wild pig; survives in India's Assam grasslands. It makes sheltered grass nests and is a sensitive **indicator of tall alluvial grassland health**, supporting conservation of the wider sub-Himalayan ecosystem.
- **IUCN: Endangered**, according to the published **2019 assessment**; the source's “Critically Endangered” label is outdated. **Wild Life (Protection) Act Schedule I** provides strong protection; legal schedule and Red List threat category are different classifications. [IUCN assessment](https://doi.org/10.2305/iucn.uk.2019-3.rlts.t21172a44139115.en).
- **Manas** holds the last original wild population; captive breeding and reintroduction have restored populations including **Orang**. Earlier release sites also included **Sonai-Rupai and Barnadi**, so “only Manas and Orang ever” is too restrictive. Habitat loss/degradation, agricultural conversion, overgrazing, poorly timed burning and altered grassland hydrology threaten survival. [Durrell recovery programme](https://www.durrell.org/news/a-milestone-for-rare-pygmy-hogs/).
- Conservation combines captive breeding, post-release monitoring, native grassland restoration and community participation. Wet grasslands also buffer floods and maintain water regimes; protecting them benefits associated species beyond this pig. Foraging disturbs soil, but the source's universal “keystone” and seed-dispersal claims need ecological evidence beyond the label.
- **2013 PYQ: (a), 1,2,3 only**—star tortoise, monitor lizard and pygmy hog naturally occur in India; spider monkeys are New World primates of Central/South America.
