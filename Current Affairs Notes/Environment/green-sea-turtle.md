# Green sea turtle: recovery and continuing threats

## October 2025: global Red List improvement
- **10 October IUCN update:** green turtle, **Chelonia mydas**, moved globally from Endangered to Least Concern following sustained conservation. This global category does not establish every regional population is secure or remove legal protection. [IUCN announcement](https://iucn.org/press-release/202510/arctic-seals-threatened-climate-change-birds-decline-globally-iucn-red-list).
- Cheloniidae marine reptile of tropical/subtropical Atlantic, Pacific and Indian waters; among the largest hard-shelled sea turtles. “Green” describes fat beneath the shell, not necessarily shell colour. Adults predominantly graze seagrass/algae; early life stages eat more animal material. Grazing supports seagrass ecology; exact diets vary by stage/location.
- India includes Gulf of Mannar, Lakshadweep and Andaman–Nicobar waters. Do not transfer Gahirmatha's famous **olive-ridley** mass-nesting/Operation Olivia account into a claim that it is the largest green-turtle rookery. Natal homing guides females to nesting areas but is not an absolute rule of return to one identical point.
- Indian Schedule I and CITES Appendix I safeguards are distinct from IUCN extinction-risk categories; commercial international trade is generally prohibited under CITES, with treaty exceptions/controls rather than a literal ban on every possible transaction.
- Fishing bycatch, habitat/light disturbance, plastic, hunting, boat strikes and climate effects persist. Sex determination responds to **nest incubation temperature**; hotter viable nests generally yield more females, while extreme heat harms survival. Nest/habitat protection, safer fisheries, community stewardship and marine cooperation require continuation after downlisting.
