# Ambergris, sperm whales and conservation trade

## Species and substance

- **Sperm whale, Physeter macrocephalus**: toothed marine mammal, family **Physeteridae**; deep-water predator feeding especially on squid; echolocation supports feeding/navigation.
- **IUCN Vulnerable**, correcting the source's Endangered; **CITES Appendix I**. [IUCN assessment](https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2008.RLTS.T41755A160983555.en).
- **Ambergris:** rare waxy intestinal substance associated with indigestible squid material; may be expelled and weather at sea. “Whale vomit” is an imprecise shorthand; it is neither plant material nor sperm oil.
- Historic perfume fixative; synthetic alternatives reduce dependence. Source **US$30,000–50,000/kg** is an unverified grey-market range, not a regulated/current price.

## Law and conservation

- Indian wildlife protection restricts sperm-whale products and illicit ambergris trade; possession/trade should not be inferred lawful merely because a piece washed ashore. [DRI enforcement account](https://dri.nic.in/writereaddata/DRI%20Bulletin%20artwork_oct24_march25.pdf).
- **CITES distinction:** naturally excreted ambergris is treated as a waste product outside the Convention's specimen provisions; this does **not override stricter national law**. Do not say CITES universally bans all ambergris. [CITES discussion](https://cites.org/sites/default/files/eng/cop/16/doc/E-CoP16-25-A3.pdf).
- Threats: historical whaling, entanglement/ship strikes, underwater noise, pollution and changing ocean conditions. Whales contribute to nutrient/carbon cycling; this does not establish a quantified climate offset from every animal.
- Response: enforce wildlife law/anti-smuggling cooperation, coastal awareness, whale-safe shipping/noise reduction and synthetic fragrance research. Luxury demand and uncertain origin create traceability/ethical problems.

## Recall and mains

- Practice: substance + perfume use true; “completely legal in India” and “plant-based” false. Overpopulation of squid is not a recognised principal threat.
- Mains: **demand/traceability → exploitation risk → national/international legal distinction → enforcement + alternatives + habitat protection**. Supplied questions are undated practice, not verified PYQs.
