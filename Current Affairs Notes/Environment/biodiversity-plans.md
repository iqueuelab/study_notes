# Biodiversity action plans and local governance

## From global targets to districts
- Arunachal Pradesh's SBSAP was presented as a people's plan integrating district-level priorities with India's NBSAP and the **Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework**, adopted at CBD COP15 in 2022.
- KMGBF has **four long-term goals and 23 targets for 2030**. Target 3: effective, equitable conservation of at least 30% of land/inland waters and marine/coastal areas.
- Other targets include halving global food waste, reducing pollution risks and reforming harmful subsidies. It does not require instant elimination of every agricultural subsidy or every use of pesticides.
- CBD Article 6 requires national strategies/plans and integration into sectoral policies. India's institutional tiers: **NBA → State Biodiversity Boards → local Biodiversity Management Committees**, with different legal roles rather than one command chain.
- Examples: Apatani wet-rice/fish systems and Pakke hornbill-nest adoption show local knowledge and community participation. Great hornbill is Vulnerable, not uniformly labelled Endangered.
- Make district baselines, budgets, rights-sensitive consultation and monitoring operational; eco-tourism is beneficial only with ecological limits and fair local returns.
