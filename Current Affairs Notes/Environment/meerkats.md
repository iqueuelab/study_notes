# Meerkats and cooperative behaviour

## Species recall
- **Suricata suricatta**, mongoose family **Herpestidae**, order **Carnivora**; small diurnal mammal, not a rodent. Global IUCN status **Least Concern** does not imply every local population is free of pressures.
- Southern African dry/open habitats, especially **Botswana, Namibia and South Africa**, extending into southwestern Angola; **Kalahari** is a key landscape. The PDF's Lesotho occurrence should not be memorised without a supported range record. [San Diego Zoo species account](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/meerkat).
- Social groups/mobs show cooperative care, grooming and sentinel calls during foraging; group size varies, often around the source's **20–30** example. Sentinel behaviour is useful in studying cooperation/kin selection, but describing it as pure self-sacrifice oversimplifies benefits and risks.
- Burrows provide shelter, escape and thermal buffering. Mainly insectivorous, with scorpions, small vertebrates and plant material also eaten; **Carnivora taxonomy** does not imply an exclusively meat diet.
- Resistance to some venom and handling skills are not immunity to every snake/scorpion toxin. Habitat degradation and inappropriate pet trade are concerns; sight, alert communication and group vigilance help predator detection.
