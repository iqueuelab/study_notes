# Lake Naivasha and invasive water hyacinth

## Location and livelihoods
- **Kenya, southern Rift Valley**, shallow **freshwater lake**, surrounded by volcanic terrain; rivers **Malewa and Gilgil**, draining central Kenyan highlands/Aberdare region.
- **Ramsar site**; papyrus (**Cyperus papyrus**), submerged **Potamogeton**, waterbirds and mammals. Supports **floriculture, fishing** and nearby **geothermal power** activity. [Ramsar record](https://rsis.ramsar.org/ris/724).
## Water hyacinth
- South American invasive floating plant, **Eichhornia crassipes** (also classified as **Pontederia crassipes**); source introduction to Kenya in **1980s** is a regional historical account.
- Nutrient enrichment supports rapid growth → dense mats shade submerged vegetation and impede surface gas exchange/navigation → oxygen/habitat and fishing problems. “Thrives in contamination” does not mean every contaminant promotes growth.
- Control nutrient inputs, monitor infestation and combine suitable mechanical/biological management with safe disposal; indiscriminate herbicide use can harm lake ecology.
- Practice's black-rhinoceros distractor does not establish absence of every rhino from the wider landscape; lake identity rests on location, hydrology and wetland ecology.
