# Nurdle spills and marine plastic pollution

## Kerala spill: 29 May 2025
- The embedded clipping links pellets on Thiruvananthapuram beaches to the sunken **MSC ELSA 3**; local observations identified mainly **LDPE/HDPE**. Volunteers, drone surveys and State pollution-control rapid-response teams were mobilised; crew rescue did not remove the continuing environmental risk.
- **Nurdles** are pre-production plastic pellets, commonly about **1–5 mm**; used for packaging, containers, pipes, films and other products. Polyethylene, polypropylene, polystyrene and PVC pellets differ chemically and in buoyancy; not every pellet floats.
- Losses during manufacture, handling/storage and transport enter drains → rivers → sea; shipping accidents can create concentrated coastal deposits. The **2021 X-Press Pearl disaster off Sri Lanka** illustrates large-scale pellet loss; the issue quotes about **1,680 tonnes** as a contemporary estimate.
- Pellets are **primary microplastics**; further fragmentation generates smaller micro/nanoplastics rather than complete biodegradation. They contaminate habitats, may be ingested by wildlife and can carry additives or sorbed pollutants. Injury, reduced feeding and toxic exposure depend on species, dose and conditions.
- The source's **230,000 tonnes/year, 11.5 trillion pellets, 220 species, second-largest source after tyre dust and 600 pellets/bottle** figures lack a consistent methodology/year. Retain the pollution mechanism rather than treating these as universal measured constants.
- Finding microplastics in human samples does not establish they came from this spill, or prove a quantified disease burden/inevitable biomagnification. Nurdles' physical and chemical hazards matter even when the base polymer is not inherently acutely toxic.
- Prevention is essential, but early shoreline containment and carefully supervised cleanup remain useful; “cleanup is impossible/prevention is the only solution” is too absolute. Protect nesting/feeding habitats and avoid harming organisms during recovery.
- The source's “no IMO rules” is misleading: **MEPC 81 approved 2024 recommendations** on packaging, declaration and stowage of pellets in freight containers. These were **non-binding recommendations**, distinct from a comprehensive mandatory pellet-specific regime; wider container-safety requirements already existed. [IMO guidance](https://www.imo.org/en/mediacentre/hottopics/pages/oceans-default.aspx).
- Response priorities: spill/source inventory, shoreline/drift monitoring, safe collection/disposal, wildlife and food-safety assessment, transparent public communication, shipper accountability and stronger loss-prevention standards.
## PYQ revision
- **2019 microbeads:** **(a)** marine-ecosystem harm. Microbeads used in some personal-care products differ in intended use from industrial nurdles, although both contribute to microplastic pollution.
- **2022 PET:** **(a), 1 and 3**—polyester fibres can blend with wool/cotton; bottles can be recycled. “Any alcoholic beverage” is too sweeping, and PET incineration produces greenhouse gases. Suitability depends on food-contact standards and conditions rather than a universal alcohol threshold.
