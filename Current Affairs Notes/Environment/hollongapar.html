Hollongapar Gibbon Sanctuary and NBWL

Editable notes

Sanctuary and primates

  • Jorhat, Assam; approximately 100–120 m altitude; terrain gently slopes southeast → northwest. Bhogdoi River supports waterlogged/semi-hydrophytic habitat at its border.

Upper canopyHollong

  • Hoolock gibbons: India's only wild apes, family Hylobatidae, arboreal/diurnal, long arms and brachiation; territorial calls and pair bonding. Male dark with pale brows, female generally brown/buff. Not monkeys.
  • Source graphic's traditional species: western Hoolock hoolock, Endangered; eastern H. leuconedys, Vulnerable, CITES Appendix I, dated graphic categories. Traditional range includes Northeast India/Myanmar and, for western, Bangladesh; species boundaries/ranges can be revised. Graphic's 20 gibbon species / 25-year lifespan are approximate dated recall, not immutable counts.
  • Bengal slow loris, Northeast India's nocturnal primate; not the only nocturnal primate everywhere in India.
  • Source fauna: elephants, tiger/leopard, jungle cat, wild boar, civets, squirrels; stump-tailed, northern pig-tailed, rhesus and Assamese macaques, capped langur. Presence list is not a current population census.

January 2025 exploration approval

  • Standing Committee of NBWL approved proposed oil/gas exploration in the sanctuary's eco-sensitive zone, not unrestricted commercial production inside the sanctuary. Environmental, wildlife and other required clearances remain distinct. Parliamentary explanation.
  • NBWL: section 5A, Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, chaired by Prime Minister, Environment Minister vice-chair; source membership 47.
  • Standing Committee: section 5B, chaired by Environment Minister. Do not give the two bodies the same chair.
  • Habitat diversion, protected-area boundary changes and relevant tourism proposals face statutory safeguards; tiger-reserve changes also involve NTCA and applicable legal processes. NBWL does not alone erase every safeguard.
