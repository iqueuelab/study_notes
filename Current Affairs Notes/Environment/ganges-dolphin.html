Environment

Ganges river dolphin

Editable notes

Species

  • Platanista gangetica, Susu; National Aquatic Animal 2009, IUCN Endangered, CITESAppendix I and Wildlife Protection Act Schedule I.
  • Ganges and Indus dolphins are now recognised as distinct species; older subspecies nomenclature persists in older sources.
  • Reduced vision; echolocation supports navigation/prey detection in turbid rivers. Confluences/deeper channels are important habitats.

Population and protection

  • First national estimation, surveys 2021–23/report March 2025: 6, 327 river dolphins overall, distinguished from 6, 324 Ganges and 3 Indus dolphins; do not label the entire combined count Ganges-only.
  • Project Dolphin 2020; Vikramshila sanctuary, Bihar. First satellite tag in Assam 18 December 2024. Official tagging account.
  • Pollution, dams/flow fragmentation, entanglement and poaching threaten survival; protect ecological flows, reduce bycatch and monitor connected river habitat.

Survey interpretation

  • Source State totals:UP2, 397; Bihar 2, 220; West Bengal 815; Assam 635; Jharkhand 162; Rajasthan/MP95; Punjab 3. River-basin totals are a different classification, not inconsistent State totals.
  • Boat visual observations plus acoustic/hydrophone methods and statistical modelling address brief surfacing/detection probability; acoustic detection is not automatically unique identification of every animal.
  • First national baseline does not alone prove a stable population trend. Dolphins can leave genetic evidence in water; “no DNA evidence possible”is wrong.
  • Bycatch, oil/blubber use, pollution and flow fragmentation need community-linked measures; reported annual 4–5%mortality is study-specific, not a universally verified national rate.

PYQ revision: threats

  • 2014 Prelims: (c), 1, 3 and 4 only—dams/barrages, accidental fishing-net entanglement and agricultural-chemical pollution can reduce Ganges dolphin populations. An increase in crocodile numbers is not the corresponding established driver.
  • Source repeats earlier habitat/threat notes; retain Platanista gangetica and the distinction from Indus dolphin rather than reverting to older subspecies nomenclature.
