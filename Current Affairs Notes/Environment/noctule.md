# Common noctule bat and migration

## Species recall

- **Nyctalus noctula**; insectivorous mammal; narrow pointed wings, strong direct flight/steep insect-chasing dives; golden to dark-brown upper body, paler underside.
- Forest/tree hollows and some buildings/crevices; open-country/settlement foraging. Hibernates in winter; urban roosts important in parts of eastern Europe.
- Source distribution spans **Europe and temperate Asia**, with peripheral localities; **IUCN Least Concern** is the source status, not absence of local threats.
- Echolocation around **25 kHz**; this is **ultrasound**, above the usual ~20-kHz human limit. Some lower-frequency components may be audible; source's blanket '25 kHz heard by children' is unreliable.

## January 2025 migration study

- Common noctules exploit **warm tailwinds ahead of storm fronts**: temperature rises/pressure falls → departures → longer travel with lower energy use.
- Study tracked **71 females**, moving generally northeast from Swiss wintering areas; **no single fixed corridor**, feeding stops and almost **400 km in one night** recorded. Study tracked portions, not every individual's entire annual migration. [Max Planck study](https://www.mpg.de/23893833/bats-surf-storm-fronts-during-continental-migration).
- Ecological role: insect control; conservation use: predict migration windows and reduce wind-turbine collision mortality.
