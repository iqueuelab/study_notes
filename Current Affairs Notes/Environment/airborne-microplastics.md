# Airborne microplastics: inhalation and pollutant carriers

## December 2025: four-city inhalation study
- IISER Kolkata-led **November 2025 Environment International** study sampled at human breathing height in **Kolkata, Delhi, Chennai and Mumbai**. **Inhalable microplastics <10 µm** are the study subset within **microplastics <5 mm**; aerodynamic behaviour and fibre shape matter. The study definition should not be generalised into a complete inhalability standard for every particle.
- Reported **mean 8.8 µg/m³**; city mass concentrations **Kolkata 14.23, Delhi 14.18, Chennai 4.00, Mumbai 2.65 µg/m³**. **11 polymers** identified; polyester/PET prominent, with PE/SBR associated with packaging, textiles, vehicle/footwear sources. These sampled conditions are not national citywide annual averages. [Research abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41202458/).
- Source estimates **132 µg inhaled/person/day**; this exposure calculation is not the amount permanently deposited in lungs. Source winter-evening counts **32.7 versus 18.8 particles/m³ (~74% higher)** use a number concentration, distinct from µg/m³. Its **<100/100–500/>500 µm** distribution concerns broader airborne plastics, not entirely the <10 µm inhalable subset.
- “Trojan horse” mechanism: surfaces can carry **lead/cadmium, phthalates and other chemicals, fungi such as Aspergillus fumigatus, and microbes/genes linked with resistance**. Toxicology/database associations identify hazards; this environmental survey does not demonstrate clinical cancer causation or establish these plastics are universally more dangerous than PM2.5.
- Tyre abrasion, synthetic fabrics (including recycled polyester), packaging, construction/waste and burning contribute. Meteorology, winter inversion, poor dispersion and activity affect exposure; occupationally exposed vendors/traffic workers merit study and protection.
- Existing **AQI has no separate microplastic indicator**; particles may overlap PM size fractions, so saying AQI captures none is too strong. Improve comparable sampling, source control, waste segregation, EPR and occupational protection; India's restrictions target specified single-use plastic items, not an already existing ban on every plastic product. Undated practice MCQ/Mains prompt retained for revision only.
