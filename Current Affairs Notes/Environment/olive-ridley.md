# Olive ridley turtles and coastal conservation

## Species and nesting
- **Lepidochelys olivacea**, olive-coloured carapace; warm Pacific/Atlantic/Indian waters. **Arribada:** synchronised mass nesting; not every individual nests only in mass events.
- Odisha **Gahirmatha, Rushikulya, Devi-mouth** landscapes important; Gahirmatha a major rookery. **IUCN Vulnerable /CITES Appendix I /Indian Schedule I**.
- Predominantly animal prey including crustaceans/molluscs; turtle species have differing diets, so “all turtles carnivorous” is false.
## Threat →safeguard
- Fishing bycatch →seasonal exclusion/enforcement and **Turtle Excluder Devices (TEDs)**, grids/escape openings within appropriate trawls, not merely an extra exit cover on any net.
- Beach development/light disturbance →nesting-habitat safeguards; litter/plastics →source control and safer waste systems.
- Temperature-dependent sex determination relates to **nest incubation temperature**, not simply ocean-water temperature; warming and extreme conditions can alter ratios/survival.
- Tagging tracks movement; avoid assuming every predator listed in a global article occurs at every Odisha site.
- Coast Guard **Operation Olivia**, source early-1980s origin, seasonal patrol/protection and unlawful-trawling control; nesting season extends beyond an absolute November–December-only window.
## PYQ recall
- **2015 national aquatic animal: Gangetic dolphin**, not olive ridley.
- **2019:** some turtles, fish and marine mammals are herbivores; some snakes viviparous—**all four true**. Examples: green turtles, plant-eating fish, dugongs/manatees and live-bearing snakes.
## May 2025: Operation Olivia results
- **19 May Coast Guard release:** over **6.98 lakh olive ridley turtles nested at Rushikulya river mouth in February 2025** under conservation support; this is not a Gahirmatha-only count. Operation Olivia runs **November–May**, correcting prose's November–December limit. [Defence Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2129578&lang=2&noshow=1&reg=48).
- Cumulative release figures: **5,387 surface patrol sorties, 1,768 aerial surveillance missions, 366 illegal-fishing boats detained**; not all achieved solely in February. Community outreach, NGO cooperation and TED promotion complement enforcement.
- Repeated biology/status/threats remain covered in this topic. Embedded infographic's “Endangered” label conflicts with formal **IUCN Vulnerable** status; its global nesting estimate **800,000 females** is undated and not the Odisha record. Temperature-dependent sex ratios concern **nest incubation**, not simply warmer ocean water.
- Non-corrosive tagging supports movement studies; **TEDs** are escape mechanisms within appropriate trawls, not merely any net with an exit cover. Gahirmatha/Rushikulya habitat protection, bycatch reduction, light/plastic controls and nesting-beach safeguards work together.

## 21 May 2025: arribada ecology and conservation ethics
- **Philopatry** is return to natal/home areas; geomagnetic imprinting is a proposed navigation mechanism. Fidelity is not absolute: some females stray to alternative sites, spreading risk and enabling colonisation. Avoid attributing every first nest to proximity to a foraging ground.
- Dense arribadas can destroy earlier clutches through overlapping digging and attract egg predators; reproductive success is not proportional to the count of nesting females. Temperature-dependent sex determination responds to **incubation temperature**: warmer viable nests generally produce more females, while excessive heat can kill embryos.
- **Correction:** source's “7 lakh nests in March 2024” conflicts with the official record: **over 6.98 lakh turtles nested at Rushikulya in February 2025**, reported by the Coast Guard on **19 May 2025**. Turtles, nests and hatchlings are different measures. [Defence Ministry/PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2129578&lang=2&noshow=1&reg=48).
- Beach patrols, anti-poaching work and community/scientist cooperation complement habitat and fishing safeguards. Reduce bright lighting, touching/handling turtles, disturbance and inappropriate tourist access. Relocation/artificial incubation should follow evidence-based protocols and monitor hatching/sex ratios.
- The source's claim that rescuing weak hatchlings necessarily reduces genetic resilience is an **argument, not an established conservation rule**. Prioritise natural habitat and qualified management while avoiding unnecessary interference. Likewise its global “30–50% decline since 1960” needs a defined population/assessment baseline.
