# Crocothemis erythraea and freshwater bioindicators

## Western Ghats confirmation: study published 26 August 2025
- **Crocothemis erythraea**, order **Odonata**, family **Libellulidae**, occurs in higher southern Western Ghats; similar **C. servilia** is common in lowlands. Male genital characters and **mitochondrial COI** analysis confirm both species; Himalayan/Ghats erythraea populations show high genetic similarity.
- **Correction:** paper was published **26 August 2025**, not 2023 as the issue states. Its authors infer a relict distribution likely shaped during **Pleistocene glaciations**; “Ice Age dragonfly” is a biogeographic interpretation, not discovery of a fossil or proof of an exact colonisation date. [Original research](https://www.worlddragonfly.org/article/tijo20-v028-a9-sadasivan1).
- Taxonomic surveys, specimens and genetic comparison resolve misidentification. Protect montane freshwater refugia and catchments; warming/habitat alteration may threaten specialised populations.
## Bioindicators
- Aquatic odonate larvae and terrestrial adults connect freshwater and adjacent habitats; species assemblages can indicate ecological condition. Presence of any one dragonfly does **not** guarantee unpolluted water—tolerances vary.
- Lichens can indicate air pollution; amphibians integrate aquatic/terrestrial stresses; algal blooms indicate nutrient enrichment; earthworm communities reflect soil condition. Use species-specific responses with chemical/physical monitoring.
- Dragonflies prey on insects, including mosquitoes, and feed birds/fish/amphibians; predation alone does not establish control of malaria/dengue transmission.
