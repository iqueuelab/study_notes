# Rock eagle owl: nesting habitat and quarrying

## December 2025: nesting-site protection in Telangana
- Embedded report describes quarrying halted for around a month to protect **five owl eggs** in Telangana. Protecting active nests illustrates how habitat safeguards can affect extractive operations; this is a local conservation example, not evidence that quarrying is prohibited everywhere.
- **Indian/rock/Bengal eagle-owl: Bubo bengalensis**; large owl with conspicuous **ear tufts**, formerly treated within Eurasian eagle-owl **Bubo bubo**, now a separate species. Ear tufts are feathers, not horns.
- Resident South Asian bird, including **India south of the Himalayas, Nepal and Pakistan**; uses **rocky hills, cliffs, ravines, scrub and dry/semi-arid landscapes**, nesting on ledges or ground depressions. The source contrasts this with humid evergreen forest and extreme desert habitat.
- Predominantly nocturnal predator of rodents, other small mammals, birds and reptiles; rodent control contributes to ecological balance. Nest disturbance can cause abandonment. Threats include quarrying/habitat loss, electrocution, persecution and illegal capture associated with superstition.
- Source status snapshot: **IUCN Least Concern; CITES Appendix II**. Global LC status does not remove local threats or trade controls. Source labels **Wild Life (Protection) Act Schedule I**; retain statutory protection but check the applicable amended schedule/taxon before using that schedule number as an examination fact.
