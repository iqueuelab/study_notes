# Lion-tailed macaque: rainforest habitat and coexistence

## Species and habitat
- **Macaca silenus**, endemic to the Western Ghats of **Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu**; **Endangered**, CITES Appendix I and Wildlife (Protection) Act Schedule I. Appendix I imposes strict trade restrictions, rather than literally banning every possible international movement.
- Black fur, silver-grey facial mane and black tufted tail; also called wanderoo. A relatively small macaque, not safely described as the world's smallest macaque. **Diurnal and largely arboreal**, living in social troops, not simply solitary; fruit/seed feeding contributes to forest seed dispersal. Leaves, buds, insects and small vertebrates supplement the diet.
- Tropical moist evergreen forests and canopy connectivity are vital. **Valparai plateau, Anamalai Hills**, is a plantation–rainforest-fragment landscape; do not equate the entire plateau with one protected-area boundary.
## Fragmentation and conservation
- Plantation conversion, roads, settlement and tourism fragment habitat → canopy gaps → ground crossings/roadkill, human-food dependence and genetic isolation. Avoid feeding, secure waste, retain native roadside vegetation, reconnect fragments and control traffic at crossing points.
- NCF documents **seven canopy bridges in two Valparai forest fragments** and monitoring of their use; canopy retention and speed management complement bridges. This is a project-specific figure, not a total for all of Tamil Nadu. [NCF field programme](https://www.ncf-india.org/western-ghats/towards-wildlife-friendly-roads).
- Issue reports a **₹50 crore Tamil Nadu endangered-species fund** and a proposed population study. Its **~2,500 mature individuals** is an undated estimate, not a new 2025 census; its **99% rainforest loss** lacks a geographic denominator and should not be read as all Western Ghats forest lost.
## PYQ recall
- **2013 habitat question: Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka only (a)**. The **2023 nocturnality** question includes Malabar civet and sambar; lion-tailed macaque is diurnal → **only two (b)**. Publisher's two-statement practice: both correct.
