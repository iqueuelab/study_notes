# Kancha Gachibowli: urban commons and land monetisation

## April 2025 dispute
- **Kancha Gachibowli, Hyderabad, Telangana:** proposed monetisation of approximately **400 acres** of green land within/adjacent to the University of Hyderabad campus triggered student protests and litigation. State revenue needs, academic land claims and ecological protection intersect.
- UoH was founded in **1974**, following the **Six-Point Formula of 1973**; the formula itself is not dated 1974. [University background](https://uohyd.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/International-Conference_Brochure.pdf).
- The source traces earlier allocation of about 2,324 acres, later diversions to public institutions, and a **2004 IMG sports-project deal involving 534 acres**, followed by cancellation and litigation. Allocation, possession, registered title and environmental permission are separate legal issues; the State's ownership claim does not resolve every ecological or university claim.
## Why an urban green commons matters
- Vegetation, rocks and water bodies provide habitat continuity, groundwater recharge, flood buffering, heat moderation and research/recreation space. Land can have substantial ecological value even without being a formally notified sanctuary.
- The source reports **233 bird species and 72 tree species from campus-level biodiversity work**; these counts cannot automatically be assigned to the disputed 400 acres. Its claim of 27 Schedule-I birds requires matching the survey and applicable amended schedules.
- **Murricia hyderabadensis**, a spider, is a recognised species. The claim that it occurs only at Kancha Gachibowli is unsupported: published records exist elsewhere. [World Spider Catalog](https://www.wsc.nmbe.ch/spec-data/19675); [research record](https://www.biodiversityjournal.com/images/pubblicazioni/biodiversity-journal-2025/biodiversity-journal-2025-16-03/biodiversity-journal-2025-16-03_541-551.pdf).
## Court intervention and legal principles
- **3 April 2025:** Supreme Court initiated suo motu proceedings, ordered an urgent site inspection/report and directed that **no tree felling be permitted until further orders**. It expressly allowed parallel High Court proceedings to continue. This interim order was not a final determination of ownership or auction legality. [Supreme Court order](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/17597/17597_2025_2_810_60673_Order_03-Apr-2025.pdf).
- **Article 21**, environmental duties under **Articles 48A and 51A(g)**, public trust, precaution and intergenerational equity provide the analytical framework; their application depends on facts and applicable law.
## Mains framework
- Fiscal stress → land-sale incentive → irreversible habitat loss → public litigation → accountable alternatives.
- Require transparent land records, independent ecological assessment, community/university consultation and lawful clearances. Evaluate alternative revenue sources and brownfield development before sacrificing connected green space.
- Ethics: stewardship of public assets, present welfare versus future generations, procedural fairness and the difference between market price and ecological/social value. Auction receipts are one-time income; recurring fiscal needs require durable financing.
