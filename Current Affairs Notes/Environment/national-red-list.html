National Red List Assessment of Indian flora and fauna

Editable notes

National assessment roadmap, 2025–2030

  • MoEFCC launched the roadmap at IUCN's Abu Dhabi Congress on 9 October: ZSI and BSI, working with IUCN-India and Centre for Species Survival India, will coordinate science-based threat assessment and aim to publish National Red Data Books by 2030. PIB announcement.
  • The issue reports assessment of approximately 11,000 species, ₹95-crore public funding and training 300 assessors as programme plans, not completed outputs. National/regional extinction risk can differ from global status; statutory Wildlife Act schedules are legal-protection lists, not identical to Red List categories.
  • Categories: EX extinct; EW extinct in wild; CR critically endangered; EN endangered; VU vulnerable; NT near threatened; LC least concern; DD data deficient; NE not evaluated. Threatened = CR+EN+VU. DD does not imply safe, and endemic does not automatically imply threatened. Red Lists can include endemic species; the PYQ rejects an inventory defined solely by endemism/hotspot residence, not inclusion of every endemic species.
  • Conservation evidence guides species/site priorities, impact assessments, planning and monitoring. National assessment complements CBD/Kunming–Montreal commitments; the 30-by-30 area target is distinct from assessing 30% of species. India overlaps Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats–Sri Lanka and Sundaland (Nicobar) hotspots; historic 47,000 plant/100,000 animal counts in the source are dated approximations.
  • IUCN, founded 1948 and based in Gland, is a membership union of governments/civil society, not simply a UN agency. ZSI dates to 1916 and BSI to 1890. 2011 Red Data Books PYQ: statement 2 only.
