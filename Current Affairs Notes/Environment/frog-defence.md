# Indian frogs: anti-predator behaviour

## October 2025: two defensive displays
- Delhi University's Systematics Lab, led by S. D. Biju, reports defensive behaviours in **Xenophrys apatani (Apatani horned toad)** from Arunachal Pradesh and **Clinotarsus curtipes (bicoloured frog)** from Kerala's Western Ghats, discussed in Herpetological Notes.
- The nocturnal horned toad uses leaf-litter camouflage and, when threatened, body inflation, distress calls and biting. The diurnal bicoloured frog raises/arches its body by extending its limbs, increasing its apparent size. These are anti-predator responses, not evidence of venom or aggression during ordinary activity.
- Significance: behavioural natural history remains poorly documented even where species diversity is catalogued. The article describes first records for these Indian observations; do not infer that body inflation, biting or body raising are globally new frog behaviours. Its 419 Indian/7,876 global frog counts are dated article figures, not a current taxonomic census. Habitat protection and field observation complement species discovery.
