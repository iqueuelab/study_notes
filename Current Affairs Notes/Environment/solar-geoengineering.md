# Solar geoengineering and stratospheric aerosol injection

## Mechanism and research status
- **SAI** proposes injecting reflective particles/precursors into the stratosphere to reduce incoming solar energy; **solar radiation modification (SRM)** differs from CO₂ removal. Stratosphere roughly **10–50 km**, but actual injection altitude depends on latitude/tropopause and design.
- Proposed sulphate aerosols scatter sunlight and persist longer than lower-atmosphere particles, with limited rain removal aloft. **Mount Pinatubo, Philippines,1991** illustrates temporary volcanic aerosol cooling (~0.5°C globally at peak), not proof a controlled deployment is harmless.
- Sulphates, calcium carbonate and diamond particles are research proposals; cost, particle microphysics, chemistry and dispersal shape feasibility. **No automatic rule that SAI works better at the poles**, nor proof nanodiamonds are harmless. [IPCC assessment](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-4/).
## Risks and alternatives
- Ozone chemistry, stratospheric heating, altered rainfall/monsoons, unequal regional effects and eventual deposition require assessment. Abrupt cessation after substantial sustained cooling can cause **termination shock**; warming gases/ocean acidification continue if emissions remain.
- **Marine cloud brightening** uses sea-salt particles to increase low-cloud reflectivity; “less risky” is not a settled universal comparison. **Space sunshades** near L1 are speculative costly engineering. **Cirrus thinning** aims to let **outgoing longwave heat escape**, not principally reflect sunlight; lumping it into incoming-solar reflection obscures the mechanism.
- These are debated climate-intervention ideas, not an established substitute for mitigation/adaptation. Governance requires transparent research, international cooperation, distributive justice and long-term responsibility.
- **2019 PYQ: reducing global warming (d)**. This does not establish no possible effect on cyclone/precipitation patterns; it identifies the proposed principal purpose.
