# Desert locusts: swarms and food-security risk

## Biology and swarm formation
- **Schistocerca gregaria**, a grasshopper-family insect, inhabits arid/semi-arid Africa, the Middle East and southwest/south Asia. Eggs need **moist sandy soil**, not simply any bare desert ground; rainfall creates temporary vegetation for breeding/feeding.
- **Rain/vegetation → breeding → crowding → behavioural/colour change → hopper bands/adult swarms**. Solitarious and gregarious forms are phases of one species, not separate species. Gregarious immature adults are pink; mature adults yellow. “Solitary harmless” means lower agricultural threat, not inability to feed on crops.
- Lifecycle varies with conditions: eggs hatch in about **2 weeks**, hoppers become winged adults around **6 weeks**, followed by maturation. Avoid treating inset's shorter timings as fixed for every climate.
## Scale and response
- Swarms commonly contain **40–80 million adults/km²** and move with winds up to about **150 km/day**. **1 km² with 40 million** can eat roughly the daily food of **35,000 people**; source omits the area/population qualifier. Each adult eats roughly its weight (~2 g) daily.
- Favourable conditions permit **20-fold increase each ~3-month generation**; 400-fold after six months. Repeated rains/cyclones can support successive generations. Crop and pasture losses threaten farming/pastoral livelihoods and food security; locusts do not attack humans or carry known human disease.
- The source reviews the **2019–2022 regional crisis**, with major **2020–2021 upsurge** across East Africa/Red Sea/southwest Asia including India/Pakistan. It is background, not a newly verified April 2025 Indian invasion.
- **FAO Desert Locust Information Service:** monitoring, forecasting and early warning; Indian Locust Warning Organisation conducts surveys/control. Early detection of breeding/hopper bands, cross-border coordination and targeted approved chemical/biological control are more manageable than late response to huge swarms; assess environmental and occupational safeguards. Embedded Delhi/Haryana movement map is historical context, not a current route. [FAO facts and surveillance](https://www.fao.org/locusts/en), [FAO FAQ](https://www.fao.org/locusts/faqs/en/).
