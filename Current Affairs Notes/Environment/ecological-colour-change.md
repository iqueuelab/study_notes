# Climate, pollution and ecological colour changes

## February 2026: colour as an ecological signal
- Pigments/structural colours support **camouflage, mate choice, UV protection and heat balance**. Climate, habitat disturbance and pollutants can alter individual pigmentation or favour different species/morphs; effects vary by organism and setting.
- **Bogert's rule/thermal melanism:** darker ectotherms can warm faster in cool conditions; warmer environments may favour lighter coloration. **Gloger's rule:** pigmentation often increases with warm/humid conditions in endotherms. These are tendencies with exceptions, not contradictory universal predictions that every animal becomes lighter or darker.
- Examples: industrial melanism in peppered moths; changing brown/grey **tawny owl** morph frequencies with milder winters; dragonfly/ladybird pigmentation trade-offs between heat tolerance and mating signals. Distinguish natural selection across generations from an individual instantly changing colour.
- **2023 ocean-colour study:** significant change over **56% of global sea surface during 2002–22**, much tending greener; this signals altered marine ecosystems. It does **not** prove all such areas have harmful algal blooms, increased total biomass or oxygen depletion. [NASA analysis](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/climate-change-lends-new-color-to-the-ocean-151894/).
- Source examples: disturbed Amazon forests lose colourful butterfly diversity, with recovery in regenerating habitats; drought/heat cause premature leaf browning; urban pollution can interact with melanin/metal binding and plant pigment/UV signals, affecting courtship and pollination. Do not universalise these examples to all urban organisms.
- Coral heat stress disrupts symbiosis → **bleaching**, increasing starvation/disease risk; bleaching is not automatically immediate death. Indian reef regions include **Gulf of Mannar/Palk Bay, Kachchh, Lakshadweep and Andaman–Nicobar**. Protect water quality, shaded/moist microhabitats and habitat connectivity; reduce heat/pollution drivers and monitor colour changes alongside biological measurements.
