# Orcas: dolphin biology and prey sharing with humans

## Observations of prey offering
- Source summarises **34 cases from 2004–2024** across five ocean regions, published in the **Journal of Comparative Psychology**: orcas approached humans and released food/prey, **waiting for a response in 33 cases**. Items included fish, invertebrates, reptiles, birds, mammals and seaweed. Selected observational reports cannot establish how frequent this is across all wild orcas.
- Plausible explanations include exploration/social learning, play and food sharing; intention, altruism, deception or “scientific thinking” are hypotheses rather than experimentally demonstrated motives. Some retrieved or reoffered items; an unusual approach is not evidence wild encounters are safe.
- **Orcinus orca**, family **Delphinidae**, largest dolphin; an apex marine predator distributed from polar to tropical oceans and coastal/open waters. Dolphins are **toothed whales/cetaceans**, so the PDF's “not whales” phrasing is taxonomically misleading: orcas are dolphins and also toothed whales, not the largest cetaceans.
- Social groups often have matrilineal relationships, learned hunting/communication traditions and strong reliance on sound/echolocation. Threats include prey decline, pollutants such as PCBs, underwater noise and disturbance; source lists global **IUCN Data Deficient**, which does not mean no population is threatened.
- Practice **(b), statement 2 only**; statement 1 wrongly makes the orca the largest cetacean. River-dolphin PYQ on the same page concerns a different species.
