# Enhanced rock weathering and carbon removal

## Mechanism, benefits and limits
- **ERW:** fine crushed silicate rock, e.g. **basalt**, on farmland accelerates weathering. CO₂+water forms carbonic acid, reacting with minerals; bicarbonate can be stored in water/transported to oceans. Grinding raises surface area; not all carbon immediately becomes limestone.
- Possible soil benefits: lower acidity, nutrients and crop growth. Rates vary with rock, grain size, rainfall/temperature, soil acidity and management; source examples are Brazilian sugar farms and Indian tea plantations.
- Source US experiment: **50 tonnes basalt/ha/year;10.5 tonnes CO₂/ha over four years**; other field tests lower. Rock mass differs from CO₂ mass; one trial is not a universal rate.
- **Measurement:** cations may arise from non-carbonic-acid reactions, so ion release alone cannot prove net atmospheric removal. Neutralising acidity can also avoid downstream CO₂ release; avoided emissions differ from removal.
- Assess grinding/transport emissions, heavy metals, inhalable dust, soil/water impacts, durability and additionality before carbon credits. Credit purchases do not prove measured removal.
- **2023 PYQ:** farmland basalt, ocean alkalinity with lime, and carbonated-water storage in abandoned mines are all discussed capture/sequestration approaches; project safety/effectiveness still need assessment.
