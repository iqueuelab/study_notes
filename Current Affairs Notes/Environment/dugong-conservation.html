Environment

Dugongs and seagrass conservation

Editable notes

Species, habitat and status

  • Dugong dugon, sea cow: herbivorous marine mammal, related to manatees; depends on shallow sunlit coastal seagrass, unlike manatees that also inhabit freshwater. Long-lived (recorded 73 years), slow reproduction makes small populations vulnerable. IUCN Vulnerable; Wildlife (Protection) Act Schedule I; regional Indian threat descriptions differ from global Red List category. CMS species facts.
  • Indian distribution: Gulf of Mannar/Palk Bay, Tamil Nadu; Gulf of Kachchh, Gujarat; Andaman and Nicobar—not the entire coastline. Inset estimates India~250 and Australia~85,500/Arabian Gulf~7,300 without matching survey dates; avoid treating these as current exact counts.
  • Seagrasses (Cymodocea, Halophila, Halodule, Thalassia) are flowering plants, not seaweeds. Stabilise sediments, shelter fish/turtles and store blue carbon. Source's dugong intake 20–30 tonnes/day is an obvious unit error; retain feeding dependence without this figure. “Low nutrients” alone is not a complete dietary-selection explanation.
  • Grazing can affect meadow renewal/carbon dynamics, but “farmers of the sea” is a metaphor, not proof every grazing intensity increases carbon sequestration. Source 516.59 km² national seagrass/434.9 tonnes CO₂/km²/year needs survey/method qualification; distinguish carbon stock from annual uptake.

Protection and community response

  • India's first Dugong Conservation Reserve, notified 21 September 2022, ~448 km² Palk Bay off Thanjavur/Pudukkottai. Source's 448.3 and 122.5 km² healthy-seagrass figures are more precise reported estimates, not the reserve's universal habitat coverage. TN Forest Department, WII/OMCAR conservation work supports mapping, monitoring and fishers' participation. Tamil Nadu notification account.
  • India CMS party since 1983; signed Dugong MoU 28 May 2008. MoU originated 2007; distinguish India's signature date from instrument creation. CMS signatories.
  • Threats: dredging/ports/reclamation and turbid polluted runoff destroy meadows; gillnets/trawling cause bycatch/drowning; hunting, boat strikes, contaminants and climate-related disturbance compound risk. Persistent mercury/organochlorine exposure is a source concern, not proof every Indian dugong is contaminated.
  • Recommended: map/restore seagrass, regulate damaging fishing/boating/development, support alternative livelihoods, train fishers in sightings/strandings, community education/ecotourism safeguards, research/population/genetic monitoring and appropriate tracking/drone/citizen-science tools. Source's blanket gillnet/trawl ban is a recommendation, not proof a nationwide ban enacted.
  • PYQ 2015 answer (c) 1 and 3: herbivorous marine animal + Schedule I protection; entire-coast statement false.

October conservation commentary

  • Article highlights fishers releasing by-caught dugongs, WII/OMCAR monitoring and seagrass mapping as an integrated conservation approach; funding, trawling, dredging/ports, pollution, climate stress and India–Sri Lanka coordination remain challenges.
  • Source 200+ dugongs in Palk Bay and claims of recovery/IUCN recognition are article reports, not a comparable long-term census proving recovery or a new IUCN status change. ~12,000 ha seagrass is habitat coverage within the ~448 km² reserve, not the reserve's total area. Existing notes retain correct Palk Bay location despite an inset saying Gulf of Mannar.
  • Repeated 20–30 tonnes/day intake error is rejected; the image itself says 20–40 kg/day, illustrating the unit confusion. Existing species/status/PYQ facts remain unchanged.
