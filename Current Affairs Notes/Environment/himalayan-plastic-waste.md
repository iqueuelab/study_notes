# Himalayan plastic waste and packaging accountability

## Himalayan Cleanup: 2024 audit reported in May 2025
- **The Himalayan Cleanup (THC)**, active since **2018**, led by **Zero Waste Himalaya (Gangtok)** and **Integrated Mountain Initiative (Dehradun)** with local partners; waste/brand audits help identify upstream packaging sources.
- Audit: **121,739 collected items; 106,857 plastic (~87.8%)**; food/beverage packaging **84.2%** of audited plastics. The audit is a sampled collection, **not a census of all Himalayan waste**. [IMI original newsletter/report account](https://www.mountaininitiative.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/IMI-NEWSLETTER-OCT-2024.pdf).
- Source reports around **70% of plastics non-recyclable**, and **71% of food packaging**; multilayer packaging/cartons often have poor local recovery economics. “Not locally recyclable/no resale value” does not imply every composite carton is technically impossible to recycle anywhere.
- Collection counts: **Sikkim 53,814 (~44% of all items); Darjeeling 36,180**; Ladakh, Nagaland and Uttarakhand also participated. Higher collected counts may reflect coverage/effort as well as pollution; they do not prove a standardised region-wide ranking.
- Risks: litter near rivers, schools, tourism sites and protected areas threatens water, landscapes and biodiversity. Mountain transport/remoteness constrain viable collection/recycling.
## Beyond cleanup
- Reduce unnecessary single-use packaging; reusable/refill systems and appropriate redesign; segregated collection and material tracking; enforce producer responsibility and brand accountability.
- Tourism/local-government coordination and community participation support prevention, but responsibility cannot rest solely on consumers. Assess alternatives across their full lifecycle; biodegradable labels are not permission to litter.
- Graphic persistence estimates are approximate environmental illustrations, not universal decomposition deadlines; fragmentation into microplastics is not complete biodegradation.
