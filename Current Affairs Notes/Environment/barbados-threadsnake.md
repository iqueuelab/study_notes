# Barbados threadsnake and endemic habitat conservation

## Rediscovery corrected to 2025
- **Tetracheilostoma carlae**, endemic Barbados threadsnake, smallest known snake (about **9–10 cm** adult). Re:wild and the Barbados environment ministry rediscovered it in **March 2025**, announced **23 July 2025**, after nearly 20 years without scientifically verified sighting. The PDF's 2024 dates are wrong. [Re:wild announcement](https://www.rewild.org/press/barbados-threadsnake-rediscovery).
- Cryptic burrower in soil/leaf litter, feeds on **ants/termites**; reduced vision, pale dorsal stripes and lateral eyes help distinguish it from introduced **Brahminy/flowerpot blind snake**. Females lay **one slender egg**; rediscovery is not proof of population recovery or removal of extinction risk.
- Described as a distinct species by **S.Blair Hedges in 2008**, named for Carla. Microscopic identification and continued field surveys matter; conserve remaining forest/gullies, control habitat degradation and invasive threats.
