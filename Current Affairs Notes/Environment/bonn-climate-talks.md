# Bonn climate talks 2025 and the road to COP30

## SB62: June 16–26, 2025
- UNFCCC subsidiary bodies met in **Bonn, Germany**, preparing technical negotiations for **COP30, Belém, Brazil**. The July 8-labelled material highlights an agenda impasse of nearly two days over developed-country finance duties under **Paris Article 9.1** and unilateral trade measures.
- **Finance:** developing countries sought predictable grants rather than debt-creating loans, accountability for contributors and the **Baku-to-Belém roadmap towards US$1.3 trillion annually**. A roadmap/goal is not money already disbursed.
- **Adaptation:** refine indicators for the Global Goal on Adaptation; measuring finance, technology and capacity-building support remained contested. Technical progress did not settle financing obligations.
- **Mitigation Work Programme:** cooperation and implementation support versus fears of externally imposed national targets; a proposed digital platform raised duplication concerns. Article 6 cooperation and transparency also advanced technically.
- **Loss and damage:** the Santiago Network concerns technical assistance; arrangements for assistance do not by themselves establish adequate funding or compensate every loss.
- **Just transition:** protect workers, vulnerable communities and Indigenous knowledge while shifting to clean energy; developing countries questioned carbon-border trade barriers. Gender negotiations included unpaid care, reproductive health, violence, diversity and intersectionality.
- India uses **LMDC** and **BASIC (Brazil, South Africa, India, China)** to press equity, finance and technology access. [UNFCCC SB62](https://unfccc.int/sb62).

## September: COP30 implementation emphasis
- COP30 President **André Corrêa do Lago**, speaking in India, emphasised implementing established climate solutions alongside treaty negotiations: renewables, forests, finance, technology and carbon-market cooperation. **Belém, Brazil, November 2025** was the planned conference; location near the Amazon adds forest relevance.
- Paris NDCs and equity remain central; India's development/energy needs accompany demands for differentiated responsibility, finance and technology. Source **2.6°C by 2100** is a scenario-dependent estimate, not a guaranteed trajectory. Its “midway” description between 2015 and 2050 is arithmetically wrong.
- US withdrawal was **not yet effective at this September issue date**: notice **27 January 2025**, effective **27 January 2026**. [UN clarification](https://press.un.org/en/2025/db250128.doc.htm). The source's claim the US had already left conflates announcement and legal effect.

## COP30 opening: preserve the Paris architecture
- In **Belém, Brazil**, India urged negotiators to preserve **equity and CBDR–RC**, and strengthen implementation, adaptation and support rather than rewrite the Paris bargain. Delegate **Suman Chandra** stressed this concern; the source reports negotiating positions, not a new treaty amendment.
- **National Adaptation Plans** identify context-specific resilience needs; **NDCs** communicate climate commitments. The issue says India's NAP and 2035 NDC were not yet submitted at that early-November stage; this is a dated status, not a timeless absence. Mitigation, adaptation, finance, technology and capacity-building are complementary.
- Finance correction: COP29's goal is **at least US$300 billion per year by 2035**, with developed countries taking the lead, alongside efforts by all actors toward **US$1.3 trillion annually**. It is not US$300 billion total spread across years; the sheet's US$1.35 trillion mixes demand figures with the agreed roadmap. The earlier US$100-billion goal predates Paris and was first exceeded in 2022, two years late; adequacy, grants and adaptation allocation remain contested. [UNFCCC finance outcome](https://unfccc.int/news/cop29-un-climate-conference-agrees-to-triple-finance-to-developing-countries-protecting-lives-and).
- **BASIC** and **LMDC** pressed differentiated responsibilities and faster developed-country action, including earlier net zero and support. Negative-emission approaches require permanence, land/ecological and accounting safeguards; they do not replace deep gross-emission reductions.
- The Brazilian presidency channelled disputed agenda matters into further consultations; acceptance of process does not remove India's substantive objections. Revision balance: development space and historical responsibility alongside credible domestic decarbonisation and resilience implementation.
