Great hornbill: ecology and conservation

Editable notes

Species and habitat

  • Buceros bicornis: great/Great Indian/concave-casqued hornbill, a large canopy bird of tall evergreen and moist deciduous forests in the Western Ghats, Northeast and lower Himalayan foothills, extending into Southeast Asia. A reported Ezhimala, Kannur, Kerala sighting highlights habitat connectivity.
  • Approximate size 95–130 cm, wingspan up to 1.5 m; large yellow bill/casque, with yellow coloration influenced by preen-gland secretions. Males are generally larger. Frugivory, particularly figs, supports long-distance seed dispersal; diet also includes small animals.
  • Female seals herself in a tree cavity, leaving a slit through which the male feeds her and the young. Long nesting cycles and dependence on large old cavity trees make logging especially damaging; breeding timing varies by region.
  • IUCN Vulnerable; CITES Appendix I; Wildlife (Protection) Act Schedule I. Threats: habitat fragmentation and hunting for meat, feathers and casques. Community nest adoption, forest protection and artificial casques replacing hunting in Nyishi traditions support conservation. State bird of Kerala and Arunachal Pradesh.

Embedded nine-species chart: habitat distinctions

  • Rufous-necked, wreathed and Austen's brown hornbills: Northeast; Narcondam hornbill: endemic to Narcondam Island in the Andamans; Malabar grey: Western Ghats; Malabar pied: forested peninsular India and Sri Lanka; Oriental pied: tropical/subtropical lowland forests; Indian grey: more widespread Indian subcontinent.
  • Species have separate conservation assessments: the Narcondam hornbill was downlisted to Vulnerable in the 2020 IUCN assessment, while some older pages still say Endangered. IUCN 2020 category changes. PYQ natural-habitat choice for great hornbill is Western Ghats, not desert, salt marsh or high-altitude Kashmir.
