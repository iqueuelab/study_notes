# State of India’s Environment 2026: interconnected risks

## Report and reference periods
- **Centre for Science and Environment/Down To Earth**, **25 February 2026**, *State of India's Environment2026*. The worksheet's release year 2025 is wrong; this annual assessment is distinct from the later *In Figures* statistical edition. [CSE report](https://www.cseindia.org/state-of-india-s-environment-2026-13024).
- Source's **2025** extreme-weather compilation: events somewhere in India on **99% of days**, **4,419 deaths**, **17.41 million ha cropped area affected**. Its2024/2023 comparison is **88%/89% days, 3,393/3,208 deaths, 3.61/2.09million ha**. These are publication-era recorded impacts; 99% means days with an event somewhere, not 99% of India's area/population affected every day.
- State event-day counts reported: **Himachal Pradesh 267, Kerala 173, Madhya Pradesh 162**. Detection/reporting and differing hazards limit simple rankings; aggregate losses alone do not attribute every event solely to anthropogenic warming.
## Resilience, wildlife and monitoring
- Flood governance should move from relief alone to prevention: restore **wetlands/lakes**, reconnect rivers and floodplains, recharge groundwater and harvest rainwater; integrate land-use rules, warning and resilient infrastructure rather than relying on construction alone.
- Source reports **43 human deaths near tiger reserves January–June 2025 vs 44 in the same 2024 period**, including four 2025 cases involving consumption of remains. This pair is not evidence of an increase in deaths over those same six months. Attacks do not make every tiger a habitual man-eater; age/injury/prey stress and human–habitat overlap require case-specific assessment.
- The issue cites **40% habitat overlap with areas inhabited by 60 million people across 20 tiger States**. This is a source spatial estimate, not a count of people attacked. Habitat fragmentation, dispersal outside reserves, prey management and local coexistence/compensation matter; not every reserve has reached the same carrying capacity.
- Air monitoring: source estimates only **15% of people within 10 km of a station**, leaving about 85% beyond that proximity measure, with small towns/industrial/peri-urban areas underserved. **Outside 10 km is not identical to zero information or zero pollution**, since satellites/models also inform exposure; expand representative monitors and public data for equitable environmental governance.
