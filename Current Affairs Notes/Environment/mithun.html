Mithun: Northeast livestock and cultural heritage

Editable notes

August 2025: livestock inclusion demand

  • Northeast scientists and tribal farmers sought Mithun's inclusion in national livestock support schemes, including NLM, for breeding, veterinary care and livelihoods; a demand is not proof inclusion has been notified.
  • Mithun/Gayal/Drung ox, Bos frontalis, semi-domesticated bovine associated with the gaur; hill forests of Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur and Mizoram, with related populations in neighbouring countries. Arunachal has the largest Indian population; state animal of Arunachal Pradesh and Nagaland. Adi Solung and ceremonial exchanges connect cattle with social/cultural life.
  • Embedded graphic: FSSAI food-animal recognition in 2023, ICAR M-ANITRA market-linkage app; traditional free-ranging herds also need habitat, breeding and health management. The source's 2019 population of about four lakh is a dated census estimate.
  • Correction: do not transfer wild gaur's conservation labels to domestic Mithun. CITES Appendix I entry for Bos gaurus explicitly excludes the domesticated Bos frontalis. The PDF's unqualified 'Mithun: IUCN Vulnerable/CITES I' conflates taxa; no separate wild-species IUCN category is established here for the domestic form. CITES appendices, ICAR Mithun research.
