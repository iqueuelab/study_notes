Pangolins and Indo-Burmese taxonomy

Editable notes

Species proposal: January 2025

  • ZSI-associated research described Manis indoburmanica, Indo-Burmese pangolin, as distinct from Chinese pangolin (M. pentadactyla) and Indian pangolin (M. crassicaudata).
  • Source locations: Arunachal Pradesh/Assam, possible Nepal, Bhutan and Myanmar extension; 180–1,830 m; dark/olive-brown scales, pinkish face. A predicted range is not confirmed presence everywhere.
  • Source reports 8% genetic difference and 3.4-million-year divergence; metrics/methods not supplied, so don't treat these as definitive universal figures.

Later taxonomic clarification

  • 1 July 2026 genomic/morphological study revalidated Manis aurita Hodgson, 1836, treating M. indoburmanica as a junior synonym under naming priority; estimated separation from M. pentadactyla about 1.8 million years.
  • Keep 2025 proposal separate from 2026 revision: lineage evidence, species delimitation and the oldest valid scientific name are related but different questions. Primary research, Mammal Diversity Database.

General recall and conservation

  • Order Pholidota, family Manidae; Africa/Asia; unique mammalian keratin-scale armour. Curl into a ball; long sticky tongue; myrmecophagous, ants/termites.
  • Many dig burrows; fossorial ≠ arboreal, and behaviour varies across pangolin species.
  • Source's traditional 8 species: 4 African + 4 Asian count predates/does not resolve newer taxonomic revisions; avoid adding the new name to eight without checking whether it replaces an older taxon.
  • Severe trafficking for scales/meat and habitat loss; scale demand in traditional medicine is not evidence of medicinal effectiveness.
  • Graphic's traditional Asian species: Indian, Chinese, Sunda and Philippine; African: giant, Temminck's ground, white-bellied and black-bellied.

Trafficking disruption: April–May 2025

  • Wildlife Justice Commission, Disruption and Disarray (2015–24): recorded major disruption of large-scale pangolin-scale/ivory trafficking after 2020, reflecting pandemic restrictions, enforcement and changing criminal methods. Reduced seizures/visible shipments do not establish an equivalent population recovery or elimination of demand; smuggling routes may adapt. WJC report.
  • Habitat extends across forest, grassland and agricultural mosaics, varying by species. Ant/termite consumption contributes to pest regulation; burrowing contributes to soil turnover/aeration. Habitat loss and hunting near settlements remain threats.
  • Indian pangolin: IUCN Endangered; Chinese pangolin: Critically Endangered; both receive Schedule I protection under India's Wildlife (Protection) Act. Indian pangolin range includes India, Nepal, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh; occurrence is not uniform across every habitat.
  • Traditionally recognised eight pangolins were moved to CITES Appendix I, effective 2017, prohibiting international commercial trade in wild-taken specimens; exceptional permitted transactions and domestic protection are separate legal questions. Preserve the existing notes' newer taxonomy qualifications. CITES.
  • Priorities: intelligence-led enforcement and financial investigations, border cooperation, scale identification, habitat/community protection and demand reduction. Traditional medicinal use of scales is not evidence of efficacy.
