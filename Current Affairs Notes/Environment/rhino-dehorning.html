Rhino dehorning: evidence, incentives and conservation trade-offs

Editable notes

December issue: evidence from Greater Kruger

  • 2025 Science study, 11 reserves, Greater Kruger, South Africa, 2017–23: 1,985 rhinos poached despite about US$74 million protection spending. Dehorning associated with 78% lower poaching while consuming 1.2% of the protection budget; intervention varied among reserves. It reduces the reward from poaching, rather than merely increasing arrest risk. Co-leading University of Cape Town.
  • Source differentiates overall ~75%, abrupt ~78% decline and ~95% relative reduction in individual risk; these are different measures, not additive effects or a guarantee of zero poaching. The 78% figure is the principal study outcome; lower observed enforcement effect does not establish enforcement is useless.
  • Rhino horn is keratin, without demonstrated medicinal efficacy. Veterinary trimming retains the living base; sedation, blindfold/ear protection and careful cutting reduce injury/stress. Horn regrowth requires repeat interventions; residual stumps can still attract poachers. Do not call it universally harmless or non-invasive.
  • Source horn-trade estimate US$874 million–1.13 billion (2012–22) is attributed to Wildlife Justice Commission, not annual current revenue. Poverty/corruption and organised networks interact with demand for status/traditional medicines.
  • India/Nepal conservation relies on intelligence, patrolling, habitat protection and community cooperation; the source's “1–2 losses in three years” is not a verified all-country total. African findings alone do not require dehorning Asian rhinos or prove it never appropriate elsewhere. Consider species-specific behaviour/welfare, local risk, cost, consultation and displacement of poaching to other reserves.
