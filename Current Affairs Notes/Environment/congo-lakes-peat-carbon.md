# Congo lakes and ancient peat-carbon release

## February 2026: Mai Ndombe and Tumba
- **Mai Ndombe and Tumba**, shallow humic/blackwater lakes in **DRC's Cuvette Centrale,Congo Basin**, receive organic matter from surrounding swamp forests/peatlands. Dissolved humic material produces dark water; persistent waterlogging and oxygen-poor soils allow partially decomposed peat to accumulate.
- **Nature Geoscience study published23 February 2026** finds lake **dissolved inorganic carbon radiocarbon ages about2,170–3,515years**; peat accounts for roughly **39–40%** of that pool. This identifies ancient peat contribution to CO₂ outgassing, not that every carbon molecule is older than3,000years or that40% of all Congo emissions are from these lakes. [Primary study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-026-01924-3).
- Mechanism: peat carbon is transformed/mobilised into aquatic inorganic carbon and then outgassed. The dominant biochemical pathway remains uncertain; the study discusses methanogenesis and subsequent methane oxidation. **Observed ancient carbon does not by itself prove recent drainage, deforestation or climate-induced collapse caused this flux**. Drying/warming and land-use change are important prospective threats requiring monitoring.
- Congo tropical peatlands store roughly **30billiontonnes carbon**; that stock differs from an annual emissions flow. Hydrology, land protection and repeat measurements matter for assessing whether leakage is natural background or increasing destabilisation.
- Lakes lie within **Ngiri–Tumba–Maindombe Ramsar wetland**, among the largest designated sites. Correct the source's “world's largest” claim: **Rio Negro,Brazil**, designated2018, is larger. [Ramsar2018 announcement](https://www.ramsar.org/news/brazil-designates-some-worlds-largest-wetlands-amazon-basin-island-reefs).
