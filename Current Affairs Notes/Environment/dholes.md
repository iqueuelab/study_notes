# Dholes and cooperative carnivore conservation

## Species and May 2025 zoo context
- **Dhole/Asiatic wild dog: Cuon alpinus**, family Canidae; native to Asia, with Indian populations in suitable forest landscapes. Social packs hunt cooperatively, including medium/large ungulates; their ecological role involves prey regulation, alongside interactions with tigers/leopards and other carnivores.
- **IUCN Endangered**; major pressures include habitat fragmentation, prey depletion, persecution/conflict and infection from domestic dogs. Protect connected habitat/prey, monitor populations and manage dog-related disease interfaces.
- The clipping highlights specialised dhole enclosures at **Indira Gandhi Zoological Park, Visakhapatnam**, describing the largest Indian **zoo/captive** collection at the time. This does not mean the zoo contains India's largest wild population; numbers/rank are dated reports. Breeding/awareness complement in-situ protection rather than replace it.
- Under the revised Act schedules, dhole appears in **Schedule I**, correcting the PDF's present-tense Schedule II claim. The older Schedule II Part II system also should not be collapsed into today's two-animal-schedule structure. [Current Schedule I](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/schedulefile?aid=AC_CEN_16_18_00007_197253_1517807324579&rid=759).
- **2017 tortoise PYQ: (a), same statutory protection level as tiger**. Schedule I listing does not mean extinct in the wild, captive-only or endemic. Statutory listing and IUCN assessment are separate systems; lawful exceptions remain governed by the Act.
