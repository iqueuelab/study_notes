# Kendu leaves, minor forest produce and community rights

## Koraput community trade: May 2025
- Source reports **eight Gram Sabhas, Boipariguda block, Koraput, Odisha**, harvested **over four lakh kendu-leaf bundles** in the 2025 season and sought independent sale; State deregulation notification was **awaited**, not already issued.
- **Kendu/tendu**, “Green Gold of Odisha”: non-timber/minor forest produce mainly used to wrap **bidis**. Tobacco use has health costs; economic importance does not imply safe consumption or verified medicinal efficacy.
- Producers include **Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Jharkhand, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra and Gujarat**. PDF calls Odisha third-largest; retain as source ranking without an identified year/volume series.
- Odisha's kendu trade has State monopoly/control arrangements. Do not generalise every State's kendu rules, or bamboo/sal-seed regulation, into one uniform national regime.

## FRA legal distinction
- **Forest Rights Act, 2006, section 3(1)(c)** recognises eligible forest dwellers' ownership, access to collect, use and dispose of traditionally collected **minor forest produce**, including kendu leaves; recognition/implementation of rights matters.
- **2012 changes amended the FRA Rules**, not the parent Act. Disposal includes processing, storage and transport, individually/collectively, for livelihood. Transit permits may be issued by the Gram Sabha's **Rule 4(1)(e) committee** or **a person authorised by the Gram Sabha**; CFRMC is the community-management mechanism, not the only possible permit issuer.
- Rules/guidance require State arrangements to align with recognised FRA rights and prevent transit formalities from restricting disposal. The source's blanket “FRA overrides every State law” is too broad; examine the specific right and conflicting rule, not presume all State conservation/regulatory powers vanish. [Tribal Affairs rules/FAQ](https://tribal.nic.in/downloads/FRA/FAQ/FAQ%20ENGLISH_Approved_compressed.pdf).
- Implementation: rights documentation, workable community permits, storage/value addition, price information, fair buyer access and timely payment; conserve regeneration and prevent fire/destructive harvesting. Removing monopoly alone does not guarantee bargaining power against intermediaries.
- Mains: livelihood autonomy and tribal governance → legal/administrative barriers → community institutions plus market support → sustainable harvesting and accountable benefit sharing.
