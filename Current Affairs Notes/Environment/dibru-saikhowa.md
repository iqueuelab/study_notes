# Dibru-Saikhowa: grassland change and feral horses

## Floodplain setting and area distinction
- Eastern **Assam, Tinsukia/Dibrugarh**, riverine habitat bounded broadly by **Brahmaputra north and Dibru south**; grasslands, semi-evergreen/moist deciduous forest, bamboo and wetland mosaics. National-park status **1999**; biosphere reserve **1997**.
- PDF/tourism sources give **425 km²** for the park, but the Forest Department and district biosphere description give **340 km² core/park +425 km² buffer =765 km² biosphere reserve**. Keep these administrative scopes distinct; do not use 425 km² as an unquestioned park statistic. [Assam Forest Department](https://forest.assam.gov.in/portlets/national-park), [district explanation](https://tinsukia.assam.gov.in/tourist-place-detail/274).
- Known for **feral horses**, free-living descendants of domestic stock; source links them to WWII military horses and estimates **~200**. These are reported origin/count claims, not a current census or a separate wild-horse species. “Only habitat in India” is too absolute without a defined population scope.
## Grasslands in flux: study reported in August
- Remote-sensing/GIS LULC study covers **1999/2000–2024**; PDF gives grassland starting share **28.78% (2000)** but omits its ending share. Do not invent a final percentage; source reports decline and conversion to shrubland. **Degraded forest 80.52 km² by 2024** is the study's reported land-cover estimate, not necessarily the entire park's degraded area under every classification.
- Native woody encroachers **Bombax ceiba (simalu), Lagerstroemia speciosa (ajar)** can reduce open-grassland habitat. Alien invaders named: **Chromolaena odorata, Ageratum conyzoides, Parthenium hysterophorus, Mikania micrantha**. Native encroachment and alien invasion differ even when both alter grasslands.
- Flood dynamics, invasive plants, settlement/resource pressure and vegetation succession interact. Protect habitat for **Bengal florican, hog deer, swamp grass babbler** and other grassland-dependent fauna; not all species named are endemic to the park.
- Restoration needs habitat-specific invasive control, floodplain monitoring, community participation and grassland conservation alongside forest protection. More trees are not automatically better for every grassland ecosystem; habitat loss can increase conflict and ecological instability.
