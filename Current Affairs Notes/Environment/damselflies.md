# Damselflies and Euphaea wayanadensis

## Wayanad discovery
- **Euphaea wayanadensis**, family **Euphaeidae**, order **Odonata**, suborder **Zygoptera**: new species described in **ENTOMON 50(1), 2025**, from the Wayanad landscape, Western Ghats, Kerala.
- Distinguished from closely similar **E. pseudodispar** by hindwing black patch, thoracic stripes and male terminal/genital structures. The study includes **morphology and mitochondrial COI molecular analysis**, with 12.9% nucleotide divergence and phylogenetic evidence—not morphology alone. [Research paper](https://www.entomon.ambadi.org.in/index.php/Entomon/article/download/1416/716).
- The daily compilation reports Kerala's **191st** and Western Ghats' **223rd** recorded odonate; these are dated checklist counts, not permanent totals.
- Rocky forest-stream habitats link terrestrial forest protection with freshwater conservation. Restricted known records are not proof of a formal threatened-category assessment.
## Damselfly versus dragonfly
| Feature | Damselflies | Dragonflies |
|---|---|---|
| Suborder | Zygoptera | Anisoptera |
| Typical build | Slender | More robust |
| Wings | Fore/hind pairs generally similar | Hindwings usually broader at base |
| Eyes | Widely separated | Usually meet/close; exceptions occur |
| Resting wings | Usually folded together; exceptions | Usually held open |
- Aquatic immature stages → winged terrestrial adults; both are predators and part of food webs. Habitat/species assemblages can indicate freshwater condition, but one sighting does not certify pollution-free water.
- Threats: stream alteration, riparian clearing, water pollution and climatic stress. Protect streams and vegetation, monitor water and conduct targeted taxonomic surveys; not all damselflies are restricted to stagnant or slow water.
