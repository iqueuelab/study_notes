# Meghalaya uranium mining: consultation and tribal rights

## Uranium mining, consultation and Khasi concerns
- **Domiasiat and Wahkaji, Meghalaya**: longstanding Khasi opposition links radiation/tailings risks, land, water, health, displacement and livelihoods. These are concerns requiring project-specific evidence and safeguards, not proof every mine has caused identical damage.
- **MoEFCC OM of 8 September 2025** exempted relevant atomic/critical/strategic mineral projects from EIA public consultation, citing existing defence/security/strategic provisions. A later parliamentary answer confirms the date/scope and says EIA, environmental management plans, expert appraisal and post-clearance monitoring remain. Exemption from consultation is not exemption from every clearance or pollution obligation. [Official confirmation](https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/186/AU29_vKghtd.pdf?source=pqals).
- Editorial criticism: an executive measure reduces affected communities' voice and may weaken trust; it advocates withdrawal, dialogue and constitutional safeguards. The government's legality rationale and criticism are competing positions, not a judicial finding invalidating this OM.
- **Khasi Hills Autonomous District Council /Sixth Schedule** and applicable land/forest rights are separate safeguards. **2013 Niyamgiri** concerns Gram Sabha determination of forest/religious rights in a particular legal context; it is not a blanket veto over all mining projects or proof identical PESA rules apply in Sixth Schedule Meghalaya.
- Way forward: understandable local-language information, independent health/environment baselines, transparent tailings/water management, meaningful participation, lawful rights adjudication, remedies and credible monitoring. FPIC is a rights principle; its precise domestic legal application must be established rather than assumed universal.
