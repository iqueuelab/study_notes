# Indian rosewood: conservation, timber trade and nitrogen fixation

## South Indian rosewood study
- **Dalbergia latifolia**, Indian/Malabar rosewood, **Fabaceae**, valuable durable dark timber for furniture, handicrafts and instruments; source calls it “ivory of the forests”. South Indian landscapes include **Nilgiris, Anamalai and Parambikulam**; these are examples rather than the entire native range.
- **IWST Bengaluru/ICFRE study, 2019–2025**, source densities per **0.1 hectare**: Tamil Nadu **2.85**, Kerala **5.38**, Karnataka **6.19**. Tamil Nadu is lowest among these sampled States; avoid treating the estimate as a national census or a proven long-term trend from one cross-section.
- MaxEnt habitat model highlights **Nilgiris, Coimbatore, Erode, Tiruppur, Dindigul and Theni**; source says **17.2%** of modelled suitable Indian habitat lies within protected areas. Modelled suitability differs from actual occupied forest/tree counts. Habitat loss, timber exploitation and climate shifts threaten persistence.
- The issue reports the **Tamil Nadu Rosewood Trees (Conservation) Act, 1995**, extended in **2010 for 15 years**, lapsed in **2025**. This source-reported lapse does not by itself establish that every felling, transit or export is now lawful; other applicable forest, private-forest, transit and trade rules require checking. Proposed tagging/certification can separate legal plantation timber from illegal harvest.
## Species and trade distinctions
- Source conservation recall: **D. latifolia Vulnerable; D. sissoo/Shisham Least Concern**. “African rosewood” is an ambiguous common name, not a single reliable taxon/status; do not apply one status to every Dalbergia.
- CITES regulates international trade, rather than universally banning possession: **Appendix I** exceptional trade; **II** controlled trade to avoid overexploitation; **III** national protection with international assistance. Dalbergia trade listings include species/product annotations and exemptions; **D. nigra** is an Appendix-I exception to the broad Appendix-II genus listing.
- Correct treaty chronology: **1963 IUCN resolution** initiated the idea; **CITES adopted 3 March 1973, entered into force 1 July 1975**. [FAO–CITES legal portal](https://citeslex.fao.org/). Avoid the PDF's “adopted 1963”.
- Root-nodule microbial symbiosis in some legumes improves soil nitrogen; long-lived trees store carbon and leaf litter cycles nutrients. A useful timber tree is not automatically a demonstrated ecological **keystone** species.
## PYQ revision
- **2022 nitrogen-fixing plants: (a), alfalfa, chickpea and clover**, already in the common bank. Rhizobium in legumes, Frankia in actinorhizal trees; Azolla is a fern hosting cyanobacteria. Amaranth, purslane and spinach are not these nodulated nitrogen-fixing legumes.
- **2016 Red Sanders: (a), statement 1 only**. **Pterocarpus santalinus**, Fabaceae, associated with southern **dry deciduous** forests, especially Andhra Pradesh; it is not a tropical-rainforest tree. Rosewood/Dalbergia and red sanders/Pterocarpus are distinct genera. Source lists Red Sanders Endangered/Appendix II; illegal timber trade is the conservation link.
