Elephants: ecology, conservation and conflict

Editable notes

Species and ecological role

  • Asian elephant: Elephas maximus, found across 13 Asian range countries; India has the largest wild population. Source global 50,000–60,000/India over 60% are approximate background estimates with varying denominators, not a new 2025 global census.

  • Asian elephant: Indian Schedule I protection; keystone/ecosystem engineer through seed dispersal, vegetation modification and habitat diversity.
  • Herds are generally matriarchal; adult males often solitary/in bachelor groups. Gestation about 22 months, calving intervals often 4–5 years; reproduction can continue beyond 40. Tusks are modified incisors and do not regrow after complete loss; tusklessness occurs in some Asian males, so “only males have tusks” is not “all males are tusked”. Trunk muscle-count graphic is approximate, not a fixed anatomical census.

Counts and conservation programmes

  • 2017 India estimate: 29,964, Karnataka highest, followed in the source by Assam/Kerala; do not label these as a completed May 2025 national estimate.
  • 23–25 May 2025 embedded clipping: synchronised southern count concluded, using block/direct count, line transects and waterhole observation; rain affected final-day detection. Karnataka sites included Bandipur, Nagarahole, BRT, Bhadra, Cauvery and adjoining forests. A count exercise concluding is not publication of a final population; non-sighting at one site does not prove absence.
  • Project Elephant, 1992: habitat/corridors, population assessment, conflict mitigation and State support. Elephant reserves may encompass protected forests and other lands; designation is not identical to a national park’s statutory regime.
  • Source 32 reserves/latest Agasthyamalai is stale: official December 2023 counted 33; Terai, UP, was another addition alongside Agasthyamalai, Tamil Nadu. MoEFCC/PIB.
  • MIKE = Monitoring the Illegal Killing of Elephants, CITES programme; roots in 1997 decisions and implementation from 2001, rather than universally “launched 2003”. Source cites ten Indian sites, including Kaziranga/Nagarahole/Wayanad. CITES programme.

Conflict and PYQ recall

  • Habitat conversion and roads/rail/mining fragment corridors; crop attraction and blocked movement drive dangerous encounters, losses and retaliatory killing. Source “over 500 human deaths annually” is a dated broad burden claim, not an exact fixed annual total.
  • Protect connected habitat, retrofit crossings/rail operations, early warnings, rapid response, compensation and community participation; control ivory/body-part trade and monitor disease.
  • 2020 PYQ: (a) 1 and 2 only—female herd leadership and gestation up to 22 months; 40-year-only calving cutoff false; Karnataka, not Kerala, led the relevant population estimate.

June 2025: Kumki management and corridor correction

  • Kumki means trained captive Asian elephants, working with mahouts in professionally managed capture/rescue, conflict response, veterinary support/patrol and training. They are not a separate species or a genetically domesticated elephant lineage.
  • Source recalls 2024 Andhra Pradesh–Karnataka MoU for deployment, expert teams, mahout training, veterinary SOPs, nutrition/health and knowledge exchange; Karnataka's 62 transfers is a source-reported cumulative count, not proof all were newly deployed to AP or that conflict was solved.
  • Embedded capture illustration depicts multiple elephants assisting veterinary-controlled restraint/transport, with named example Khaleem and Madukkarai Maharaj; its 7–8-year training is illustrative, not a universal age/duration. Capture/relocation require animal welfare, trained staff, veterinary judgment and continued habitat/community measures.
  • Correct repeated population 2017: 29,964, not PDF 27,312. Corridor count: 150 across 15 range States, ground-validated in 2023 report, not source 138. MoEFCC corridor report.
  • Graphic's “four main species” conflates Asian subspecies with species: Indian/Sumatran are Asian elephant populations/subspecies; African savanna/forest are separate species. Existing topic lists conservation statuses/gestation/ecology; no new completed national census is established here.
  • Infographic recall: Gaj Sochna app 2022, Gaj Yatra 2017, Haathi Mere Saathi 2011, source National Elephant Corridor project 2005; names/years are source background, not proof statutory protection or nationwide success. MIKE timing correction already recorded.
  • IUCN, membership union headquartered Gland, Switzerland; RedList threatened categories Vulnerable, Endangered, Critically Endangered. Old pink/green page conventions are not contemporary legal protection schedules.

October 2025: DNA-based national baseline

  • SAIEE 2021–25 (Synchronous All-India Elephant Estimation) reports 22,446 wild elephants. DNA from dung identifies individuals and helps account for repeat samples; spatial sampling/habitat assessment complements genetic work. A modelled estimate is not a literal genetic identification of every elephant.
  • The new design differs from the 2017 estimate of 29,964; subtraction alone cannot establish a measured population decline. The PDF's “2024 report” label must not erase the estimation exercise's 2021–25 scope. WII elephant programme.
  • Source-reported field effort: 6.67 lakh km walked, 1.88 lakh transects and over 21,000 dung samples. These are survey-effort measures, not separate elephant totals. The Western Ghats form the largest reported population landscape; Karnataka leads the source's State figures, with Assam another major stronghold.
  • Data caution: the PDF's four landscape counts (11,934; 4,990; 2,061; 1,890) sum to 20,875, not its national 22,446; its landscape percentages likewise do not cover 100%. Do not use this table as a reconciled national breakdown without checking the full report. The source's old 101-corridor figure is superseded by the 2023 report's 150 already explained above.
  • New-baseline monitoring should track fragmentation, plantations/invasive species, fires, electrocution/rail mortality and conflict. Connect habitats across reserve boundaries, retrofit safe crossings, restore native food/water resources and combine early warnings/rapid response with fair compensation. Source Nilgiris–Coimbatore death figures lack a clear time period and are not retained as an annual rate.
