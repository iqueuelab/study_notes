Sierra Leone and Nyangai coastal displacement

Editable notes

Nyangai case study

  • Nyangai, in the Turtle Islands off southern Sierra Leone's Atlantic coast, illustrates erosion, inundation and saltwater intrusion. Reported remaining dimensions are about 200 ×100 m, after loss of over two-thirds of its land in less than a decade; the article places residents at under 300 from roughly 1,000. These are report estimates, not a new national census.
  • Loss of homes, coconut/mango trees, fields and fishing access combines with unsafe drinking water, sanitation and health-service gaps. Remoteness (reported seven-hour canoe access from Freetown) complicates relocation; residents reported inadequate practical support. Attribution must consider local erosion processes as well as sea-level rise.
  • Climate justice: communities with small emissions face severe losses; planned relocation needs livelihoods, consent, services and finance. “Climate refugee” here is descriptive, not automatic status under the 1951 Refugee Convention. The claimed June 2024 estimate of two million people at risk needs the original report and is not retained as verified.

Geography, society and economy

  • West African Atlantic state; Guinea north/east, Liberia southeast; capital Freetown with a major natural harbour. Coastal mangroves give way to inland hills/uplands. Rivers: Rokel, Taia, Sewa and Moa. Map: Lungi, Banana Islands, Sherbro Island, Turtle Islands and Kilimi National Park.
  • English is official; Krio is widely used. Presidential constitutional republic with a unicameral legislature; the source's ~7.5-million population is undated and should not be treated as a July 2025 estimate.
  • Freetown-area settlements of freed Black people: 1787 arrivals from London, 1792 from Nova Scotia, 1800 Jamaican Maroons; 1808 Crown colony concerned Freetown/peninsula rather than all modern Sierra Leone. Independence 1961; civil war ended 2002.
  • Farming includes rice, cassava, cocoa and oil palm; minerals include diamonds, bauxite, gold and rutile (TiO₂). Post-war infrastructure, education and health deficits interact with coastal/mangrove degradation.
