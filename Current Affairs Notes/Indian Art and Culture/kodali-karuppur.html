Indian Art and Culture

Kodali Karuppur sari and textile-craft revival

Editable notes

Technique, patronage and motifs

  • Named for Karuppur near Kumbakonam, Tamil Nadu; combines hand painting, block printing and brocade weaving, including gold/silver effects. Textile heritage connects Thanjavur Maratha patronage with Saurashtrian weaving communities.
  • Source associates royal patronage with Serfoji II and gives 1787–1832 as a broad patronage period; it should not be treated as his lifetime or an uninterrupted reign. Worn by Thanjavur royal women; presented as khillat/clothes of honour, entering wedding trousseaux in Maratha courts such as Baroda, Kolhapur and Satara; related turbans for men.
  • Motifs: geometric/linear patterns, vines, stars, Veldarri chevrons and thazhambu/screw-pine flower. Source's 400–500 migrant families is an undated historical estimate, not a present artisan census.

Threatened transmission and GI distinction

  • Dwindling traditional weavers threaten knowledge transmission; apprenticeships, documentation, fair artisan incomes, authentic markets and design support are revival priorities.
  • Kodali Karuppur Saree application 409 is listed withdrawn, not registered; distinguish it from separately registered Karuppur Kalamkari Paintings (424). Absence of a GI does not mean the craft has no possible legal protection of any kind. GI sari record, painting record.
  • Practice: Tamil Nadu/Thanjavur patronage is correct; a claim that this sari currently has a registered GI is unsupported by that record. Do not transfer the painting's registration to the sari.
