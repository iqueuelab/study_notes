# Booker Prize: English-language fiction and the 2025 winner

## November 2025: David Szalay's Flesh
- **David Szalay**, Hungarian–British writer, won the **2025 Booker Prize for Flesh** on **10 November 2025**. The novel follows István from Hungary into wealth in Britain, exploring bodily experience, class, migration, chance and ambition. **£50,000** winner award; shortlisted authors receive **£2,500**. [Official announcement](https://thebookerprizes.com/media-centre/press-releases/flesh-by-david-szalay-wins-the-booker-prize-2025).
- Established **1969**; administered by the **Booker Prize Foundation**. Recognises an eligible single work of fiction originally written in **English**, published in **UK/Ireland** under prize rules; any nationality eligible since **2014**. Publisher eligibility/publication-window rules matter; self-publication alone is not an eligible submission route.
- Distinguish **International Booker Prize**, established **2005**, whose annual translated-book format began **2016**: English translation published in UK/Ireland; author and translator share its main prize. Existing Heart Lamp notes explain the 2025 translation winner.
- Indian/Indian-origin winners: **V. S. Naipaul—In a Free State (1971); Salman Rushdie—Midnight's Children (1981); Arundhati Roy—The God of Small Things (1997); Kiran Desai—The Inheritance of Loss (2006); Aravind Adiga—The White Tiger (2008)**. Rushdie's later anniversary accolades are distinct from winning the annual prize again.
- Other recall: **Bernice Rubens, The Elected Member (1970)**, first woman winner; **Kazuo Ishiguro, The Remains of the Day (1989)**; **Samantha Harvey, Orbital (2024)**. Awards are edition-specific cultural recognition, not constitutional or government honours.
