# Easterine Kire and Naga literary memory

## Author and recognition
- Naga writer **Easterine Kire**, English-language novels/poetry rooted in oral traditions, memory and community experience.
- **Spirit Nights (2022)** won the **Sahitya Akademi English award for 2024**; award year and publication year differ. [Akademi newsletter](https://sahitya-akademi.gov.in/e-newsletter/Jan-March_2025_Newsletter.pdf).
- **When the River Sleeps (2014)**: **Hindu Prize 2015**. Other works: **A Naga Village Remembered (2003), A Terrible Matriarchy (2007), Mari (2010), A Respectable Woman (2018)**; later editions can bear other years. “Mari 2003” confuses separate books. [Publisher-supplied bibliography](https://books.google.com/books/about/A_Respectable_Woman.html?id=q0YEEQAAQBAJ).
## Themes and significance
- Folklore/spirit beliefs, human–nature relationships, women and social expectations, war's impact, intergenerational memory and cultural identity.
- Kohima's wartime/post-war experience appears in her fiction; literary memory illuminates lived history but does not replace documentary evidence for every event.
- Preserve oral knowledge and indigenous voices; translate/support regional writing without treating all Northeast communities as one undifferentiated culture.
- Novels can challenge stereotypes and expose social change while sustaining storytelling traditions; award recognition improves visibility beyond the region.
