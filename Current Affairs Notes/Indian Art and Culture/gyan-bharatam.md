# Gyan Bharatam and manuscript conservation

## Budget announcement and May 2025 status
- **Union Budget 2025–26:** survey, documentation and conservation covering **over one crore manuscripts** and a **National Digital Repository of Indian knowledge systems**. Intended coverage is not manuscripts already digitised or publicly available. [Budget highlights](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2025-26/doc/bh1.pdf).
- **Ministry of Culture** initiative builds on **National Mission for Manuscripts (NMM), established 2003**, with resource/conservation/partner-centre networks. Source reports annual allocation **₹3.5 crore →₹60 crore**; annual budget is distinct from later multi-year mission outlays.
- Page-one clipping **anticipated a June 9 launch** and considered an autonomous preservation body; these were plans, not proof of a completed May launch. It reports metadata for **52 lakh manuscripts**, around **three lakh digitised**, and roughly one-third of those uploaded—different stages and source snapshots, not one universal completed digital-access count.
- Scope includes museums, universities, libraries, temples/mutts and private collections; central cataloguing, conservation facilities, digitisation, transcription/research and access require institutional/custodian cooperation.

## What counts as a manuscript?
- Handwritten compositions on **paper, palm leaf, birch bark, cloth** and other media, with historical/scientific/literary/aesthetic significance. NMM's **75-year age criterion** is a programme definition, not a universal linguistic definition prohibiting newer handwritten manuscripts.
- Printed books, lithographs and photographic reproductions are not original handwritten manuscripts in this classification. **Language ≠ script**: a language can be written in different scripts; the source incorrectly lists “Maithili” generically as an ancient script (the language's historic script is **Tirhuta/Mithilakshar**).
- Themes: religion/philosophy, literature/grammar, medicine, astronomy, mathematics, history/genealogy, arts and agriculture. Source's **80 scripts /75% Sanskrit** need a dataset and reference date; do not assume a verified full inventory exists.
- **Bakhshali**, birch-bark mathematical manuscript using a zero placeholder: source's blanket **third–fourth-century** date is obsolete/disputed. Oxford's **2024 revised radiocarbon study** models production across approximately **799–1102 CE**, with uncertainty; material dating is not automatically the date a mathematical idea originated. Avoid an unqualified “world's earliest zero” claim. [Oxford primary report](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A5a6d1dd7-f20c-4209-adb6-33849f5b08f4).

## Institutions, constitutional links and implementation
- **National Library, Kolkata**, and **Asiatic Society, founded 1784 by William Jones**, contribute to textual heritage. Source's 3,600-library-manuscript count is an approximate collection snapshot.
- **Article 29(1)** protects any section of citizens with a distinct language/script/culture, not minorities alone. **Article 49** concerns protected monuments/objects of national importance; **Article 51A(f)** is the duty to preserve composite heritage. The source supplies no specific judgment establishing a universal standalone **Article 21 entitlement to every cultural asset**.
- Digitisation enables research/access but does not replace physical conservation; preserve provenance, metadata, rights/consent, backup formats, script-reading expertise and scholarly verification. Scans alone do not validate every scientific/medical claim in a historic text.
- Mains: protect fragile diverse knowledge → catalogue and conserve originals → accurate digitisation/translation → equitable research access and community stewardship. Global soft power and curricula are potential uses, not automatic outcomes.
