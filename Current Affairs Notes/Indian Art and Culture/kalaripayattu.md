# Kalaripayattu and Indian martial arts

## Kerala tradition

- **Kalari**: training ground/gymnasium; **payattu**: martial practice. Integrates physical conditioning, armed/unarmed combat and mind–body coordination; one of India's longstanding martial traditions.

| Training stage | Recall |
| --- | --- |
| **Maippayattu** | Body conditioning |
| **Kolthari** | Wooden weapons: sticks/staves |
| **Angathari** | Metal weapons |
| **Verumkai** | Bare-hand fighting; anatomical knowledge |

- Northern/Malabar styles named: **Vattenthirippu, Arappukkai, Pillathangi**.
- **Parasurama origin story is tradition**, not verified historical founding. Bodhidharma/Shaolin transmission narrative is disputed; source **fifth-century / Thanjavur once part of Kerala** claim should not be learnt as settled history.
- January controversy: role in **38th National Games, Uttarakhand**, beginning **28 January 2025**; demonstration status and competitive-medal inclusion are distinct.

## Regional martial-art map

- **Huyen Langlon/Thang-ta, Manipur**: sword/spear armed practice and **Sarit Sarak** unarmed combat.
- **Lathi Khela, Bengal**: staff; **Gatka, Punjab**: Sikh martial practice with sticks/swords, linked to **Guru Hargobind** and later Sikh tradition; source federation **2018** is an organisational date, not origin of the art.
- **Silambam, Tamil Nadu**: staff/other weapons, Tamil diaspora including Malaysia; deity/sage origin accounts are traditional.
- **Kathi Samu, Andhra Pradesh**: sword practice in **garidi**, stick practice as preparation; **Paika Akhada, Odisha**: warrior training with rhythmic performance/drums.
- **Pari Khanda, Bihar/eastern tradition**: sword–shield; links to Chhau practice do not make every local martial form separately UNESCO-listed.
- **Thoda, Himachal Pradesh**: archery-based sport/performance, Baisakhi and Mahabharata-themed groups; **Mallakhamb, Maharashtra/Madhya Pradesh**: gymnastic pole/rope practice, malla = wrestler, khamb = pole.
- **Kalaripayattu, Mallakhamb, Gatka, Thang-ta** inducted into Khelo India Youth Games programme in the source's historical account; not proof all described arts are medal events in every competition.
