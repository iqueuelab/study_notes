# International Booker Prize and Heart Lamp

## 20 May 2025 award
- **Heart Lamp**, **Banu Mushtaq**, translated from **Kannada by Deepa Bhasthi**, published by **And Other Stories**, won at **Tate Modern, London**. First short-story collection and first Kannada-language winner; Bhasthi first Indian translator to win. Mushtaq is the second Indian author winner after Geetanjali Shree's **Tomb of Sand (2022)**.
- Twelve stories centre women's everyday struggles and resilience in southern Indian Muslim communities, engaging gender, religion, class/caste and power; the author's **Bandaya Sahitya** association links protest literature with marginalised voices. Translation makes regional literature accessible while preserving linguistic/cultural texture. [Official winner announcement](https://thebookerprizes.com/media-centre/press-releases/heart-lamp-wins-the-international-booker-prize-2025).
- Prize established **2005**; current annual translated-book format began **2016**. Eligible fiction translated into English and published in **UK/Ireland**. **£50,000 split equally author/translator**; source shortlisted author/translator award **£2,500 each**.
- Longlist **12–13**, shortlist **six**; selection dates vary by year, so source March/April/May calendar is not an immutable rule. Distinguish this translation prize from the separate Booker Prize for English-language fiction.
