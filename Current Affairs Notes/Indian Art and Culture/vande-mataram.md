# Vande Mataram: national song and freedom-movement history

## November 2025: 150-year commemoration
- **Bankimchandra Chattopadhyay**, Sanskrit/Bengali composition meaning **“Mother, I bow to thee”**, incorporated in **Anandamath (1882)**. Official commemoration treats **7 November 1875** as the believed composition date; retain that qualification rather than asserting an uncontested manuscript date. **7 November 2025** launched the year-long commemoration. [Culture Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2186757&lang=2&reg=48).
- **Rabindranath Tagore sang it at the 1896 Calcutta Congress**; it became a rallying cry of anti-colonial mobilisation, notably Swadeshi. First two stanzas praise the motherland; later verses use religious/goddess imagery, generating objections and a debate about inclusive national symbolism.
- **1937 Congress decision** recommended the first two stanzas for national gatherings. The PDF's account of a Bose-chaired committee including Gandhi is not independently established here; do not memorise that roster. Congress adoption at gatherings is distinct from post-independence national status.
- **24 January 1950**, Constituent Assembly President Rajendra Prasad stated that Vande Mataram should be honoured equally with Jana Gana Mana. This statement is not an Article of the Constitution prescribing identical anthem/song protocols. National Anthem obligations should not automatically be transferred to every song performance.
- The PM's criticism of the 1937 choice is **November 2025 political commentary**, despite the source's erroneous 2024 label. Assertions that the choice caused Partition are political interpretation, not demonstrated historical causality. Evaluate symbolism, freedom-struggle mobilisation and accommodation of religious diversity separately.

## February 2026: performance protocols and conscience
- Source critiques the reported **MHA performance guidelines** prescribing a full **six-stanza rendition** at specified official occasions, with standing instructions and school-related guidance. Treat these as executive performance instructions; the editorial's conclusion that the order is unconstitutional is an argument, not a judicial ruling invalidating it.
- Existing history covers **Anandamath 1882,1937 first-two-stanza compromise and 24 January 1950 Rajendra Prasad statement**. The Assembly President's statement honouring the song equally with the anthem was not an Article prescribing identical anthem/song protocols, nor proof every historical detail in the PDF's committee roster is established.
- **Article 51A(a)** expressly mentions the Constitution, National Flag and National Anthem; the **42nd Amendment 1976** inserted Fundamental Duties. At this issue date, the **1971 Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act** did not give the song the same express statutory treatment as the anthem. Do not backdate any later statutory change.
- **Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala(1986)** protected schoolchildren who stood respectfully but declined to sing the anthem on religious grounds under **Articles 19(1)(a)/25**. Its principle of conscience/expression is relevant, while application to a particular new order requires its text and legal adjudication; respectful silence is distinct from obstructing a performance.
- Balance national symbolism and civic respect with freedom of conscience, secularism and proportionate executive action. No new offence or automatic penalty follows merely from the editorial's claim.
