# Kala Utsav and Indian painting traditions

## Artists in residence: July 2025
- **29 Sohrai, Pattachitra and Patua artists** stayed at Rashtrapati Bhavan **14–24 July 2025**, creating, interacting and exhibiting traditional art under **Kala Utsav–Artists in Residence**. Source recalls an earlier Artists-in-Residence programme inaugurated in 2013; do not date every detail of the present Kala Utsav programme to that launch. [President's account](https://www.presidentofindia.gov.in/press_releases/saoharaaya-patatacaitara-aura-pataua-kalaa-kae-kalaakaaraon-nae-avaasaiya-kalaakaara).
| Tradition | Region and medium | Recall |
|---|---|---|
| **Sohrai** | Jharkhand; murals on house walls with earth/clay pigments | Women artists; harvest festival after Diwali; animals, plants and local symbols |
| **Pattachitra** | Odisha and Bengal; painted cloth/scroll traditions | Jagannath, Krishna and epic imagery; cloth preparation may use chalk and tamarind paste; regional forms differ |
| **Patua** | West Bengal; narrative cloth/paper scrolls | Patua artists sing while unrolling images; religious, folk and contemporary social/political themes |
- Visibility supports artist livelihoods, cultural diversity and transmission to younger generations.
## PYQ-linked painting recall
- **Bani Thani → Kishangarh school**, Rajasthan; **Nihal Chand**, mid-eighteenth century, associated with **Sawant Singh/Nagari Das**; elongated eyes, graceful devotional Radha–Krishna imagery.
- **Padmapani → Ajanta, Cave 1**, Maharashtra; lotus-bearing bodhisattva/compassion in Buddhist art. Ajanta's phases span roughly second century BCE–sixth century CE; murals on prepared plaster are distinguished from true wet-plaster fresco.
- **Kalamkari → hand-painted cotton textile, South India**, especially Andhra Pradesh: **Srikalahasti**, freehand pen and epic themes; **Machilipatnam**, block-printed floral/decorative tradition. Processes use indigo, myrobalan, pomegranate and iron preparations; myrobalan is not simply a black pigment.
