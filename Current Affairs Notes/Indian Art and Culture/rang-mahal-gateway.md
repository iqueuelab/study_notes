# Rang Mahal gateway: Hyderabad heritage restoration

## Historical and architectural significance
- Surviving gateway of Khair-un-Nissa’s Rang Mahal in the former British Residency precinct, Hyderabad; associated with Khair-un-Nissa and **James Achilles Kirkpatrick**, British Resident at the Nizam’s court, in the early nineteenth century.
- The palace no longer survives; gateway features include **paired tapering columns, incised stucco, flaring overhangs and jharokhas**. The entrance to a pleasure garden illustrates Deccan aristocratic architecture and cultural interaction.
- The issue reports restoration with the **Deccan Heritage Foundation** and the university occupying the former Residency precinct. DHF lists restoration of the Rang Mahal pavilion and garden among completed projects. [DHF project register](https://www.deccan-heritage-foundation.org/restoration-projects/).
## Conservation approach
- Protect surviving original fabric, including remaining columns; document condition before intervention, use compatible repairs and maintain the restored site.
- Adaptive reuse: cultural programmes, interpretation, conservation training for students/local communities, garden revival and responsible tourism.
- Link heritage conservation with urban green space and community participation. HRIDAY and Smart Cities illustrate urban-heritage policy themes; the issue does not establish that either funded this restoration.
- The Residency precinct has broader colonial history; a specific gateway connection to **1857** requires supporting evidence rather than inference from its location.
