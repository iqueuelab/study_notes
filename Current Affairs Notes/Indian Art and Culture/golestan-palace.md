# Golestan Palace: Persian architecture and heritage protection

## March 2026: heritage at risk
- **Golestan (“rose/flower garden”) Palace, Tehran, Iran**: Safavid-origin complex developed principally into a **Qajar** royal residence, governing seat and artistic centre. Gardens/pools, mirror-work, tile decoration and Persian craftsmanship combine with European motifs and technologies; its characteristic ornament is largely **19th century**, rather than all surviving buildings dating to the 16th century. Later Pahlavi use altered parts of the setting.
- **UNESCO World Heritage inscription: 2013**, criteria ii, iii and iv. The Qajars came to power in 1779; do not assume that every later palace expansion or Tehran's capital designation happened in that year. [UNESCO property record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1422).
- UNESCO's **2 March** statement reported debris/shock-wave damage after an airstrike on **Arag Square in the property's buffer zone**. It circulated heritage-site coordinates to the parties and recalled protection under the **1954 Hague Convention** and **1972 World Heritage Convention**. This report does not establish that the palace itself was the intended target. [UNESCO statement](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-expresses-concern-over-protection-cultural-heritage-sites-amidst-escalating-violence-middle).
- Background sequence shown in the issue: Achaemenid → Hellenistic → Parthian → Sasanian → Islamic-era dynasties → Safavid → Qajar → Pahlavi → Islamic Republic (1979). The infographic's broad eras should not be read as a complete, uninterrupted dynasty list; Safavid and Qajar art are distinct historical contexts.
