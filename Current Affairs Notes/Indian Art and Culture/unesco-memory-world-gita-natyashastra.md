# Bhagavad Gita and Natyashastra: UNESCO documentary heritage

## April 2025 inscription
- **India's nominated manuscript holdings at Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute (BORI), Pune** entered UNESCO's **Memory of the World International Register in 2025**: the **Manuscript Collection of Bhagavadgītā** and **Manuscript of the Nāṭyaśāstra of Bharatamuni**. Recognition concerns preserved documentary collections, rather than every edition of either text or a World Heritage site. [UNESCO Gita entry](https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/manuscript-collection-bhagavadgita-ancient-samgraha-grantha-indian-thought-world-wide-readership-and), [Natyashastra entry](https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/manuscript-natyasastra-bharatamuni-seminal-text-indian-performing-art).
- The 2025 round added **74 collections**, taking the international total to **570**; this is a dated total. [UN India announcement](https://india.un.org/en/293100-unesco-adds-bhagavadg%C4%ABt%C4%81-and-n%C4%81%E1%B9%ADya%C5%9B%C4%81stra-memory-world-register).
## Programme distinction
| UNESCO mechanism | What it safeguards |
|---|---|
| Memory of the World, launched **1992** | Documentary heritage: manuscripts, archives, recordings, audiovisual holdings; preservation and access |
| World Heritage Convention, **1972** | Cultural/natural sites of outstanding universal value |
| Intangible Cultural Heritage Convention, **2003** | Living practices, knowledge and expressions |
- MoW operates through **international, regional and national** structures; inscription on one register does not automatically mean inscription on another. It promotes conservation, digitisation, cataloguing and wider access while respecting cultural contexts. International nominations are normally considered in biennial cycles, with up to two national nominations per cycle; joint nominations are distinct. [UNESCO programme](https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/about).
## Texts to remember
- **Bhagavad Gita:** **700 verses, 18 chapters**, within the **Bhishma Parva of the Mahabharata**; Krishna–Arjuna dialogue in the epic's narrative. Dharma, **Karma Yoga**, **Jnana Yoga** and **Bhakti Yoga** connect duty, action, knowledge and devotion. Its literary/religious setting should not be mistaken for proof of literal historical events or a secure composition date.
- **Natyashastra:** Sanskrit performing-arts treatise traditionally attributed to **Bharata Muni**; ancient compilation, with approximate dating commonly spanning **2nd century BCE–2nd century CE**, rather than a known author's precise publication date. Covers **natya** (drama), **abhinaya** (expression/performance), **rasa** (aesthetic experience), **bhava** (emotion) and **sangita** (music).
- **Verse-count caution:** the source's 36,000 refers to a larger Natya/Gandharvaveda tradition in UNESCO's description. Do not present it unqualified as the size of the commonly studied Natyashastra, normally described as about **6,000 verses in 36 chapters**. [IGNOU teaching material](https://www.egyankosh.ac.in/bitstream/123456789/84054/1/Block-3.pdf).
## Earlier documentary examples and significance
- **Rigveda manuscripts: 2007**, correcting the source's 2005; **Saiva manuscripts in Pondicherry: 2005**. Other international entries include Abhinavagupta's works and the first NAM summit archives (**2023**), and Dutch East India Company archives (**2003**, multinational holdings). [UNESCO Rigveda](https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/rigveda).
- World examples in the source: Sri Lanka's **Mahavamsa**, Sakubei Yamamoto drawings, Shaiva Siddhanta manuscripts, Frankfurt Auschwitz trial recordings and Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's **7 March 1971** speech recording. Documentary value includes historical evidence and transmission across generations.
- India's benefits: preservation of fragile manuscripts, scholarly access, cultural diplomacy and soft power. Recognition creates an opportunity for sustained archival care; it does not by itself finance or complete conservation.
