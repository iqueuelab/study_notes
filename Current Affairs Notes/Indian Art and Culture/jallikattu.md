# Jallikattu: culture, welfare and law

## Tradition and debate
- Tamil Nadu **Pongal**, January harvest festival; bull-taming event. **Jalli**: coins; **kattu**: tied—traditional prize associated with the bull's horns.
- Agrarian identity, bravery, human–animal relationships; supporters link breeding value to indigenous **Kangayam, Pulikulam, Bargur** cattle. Conservation benefit is an argument to evaluate, not proof every event protects biodiversity.
- Risks: bull stress/injury, tail twisting/poking/drugging, participant/spectator injury; rural livelihoods and cultural continuity must be weighed against preventable harm.
## Legal timeline: correct the source
| Year | Development |
| --- | --- |
| **2014** | **AWBI v A. Nagaraja**: Supreme Court prohibited Jallikattu under the then law; highlighted animal welfare/compassion |
| **2017** | Tamil Nadu amended the **Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960**, with rules permitting regulated Jallikattu |
| **18 May 2023** | Constitution Bench upheld relevant Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Maharashtra amendments concerning Jallikattu, Kambala and bullock-cart racing |
- January 2025 source incorrectly calls constitutional validity still undecided. The 2023 decision requires statutory safeguards; it is not blanket permission for cruelty. [Supreme Court judgment](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2016/1216/1216_2016_3_1501_44624_Judgement_18-May-2023.pdf).
- **Article 51A(g)** compassion; **48** animal husbandry, **48A** environmental/wildlife protection; cultural claims invoke **29**. Do not treat source's Article 21 reference as an unqualified holding that animals have the same enforceable fundamental rights as persons.
- Wildlife protection legislation is not the central regulating statute for a domestic bull sport. Mains: cultural participation with independent welfare inspection, veterinary checks, safety barriers, accountability and enforcement.
## Breed trap
- **Gir is indigenous to India (Gujarat)**. A breed being outside the typical Tamil Nadu Jallikattu breed list does not make it foreign to India.

## May 2025 cultural additions and comparison
- Also **Eruthazhuvuthal**; variants **Vadi manjuvirattu, Veli virattu, Vatam manjuvirattu**. Popular districts: Madurai, Tiruchirappalli, Theni, Pudukkottai and Dindigul; **Pulikulam**, associated with Sivagangai, is an important native breed. Participant prize and owner prestige are cultural features, not a universal competition rule.
- Ancient Tamil bull-embracing traditions have literary associations; the source's purported **Mohenjo-daro seal chronology/continuous Jallikattu lineage** and unqualified **“Silappadikaram is Sangam-era”** claim should not be treated as established dating. Artefact interpretation does not prove uninterrupted modern practice.
- **Concurrent List entry 17: prevention of cruelty to animals**. The **2023 Constitution Bench** upheld regulated statutory amendments but did not itself certify Jallikattu as an “essential cultural practice” or give animals all human Article 21 rights. [Supreme Court judgment](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2016/1216/1216_2016_3_1501_44624_Judgement_18-May-2023.pdf).
| Comparison | Recall |
|---|---|
| Kambala | Coastal Karnataka buffalo racing in wet paddy tracks |
| Bullock-cart racing | Maharashtra and other States; Maharashtra amendment included in 2023 judgment |
| Cockfighting | Associated with Sankranti in Andhra Pradesh; cruelty/betting concerns; tradition is no blanket legal exemption |
| Camel racing | Rajasthan fairs including Pushkar; permission/safety depend on applicable rules |
| Dhirio | Goa bull fights, distinct from bull-embracing Jallikattu; legality cannot be inferred from Tamil Nadu's amendment |
