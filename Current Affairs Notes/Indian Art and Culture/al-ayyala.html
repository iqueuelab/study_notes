Al-Ayyala: Gulf performing heritage

Editable notes

Performance and safeguarding

  • Oman and UAE traditional performing art, inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage, 2014; not a built World Heritage Site.
  • Chanted poetry, drumming and synchronised movement evoke battle, pride and unity: two facing rows of men carry thin bamboo sticks representing weapons; drums, tambourines and cymbals accompany movement. Approximate row sizes vary, not a fixed rule of precisely 20.
  • UAE performances include young girls in front moving their long hair; the source's generic “women” description misses UNESCO's age-specific account. The lead performer directs rhythm/movement; skill passes through community/family participation, with public teaching supporting preservation. UNESCO.
  • Weddings, festivals and official welcomes sustain social identity. May 2025: performance welcomed Donald Trump in Abu Dhabi during a Gulf tour including Saudi Arabia, Qatar and UAE; source links tour to defence, investment and AI cooperation.
  • Soft power: hospitality and living heritage communicate identity; compare India's cultural diplomacy through yoga/classical dance without assuming cultural performance itself establishes a defence or investment contract. Safeguarding requires practitioners and intergenerational transmission, not inscription alone.
