# Somnath Temple: sacred geography and reconstruction

## January 2026 commemoration
- **Somnath Swabhiman Parv, 8–11 January 2026**, commemorates a millennium since **Mahmud of Ghazni's 1026 attack**. The temple stands at **Prabhas Patan near Veraval, Saurashtra, Gujarat**, on the Arabian Sea coast, and is one of Shiva's **12 Jyotirlingas**. [Official backgrounder,10 January](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2213128&lang=1&reg=6).
- Repeated destruction and rebuilding make it a case study in historical memory, heritage conservation and cultural continuity. Distinguish contemporary commemorative framing from evidence for every ancient reconstruction narrative.
## Reconstruction and PYQ anchors
- **November 1947:** Vallabhbhai Patel initiated the post-independence reconstruction effort; **K.M. Munshi** was also central. **11 May 1951:** installation ceremony associated with **President Rajendra Prasad**, not S. Radhakrishnan. The current temple uses the Chalukyan architectural tradition, with sanctum, assembly hall and main shikhara.
- **Al-Biruni** described Somnath. For the **2022 Prelims** statements: Jyotirlinga status and Al-Biruni description are correct; attribution of pranpratishtha to Radhakrishnan is wrong: **(a)1 and 2 only**.
- Reconstruction also raises questions about the relationship between religious heritage, public leadership, popular funding and secular governance; preserve this analytical dimension alongside religious tourism and coastal sacred geography.
