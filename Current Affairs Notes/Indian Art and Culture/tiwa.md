# Tiwa (Lalung): community and agrarian festivals

## Community, language and livelihoods
- **Tiwa/Lalung** communities live in **Assam and Meghalaya**, including hill and plains settings; the issue names Karbi Anglong, Morigaon and Nagaon. Tiwa belongs to the **Tibeto-Burman** linguistic grouping; language use and practices vary between communities.
- Hill livelihoods include **jhum/shifting cultivation, horticulture and cash/vegetable crops**; plains communities practise more settled paddy agriculture. Do not turn this broad contrast into a rule describing every household.
- Tiwa/Lalung is recognised among Assam's Scheduled Tribes; recognition is State/list-specific. The **Lalung (Tiwa) Autonomous Council Act, 1995** establishes a statutory Assam council; statutory autonomy is distinct from assuming Sixth Schedule status. [Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/11575/1/the_lalung_%28tiwa%29_autonomous_council_act%2C_1995.pdf).
## Agrarian festivals
- Source describes **Khelchawa** at harvest-end around April; **Yangli**, generally triennial, at the beginning of sowing, seeking crop protection; **Langkhun** community prayers for a good harvest, illustrated in Karbi Anglong. Feasting, dance and oral traditions sustain collective identity.
- The source's “people lifted from below” explanation of the name is a reported origin interpretation, not proof of historical migration. Its practice answer is **(b), statements 2 and 3**; the false Meghalaya-only recognition statement must not be memorised as fact.
