# Khurja pottery and the ceramic cluster

## Craft, location and production
- **Khurja, Bulandshahr district, Uttar Pradesh**: ceramic crockery/tea sets, decorative pieces and tiles; characteristic colourful glazes and floral designs. The GI registry records **Khurja Pottery**, handicraft **class 21**, application **178**, certificate dated **23 March 2015**. GI protects the registered geographical identity; it does not confer a monopoly over all pottery. [GI registry](https://search.ipindia.gov.in/GIRPublicSearch/Application/Details/178).
- Clay preparation → moulding/wheel shaping → drying/decorating → glazing → kiln firing; exact firing/decorating order varies by product. **Ceramics** are inorganic non-metallic materials hardened by heat; glaze provides a glassy surface and can reduce porosity.
- Historical origins have competing migration/patronage traditions. Do not memorise the worksheet's internally inconsistent “Timur, an Afghan ruler, about 500 years ago”: Timur's Indian invasion was in 1398, and official ODOP material instead associates the craft with Feroz Shah Tughlaq. [UP ODOP](https://odopup.in/pdf/20180305_ODOP-English.pdf?cd=MQAxADYA).
## March 2026: energy and circular-economy links
- The current clipping concerns **ceramic units threatened by gas shortages during the West Asia crisis**: kiln-dependent small industries face fuel costs/supply interruption, lost orders and livelihood risk.
- **Anokhi Duniya** is described in the background as a ceramic-waste reuse park—an example of recycling and craft tourism. Its “world first” and opening-by-September statements are undated promotional/forecast claims, not evidence of a March 2026 opening. Heritage protection, reliable cleaner kiln energy, waste reuse and artisan markets are complementary priorities.
