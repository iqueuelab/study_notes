# Kathputli and Indian puppetry traditions

## Rajasthan string puppetry
- **Kathputli**: **kath (wood) + putli (doll)**; traditional **string-puppet theatre of Rajasthan**, associated with **Bhat/Nat** travelling performers. The issue reports about **250 Jaipur families** sustaining the tradition, a local livelihood account rather than a national artist census.
- Wooden carved heads/upper bodies, cotton and fabric costumes; stylised large eyes, expressive faces and colourful court-inspired dress. Most lack legs: long skirts conceal the lower body and enable fluid movement. Finger-controlled strings animate neck, arms and body; precise string numbers vary by character/design.
- Folk songs, narration and **dholak** accompany stories of rulers, warriors and heroes, including **Amar Singh Rathore**, humour, satire and social messages. Oral performance combines craft, music, theatre and inherited skill. [Tourism Ministry account](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/rajasthan/kathputlis-of-rajasthan), [official handicrafts compendium](https://handicrafts.nic.in/cmsUpload/20170506115215GI_BOOK_FINAL_ilovepdf_compressed.PDF).

- The source's broad ancient-literature dates are not precise evidence of Kathputli's continuous chronology. Preserve the tradition through apprenticeship, performance opportunities, fair artist incomes and community-led cultural tourism, alongside museum documentation and schooling.
