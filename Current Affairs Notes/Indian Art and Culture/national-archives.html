Indian Art and Culture

National Archives of India and documentary heritage

Editable notes

Institution and access

  • National Archives of India (NAI), Ministry of Culture, holds non-current Government of India records of enduring value in trust for administrators, researchers and the public; a major South Asian archival repository. Preservation, research/legal evidence and public accountability are core purposes.
  • 11 March 1891: Imperial Record Department established at Calcutta. 1911 capital shift must not be confused with completed physical relocation: Delhi building plan sanctioned 12 October 1912, office opened 1 November 1926, records transfer completed March 1937. NAI milestones.
  • Implements Public Records Act 1993 /Rules 1997; archival transfer, access and declassification are governed processes. The source's absolute “classified documents are not stored” is unsafe: distinguish accessible/declassified holdings from restrictions on particular records.
  • Regional office Bhopal; records centres Bhubaneswar, Jaipur, Puducherry. Holdings include files/manuscripts, maps/treaties/cartographic records, debates/censuses/travelogues, proscribed literature and gazettes in many languages including Sanskrit, Persian and Odia.
  • Abhilekh PATAL = Portal for Access to Archives and Learning: searchable reference media and digitised records. Source snapshots 34 crore pages, 2.7 million records, 71,792+ digitised documents represent different units and dated coverage; portal records do not mean all pages digitised.

28 April 2025 acquisition

  • NAI acquired Dr A.P.J. Abdul Kalam's private papers on 28 April 2025, enriching scientific/presidential documentary heritage. Private archives complement official records; their acquisition does not establish universal online availability. Culture Ministry.
