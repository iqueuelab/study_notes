Keezhadi: flood sediments, OSL dating and settlement history

Editable notes

December 2025: dating flood burial

  • Keezhadi, Sivaganga district, Tamil Nadu, on the Vaigai floodplain: brick walls, drainage/canals, floors, pottery/tiles illuminate settlement, craft and urban planning relevant to Sangam studies. Archaeological evidence supplements literary description; neither a flood-deposit age nor a poem gives the exact construction date.
  • PRL–Tamil Nadu Archaeology study used optically stimulated luminescence (OSL) on quartz in four sediment samples from KDI-1/KDI-2. Light-tight tubes/red-light handling preserve the burial signal; measured stored radiation dose divided by environmental dose rate estimates time since last sufficient sunlight exposure.
  • Source rounded pairs: KDI-1 80 cm/~670 years, 150 cm/~1,170 years; KDI-2 290 cm/~940 years, 380 cm/~1,140 years. Depth-age order applies within a pit, not a rigid ordering across different pits. Coarser sand followed by fine silt/clay and deposits over structures support high-energy inundation followed by settling.
  • The Current Science 129(8), 712–718 (2025) abstract places flooding/burial at ~1,140 years ago; issue summarises ~1,155 BP. Treat these as approximate/rounded burial estimates with uncertainty, not a precise calendar year or definitive proof all Keezhadi was abandoned simultaneously. Research abstract catalogued by IISc.
  • River migration, wet/dry variability and flood processes can bury/damage settlements and affect preservation; Vaigai's present separation from the site is consistent with landscape change. Evidence supports a flood-burial interpretation, not demonstration modern anthropogenic climate change caused it. The source's informal ~5,000-years-to-present “Late Holocene” is not the formal 4.2-ka boundary; avoid treating a broad teaching label as exact geological dating.
