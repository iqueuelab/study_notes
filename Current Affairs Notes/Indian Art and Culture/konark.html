Indian Art and Culture

Konark Sun Temple: Kalinga architecture

Editable notes

Core recall

  • Surya / Sun Temple, Konark, Puri district, Odisha, Bay of Bengal coast; Narasimhadeva I, Eastern Ganga dynasty, mid-13th century (c.1250; reign 1238–64).
  • UNESCO World Heritage, 1984; culmination of Kalinga/Odisha temple architecture, within the broader Nagara tradition. “Kalinga” and “Nagara” need not be mutually exclusive answer choices.
  • Monument conceived as Surya's chariot: 24 wheels = 12 pairs, intended seven horses. PDF says six; UNESCO's short description also says six, but its detailed outstanding-value statement specifies seven. Use seven for original symbolism, distinguish surviving sculptures. UNESCO record.

Architecture and sculpture

  • East-oriented; surviving Jagamohana assembly hall, ruined principal sanctuary (vimana/deul) and dance hall (natamandira). Source's “100-foot” figure should not describe the entire original tower indiscriminately.
  • Carved wheels can indicate solar time; reject universal minute-perfect accuracy under all conditions.
  • Khondalite is a major building stone; other materials occur too. Sculpture: deities/myths, animals, foliage, musicians/dancers, warriors and social life.
  • Historic name Black Pagoda; counterpart White Pagoda: Jagannath Temple, Puri. Do not infer the whole monument is made of black granite.
  • Chandrabhaga / Magha Saptami traditions in the area; distinguish religious association from assuming routine worship continues inside the ruined main sanctuary.

Conservation

  • Salt-laden air, erosion/cyclones, biological weathering, structural weakness and tourism pressure; ASI conservation and protected/regulatory zones.
