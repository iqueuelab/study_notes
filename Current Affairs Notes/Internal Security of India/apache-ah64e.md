# Apache AH-64E attack helicopter

## Capability and July delivery forecast
- **Boeing AH-64 E Apache Guardian, US-origin, multi-role attack helicopter**: open-system architecture, digital interoperability, targeting/night/thermal vision and survivability aids; Hellfire missiles, rockets and 30-mm chain gun. Improved engines/lift do not guarantee equal payload at every altitude or make it an invisible stealth aircraft.
- **IAF: 22 ordered in 2015, deliveries completed in 2020; Army: six ordered in 2020**. At this issue date Army deliveries were awaited: clipping forecasts the first three by **15 July 2025**, not already operational Army aircraft.
- Roles: anti-armour, armed reconnaissance/close combat support and coordinated air–ground operations; terrain/weather, payload, maintenance and crew training affect performance. Foreign users of the Apache family do not all operate the same E configuration.

## 22 July 2025: Army delivery
- The embedded newspaper clipping reports the Army received its **first three AH-64E helicopters at Hindon on Tuesday, 22 July**, with joint receipt inspection before deployment to **Jodhpur, Army Aviation Corps**. This updates the earlier forecast; arrival is distinct from inspection, induction and full operational deployment.

## December 2025: final Army delivery
- Source reports the final **three AH-64E Apaches arrived at Hindon**, completing delivery of the Army's **six-aircraft 2020 order**, valued at about **US$600 million**. Intended unit **451 Army Aviation Squadron, Jodhpur**, raised **March 2024**; first three arrived in July, already recorded.
- Western-front deployment supports anti-armour and close air support alongside reconnaissance, night sensors, Hellfire missiles, rockets and 30 mm gun. **Apache is an attack helicopter; Chinook is a heavy-lift transport**. No new general capability list is needed beyond existing notes.
- **Arrival/completion of delivery ≠ completed inspection, induction, crew conversion or full operational readiness**; the PDF's “full operationalisation” goes beyond arrival evidence. Its **~15-month delay** is a source estimate attributed to supply-chain disruptions; do not assume an independently established causal allocation to pandemic/geopolitics.
- Army-controlled aviation complements IAF assets and requires joint command, communication, sustainment and logistics; a rigid claim Army only does close support while IAF only does deep strikes oversimplifies operational roles. Foreign supply dependence makes domestic maintenance and reliable spares important.
