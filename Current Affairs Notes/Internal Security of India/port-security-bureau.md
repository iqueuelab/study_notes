# Bureau of Port Security: ship, port and cyber security

## December 2025: Bureau of Port Security announced
- **19 December announcement**: proposed statutory **BoPS under section 13, Merchant Shipping Act 2025**, within **Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways**, modelled on **BCAS**. This announcement does not establish that the new institution was already fully operational. [Home Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2206443&lang=1&reg=6).
- DG to be an **IPS officer, Pay Level 15**; **DGS/DGMA** to act as DG during the **one-year transition**. Regulator oversees ship/port-facility security, information exchange and a dedicated **cybersecurity division** for port IT infrastructure.
- **Graded, risk-based security** considers vulnerability, location and trade potential; cyberattacks, GPS spoofing, data theft and logistics disruption connect economic and national security.
- **CISF remains a Recognised Security Organisation**, conducting assessments/plans and training private port-security agencies; BoPS does not imply CISF’s functions simply end. Regulation, physical security and operational policing are distinct functions.
- Source repeats the old **7,516.6-km coastline**. Official revised measure is **11,098.81 km**, promulgated **29 April 2025**, reflecting finer GIS/high-water-line measurement; it does not itself alter CRZ jurisdiction. [Earth Sciences Ministry, 4 December](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2198800&lang=1&reg=3).
- Revision MCQ: Ministry is **MoPSW**, not MHA; source answer **(b), statements 1/3/4**. It is an undated practice question, not a PYQ.

## December 25 revision: governance and legal corrections
- **BoPS announcement, section 13 Merchant Shipping Act 2025, MoPSW, BCAS model, CISF recognised security role and proposed cyber division** repeat 20 December coverage. Proposed constitution/transition must remain distinct from a fully operational regulator.
- Source adds growth context: cargo **974→1,594 MMT**, capacity **+57%**, turnaround **48 hours**, coastal shipping **+118%**, inland-waterway cargo **18.1→145.5 MMT**, and **nine Indian ports** in a container-performance index. These are source-reported mixed-period indicators, not one harmonised 2025 audited series; improvement claims need baseline, port coverage and index edition.
- **Indian Ports Act 2025, Coastal Shipping Act 2025 and Merchant Shipping Act 2025** are separate reforms; source's **“Bureau of Port Security Act 2025” is not a separate verified Act**. ISPS security standards, physical protection, operational policing and cyber resilience involve complementary agencies; BoPS does not automatically replace Navy, Coast Guard or State marine police.
- Non-major-port regulation raises State/Union coordination and federal concerns; inspection/security powers require legal safeguards and accountability. The existence of regulatory oversight does not by itself erase State ownership or demonstrate every civil-liberties criticism against the wider legislation applies to BoPS.
- **Maritime India Vision 2030 (2021)** supplies development context; source coastline **7,500+ km** is an older measure. Existing 20 December note retains official revised **11,098.81 km** and measurement qualification. Security planning must cover port IT, logistics, access control, trafficking, maritime terrorism and information-sharing alongside physical expansion.
