# National Investigation Agency

## Legal basis and case allocation
- **NIA Act, 2008**, enacted after the 26/11 Mumbai attacks; agency constituted in **2009**, under the **Ministry of Home Affairs**. Specialised national investigation/prosecution of **scheduled offences**, with coordination and capacity-building roles.
- Under **section 6**, state police report suspected scheduled offences through the state government to the Centre; the Centre determines whether NIA investigation is warranted, and may also direct investigation suo motu. State consent is not a statutory precondition to a Central direction. Police continue investigation until NIA takes over.
- **Section 8** permits investigation of connected offences; this does not make every connected law a scheduled law. State cooperation, evidence sharing and terror-financing expertise support investigations.

## Schedule: scope matters
| Coverage | Qualification |
|---|---|
| UAPA; Atomic Energy Act; Anti-Hijacking Act; WMD Act | Named statutory coverage |
| Civil-aviation, maritime-navigation and SAARC-convention implementation Acts | Statutes implementing conventions, not treaties alone |
| Explosive Substances Act | Added in 2019 |
| IPC provisions in the amended Act | Chapter VI; trafficking sections 370/370A; counterfeit currency sections 489A–489E; not all IPC crimes |
| Arms Act | Specified prohibited-arms offence, section 25(1AA); not every arms case |
| IT Act | Section 66F cyber-terrorism; not every cybercrime |
- **NDPS Act is not a general entry in the NIA Schedule**. Drug offences can arise in a connected scheduled-offence case; do not equate this with jurisdiction over all narcotics cases. IPC references describe the statutory wording used in the source-period material; criminal-code transition requires applicable corresponding provisions.

## 2019 amendment and safeguards
- Expanded scheduled coverage to trafficking, prohibited arms, cyber-terrorism and explosive-substance offences. Counterfeit currency was already covered; it was not newly introduced by this amendment.
- Extraterritorial investigation is subject to **international treaties and the host country's domestic law**; it is not unilateral authority to police foreign territory. The Centre may direct a case involving applicable scheduled offences abroad as if committed in India.
- Centre may designate **Sessions Courts as Special Courts in consultation with the Chief Justice of the High Court**; states also have statutory special-court provisions. Specialised courts aim to improve trials, without guaranteeing prompt disposal.
- Mains balance: national/transnational threats require coordination; police being a State List subject raises federal concerns. Strengthen state cooperation, trained staff, judicial oversight, due process and timely trials; allegations of misuse require case-specific evidence.
