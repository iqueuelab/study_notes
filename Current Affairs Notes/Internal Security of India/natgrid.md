# NATGRID: integrated intelligence and data safeguards

## December 2025: reported NPR linkage
- **National Intelligence Grid (NATGRID)**, under **Ministry of Home Affairs**, integrates access to multiple government/private datasets for **authorised security and investigating agencies**. Conceived after **26/11**, in **2009**; the issue describes expanded operation from **2024**. A platform/upgrade date is distinct from creation of the organisation or inauguration of facilities.
- The report says NPR linkage enables access to family-level records for about **119 crore residents**, and describes roughly **45,000 queries/month**. These are source-era coverage/use figures, not proof the entire current population has accurate updated records or that all users may search without authorisation.
- **Gandiva**, the analytical layer, is described as supporting multi-source analysis, facial recognition and **entity resolution** (linking identities/places/transactions). Matches require investigation and error checks; an algorithmic association is not proof of guilt.
- The **Organised Crime Network Database**, developed on NATGRID infrastructure, supports sharing between **NIA and State ATSs** against terrorist, drug and organised-crime networks. Intelligence access may precede an FIR according to the report; it does not itself establish permission for unlimited surveillance or removal of other legal safeguards.
- Government assurances cited: **query logging, recorded purpose, senior-officer oversight and access checks**. Assess legality, necessity/proportionality, role-based permissions, auditability, security, retention, correction/remedies and independent accountability. Family links and biometric identifiers raise privacy/false-match risks even when access aids investigations.

## January 2026 editorial: intelligence integration and oversight
- The issue critiques NATGRID's evolution from post-26/11 counterterror intelligence-sharing into wider policing. Its original design allowed **11 central agencies** to query **21 source databases**; these are design-era counts, not necessarily the complete current roster. Middleware links existing records rather than independently originating every dataset.
- Existing notes cover the reported **~45,000 monthly queries**, State/SP-level access, NPR family records and **Gandiva entity resolution**. AI inference can create false links and bias; family association or an automated match is not proof of guilt.
- The editorial questions the sufficiency of **executive authorisation versus a dedicated parliamentary framework** and calls for independent oversight. Its label “digital authoritarianism” is an argument, not a judicial finding; the sheet's categorical claim of no safeguards omits the government's reported logging, purpose and senior-authorisation controls already noted.
- Apply **Puttaswamy** privacy principles—lawful basis, legitimate aim, necessity/proportionality and procedural safeguards—to data access, retention, audit, correction and remedies. Stronger parliamentary/judicial accountability, professional policing and cybersecurity complement intelligence sharing. More databases alone cannot repair failures of training, coordination or institutional accountability.
