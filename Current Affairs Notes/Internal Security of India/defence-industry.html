Internal Security of India

Defence manufacturing and exports

Editable notes

Dated figures and targets

  • FY2023-24 exports: Rs 21, 083 crore, source reports 32.5% annual growth, private sector 60% / DPSUs 40%.
  • FY2024-25 defence allocation reported: Rs 6.22 lakh crore. Source production figures: Rs 74, 054 crore (2016-17) to Rs 108, 684 crore (2022-23), with 21.96% private share in the latter year.
  • Export ambition: Rs 50, 000 crore by 2028-29/2029. Keep a target separate from an achieved outcome.
  • Care Edge forecast cited: sector growth 20% CAGR and exports 19% CAGR over FY24-FY29; these are projections, not observed growth.

Drivers and constraints

  • Indigenisation, procurement demand, export facilitation, technology transfer and startups; benefits include strategic autonomy, employment and technology spillovers.
  • Constraints: critical subsystem/design gaps, R&D and finance, coordination, certification, long procurement cycles and dependence on imported technologies.

Policy and strategy

  • FDI: source cites 74% automatic route and up to 100% government route subject to applicable conditions; defence corridors in UP and Tamil Nadu; Strategic Partnership Model and export-promotion support.
  • Do not memorise the source's defence TUFS claim without verification: TUFS is associated with textiles. The alleged 60% import share/startup count also lacks a clear baseline.
  • Modern warfare requires cyber/AI/space skills, joint-service training and multi-domain readiness; defence attachés support military diplomacy. Army War College: Mhow.

Mains answer plan

  • Security need → domestic capability and exports → technology/finance gaps → predictable procurement, R&D, supplier development and jointness.

Aero-engines: January 2025 development debate

  • DRDO chief Samir V. Kamat called for foreign co-development of advanced engines; source describes demonstrated fourth-generation engine technologies and a future sixth-generation ambition, not an operational Indian sixth-generation engine.
  • Desired thrust-to-weight ratio above 10, high-temperature single-crystal turbine blades, powder-metallurgy discs, ceramic-matrix composites; lighter, heat-resistant components improve efficiency/performance.
  • Engine 'generation' terminology is not a universal substitute for fighter-aircraft generation; materials demonstrated are not proof a complete certified engine is ready.
  • Test/manufacturing gaps: high-altitude facility, flying test bed, large forging capacity, source 50, 000-tonne press, skilled workforce and certification.
  • Source investment proposal ₹40, 000–50, 000 crore, approximate $4–5 billion; research share 5% → suggested 15% of defence budget is an argument, not a notified requirement. Unspecified cross-country R&D percentages are not comparable verified series.
  • Safran (France), Rolls-Royce (UK), GE (USA) cited as potential partners; discussion does not mean all proposed contracts signed.
  • Benefits: autonomy, potential exports/jobs and spillovers to civil aviation, space and materials. Risks: IP/technology-transfer restrictions, dependence, delays and cost. Evaluate actual design access, domestic capability and certification milestones.

Him Kavach: cold-weather protection, January 2025

  • DRDO modular multi-layer clothing system, reported to have cleared user trials; designed temperature range +20°C to −60°C, rugged/all-weather operation.
  • Add/remove insulating layers; breathability and moisture management maintain comfort. Passive insulation retains body heat, not proof the clothing actively generates warmth.
  • Earlier ECWCS, Extreme Cold Weather Clothing System, associated with DIPAS, three-layer protection; source's blanket claim it fails in all Siachen conditions is overstated.
  • Trials/design range are not a guarantee of zero cold injury or proof universal troop rollout; exposure, fit, activity, shelter and acclimatisation still matter.

Trajan: January 2025 export report

  • Larsen & Toubro + France's KNDS, 155 mm / 52-calibre towed artillery; reported Armenian order, not proof every component is Indian-origin.
  • Indigenous components include auxiliary power unit, control panel and rolling gear assembly. Range over 40 km, ammunition-dependent; modular mobility, loading/fire-control assistance and varied-terrain deployment.
  • Towed gun ≠ self-propelled howitzer; barrel calibre designation differs from bore diameter. Export prospects depend on orders, delivery, spares/training and technology cooperation.

T-72 bridge-laying tanks: 21 January 2025 contract

  • 47 BLTs, Heavy Vehicles Factory, Armoured Vehicle Nigam Limited, ₹1, 560.52 crore (source rounded ₹1, 561 crore), Buy (Indian-IDDM) procurement. Defence Ministry.
  • Mechanised formation bridging obstacles during offensive/defensive movement; bridge-laying support vehicle differs from a tank firing its main gun. Mobility, indigenous manufacture, jobs and reliable lifecycle support are the policy links.

Aero India 2025

  • 15 th edition, 10–14 February, Yelahanka, Bengaluru; source 931 exhibitors / 150 foreign companies / around 90 participating countries are event figures, not continuing industry totals.
  • Su-57 and F-35 display, LCA/AMCA projects, BRIDGE Defence Ministers' Conclave, CEO roundtable and i DEX promote defence diplomacy, start-ups/MSMEs, supply chains and investment.
  • Source ₹1.27 lakh crore production / ~₹21, 000 crore exports refer to earlier reported annual performance; ₹1.60 lakh crore / ₹30, 000 crore figures are projections, not then-achieved FY2025–26 outcomes.
  • Jet-engine dependence, procurement delays, skills and IP/technology transfer remain constraints. Exhibition participation is not a procurement commitment; “Tejas production stabilised” should not erase delivery/engine bottlenecks.

Export figures: denominator traps

  • February repeat cites 28% historical export CAGR /19% forecast to FY29; different periods and projections must remain separate.
  • Myanmar 31% /Sri Lanka 19% are not a verified current breakdown of every defence-export item; recipient shares vary with year, data and equipment coverage.
  • Source says defence budget both 1.9–2.8% and 3%GDP /60% imports without matching scope; don't memorise them as one consistent series.
  • Tamil Nadu/UP corridors, FDI subject to conditions, strategic partnerships, export facilitation, i DEX/R&D matter; the alleged defence TUFS programme confuses textile policy, and a draft promotion policy is not automatically operational.
  • Improve competitive products, certification, delivery/spares, finance and end-use compliance; domestic procurement preference alone cannot create global demand.

29 May 2025: delivery discipline and design

  • At the CII Annual Business Summit, Air Chief Marshal A. P. Singh criticised unrealistic contract commitments and project delays, urged private industry's contribution and stressed indigenous design/development, not assembly alone. His emphatic delay remarks are a speaker's assessment, not an audited demonstration that every Indian defence project missed its deadline.
  • 27 May: Defence Minister approved the AMCA Programme Execution Model. ADA will execute with an Indian industry partner chosen through competition; private and public sectors receive opportunities. Approval of the model is not proof a private partner was selected, aircraft produced or fighter entered service. Defence Ministry.
  • His praise of Operation Sindoor concerns operational performance; the speech does not establish a new combat event on 29 May. Existing Operation Sindoor notes retain chronology and attribution.
  • Capability requires design/IP, prototype testing, certification, supply chains, skilled staff and predictable orders; production capacity alone is not frontier technology. Low output, uncertain returns and service-industry trust can inhibit scale.
  • Improve realistic milestones, procurement decisions, accountable delivery, prototype/R&D support and competitive partnerships. The current capital procurement framework is Defence Acquisition Procedure 2020; source's generic DPP reference should not be treated as the latest named instrument. Proposed incentives/PPPs are recommendations rather than accomplished reforms.
  • Practice answer: delayed delivery → preparedness gap → technology/contract/capacity constraints → credible milestones, competition, design capability and lifecycle support.

June 2025 speech and source corrections

  • Clipping repeats Defence Minister Rajnath Singh's ₹50,000-crore exports by 2029 goal at IIT Mandi's 16th Foundation Day; it is a speech report, not exports already achieved or necessarily a newly announced June target. Its ~88% ammunition self-sufficiency is a speaker/source figure requiring category/year, not all defence equipment self-sufficiency.
  • Official FY2024–25 exports ₹23,622 crore is distinct from prose's recycled FY2023–24 figures and forecasts. CareEdge 20% production /19% export CAGR through FY2029 remain forecasts; source's 28% historical CAGR needs its stated period. Defence export brief.
  • Existing notes retain the denominator cautions for Myanmar/Sri Lanka recipient shares, private/DPSU shares, budget/GDP and import spending, and correct the misattributed defence TUFS.
  • June prose also mislabels administration as “Ministry of Industrial Promotion” and presents an “India Defence Mart” export-licensing portal/universal defence EPC without verified legal identity. Use actual MoD/DDP export-authorisation mechanisms, Defence Exports Promotion Scheme, industrial corridors and applicable investment/procurement policies; do not invent a newly operational agency from generic names.
  • Proposed Green Channel expansion and advanced-system/startup support are suggestions; 194 startups is an undated source snapshot, not India's complete current ecosystem. Private share of exports does not equal private share of all production/procurement.
  • Mains: predictable procurement and finance, design/R&D, export certification, reliable delivery/spares, partnerships and end-use compliance; targets and exhibition participation do not establish actual sales or complete technology autonomy.

DAC: 3 July 2025

  • Acceptance of Necessity ≈₹1.05 lakh crore, 10 capital proposals, Buy (Indian-IDDM): armoured recovery vehicles, electronic warfare, tri-services common inventory management, SAMs, moored mines, mine countermeasure vessels, super-rapid gun mounts and submersible autonomous vessels.
  • Mobility, air defence, logistics and maritime safety are intended benefits. AoN ≠ signed contract ≠ delivery; the indigenous category requires applicable design/development/manufacture criteria. MoD.

23 September 2025: WhAP production in Morocco

  • Rajnath Singh and Moroccan Defence Minister Abdelatif Loudyi inaugurated TASL's Berrechid facility, 20,000 m², for WhAP 8×8, jointly designed with DRDO. Official wording: first such plant by an Indian private company in Africa and Morocco's largest defence manufacturing facility; avoid extending that claim to every historical overseas defence activity. Defence Ministry.
  • Modular variants: infantry fighting vehicle, armoured personnel carrier, reconnaissance, command post, mortar carrier and ambulance; remote weapon-station/ATGM options. Production had begun ahead of schedule, with initial Royal Moroccan Army deliveries planned October 2025. A factory opening is distinct from completed delivery of the full order.
  • Around one-third components/subsystems sourced/assembled locally initially; 50% local value addition is a future aim. Moroccan localisation is not the same as Indian indigenous-content percentage.
  • Strategic value: co-production, African/European market access, Moroccan skills/suppliers and India–Africa cooperation. Reliable quality, delivery, spares and scale determine export credibility.

23 October 2025: tri-service procurement proposals

  • DAC, chaired by Defence Minister Rajnath Singh, approved approximately ₹79,000 crore in proposals. Army AoNs: NAMIS (Tracked) Mk-II, Ground Based Mobile ELINT System and High Mobility Vehicles with material-handling cranes—anti-armour/fortification attack, emitter intelligence and logistics respectively.
  • Navy: Landing Platform Docks, 30 mm Naval Surface Gun, Advanced Light Weight Torpedoes, Electro Optical Infra-Red Search and Track System, and smart ammunition for 76 mm Super Rapid Gun Mounts. LPDs support amphibious joint operations/HADR; NSTL–DRDO torpedoes target submarines. The official release links the 30 mm gun to low-intensity maritime operations and anti-piracy, rather than defining it solely as an anti-aircraft system.
  • Air Force: Collaborative Long Range Target Saturation/Destruction System (CLRTS/DS), with autonomous take-off/landing, navigation, detection and payload delivery. MoD, 23 October.
  • AoN is approval of the procurement requirement, not contract award, delivery or operational induction. Existing notes retain DAP 2020, indigenous procurement and delivery/design constraints. Passive EO/IR sensing differs from active radar; saturation seeks to overwhelm defences, but the brief release does not establish a precise swarm architecture or number of aircraft.
  • Modernisation requires interoperability, testing, realistic schedules, training and lifecycle support as well as domestic industrial capacity. Do not assume every proposed subsystem is already indigenous or that approval instantly eliminates import dependence. The source's AoN MCQ has malformed option labels; preserve its meaning for revision (initial approval) without entering it as a PYQ. Its defence-modernisation mains prompt is also undated practice.

22 December 2025: India Optel–Safran production agreement

  • India Optel Limited (IOL), MoD Mini Navratna DPSU, signed with France's Safran Electronics & Defense, following January 2024 MoU. Transfer of production covers SIGMA 30N digital ring-laser-gyro inertial navigation for artillery, air defence, missiles and radars; CM3-MR direct firing sight for artillery and anti-drone use. MoD.
  • IOL undertakes manufacture, final assembly, testing, quality control and lifecycle support to Indian Army requirements. Agreement creates a route to local production/support; it does not establish that all units have already been delivered or that every design/IP right transferred.
  • Inertial navigation estimates position/orientation from internal sensors; fire-control sights support accurate aiming. Capability, maintenance and supply assurance depend on production qualification, technology absorption and reliable lifecycle support, alongside the broader indigenisation framework already covered.

29 December 2025: tri-service AoNs

  • DAC chaired by Defence Minister, approximately ₹79,000 crore Acceptance of Necessity. Army: artillery loiter munitions, low-level lightweight radars, Pinaka long-range guided ammunition, IDD&IS Mk II. Navy: bollard-pull tugs, HF software-defined radios/manpack and leasing high-altitude remotely piloted aircraft. IAF: automatic take-off/landing recording, Astra Mk II, Tejas full-mission simulator and SPICE 1000 guidance kits. Defence Ministry.
  • Precision strike, counter-drone detection/interdiction, harbour handling, maritime surveillance, training and secure communications serve distinct roles. AoN approves need/procurement proposals; it does not establish signed contracts, delivery or induction. The identical₹79,000 crore October headline concerned different systems and must not be merged as the same meeting.
  • Source's DAC MCQ is unyearred practice: neither claim correct—DAC is chaired by Defence Minister, not CDS; service/designated agencies conduct trials. Do not enter it as an authentic PYQ.
