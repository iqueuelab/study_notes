# Fujian: electromagnetic catapults and Chinese naval power

## 5 November 2025: commissioning
- **Fujian (Type 003, hull 18)** commissioned at **Sanya, Hainan, 5 November 2025**, announced 7 November; launched **June 2022**. China's third carrier, conventionally powered and its first with **electromagnetic catapults**. [Chinese government account](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202511/07/content_WS690d990cc6d00ca5f9a0764d.html).
- **Liaoning** refurbished from the former Soviet hull acquired from Ukraine; **Shandong** was already domestically built. Fujian's distinction is independently designed/built catapult carrier, not China's first domestic ship of every kind. Reported displacement **80,000-plus tonnes** and **60-plus aircraft** are design/source estimates, not proof of achieved deployed capacity.
- Catapult launch supports heavier aircraft and early-warning platforms with finer acceleration control; **ski-jump**, steam catapult and electromagnetic launch are different systems. Ceremony displayed **J-35, J-15T and KJ-600**. A new launch system still needs training, maintenance and operational integration.
- Conventional propulsion requires fuel; nuclear propulsion extends ship endurance but does not remove aircraft-fuel, crew or supply constraints. Source wrongly counts **John F. Kennedy** as already operational alongside Gerald R. Ford; avoid memorising its Ford-class service total.
- Strategic setting: first island chain **Japan–Taiwan–Philippines**; second around **Marianas/Guam–Palau**. Greater blue-water capacity may affect Taiwan, South China Sea and Indian Ocean balance, demanding Indian surveillance, partnerships and credible maritime capability. Chinese **2035 modernisation/2049 world-class** goals are ambitions, not completed parity.
- Commercial port financing at **Gwadar, Hambantota and Kyaukpyu** is not proof each is an operational Chinese naval base; distinguish **Djibouti**. Carrier commissioning does not itself demonstrate unrestricted distant sustained combat operations.
