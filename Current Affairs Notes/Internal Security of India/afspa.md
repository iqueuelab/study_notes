# AFSPA: disturbed areas, security powers and accountability

## September 2025 extension
- MHA's 26 September notifications extended disturbed-area status for six months from **1 October 2025**, unless withdrawn earlier: Manipur except 13 police-station jurisdictions in five districts; Nagaland nine districts plus 21 police-station areas in five others; Arunachal Pradesh Tirap, Changlang and Longding plus specified Namsai police-station areas. These are issue-date boundaries, subject to later review. [MHA notification register](https://origin1504-mha.nic.in/en/commoncontent/armed-forces-special-power-act-1958).
## Statutory powers and safeguards
- **Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act, 1958**: Section 3 disturbed-area declaration by Governor/UT Administrator or Centre; Section 4(a) permits specified officers, after due warning and where necessary for public order, to use force even causing death against contravention of specified prohibitory laws/orders. It is not an unrestricted power to shoot on mere suspicion.
- Section 4(c) arrest without warrant on specified grounds; 4(d) entry/search without warrant; Section 5 requires handing arrested persons to police with least possible delay. Section 6 requires prior Central sanction for prosecution/suit/other legal proceedings for acts done or purported under the Act; this is not blanket freedom from investigation or all accountability. J&K has a separate 1990 Act.
- Naga People's Movement of Human Rights judgment upheld constitutionality subject to safeguards, including six-monthly review and minimum necessary force; the decision was delivered in 1997 and commonly reported as (1998) 2 SCC 109. Jeevan Reddy Committee (2005) and Second ARC (2007) recommended repeal; Reddy proposed incorporating necessary provisions into UAPA and grievance mechanisms.
- Operational freedom in insurgency/weak civil administration must be assessed against allegations of excessive force, impunity and alienation. Periodic evidence-based review, civilian oversight, prompt investigation, remedies and trust-building complement security operations. Tripura withdrawal 2015 and Meghalaya withdrawal 2018 are historical examples.
- **2015 Mains revision:** discuss Sections 4(a), 4(c), 4(d), 6 alongside the Supreme Court's safeguards, security necessity and accountability reforms. The Meghalaya removal MCQ is labelled CAPF 2018, so excluded from CSE banks.
- The original **2015 GS-III paper, question 17**, specifies **12.5 marks**; its general instructions limit each answer to **200 words**. The supplied panel’s 250-word limit is incorrect.
