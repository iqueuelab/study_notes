Internal Security of India

AMCA: fifth-generation fighter development

Editable notes

27 May 2025: execution model approved

  • Defence Minister approved Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA) programme execution through industry partnership. Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA) executes the programme; public/private Indian companies may compete independently or in consortia, with equal opportunity. HAL is not the automatically selected manufacturer; collaboration model does not mean HAL and every private bidder already won contracts. ADA to issue an expression of interest for development partners. MoD approval.
  • Source reports March 2024 CCS development approval, roughly ₹15,000 crore; first prototype 2028–29, induction 2034 are projected milestones, not completed aircraft or assured delivery dates.

Design concepts and technology

  • Intended twin-engine, 25-tonne class fifth-generation stealth fighter, internal weapons bay, low-observable shaping/materials, diverterless supersonic intake and advanced avionics/sensor integration. Internal weapons reduce radar signature; external carriage adds payload while compromising stealth. Stealth reduces detection probability, not invisibility.
  • Source indicative load figures 1,500 kg internal weapons/5,500 kg external/6,500 kg internal fuel; load configuration and fuel are not interchangeable categories. Inset includes 17.6 m length,11.13 m wingspan,25,000 kg maximum takeoff weight, ~Mach 2.15,20,000 m ceiling,1,620 km combat-range claim: development-design figures need configuration/testing confirmation, not operational specifications.
  • Mk 1 planned GE F414 engines; higher-thrust indigenous/co-developed engine envisaged for Mk 2. Source's Safran collaboration is an option/discussion, not a final delivered co-developed engine. Generation labels (including inset's fourth/4.5/fifth examples) are broad capabilities shorthand, not a universal legal classification.

Strategic assessment

  • China's J-20/FC-31/J-35 developments motivate capability planning; source's Pakistan J-35 “acquisition” should not imply verified delivery/operational induction as of this issue.
  • Opportunities: aerospace R&D, skilled employment, supplier ecosystem, composites/avionics/propulsion learning, dual-use spillovers and reduced long-term import dependence. Foreign engines still create supply/technology dependencies.
  • Challenges: engine maturity, stealth integration, testing/certification, funding, transparent procurement and public/private production capability. Stage-gated milestones, realistic timelines, sustained R&D and carefully structured technology collaboration matter.
  • Undated practice: statements 1 and 3 supported; statement 2 wrong because ADA leads, with competitive industry partnership—answer (c) 1 and 3 only.
