Internal Security of India

26/11 and India’s counter-terrorism architecture

Editable notes

Attack and April 2025 development

  • 26–29 November 2008: ten Lashkar-e-Taiba terrorists attacked Mumbai after maritime infiltration; targets included CST, Taj/Oberoi hotels and Nariman House. 166 victims died, not “over 170 victims”; counting slain attackers separately changes totals.
  • 10 April 2025: NIA secured Tahawwur Hussain Rana's extradition from the US under the bilateral treaty following judicial proceedings. Extradition permits prosecution; it is not itself an Indian conviction. NIA release.

Security reforms

LayerReform and function
CoastNavy overall maritime-security responsibility; Coast Guard coastal coordination with marine/state police; patrols, radar/sensor networks and vessel identification
IntelligenceStrengthen IB's Multi-Agency Centre (MAC), State links and continuous actionable intelligence-sharing; MAC existed before 26/11
Investigation/lawNIA Act 2008 and UAPA amendments; NIA investigates scheduled offences through statutory processes, not a general replacement for State policing
Rapid responseNSG hubs, trained State quick-response units, better weapons, communications and deployment logistics
CooperationCross-border evidence, extradition, terror-financing action and diplomatic pressure
  • Four early NSG hubs: Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, operationalised 30 June–1 July 2009; not a timeless claim that NSG has only four hubs. Operation Black Tornado relates to the Mumbai response. Government account.
  • MHA's 2018–19 report says vessels above 20 metres length were mandated to carry AIS. That is a length-based source-era rule, not 20 tonnes, nor proof every small fishing boat is covered/tracked. AIS/radar/data fusion complement patrols; transmission gaps remain. MHA report.
  • Coastal Surveillance Network combines radar, AIS and day/night cameras; State police and fishing communities remain essential. Coast Guard.

Gaps and strategic assessment

  • Local police capacity, intelligence-to-action coordination, small-vessel identification, interoperable communications, cyber intelligence and accountability need continued investment.
  • Proposed NCTC encountered federalism/State-policing objections and did not become the established institution described by its proposal.
  • Pakistan entered FATF increased monitoring in June 2018, and was removed in October 2022 following action-plan progress. This multilateral AML/CFT assessment was not an automatic punishment solely for 26/11. FATF.
  • Strategic restraint after 26/11 and later Uri 2016/Balakot 2019 responses are separate decisions. The earlier restraint does not automatically establish legal justification for every later use of force; assess evidence, proportionality, deterrence and escalation.
