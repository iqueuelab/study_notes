Internal Security of India

Border Security Force: mandate and capabilities

Editable notes

Origin and role

  • BSF raised 1 December 1965 after the India–Pakistan war; central border-guarding force under MHA, governed by BSF Act 1968. “First line of defence”; motto Duty Unto Death.
  • Guards primarily India–Pakistan and India–Bangladesh international borders, prevents infiltration/smuggling/transnational crime and supports the Army in war; deployments also include LoC duties alongside Army and internal-security/anti-Naxal assignments. CAPF is not interchangeable with the regular military Armed Forces.
  • Logo: National Emblem, grain ears and BSF lettering/motto. Source calls it one of seven CAPFs: MHA listings often group CAPFs, Assam Rifles and NSG, while narrower classifications differ; name the force rather than infer all seven have identical command structures.

Capability and legal limits

  • Air/water wings, artillery and specialised units support varied terrain; camel/dog units aid border operations. Tear Smoke Unit produces riot-control munitions. Leadership is a Director General, normally an IPS officer.
  • Source 186 battalions /2.57 lakh personnel is an older snapshot, not a May 2025 verified strength: official parliamentary broadcaster's December 2023 description gives 193 battalions including four disaster-management battalions, over 2.65 lakh personnel; sanctioned and deployed strengths differ. Sansad TV profile.
  • Notified powers include specified arrest/search/seizure under relevant passport, customs, NDPS and arms laws; scope depends on law, notification and geography. They do not confer unlimited nationwide policing or replace State police responsibility for ordinary crimes.
  • May issue links BSF's border role to India–Pakistan escalation; preparedness, locally informed surveillance, force coordination and accountable law enforcement matter alongside infrastructure. Avoid assigning a precise contemporary troop deployment from a general profile.
