Remount and Veterinary Corps: Army animals and high-altitude support

Editable notes

Republic Day 2026 and military animals

  • The Army's Remount and Veterinary Corps (RVC) breeds/rears, trains and provides veterinary care for military animals. Horses support mounted/ceremonial functions, mules/ponies carry supplies in difficult high-altitude terrain, and dogs detect explosives, track, guard and assist search/rescue; the human Army Medical Corps has a different remit.
  • Official Republic Day preview describes the Him Yodhas animal contingent with Bactrian camels, Zanskar ponies, black kites and Indian dog breeds (Mudhol Hound, Rampur Hound, Chippiparai, Kombai and Rajapalayam). Sensors/protective equipment illustrate how animal capabilities complement modern surveillance and logistics. Defence Ministry preview, 25 January.
  • Source history: origins in Bengal's Stud Department (1779); Army Veterinary Corps in 1920; present RVC name after separation of Military Farms in 1960; Meerut centre/headquarters tradition. Motto: Pashu Seva Asmakam Dharma—service to animals is our duty; President's Flag awarded 1989. The PDF's 1950 name should not be used to erase the intermediate Remount, Veterinary and Farms organisation.
  • Veterinary prevention/treatment, fitness and animal welfare sustain operational readiness. RVC also supports equestrian training, remote-area veterinary assistance, peacekeeping and cooperation with friendly countries. Source's “first time” claim concerns a particular composite parade contingent, not the first historical appearance of Army animals or dog squads.
  • The five-option corps question is practice, without an authentic CSE year; answer RVC.
