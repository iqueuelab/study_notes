# India–Bangladesh border fencing

## Border map and incident

- **4,096.7 km**, India's longest land boundary with a neighbour; **West Bengal, Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram**.
- Plains, hills, forests and rivers: source's claim of **no natural obstacles** conflicts with its riverine-terrain discussion.
- January 2025 disputes: **Kaliachak III, Malda** and **Mekhliganj/Cooch Behar near Dahagram–Angarpota**, **10 January** incident; diplomatic summons **13 January**. **BSF** versus **Border Guard Bangladesh (BGB)**.
- Source fencing **3,141 km** and Bengal **81.5% as of 2023** are dated; not full fencing. About **900 km riverine stretch** cited, physical fencing difficult; surveillance alternatives vary by terrain.

## Agreements and competing claims

- **1975 joint guidelines**, **150-yard** restriction concerning defence structures; disagreement over classification of wire/single-row fencing and agreed exceptions. Don't turn either party's legal claim into a settled finding of breach.
- India: fence deters illicit movement/cattle smuggling/trafficking; Bangladesh: proximity and CCTV/smart-surveillance objections. A fence does not itself change the international boundary.
- Challenges: land acquisition, settlements/cross-border access, difficult terrain, pending consent/negotiations; source blames State non-cooperation, but this is an attributed explanation, not proof of sole responsibility.
- Solution: joint surveys/flag meetings, documented consent where required, phased land/access arrangements, sensitive riverine monitoring and proportionate enforcement respecting residents' rights.
