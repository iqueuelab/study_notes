# Defence exports: production, innovation and strategic autonomy

## Dated figures: keep the measures separate
| Measure | Period and figure |
| --- | --- |
| Defence exports | **FY 2024–25: ₹23,622 crore**, up **12.04%** over FY 2023–24 |
| Private-sector exports | FY 2024–25: ₹15,233 crore |
| DPSU exports | FY 2024–25: ₹8,389 crore; growth 42.85% |
| Export authorisations | FY 2024–25: 1,762, against 1,507 previously |
- Exports measure sales abroad; **production** measures output, and the defence **budget** measures government allocation. They cannot be interchanged. [MoD, 1 April 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2117348).
- FY 2023–24 production was about **₹1.27 lakh crore**; exports were ₹21,083 crore. CareEdge's FY24–FY29 growth rates in the source are forecasts, not realised growth or government guarantees. [MoD background](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2114546).
- **₹50,000 crore exports by 2029** is a target. Source recipient percentages and a fixed 60:40 private/DPSU split lack a compatible current denominator; do not memorise them as FY 2024–25 facts.
## Why it matters
- Domestic design/manufacturing can strengthen supply security, strategic autonomy, jobs, skills and foreign-exchange earnings; export partnerships can support defence diplomacy.
- Electronics, metallurgy and precision engineering gain spillovers. Domestic assembly does not by itself eliminate dependence on imported engines, sensors, components or intellectual property.
## Verified policy tools
- Simplified online export-authorisation processing; indigenisation through **SRIJAN** and positive indigenisation lists; innovation through **iDEX** and DRDO's **Technology Development Fund**.
- Defence industrial corridors: **Uttar Pradesh and Tamil Nadu**; strategic partnerships aim to develop domestic capability through selected collaborations.
- Defence FDI: up to **74% automatic route** for companies seeking new industrial licences; above that and up to 100% through government approval subject to conditions. Investment ceilings do not waive security review or export controls. [Parliamentary reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/254/AU669.pdf?source=pqars).
- **TUFS is a textile-sector scheme**, not a general defence modernisation scheme. The source's “India Defence Mart”, generic “SPDE” and defence “Export Promotion Council” labels should not replace verified official mechanisms.
## Constraints and way forward
- Long testing/procurement cycles, finance and working-capital constraints, limited critical technologies, certification and after-sales support challenge expansion.
- Invest in R&D, predictable procurement, start-up/MSME finance, quality assurance, competitive supply chains and lifecycle maintenance; improve coordination between defence and industrial-policy institutions.
- Judge success by indigenous technology depth, reliability and sustainable customer support, alongside export value; published forecasts and start-up counts need dates and definitions.
