# UAPA: security powers and safeguards

## Statutory distinctions
- **Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967**, MHA: unlawful activities/associations and terrorist offences; amendments include **2004, 2008, 2019**. TADA lapsed **1995**, POTA repealed **2004**; separate histories.
- Terrorist acts, financing, proceeds/property and organisational activity; law has specified extraterritorial application, not limited only to citizens inside India.
- **Unlawful-association declaration**, **terrorist-organisation listing** and **individual terrorist designation** are different processes. **2019** enabled individual designation; administrative designation is **not itself criminal conviction or an automatic detention order**.
- NIA investigates scheduled offences under its own Act; state police also have roles. Source graphic: 2019 permits **NIA Inspector-or-above** investigation in relevant circumstances and **NIA DG** approval for seizure/attachment where NIA investigates; not DGP-only for every case. [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/1470?sam_handle=123456789%2F1362).
## Custody and rights traps
- UAPA prosecution/remand is **not the same legal framework as preventive detention**. Court may extend the relevant investigation period **from 90 to 180 days subject to conditions**; not automatic indefinite custody or extensions beyond 180 merely 'after informing court'.
- Strict bail conditions, including **section 43D(5)** for relevant terrorist offences; punishments vary by offence and consequences. Graphic's 'death/life for all' and 'absolute government powers' are overstatements.
- **Article 22(1)** and written grounds of arrest: the graphic reports a police argument, not settled law. Supreme Court affirmed written grounds in **Prabir Purkayastha, 2024**; see its reaffirmation in [Supreme Court judgment](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2024/56321/56321_2024_9_1502_65444_Judgement_06-Nov-2025.pdf).
## Mains evaluation
- Need: terrorism/secession threats and disrupted financing; risks: lengthy incarceration, vague application, chilling speech and misuse allegations.
- Treat allegations involving activists/journalists/students as allegations; peaceful dissent is not by itself proof of terrorism.
- Safeguards: precise statutory grounds, evidentiary scrutiny, lawful arrest/remand, independent oversight, timely trials, reasoned review and accountability. Security and fundamental rights require effective institutions.

## September 2025: tribunal review of association bans
- Embedded clipping reports **two UAPA tribunals upheld bans on Awami Action Committee (AAC), headed by Mirwaiz Umar Farooq, and Jammu and Kashmir Ittihadul Muslimeen (JKIM), led by Masroor Abbas Ansari**. Tribunal headed by Justice Sachin Datta found sufficient cause for the March 2025 unlawful-association notifications under **section 3(1)**.
- Tribunal confirmation addresses statutory association-ban review, not criminal conviction of every member. Allegations of secessionist links/terror support cited by the government and tribunal must not become assumptions about all political dissent.
- Pages 6–8 repeat UAPA basics; existing notes distinguish preventive detention from criminal remand, individual terrorist designation from arrest/conviction, and the conditional **90-to-180-day investigation extension**. The 2019 change enabled individual terrorist listing; do not infer it made every security allegation automatically terrorism.
- **2019 mains PYQ (250 words, 15 marks)**: evaluate UAPA/NIA amendments against the security setting and rights objections. Discuss individual designation, NIA investigation/seizure powers, financing and cross-border threats alongside evidence, due process, bail/trial delay and independent oversight.
- Original **11 March 2025** government declarations specified **five-year bans** under section 3(1); confirmation is a later review step. [MHA declaration record](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2110470&lang=2&reg=48).
