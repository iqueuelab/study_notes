Internal Security of India

Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre (I4C)

Editable notes

Institution and April 2025 context

  • I4C, New Delhi, coordinates the national response to cybercrime under MHA: online financial fraud, phishing, ransomware, cyberbullying and extremist misuse of cyberspace. Scheme approved 5 October 2018; became an attached office on 1 July 2024. The issue's headline should not imply that attachment began in April 2025. MHA.
  • Nodal coordination among police/other law-enforcement agencies, industry and academia; threat intelligence, forensic/R&D tools, training, cyber hygiene and recommendations on legal/policy changes. International cybercrime cooperation operates with the competent MHA authorities; I4C is not a replacement for the entire MLAT machinery.

  • Component names can evolve: MHA's current list calls the investigation-support component National Digital Investigation Support Centre (N-DISC); retain the table as the issue's framework rather than assume permanent naming.
  • 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in enable rapid reporting; Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System links police/banks/intermediaries to intercept fraud proceeds. Reporting promptly helps preserve evidence and funds but does not guarantee recovery. CyberDost and Cyber Crime Volunteers support awareness/public participation.

Assessment

  • Digital payments, banking and data flows make cybercrime an economic and security concern; online propaganda/funding can intersect with terrorism. DPDP Act 2023 concerns personal-data protection and is not a substitute for criminal investigation.
  • Constraints: underreporting, shame/fear, cross-border jurisdiction, uneven district skills/labs, scarce resources and fast-changing AI/deepfake/crypto-enabled offences.
  • Strengthen district training, state forensic labs linked to national support, inter-state/cross-border SOPs, rapid bank coordination, research grants and school/college cyber-hygiene education. Coordination is an institutional strength; claims of universal rapid redressal or an inevitable largest cyber-police force are aspirations, not measured achievements.

June 27 enforcement headline

  • Source lists nine arrests by CBI in a cybercrime crackdown. This is an agency action reported in the contents; CBI's investigation and I4C's coordination are different institutional roles. No further case detail is supplied here.

Digital-arrest fraud

  • Fraudsters impersonate police/CBI/ED through calls or video, fabricated warrants/courtrooms and AI voices; allege money-laundering/drug offences, isolate victims and demand transfers for “bail” or “clearance”. Digital arrest is not a lawful arrest procedure.
  • Break contact, verify agencies independently and report promptly through 1930/cybercrime.gov.in; preserve call, account and transaction evidence. Never transfer funds merely to “prove innocence”. Isolation, fear and social stigma are vulnerabilities exploited by criminals.
  • I4C supports interstate investigation, threat analytics, training and bank/platform coordination; it does not supplant state police jurisdiction. The clipping reports a life sentence in one case, not a universal statutory penalty for every online fraud.
