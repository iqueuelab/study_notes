Internal Security of India

Left-wing extremism: security and governance

Editable notes

Roots and geography

  • Violent Maoist/Naxalite insurgency challenges constitutional authority; Naxalbari uprising, West Bengal, 1967 is the historical origin reference. Ordinary lawful left political activity is distinct from armed extremism.
  • Source's historical Red Corridor includes parts of Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha, Bihar, West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh and Kerala; extent changes over time, so an old map is not a current affected-district list.
  • Jal–Jungle–Jameen grievances: land dispossession, forest/resource access, exploitation, exclusion, weak local services and representation; insurgent coercion and ideology interact with these conditions.

January 2025 trigger

  • Embedded newspaper reports a 6 January 2025 Bijapur IED attack, killing eight security personnel and a civilian driver, on personnel returning from Abujhmad operations; reported incident details remain dated.
  • Forest terrain, intelligence gaps, illicit arms and route-security risks make operations difficult; falling aggregate violence does not eliminate local threat.

Way forward

  • Coordinated Centre/state planning, trained local police, intelligence sharing, lawful operations and protection of civilians.
  • Land/forest rights, accountable services, education/jobs, local recruitment, democratic representation and credible surrender/rehabilitation routes build trust.
  • Source 2021 Peace March, 'Bastar maange hinsa se azadi', illustrates community demand for freedom from violence.
  • Evaluate outcomes through sustainable safety and access to justice/services, not only encounters or area-clearing claims.

Development and democratic legitimacy

  • Naxalbari uprising, West Bengal, 1967 is the Indian movement's historical anchor; violent extremist organisations differ from lawful left-wing politics.
  • Land/forest rights, displacement, weak services and representation can create grievances; poverty or tribal identity does not mechanically cause insurgency.
  • SAMADHAN (2017): Smart leadership; Aggressive strategy; Motivation/training; Actionable intelligence; Dashboard-based KPIs/KRAs; Harnessing technology; Action plan for each theatre; No access to financing.
  • Security operations must coexist with lawful policing, Centre–State coordination, local recruitment, PESA/FRA implementation, public services and credible rehabilitation.
  • Reported encounter totals are attributed official/news counts, not by themselves proof every death was lawful or that extremism has ended. Accountability and trust affect durable outcomes.

Source infographic: past attacks, not April 2025 incidents

  • 11 March 2017: reported Sukma attack killed 12 CRPF personnel; 24 April 2017: 25 CRPF personnel killed at Burkapal, Sukma.
  • 27 October 2018: reported Bijapur IED attack killed four CRPF personnel; 9 May 2020: a police sub-inspector killed in a Bijapur encounter.
  • 22 March 2020: reported Sukma/Minpa attack killed 17 state-police personnel; 23 March 2021: five DRG personnel killed in a Narayanpur bus IED attack.
  • These dated examples illustrate ambush/IED and movement-security risks. Incident casualty figures are the infographic's reported counts; they do not establish today's violence levels or justify collective blame of tribal communities.
  • SAMADHAN (2017) remains a combined leadership, training, intelligence, technology, theatre-planning and financing-denial doctrine. Sustainable safety requires lawful operations alongside land/forest rights, local services, democratic participation and rehabilitation. MHA doctrine announcement.

June 24 security and development

  • Government's March 2026 eradication target remains a target; older Red Corridor maps are not precise June 2025 district lists.
  • National Policy and Action Plan 2015, Special Central Assistance for infrastructure/services, local policing and rehabilitation complement SAMADHAN. Greyhounds and CRPF's Bastariya Battalion illustrate terrain skills/local recruitment.
  • Source invokes the Planning Commission expert group constituted 2006, chaired by D. Bandyopadhyay (report 2008): exclusion, tribal resource loss and poor governance require rights, rehabilitation and services. The Forest Conservation Act alone does not explain tribal grievances.
  • Operation Green Hunt is a common media label, not a reliable formal nationwide operation title. Poverty/tribal identity do not justify collective suspicion; lawful policing and accountability matter.
  • Adjacent 2009 Deepak Parekh Committee PYQ concerns financing infrastructure, not minority socio-economic surveys.

Salwa Judum judgment and judicial independence

  • August clipping reports 18 retired judges objected to Home Minister Amit Shah's comments on Justice B. Sudershan Reddy, who co-authored the 2011 Nandini Sundar v State of Chhattisgarh judgment. Their statement warns misrepresentation/personal attacks may chill judicial independence; the assertion LWE would otherwise have ended before 2020 is political counterfactual, not an established result.
  • Salwa Judum began in Chhattisgarh in 2005. Court rejected use of inadequately trained tribal youth as armed SPOs for anti-Maoist operations, invoking Articles 14 and 21, and required cessation/disarming and protection/investigation. Rule of law and civilian rights constrain counterinsurgency. Supreme Court judgment.
  • Policy response still combines security, lawful policing and local intelligence with land/forest rights, rehabilitation, roads, services and trusted representation. Existing 2015 action plan, SAMADHAN, SCA, Greyhounds/Bastariya Battalion and Bandyopadhyay report notes cover the repeated body; old Red Corridor maps are not current district lists.
  • The supplied 2025 Mains question asks definition, impact on people and government measures (150 words, 10 marks). Frame March 2026 elimination as a government target; sustainable outcomes require civilian safety and inclusive development, not merely encounter totals.
