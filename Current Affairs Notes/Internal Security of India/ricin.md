# Ricin: biotoxin and biosecurity

## November 2025: suspected toxin plot
- The issue reports Gujarat ATS arrests over an alleged **ricin-related plot** and alleged ISKP links. These are investigative allegations, not convictions or evidence that a successful attack occurred.
- **Ricin** is a poisonous protein in **Ricinus communis (castor) beans** and residual castor-processing material. It enters cells and blocks protein synthesis by disabling ribosomes, causing cellular/organ injury. Properly processed castor oil is distinct from poisonous raw beans/residue.
- Exposure may occur through inhalation, ingestion or injection; symptoms/severity depend on route and dose. Respiratory distress, gastrointestinal illness and organ injury are possible. Poisoning is **not a contagious infection**. No specific antidote is established; treatment is supportive. Do not memorise one universal lethal dose or fixed symptom/death timetable. [CDC public facts](https://www.cdc.gov/chemical-emergencies/chemical-fact-sheets/ricin.html).
- A biological-origin toxin is also a **CWC Schedule 1 chemical**. Correct the sheet's “research only” exception: strictly controlled **research, medical, pharmaceutical and protective purposes** are permitted under the Convention's limits; weapon use is prohibited. India's **Chemical Weapons Convention Act 2000/National Authority** and WMD Act 2005 support relevant obligations. [CWC Act text](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1916/1/a2000__34.pdf). [OPCW schedule](https://www.opcw.org/chemical-weapons-convention/annexes/annex-chemicals/schedule-1), [permitted-purpose regime](https://www.opcw.org/chemical-weapons-convention/annexes/verification-annex/part-vi-regime-schedule-1-chemicals-and).
- **Georgi Markov, London, 1978** is the historical poisoning example. Prevention priorities are industrial-residue security, clinical recognition, laboratory readiness, coordinated reporting and public communication; technical preparation or dispersal details are unnecessary for revision.
