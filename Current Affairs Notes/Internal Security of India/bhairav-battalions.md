# Bhairav Battalions and Indian Army transformation

## January 2026: planned parade debut
- The daily issue reports that the newly raised **Bhairav Battalions** will make their Army Day Parade debut at **Jaipur on 15 January 2026**. This is a scheduled appearance as of 12 January, not a completed event. It identifies **2 Bhairav (Southern Command, Desert Falcons)** and **4 Bhairav (South Western Command)**; source war cry **Raja Ramchandra ki Jai**.
- Designed as fast, offensive, technology-enabled units bridging conventional infantry and specialised forces, with reconnaissance and precision-action capabilities beyond the immediate front. Their role should not be equated with every capability or selection standard of Para Special Forces.
- Source-reported scale at issue date: **15 raised**, plans for roughly **25**; these are dated reporting/planning figures. The source's infographic adds a lean approximately 250-person formation, mixed special-forces/infantry experience and motto **Abhayam Bhairav**; treat establishment details as source-reported, not a universal notified structure.
## Technology and integrated operations
- Emphasis on drones for surveillance, target identification and precision effects; the wider Army goal of **over 1 lakh trained drone operators** is a training ambition, not evidence all were already qualified or posted to Bhairav units.
- Broader transformation includes **Rudra Brigades**, integrating infantry, mechanised elements/tanks, artillery, special forces, UAVs and logistics, and upgrades to battlefield-management systems. Hybrid threats combine conventional combat, cyber/information operations and unmanned systems; speed, interoperability and training matter alongside equipment.
- Distinguish new battalions, integrated brigades and existing Para Special Forces as different organisational levels/functions; lessons from recent conflicts and Operation Sindoor motivate adaptation without proving every claimed operational capability.
