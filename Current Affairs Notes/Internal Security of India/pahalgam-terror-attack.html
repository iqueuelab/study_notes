Internal Security of India

Pahalgam attack and counter-terrorism strategy

Editable notes

24 April 2025: attack and strategic response

  • Terrorists attacked tourists at Pahalgam, Jammu and Kashmir, on 22 April 2025. The targeting of tourism threatens livelihoods, confidence and Kashmir’s economic integration. Government account of the site visit.
  • The source reports a responsibility claim by The Resistance Front (TRF), described as a Lashkar-e-Taiba proxy. Distinguish this contemporary attribution from completed investigation or proof of every alleged link to Pakistan’s intelligence establishment.
  • The tourist season and US Vice-President J. D. Vance’s visit gave the attack wider symbolism. Claims about perpetrators’ precise timing/motives or Pakistan’s “managed escalation” doctrine are editorial assessments, not established investigative findings.
  • Security questions: local intelligence, inter-agency coordination, surveillance-to-response gaps and protection of vulnerable tourist routes. A heavily secured region does not guarantee that every remote location is secured.
  • Deterrence: compare Parliament attack (2001), Mumbai (2008), Uri (2016), Pulwama/Balakot (2019) and the wider Kargil (1999) conflict cautiously; these are different episodes. Episodic retaliation alone cannot establish durable deterrence.
  • Proposed response: political consensus, actionable intelligence, police/rapid-response capability, disruption of financing and cyber networks, evidence-based diplomacy and calibrated coercion mindful of escalation. These are policy recommendations, not measures all already implemented.
  • Protect local livelihoods and avoid collective suspicion of Kashmiris; alienation undermines cooperation and information-sharing. Use UNSC/FATF and bilateral partnerships for counter-terrorism cooperation; G20 is a diplomatic forum rather than a sanctions authority.

1 May 2025: airspace restrictions and livelihood shock

  • India barred Pakistan-registered and Pakistani-operated/owned aircraft from its airspace after Pakistan's 24 April restriction on Indian carriers. The issue reports Indian restrictions effective from midnight 1 May, initially until 23 May. Its “Wednesday, May 1” is a calendar error: Wednesday was 30 April 2025.
  • A NOTAM communicates time-sensitive operational information to aviation users. Sovereign airspace restrictions force rerouting: longer distance → fuel and crew costs → delays, payload/refuelling constraints and possible higher fares. Effects vary by route; the source's 2–2.5-hour addition is not universal.
  • Pakistan also loses overflight revenue; the issue recalls 2019 losses and reports PIA had already avoided Indian airspace from 24 April. Historical estimates (nearly US$100 million, roughly 400 flights/day) are not audited forecasts for 2025; claims that the costs are trivial for India or necessarily worse for Pakistan are editorial judgments.
  • India's treaty decision was holding the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance, not demonstrated legal termination. Visa revocation had exceptions, including existing long-term visas for Hindu Pakistani nationals; do not read the source's “all visas” literally. Attari closure and SAARC visa restrictions formed part of the broader response.
  • Kashmir Valley received about 3.5 million tourists in 2024, approximately 1.6 times the 2012 peak (not a 160% increase). The issue reports over 80% upcoming bookings cancelled after Pahalgam; this is a contemporary reported estimate, not a final annual collapse.
  • Its 2024–25 J&K budget chart allocates about 46% of total receipts to central grants, 10% centrally sponsored schemes, 18% own taxes, 8% non-tax receipts and 17% borrowings. Borrowing is a capital/debt receipt, not revenue; rounded shares need not total exactly 100%.
  • The newspaper chart places civilians at 24% of militancy-related deaths in 2024, the highest share since 2005—not necessarily the largest absolute number. Anantnag exceeded 10% of the UT's civilian toll in seven of the preceding ten years. The clipping also reports approximately 56% civilian share in 2025 so far, including Pahalgam: a partial-year comparison.
  • Security and economic recovery reinforce each other: protect tourist routes, improve actionable local intelligence, support affected tourism workers and rebuild confidence without collective suspicion. These are policy priorities rather than achieved outcomes.

2 May 2025: deterrence and escalation debate

  • Source reports Pakistani exercises/forward deployment of Chinese SH-15 155-mm self-propelled howitzers, air defence/radars, JF-17, J-10 and F-16 aircraft and Saab airborne warning systems. Named drills Fiza-e-Badr, Lalkar-e-Momin, Zarb-e-Haidari are source-reported; equipment capability and political signalling do not establish an imminent war.
  • The LoC and International Border differ; Rajasthan's Longewala–Barmer axis is an international-border sector. China–Pakistan defence ties add to India's two-front planning requirements; alleged domestic diversion/encirclement motives are strategic interpretations.
  • Discussion between Sharat Sabharwal and Lt Gen Deependra Hooda argues for clear political objectives, precise/calibrated action and preparation for retaliation. A military “free hand” does not remove civilian political responsibility. Publicity can constrain the opponent's room for de-escalation; military force is one instrument alongside diplomacy, financial pressure and intelligence.
  • Compare Mumbai 2008 diplomatic pressure, Uri 2016 cross-LoC strikes and Pulwama/Balakot 2019 retaliation carefully: different contexts produced different escalatory dynamics. The 2021 LoC ceasefire reaffirmation reduced western-front pressures and helped attention to the China front; renewed firing creates risk rather than proving its formal termination.
  • Protect tourist livelihoods and local cooperation, improve surveillance/intelligence/rapid response, sustain diplomatic channels and evidence-based counter-terror financing work. FATF assesses financial-system deficiencies; Quad is a diplomatic grouping rather than a Pakistan-sanctions mechanism. Proposed actions in the editorial are not all completed government measures.

4 May 2025: trade, maritime and postal restrictions

  • DGFT Notification 06/2025–26, 2 May 2025: immediate prohibition on direct/indirect import or transit of goods originating in or exported from Pakistan; exceptions require prior government approval. This is an import restriction, not evidence of a blanket worldwide ban on all bilateral transactions. Commerce Ministry.
  • The clipping reports Pakistani-flagged ships barred from Indian ports, Indian-flagged ships barred from Pakistani ports, and suspension of inbound mail/parcels from Pakistan through air/surface routes. Keep these source-reported 3 May measures distinct from the earlier Attari/visa/airspace decisions.
  • Another clipping reports a survivor’s allegation that tourists were directed towards attackers by a guide. It is an investigation lead/allegation, not a proven finding against local guides as a group.

6 May 2025: civil-defence preparedness

  • After the 22 April Pahalgam attack, which killed 26, the issue reports MHA instructions for 7 May civil-defence drills: air-raid sirens, civilian/student training, crash blackouts, camouflage of vital installations and rehearsed evacuation. As of this issue these were planned exercises, not completed operations.
  • Source reports a Ferozepur cantonment 9–9:30 pm blackout rehearsal, eleven consecutive nights of LoC firing, defence consultations and cross-party condemnation. These are dated reports, not a permanent border condition. The headline's “first since 1971” requires independent corroboration and should not imply there had been no local drills since 1971.
  • Civil defence tests warning → shelter/blackout → evacuation → rescue/medical response; coordination, accessible communication and protection of vulnerable groups matter. Later verified: South Goa reported its 7 May Operation Abhyaas exercise on 8 May. District/PIB account.
  • Diplomatic measures included reducing mission strength and India's Indus Waters Treaty held in abeyance decision; “suspended” is the PDF's shorthand, not treaty termination. Pakistan's response concerning bilateral agreements/Simla should not be treated as proof the LoC automatically ceased to exist or that unilateral treaty termination was legally established.
