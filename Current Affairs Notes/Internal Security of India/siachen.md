# Siachen: geography, strategic control and altitude risks

## Map and historical anchor
- **Karakoram, northern Ladakh**; glacier near India–Pakistan–China territorial interfaces, **Saltoro Ridge** to its west, **Nubra Valley** connection southward.
- Source's 'largest glacier outside polar regions' is incorrect: NASA describes Siachen as **second-longest midlatitude glacier**, not the world's largest non-polar glacier. [NASA geography](https://eol.jsc.nasa.gov/Collections/EarthObservatory/articles/SiachenGlacier.htm).
- Source approximately **75 km** refers to glacier/sector length, not a surveyed joint 75-km India–Pakistan–China border; avoid conflating glacier length with boundary length.
- **Operation Meghdoot, 13 April 1984**, secured strategic glacier/Saltoro heights ahead of anticipated Pakistani action; not simply reclaiming territory after an already-completed Pakistani occupation. [Defence Ministry account](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2017834&lang=2&noshow=1&reg=48).
## Cold + altitude + logistics
- High posts may reach **20,000–23,000 feet**; glacier's entire length is not uniformly at these elevations. Extreme winter/source range **−20°C to −60°C** varies by place/time.
- Falling pressure → **lower oxygen partial pressure** → hypoxia; oxygen's atmospheric percentage stays broadly similar, not progressively replaced by another gas.
- Risks: altitude illness, cold injury, snowstorms/avalanches, sleep disturbance, appetite/weight loss, impaired memory and difficult casualty evacuation.
- Response: acclimatisation, suitable clothing/shelter, nutrition, medical monitoring, oxygen/evacuation capacity and reliable logistics; claiming survival is universally impossible without acclimatisation is too absolute.
- Source's recovered **Malkhan Singh**, aircraft crash **7 February 1968**, illustrates prolonged ice preservation and recovery challenges; the crash story is separate from Operation Meghdoot and Siachen's border history.
