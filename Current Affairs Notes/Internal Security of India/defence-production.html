Defence manufacturing: production, private participation and self-reliance

Editable notes

FY 2024–25 snapshot reported in August 2025

  • Total ₹1,50,590 crore; source records private output ₹33,979 crore / 22.56%, up from 19.39% in FY 2022–23 and 20.93% in FY 2023–24. 9 August PIB confirms the total and rounds shares to 23% private / 77% public, with 18% annual growth. Retain this source-period release rather than silently substitute later revised estimates. Defence Ministry.
  • Source breakdown: DPSUs 57.5%, erstwhile ordnance-factory entities 14.49%, other PSUs 5.4%, private 22.56%; rounding prevents an exact 100% sum. FY 2014–15 production ₹46,429 crore is the source's historical comparator. Source budget ₹2.53 lakh crore in FY 2013–14 → ₹6.81 lakh crore budgeted in FY 2025–26 measures expenditure allocations, not production value.
  • Make in India, procurement/indigenisation reforms, FDI changes and defence corridors can support suppliers, R&D and exports. Evaluate technology depth, delivery/quality, competition and imported content: rising rupee production alone is not complete self-reliance.
  • Correction: private defence manufacturing was opened in 2001, not 2016–17; the latter is this chart's comparison start. Thus source practice statement 2's premise is defective; do not repeat its “both correct” answer unqualified. Public-sector production remained dominant in this snapshot.

December 2025: industrial-base reform agenda

  • Editorial connects 2047 development with defence R&D, manufacturing, testing/certification, supplier/MSME networks and lifecycle support. Existing notes cover dated production/export totals, private entry in 2001, FDI and corporatisation; avoid repeating those as new December reforms.
  • New policy emphasis: predictable procurement and export licensing; faster but credible trials/certification, technology-transfer/JV approvals, competitive finance and reliable after-sales service. Global conflicts expose ammunition, spare-parts and foreign-supply vulnerabilities, while export opportunities depend on demonstrated quality and delivery.
  • Suggested institutional division: DRDO prioritises frontier research; industry scales production/commercialisation. This is an editorial reform proposal, not an order abolishing DRDO’s development/testing role.
  • Proposed professionally staffed single-window defence-export facilitation agency, integrated test facilities, export credit, government-to-government arrangements and service guarantees are recommendations; do not present the proposed agency as established.
  • ₹50,000 crore exports by 2029 remains a target already recorded. Source’s 80+ export destinations is a dated rounded description, not a new verified fixed country count. Defence production, exports, procurement and domestic technology content measure different things; imported components/IP may persist despite domestic assembly.
