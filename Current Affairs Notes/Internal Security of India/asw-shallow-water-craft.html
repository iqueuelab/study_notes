ASW shallow-water craft: Arnala and Mahe programmes

Editable notes

December 2025: Anjadip delivery and programme distinctions

  • Anjadip delivered 22 December at Chennai, third of eight GRSE ASW-SWCs. Delivery is not commissioning: the PDF's “inducted/joins Navy” heading should not become an INS commissioning date. GRSE–L&T Kattupalli collaboration, Indian Register of Shipping classification, ~77 m, waterjets and over 80% indigenous content. Defence Ministry.
  • Lightweight torpedoes, indigenous ASW rockets and shallow-water sonar enable littoral submarine detection/engagement; surveillance and mine-laying complement layered maritime defence. Name recalls Anjadip Island off Karwar and former Petya-class INS Anjadip, decommissioned 2003.
  • INS Androth, second GRSE/Arnala-class craft, commissioned 6 October 2025, Visakhapatnam, presided by Vice Admiral Rajesh Pendharkar; 77 m, ~1,500 tonnes, three diesel-driven waterjets. The PDF's two-engine description is unreliable. Commissioning release.
  • INS Arnala commissioned 18 June 2025, not June 2024; named for coastal fort off Maharashtra, not a Lakshadweep island. Overall programme comprises 16 ships: eight GRSE and eight CSL, not four GRSE plus four L&T. GRSE's construction partnership does not halve the eight-ship contract. Arnala release.
  • INS Mahe is the lead of the separate eight-ship Cochin Shipyard Mahe-class series, about 80% indigenous; Army Chief General Upendra Dwivedi presided over its November 2025 commissioning. It is background here, not a new 23 December event. Joint commissioning symbolism supports tri-service integration; proposed theatre commands should not be presented as already fully implemented.
  • Shallow-water vessels complement aircraft, submarines and larger surface ships. They improve near-shore coverage and manoeuvrability, but submarines can still exploit complex acoustic/coastal conditions; small size does not guarantee detection.
