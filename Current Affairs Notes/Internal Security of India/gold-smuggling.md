# Gold imports and smuggling: incentives and enforcement

## Economy and corrected tax context
- Jewellery, investment and cultural demand; imported gold affects the merchandise balance and forex demand. Gold has store-of-value/collateral uses; jewellery manufacture and recycling create output/jobs, so “no economic output” is too broad.
- **23 July 2024** Budget cut customs duty on gold/silver to **6%**, from the earlier 15% structure; **GST is separate**. The source's February claim of “15% including GST/customs” is stale/inconsistent with its later duty-cut paragraph. [Budget 2024 summary](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2035618&lang=2&reg=48).
- Source **750–900 tonnes imports**, **7.5% GDP**, **four million jobs**, demand shares lack consistent period/scope; do not equate the entire gems/jewellery sector with gold alone.
- Domestic production limited; **Kolar Gold Fields** is a historic closed-mine example, not a principal then-operating source. Recycling can reduce import needs.
## Why illicit trade persists
- Tax/price arbitrage, demand, laundering and organised cross-border networks; land borders, airports and coast routes create enforcement challenges.
- Fiscal leakage, hawala, corruption and possible criminal/terror-financing links; **not every gold import or smuggling case is automatically terrorism**.
- Customs/DRI, ED and financial intelligence cooperate within respective laws; **DGGI's GST role is distinct from DRI customs-smuggling investigations**.
## Response
- Proportionate duty design, risk-based inspection, legal trade traceability, anti-corruption controls and financial investigation; international information sharing improves network detection.
- **Gold Monetisation Scheme / Sovereign Gold Bonds** were policy alternatives to idle/physical gold demand; dated discussion is not proof every scheme/tranche remains open now. SGBs are securities, not the same product as unregulated “digital gold”.
- Evaluate legitimate demand, enforcement cost and revenue before recommending further tax cuts; technology cannot replace sound intelligence and due process.
