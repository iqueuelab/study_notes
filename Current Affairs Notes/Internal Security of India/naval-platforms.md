# Vaghsheer, Surat and Nilgiri

## Match the platform
| Platform | Project/class | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| INS Vaghsheer | Project 75 / Kalvari (Scorpene) | Sixth/final diesel-electric submarine |
| INS Surat | Project 15B | Fourth/final stealth destroyer |
| INS Nilgiri | Project 17A | Lead stealth frigate |
- Built at **Mazagon Dock, Mumbai**; Scorpene construction involves France's **Naval Group**.
- January 2 source anticipated commissioning on January 15. **All three were commissioned 15 January 2025**. [Official commissioning](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2092999&lang=2&reg=48).
## Capabilities and distinctions
- Vaghsheer: acoustic stealth, torpedoes, anti-ship missiles, sonar; anti-surface/submarine warfare, intelligence and surveillance. It is **conventionally powered**, not a nuclear ballistic-missile deterrent submarine.
- Surat strengthens surface/air defence; Nilgiri adds multipurpose stealth and anti-submarine capability.
- Significance: Indian Ocean security, fleet modernisation, indigenous shipbuilding, technology transfer and domestic supplier skills. Do not assume all design/IP is indigenous merely because construction occurs in India.
- The 6 January source again mentions Vaghsheer, but misprints its commissioning year as 2024; the verified date remains **15 January 2025**.

## Corrected January 16 recap
- **Nilgiri, Surat and Vaghsheer commissioned together on 15 January 2025, Naval Dockyard, Mumbai**; source's repeated **28 December 2022** dates are wrong. All three built by **MDL**, not Nilgiri by GRSE; GRSE builds other Project 17A ships.
- Nilgiri roughly **6, 670 tonnes**, Surat roughly **7, 500 tonnes** in source; surface ships feature missile/air-defence/ASW suites. Source's Nilgiri **over 70% indigenous-content** statement is approximate, not proof every component indigenous.
- Vaghsheer: conventional diesel-electric Scorpene, torpedoes/anti-ship missiles. Source incorrectly claims **AIP already fitted** and **BrahMos firing**; AIP integration is a separate retrofit programme, not commissioning configuration. [Naval Group AIP integration agreement](https://www.naval-group.com/en/drdos-fuel-cell-based-air-independent-propulsion-system-soon-be-fitted-onboard-ins-kalvari).
- Name/history links: Nilgiri commissioning rhetoric recalls **Chola maritime heritage**, Surat **Gujarat–West Asia trading ties**. Indian Ocean shipping/choke points **Hormuz, Malacca** explain fleet significance; rhetoric is not evidence of expansionist territorial policy.
- Source's **39 of 40 recent platforms made in India** is a dated political statement; **5, 000+ listed items for indigenisation** does not mean every listed item is already manufactured domestically.

## Practice-test traps
- **Surat** is the fourth/final Project 15B destroyer; a question stating only roughly 7, 500 tonnes and stealth does not uniquely distinguish it from **Visakhapatnam**, another ship of the class.
- The 19 January key incorrectly assigns BrahMos capability to **Vaghsheer**. Do not treat a Scorpène submarine as a BrahMos-equipped surface warship.

## Project-75I: January 2025 tender reporting
- Source reports **MDL + Germany's TKMS** cleared technical evaluation; **L&T + Spain's Navantia** bid unsuccessful. **Technical qualification ≠ signed contract, final price or commissioned fleet**.
- **Six conventional submarines** with **air-independent propulsion (AIP)**, distinct from existing **Project-75 Scorpene/Kalvari** boats and nuclear submarine programmes.
- Source benchmark **₹43, 000 crore**, liable to revision; indigenous-content requirement **45% first → 60% sixth**. Proven AIP performance, technology transfer, delivery schedules and lifecycle support matter.
- **Class 214 / 212CD** are referenced TKMS design families; not proof the proposed Indian derivative is already operational. Navantia's **S-81 Isaac Peral**, S-80 family, commissioned **2023**.
- AIP permits longer submerged endurance without atmospheric air for propulsion; **not unlimited endurance or conversion into a nuclear submarine**. Cost, localisation and skilled production remain challenges.

## Project 76 and P75I: February 2025 plans
| Programme | Distinction |
| --- | --- |
| P75 | Six French-design Scorpene/Kalvari boats built at MDL; sixth **Vaghsheer commissioned 15 January 2025** |
| P75I | Proposed six advanced conventional submarines with AIP, foreign partnership/Strategic Partnership framework |
| P76 | Proposed indigenous conventional-submarine design; source goal **90–95% local content**, ~3, 000 tonnes |
| SSBN /SSN | Nuclear ballistic-missile deterrent submarine /nuclear-powered attack submarine; propulsion and weapons roles differ |
- Source P76 **CCS approval in two months /2–3-year design /five-year build** and P75I **₹70, 000 crore /future delivery** are estimates, not confirmed completed contracts/schedules.
- **MDL–TKMS** reported bidder/evaluation progress does not itself constitute a signed six-boat contract; source's assertion all other candidates simply “dropped out” oversimplifies the process.
- **AIP** improves submerged endurance for conventional submarines, not unlimited endurance or nuclear propulsion. Retrofit in existing Scorpenes differs from the planned P75I requirement.
- Source “five commissioned /Vaghsheer to commission” is stale; spell **Khanderi**, not Kandheri. P75 signing/construction history differs from the broader 30-year submarine plan.
- Land-attack cruise-missile development and conventional sea denial do **not automatically confer nuclear second-strike capability**; nuclear deterrence relies on the appropriate weapons/platform system.
- Costs, engine/AIP reliability, acoustic stealth, electronics/IP, skilled workers/crews, trials and spares are constraints; **INS Satavahana** supports submarine training.
- Three additional Scorpenes and source conventional-fleet counts are dated proposals/snapshots. Indigenous percentage ambitions must be tested against design access and delivered critical systems.

## INS Tamal
- Russian-built Talwar-class follow-on frigate, Project 1135.6; 2016 arrangement covered two Russian-built and two Goa-built ships. INS Tushil commissioned 9 December 2024.
- February issue's June delivery was a forecast: **Tamal actually commissioned 1 July 2025 at Yantar, Kaliningrad**. [Navy release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2141361&lang=2&reg=48).
- Transition towards indigenous construction does not imply every component is Indian or prohibit every future import.

## Arabian Sea readiness: 28 April 2025
- After the **22 April Pahalgam attack killed 26 civilians**, the issue reports long-range precision anti-ship firings and maritime deployments; this demonstrates readiness, not proof that war or a specific retaliatory operation had begun.
- Distinguish **INS Surat's MRSAM air-defence interception** from anti-ship strikes: MRSAM attacks aerial threats, including sea-skimming targets; DRDO–Israel Aerospace Industries cooperation underpins the system. The source names Kolkata-class destroyers, Project 17A/Nilgiri frigates, Russian-origin Talwar/Krivak frigates, **INS Vikrant**, **P-8I** reconnaissance aircraft and fleet tankers. Tankers extend deployment endurance; aircraft add surveillance and targeting support.
- **Sea control** enables one's use of a specified maritime area; **sea denial** prevents an adversary's use without necessarily controlling it. Credible strike capability, surveillance and replenishment support deterrence and protection of trade/energy routes connected to **Hormuz and Bab-el-Mandeb**.
- Strategic interpretation: Pakistan crisis signalling and China's growing IOR presence/Gwadar interests make readiness relevant; claims that allies were reassured or that particular Chinese reactions followed remain analysis, not demonstrated outcomes.
- Legal precision: Article **51(c)** urges respect for international law; Article **73** concerns Union executive power and treaty/agreement rights, not a blanket licence to act anywhere at sea. India's 1976 maritime-zones legislation distinguishes sovereign territorial waters from EEZ resource rights. Navigational warnings protect shipping; the source's assertion that Pakistan issued the notice for Indian firings is unverified and should not be memorised as a legal requirement.
- Readiness does not itself establish a settled doctrine of “pre-emptive self-defence” or prove a future use of force lawful. Assess necessity/proportionality under the applicable law and civilian protection in hostilities; avoid the source's unattributed naval quotations. [UNCLOS overview](https://www.un.org/Depts/los/convention_agreements/convention_overview_convention.htm).

## Tavasya and the four-frigate programme
- **Tavasya launched22 March2025**, Goa Shipyard; the PDF's June launch date is wrong. It is the second Goa-built additional Project1135.6 frigate; **Triput launched23 July2024**. Distinguish launch, outfitting/trials and commissioning. [MoD launch release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2114025&lang=2&reg=48).
- Four-ship India–Russia arrangement: **Tushil/Tamal built in Russia**, **Triput/Tavasya at GSL** with Russian collaboration. **Ukrainian Zorya-Mashproekt turbines** show the multinational supply chain; technology transfer and local construction do not mean wholly indigenous components.
- Source's Tamal 'June2025 commissioning' is stale: its embedded June23 headline says **scheduled1July2025**. At this issue date it was a planned commissioning, not already completed; the common February note contains the subsequently verified July1 completion.

## Udaygiri delivery and Tamal commissioning
- **Udaygiri delivered 1 July 2025**, second Project 17 A ship, MDL Mumbai, **37 months after launch**. Delivery is not commissioning; at this issue date it should not be described as a commissioned INS. Previous Udaygiri served 31 years before decommissioning **24 August 2007**.
- **P 17 A=seven Nilgiri-class frigates** designed by Navy's Warship Design Bureau, successor to Shivalik/P 17; **MDL four/GRSE three**. Modular construction and pre-outfitting improve production; around 75% indigenous content does not imply every subsystem is domestic.
- Names: **Nilgiri, Udaygiri, Taragiri, Dunagiri, Himgiri, Vindhyagiri, Mahendragiri**. Armament includes supersonic SSMs, MRSAM,76-mm gun,30-mm/12.7-mm close-range systems; sensor/sonar integration supports air/surface/subsurface roles and blue-water operations.
- Tamal's **1 July commissioning in Kaliningrad** is already recorded; add that it is **eighth Project 1135.6 ship/second Tushil follow-on**, equipped for air/surface/subsurface/electronic warfare, with BrahMos. Earlier ships: **Talwar/Trishul 2003, Tabar 2004, Teg/Tarkash 2012, Trikand 2013, Tushil 2024**; source's “Tushil upcoming” is stale.
- “Last foreign-built major warship” describes this procurement transition, not a legally permanent end to all imports or proof complete technological independence. Different batches' missiles/crew/speed need variant qualification; do not apply every Tamal specification to every earlier Talwar ship.
- Project 1135.6 practice MCQ concerns **stealth frigate construction**, not a supplied PYQ.

## Himgiri delivery: 31 July 2025
- **Himgiri (Yard 3022)**, third delivered **Project 17A/Nilgiri-class** frigate and first built by **GRSE, Kolkata**, delivered 31 July. Delivery precedes commissioning.
- Predecessor **INS Himgiri**, Leander class, decommissioned **6 May 2005** after 30 years, correcting source's May 2025. [Defence Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2150864&lang=2&reg=48).
- Existing notes cover seven ships, MDL 4/GRSE 3, WDB, modular construction, indigenous supply chains and multirole warfare. Practice explanation establishes use of modular construction, not its unsupported **“first Indian naval project”** assertion.
- Embedded classification: carriers operate aircraft; destroyers/frigates multirole warfare; corvettes smaller combatants; submarines underwater; patrol, research, training, amphibious, minesweeping ships have distinct tasks. Old **Vikrant “under construction”** label is stale.

## INS Satpura and Singapore exercise
- **INS Satpura**, second **Project 17/Shivalik-class stealth frigate**, built by **Mazagon Dock, Mumbai**, commissioned **20 August 2011**; Eastern Fleet, Visakhapatnam. Named after the Satpura range.
- Source reports arrival in **Singapore for a bilateral maritime exercise**. Anti-air, anti-surface and anti-submarine roles support interoperability and maritime cooperation. It is neither a **Project 15A Kolkata-class destroyer** nor a GRSE-built frigate.

## September fleet-construction snapshot
- Issue reports **54 vessels under construction in Indian yards**, around **10 planned inductions by end-2025**, completion by 2030 and ambitions for **200-plus ships/submarines by 2035**, possibly 230 by 2037. These are dated reported counts/plans, not completed deliveries or fixed fleet forecasts.
- Indigenous construction supports skilled employment and supply chains in design, metallurgy, electronics and weapons; MDL, GRSE, GSL and HSL play roles. Delays, critical imported subsystems, finance, integration and trials remain constraints.
- **Tamal commissioned 1 July 2025** is already covered; “last foreign-built major warship” describes a procurement transition. Astradharini's **2016 PYQ answer is torpedo launch/recovery vessel (c)**, not a submarine/carrier; source recalls 2015 commissioning and replacement of Astravahini.

## INS Ikshak: commissioning, 6 November 2025
- **INS Ikshak**, third **Survey Vessel (Large)**, commissioned at **Naval Base Kochi on 6 November 2025**, with CNS **Admiral Dinesh K. Tripathi**; correct the PDF's **7 November 2024**. Built by **GRSE, Kolkata** with over **80% indigenous content**; first of the class based at Southern Naval Command. [Defence Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2186562&lang=2&noshow=1&reg=48).
- Four-ship class: **Sandhayak, Nirdeshak, Ikshak, Sanshodhak**; distinguish the first two ships' commissioning in **2024** from the source's Sandhayak 2023 error. The fourth was under construction at the issue date. GRSE leads the programme with L&T construction collaboration.
- Primary role: coastal/deep-water **hydrographic surveys**, bathymetry and charting ports, harbours/navigation channels. Sensors include **multibeam echo sounder, AUV, ROV and survey motor boats**; data supports safe naval/merchant navigation, marine science and coastal planning. Hydrographic survey vessels are distinct from destroyers/frigates/submarines.
- Source describes approximately **3,400 tonnes**, **18 knots**, **6,500-nautical-mile endurance** and around **150 personnel**, plus HADR, medical/SAR support and dedicated women's accommodation. These are reported vessel specifications, not those of every Navy platform.
- Hydrographic cooperation with Indian Ocean partners combines maritime safety, blue-economy services and defence diplomacy. Better charts support maritime domain awareness but do not themselves create new sovereignty or jurisdiction.
