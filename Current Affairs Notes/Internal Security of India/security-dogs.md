# Indigenous dog breeds in security forces

## October 2025: operational trials and training
- Source reports BSF training **150 Mudhol and Rampur hounds** for border/security tasks; treat this as a dated training cohort, not proof all have passed trials or replaced imported working dogs.
- **Mudhol Hound:** Karnataka; agile, lean, high stamina and hunting/tracking heritage. Issue links Army training at **Remount and Veterinary Corps, Meerut**, from 2016 and trials with **15/16 Corps**. **Rampur Hound:** Uttar Pradesh, sighthound associated with Rampur's breeding history, speed and endurance.
- Roles include patrol/tracking, search and rescue, explosives/narcotics detection and counter-insurgency support. Performance requires task-specific scent training, handler bonding, veterinary care, fitness and evaluation; speed or indigenous origin alone does not establish detection competence.
- Belgian Malinois, German Shepherd, Labrador and Bloodhound serve different working roles; avoid treating every force's breed mix as identical. Indigenous breeds may reduce some procurement/maintenance dependence, but cold tolerance, health, breeding diversity and lifelong welfare remain practical constraints.

## January 2026: planned Republic Day animal contingent
- **Remount and Veterinary Corps (RVC)** planned a specially curated contingent: **2 Bactrian camels, 4 Zanskar ponies, 4 raptors, 10 Indian-breed Army dogs and 6 conventional military dogs**. This is a pre-parade announcement, not proof the parade had already occurred. RVC breeds, trains, treats and supports operational animals. [Akashvani](https://newsonair.gov.in/indian-army-animal-contingent-to-march-on-kartavya-path-during-republic-day-parade-2026/).
- Cold-adapted **two-humped Bactrian camels in Ladakh** assist high-altitude patrol/logistics; **Zanskar ponies** support mountain supply lines. Their presence in Ladakh does not make the Bactrian camel species endemic to India. Source load/endurance figures (**camels up to 250 kg; ponies 40–60 kg, 15,000 ft, −40°C, up to 70 km/day**) are reported capabilities dependent on conditions, not universal safe working limits.
- **Mudhol, Rampur, Chippiparai, Kombai and Rajapalayam** are indigenous-breed examples; the source does not establish all are the ten selected parade dogs. Existing notes cover tracking, mine/explosive detection, rescue and Meerut training. Raptors are reported for surveillance/bird-strike control; the generic birds-of-prey graphic does not identify the four parade birds.
- Animals complement vehicles, drones and sensors in difficult terrain; reliable handlers, feed, veterinary care and welfare remain necessary. “No fuel” does not mean no logistical footprint or undetectability.
