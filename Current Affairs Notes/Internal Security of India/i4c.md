# Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre (I4C)

## Institution and April 2025 context
- **I4C**, New Delhi, coordinates the national response to cybercrime under **MHA**: online financial fraud, phishing, ransomware, cyberbullying and extremist misuse of cyberspace. Scheme approved **5 October 2018**; became an **attached office on 1 July 2024**. The issue's headline should not imply that attachment began in April 2025. [MHA](https://www.mha.gov.in/en/divisionofmha/cyber-and-information-security-cis-division).
- Nodal coordination among police/other law-enforcement agencies, industry and academia; threat intelligence, forensic/R&D tools, training, cyber hygiene and recommendations on legal/policy changes. International cybercrime cooperation operates with the competent MHA authorities; I4C is not a replacement for the entire MLAT machinery.
## Components and citizen access
| Source's seven-component framework | Role |
| --- | --- |
| National Cybercrime Threat Analytics Unit | Analyse threats and issue intelligence/alerts |
| National Cybercrime Reporting Portal | Online reporting, including focus on women/children |
| National Cybercrime Training Centre | Investigator capacity building |
| National Cybercrime Research and Innovation Centre | Tools and indigenous research |
| Joint Cybercrime Coordination/Investigation Team platform | Cross-state operational coordination |
| Cybercrime Ecosystem Management Unit | Stakeholder coordination and awareness |
| National Cybercrime Forensic Laboratory | Digital evidence support |
- Component names can evolve: MHA's current list calls the investigation-support component **National Digital Investigation Support Centre (N-DISC)**; retain the table as the issue's framework rather than assume permanent naming.
- **1930** and **cybercrime.gov.in** enable rapid reporting; Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System links police/banks/intermediaries to intercept fraud proceeds. Reporting promptly helps preserve evidence and funds but **does not guarantee recovery**. **CyberDost** and Cyber Crime Volunteers support awareness/public participation.
## Assessment
- Digital payments, banking and data flows make cybercrime an economic and security concern; online propaganda/funding can intersect with terrorism. **DPDP Act 2023** concerns personal-data protection and is not a substitute for criminal investigation.
- Constraints: underreporting, shame/fear, cross-border jurisdiction, uneven district skills/labs, scarce resources and fast-changing AI/deepfake/crypto-enabled offences.
- Strengthen district training, state forensic labs linked to national support, inter-state/cross-border SOPs, rapid bank coordination, research grants and school/college cyber-hygiene education. Coordination is an institutional strength; claims of universal rapid redressal or an inevitable largest cyber-police force are aspirations, not measured achievements.

## June 27 enforcement headline
- Source lists **nine arrests by CBI in a cybercrime crackdown**. This is an agency action reported in the contents; CBI's investigation and I4C's coordination are different institutional roles. No further case detail is supplied here.

## Digital-arrest fraud
- Fraudsters impersonate police/CBI/ED through calls or video, fabricated warrants/courtrooms and AI voices; allege money-laundering/drug offences, isolate victims and demand transfers for “bail” or “clearance”. **Digital arrest is not a lawful arrest procedure.**
- Break contact, verify agencies independently and report promptly through **1930/cybercrime.gov.in**; preserve call, account and transaction evidence. Never transfer funds merely to “prove innocence”. Isolation, fear and social stigma are vulnerabilities exploited by criminals.
- I4C supports interstate investigation, threat analytics, training and bank/platform coordination; it does not supplant state police jurisdiction. The clipping reports a life sentence in one case, not a universal statutory penalty for every online fraud.

## November 2025: Supreme Court attention to digital-arrest fraud
- The issue reports Supreme Court concern over **more than ₹3,000 crore** lost and an **Ambala elderly couple** targeted using fake police/judicial identities. Treat this as the hearing's reported aggregate, without assuming a defined annual loss series or that every victim was elderly. Its **November 2024** context label is wrong: the suo motu case arose from a **21 September 2025 complaint** about fraud between **3–16 September 2025**. [Supreme Court, 17 October 2025 order](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/59769/59769_2025_2_7_65003_Order_17-Oct-2025.pdf).
- Fake warrants/courtrooms, video calls, voice cloning and deepfakes exploit authority/fear; threatened “bail” or “clearance” payments are extortion, not valid digital arrest. Cross-border scam compounds can combine money laundering with trafficking/forced criminality; affected Indians abroad may also be victims.
- As of this issue, **stronger orders/CBI involvement are under consideration**; do not import a later final investigation order. Existing criminal-law provisions cover cheating, extortion, forgery/conspiracy alongside relevant IT Act provisions: AI involvement does not mean there is no applicable criminal law. DPDP legislation is not a substitute for fraud investigation.
- Revision priorities: rapid bank/police/platform coordination, fund tracing and evidence preservation; specialised investigators; victim-sensitive support; cross-border cooperation and prevention of deceptive overseas recruitment. Existing notes retain **1930/cybercrime.gov.in**, I4C functions and independent verification advice.

## 1 December 2025: nationwide digital-arrest investigation
- The issue reports the Supreme Court entrusting a coordinated pan-India **digital-arrest investigation to CBI**, including investigation of bankers facilitating mule accounts, and asking States to accord **Section 6, Delhi Special Police Establishment Act 1946 consent**. This advances the earlier hearing-stage consideration recorded in these notes. The PDF's “overrules State consent” shorthand should not obscure its own direction that States provide consent; it does not repeal Section 6 or abolish consent requirements for every future CBI case.
- **Digital arrest → impersonation/extortion**; **investment fraud → promised returns followed by staged deposits/advance-tax demands**; **part-time-task fraud → small initial rewards followed by large paid ‘premium’ tasks**. Immediate investigation priority is digital arrest; consideration of monitoring other categories is prospective, not proof all investment/job cases were already transferred.
- Reported directions cover **CBI–Interpol cooperation**, State/regional cybercrime coordination linked with I4C, preservation/sharing of platform records and stronger telecom KYC/SIM controls. RBI was asked to respond on AI/ML detection of suspicious accounts and money layering: a request for a response is not proof a new automated freezing system already exists.
- The ₹3,000-crore loss figure repeats the earlier hearing's reported aggregate; do not turn it into a measured December-only loss. Prevention and rapid evidence/fund preservation remain essential alongside criminal investigation. [Supreme Court order, 1 December 2025, reproduced](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/67470969/).
