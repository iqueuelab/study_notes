Lactase persistence and gene–culture coevolution

Editable notes

Concepts and the March 2025 study

  • Lactase breaks milk sugar lactose → glucose + galactose. Activity usually falls after weaning; adult persistence varies genetically. Malabsorption may cause gas/bloating/diarrhoea but is distinct from milk allergy and not every low-lactase individual has identical symptoms.
  • Different regulatory variants supporting the same adult trait illustrate convergent evolution. Dairying can alter selection pressures: gene–culture coevolution; adoption of livestock and spread of alleles need not occur simultaneously in every population.
  • PNAS, 10 March 2025: identified an East Asian-specific Neanderthal-derived haplotype near LCT, apparently positively selected before dairying. It does not prove that this haplotype causes East Asian lactase persistence; phenotype samples were unavailable and regulatory/immune-cell associations are indirect. Immune-function selection is a hypothesis. Original study.
  • Study estimates selection timing varied: approximately 24,500–27,000 years ago in Han populations, later in the analysed Vietnamese group; the source's blanket “over 30,000 years” is inaccurate. Ancient DNA includes an Amur-region individual ~14,000 years old. Reported 28.9% denotes haplotype carriers, not a demonstrated milk-tolerance rate.
  • Introgression = genetic material entering a population through interbreeding. Neanderthal ancestry varies; blanket “Africans have zero” and a universal 80,000–120,000-year admixture date oversimplify complex evidence. Do not equate age of a sequenced Neanderthal individual with date of admixture.
  • Takeaway: natural selection at a locus can reflect multiple functions; physiological adaptation, haplotype frequency and cultural practices require separate evidence. The finding complicates, rather than disproves, gene–culture coevolution.
