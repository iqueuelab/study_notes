# Kandha communities: identity, rights and gender change

## Identity and livelihood
- **Kandha/Khond/Kondh** communities concentrated in Odisha—**Kandhamal, Rayagada, Koraput, Ganjam, Kalahandi**—with related communities in neighbouring States. **Kui/Kuwi** are Dravidian languages; subgroup geography/language varies rather than one uniform identity.
- Scheduled Tribe identification operates under **Article 342**, through State/UT-specific notified lists; the Article does not by itself provide one universal all-India community entry.
- Forest produce (**mahua, sal seeds, honey, tamarind**), farming, livestock and historically **podu/shifting cultivation** support livelihoods; MGNREGS/other employment diversify income. Not all households practise shifting cultivation or traditional hunting.
- Source reports **~1.4 million /17% of Odisha ST population** as Census 2011 but provides no precise notified-category table; treat as an unverified demographic claim rather than memorise the mismatched rounded baseline. Education/health disadvantages need subgroup- and sex-disaggregated data.

## Tattooing: local oral history and transformation
- **May 2025 Kandhamal/Daringbadi case study:** older women describe painful geometric facial tattooing near **age ten**, undertaken by experienced older women, with needle injury, infections and lengthy recovery. The newspaper records one survivor's **two-month recovery**; not a standard medical duration for every case.
- Community recollections explain the practice as deterrence against exploitation by landlords/rulers/colonial agents; over time it became associated with womanhood, marriageability and belonging. This is **reported oral history**, not a proven uniform origin for every Kandha subgroup.
- Ear-rim silver ornaments also communicate marital/community identity in the described locality. Source describes patriarchal/patrilineal norms, indigenous medicine/folklore and worship of **Dharani Penu**, earth deity; **Kedu** ritual and historic **Meriah** practices vary and should not stereotype contemporary communities.
- Decline since **mid-1990s**, linked to education, awareness and sustained family counselling; report says facial tattoos are rare below age 40 in the community surveyed. It does not prove the practice is extinct across all communities.
- Older survivors welcome younger girls' freedom from coercive tattooing; modern voluntary fashion tattoos have a different consent/social context. **Preserve cultural identity while protecting bodily autonomy and child welfare**; community-led reform need not erase language, folklore or collective belonging.

## Rights and development
- Risks: land alienation, mining/project displacement, forest degradation, exploitative credit/intermediaries, weak health/education/sanitation and climate-sensitive livelihoods. **Niyamgiri issues particularly involve Dongria Kondh**; do not equate every Kandha community with one subgroup or project.
- **FRA 2006:** recognition of qualifying individual/community forest rights; **PESA 1996:** Gram Sabha powers in **Fifth Schedule areas**, not automatically every place inhabited by tribal people. Tribal-development earmarking and **Odisha Tribal Empowerment and Livelihood Programme** support development; statutory schemes do not themselves prove secure title or service delivery.
- Priorities: consent-sensitive services, girls' education, recognition of rights, fair market/credit access, habitat protection and community participation. Avoid framing a living community solely through historic sacrifice or traumatic customs.
