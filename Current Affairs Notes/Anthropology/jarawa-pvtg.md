# Jarawa and Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups

## Jarawa: habitat and protection
- Indigenous **Middle and South Andaman** community; a **PVTG**, traditionally hunting, gathering and fishing in tropical forests/coastal habitats. Contact with outsiders increased in the late 1990s; infection exposure, poaching, encroachment, tourism and the Andaman Trunk Road threaten habitat and autonomy.
- The graphic identifies their language as **Ongan**; do not repeat the text's claim that it is unrelated to every other Andamanese language. Claims of descent from the extinct Jangil or a precisely dated migration are not established here. Source population **250–400** is an undated estimate, not a verified July 2025 census.
- Graphic reports **19 Jarawa members enrolled on electoral rolls**; inclusion is distinct from compulsory assimilation or loss of habitat protections. Protect voluntary contact, culturally appropriate healthcare, land/resource access and community consent. [Government Jarawa policy](https://tribal.nic.in/downloads/PVTG/The%20Andaman%20And%20Nicobar%20Gazette%20dated%2021-12-2004-Policy%20on%20Jarawa%20Tribe%20of%20Andaman%20Islands.pdf).
## PVTG recall
- **75 groups across 18 States and one UT**, Andaman & Nicobar Islands. Core identification characteristics: pre-agricultural technology, stagnant/declining population, very low literacy and subsistence/economic backwardness. Remoteness increases vulnerability but is not a substitute for the core criteria. [Ministry of Tribal Affairs](https://tribal.nic.in/PM-JANMAN.aspx).
- Examples: Jarawa, Great Andamanese, Onge, Sentinelese; Birhor/Asur; Baiga; Saharia; **Irular and Konda Reddi**. Source village/district/population totals lack a common dated survey basis and should not be memorised as an exact current count.
- **2019 PYQ:** statements **1, 2 and 4** correct; **95 groups** is false. Supplied page lacks the answer-option list, so the incomplete question is flagged rather than reconstructed.
