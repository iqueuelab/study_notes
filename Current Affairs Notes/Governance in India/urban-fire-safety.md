# Urban fire safety and building-code enforcement

## 18–19 May 2025: urban fire reports
- Embedded Hyderabad clipping reports **17 family members killed, including eight children**, near Charminar; **17 rescued**. Cause was **under investigation** in that report, not a proven electrical fault. A separate Maharashtra garment-unit clipping reported **eight deaths**.
- PDF cites NCRB ADSI **2022: 7,435 deaths from over 7,500 fire accidents**; keep as a source-reported dated statistic pending table-level confirmation, not a current annual total.
## Rules, institutions and corrections
- **BIS National Building Code 2016**, first published **1970**, is a **model code** adopted through enforceable building/fire regulations and other applicable instruments. **Part 4 = Fire and Life Safety**; requirements depend on building occupancy/height/risk. [BIS NBC](https://www.bis.gov.in/standards/national-building-code/?lang=en).
- **Twelfth Schedule item 7** covers municipal fire services; it is **not the Seventh Schedule State List**, as the PDF claims. State/local law, municipal building controls and fire services share implementation responsibilities.
- **Model Building Bye-Laws 2016** guide adoption by States/UTs; NDMA preparedness guidance complements local enforceable rules. Codes on paper do not establish inspection/NOC compliance.
- Three layers: **prevention** (safe electrical/gas systems, fire-resistant construction/compartmentation, zoning); **life safety** (clear protected exits, occupant-load planning, signage/emergency light, smoke control and drills); **protection** (appropriate alarms, sprinklers/mist, risers/water supply and maintained firefighting systems). [BIS user guide](https://www.bis.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Booklet-Guide-for-Using-NBC-2016.pdf).
- Do not universalise every listed system or two-hour staircase requirement across every building type. **Metal sheets are not inherently highly flammable**; heat transfer, structural behaviour and combustible contents matter. Generic “smog-control devices” cannot substitute for engineered smoke management.
## Compliance failures and response
- Unsafe alterations/combustibles, blocked escape routes, absent/expired NOCs, irregular audits, staff/training shortages, ageing equipment, poor planning and weak public preparedness create preventable risk. PDF's Rajkot example is a reported case, not proof every allegation was adjudicated.
- Adopt/enforce appropriate norms, risk-based mandatory audits of high-rises/schools/hospitals/malls, transparent NOCs, penalties, independent checks and accountable maintenance. A **uniform binding national code** is a reform proposal in this issue, not already enacted law.
- Invest in trained firefighters, suitable vehicles/equipment, water access, GIS dispatch, inspections and learning from incidents; integrate planning with emergency access.
- Practise evacuation, keep exits clear, use designated stairs, protect children/elderly/disabled occupants; avoid ordinary lifts, re-entry and panic. Smoke exposure can be lethal before direct burns.
## Mains practice framework
- Introduce the enforcement gap → organise causes into legal/institutional/building/community failures → recommend enforceable standards, audits/capacity, maintained detection/suppression and public drills → conclude with accountable prevention and urban resilience.
