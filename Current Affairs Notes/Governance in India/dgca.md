# DGCA and civil aviation oversight

## Institutional map
- **Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA)**: India's civil aviation **safety regulator**, under the Ministry of Civil Aviation; headquarters **New Delhi**. Regulates air transport, aircraft registration/airworthiness, operator and aerodrome certification, professional licensing (including pilots, aircraft maintenance engineers and air traffic controllers), inspections and enforcement.
- **BCAS** sets civil aviation **security** standards; **AAIB** independently investigates accidents and serious incidents under the applicable investigation rules; DGCA retains safety oversight and specified incident responsibilities. **AAI** provides airport infrastructure and air-navigation services; DGCA regulates/certifies relevant facilities. Airport development and all accident investigation should not be assigned wholesale to DGCA. [Ministry organisation map](https://www.civilaviation.gov.in/about-ministry/organization-setup); [AAIB investigation process](https://www.aaib.gov.in/investigationprocess.html).
- ICAO provides international standards/recommended practices; DGCA aligns Indian safety requirements with them and coordinates internationally. Bilateral air-service agreements are handled through the Ministry, rather than being solely a DGCA mandate. Training/competency requirements for cabin crew and dispatchers do not establish that every category has the same individual licensing system.
## Passenger accessibility example
- April's source recalls an **80-year-old Air India passenger's death after wheelchair assistance was unavailable**, followed by a DGCA show-cause notice. This is an **older case**, not evidence of a new April 2025 incident. The notice is an enforcement stage and should not itself be equated with a final finding.
- Revision lesson: airlines need adequate assistance, trained personnel and accountability for passengers with reduced mobility; safety and dignity extend beyond flight operations.
## Reform and capacity
- **eGCA** digitises DGCA licensing/certification and service delivery; it is distinct from passenger biometric processing under Digi Yatra. [Parliamentary review](https://sansad.in/getFile/rsnew/Committee_site/Committee_File/ReportFile/20/173/339_2024_9_11.pdf?source=rajyasabha).
- **Drone Rules 2021** concern emerging unmanned-aircraft regulation; **UDAN (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik)** is regional-connectivity policy requiring viable routes and safe airport/aircraft operations, not a DGCA-owned welfare scheme.
- Proposed **Civil Aviation Authority** restructuring mentioned in the source is not proof that a replacement authority had been established.
- Rapid aviation growth creates inspection/workforce and regulatory-capacity demands; smaller-airport modernisation, credible enforcement and ICAO compliance require coordination. Sustainability work includes noise/emissions standards, green infrastructure and appropriate technology, with policy intentions distinguished from completed outcomes.
