Governance in India

Preventive health and non-communicable diseases

Editable notes

Causes and consequences

  • Major NCD groups: cardiovascular diseases, cancers, diabetes and chronic respiratory disease.
  • Risks: tobacco, harmful alcohol use, unhealthy diet, inactivity, obesity, raised blood pressure, glucose/lipids; age, genetics, air pollution and social conditions also matter.
  • Premature death/disability → productivity loss and household treatment spending → financial insecurity and pressure on health services.
  • January 2025 source reports 65% of deaths, versus 50% in 2010–13, and an economic-loss projection over ₹280 lakh crore by 2030 attributed to WHO. Different periods/methods and the projection's scope need checking; these are not observed 2030 costs.

Prevention is broader than screening

StageExample
Primary preventionTobacco control, healthier food/activity, cleaner environments
Early detectionAppropriate risk-based screening and diagnosis
Follow-up careReferral, affordable treatment, adherence and rehabilitation
  • Strengthen Ayushman health/wellness primary-care centres, community outreach and trained teams; ensure tests lead to treatment, not just higher screening counts.
  • AI-supported tools may assist validated screening; require accuracy, clinical oversight, privacy and workable referral pathways.

January 2025 editorial proposals

  • Subsidised private screening, especially 40–60-year-olds, public/private partnerships and funding from health-related levies.
  • Source describes ₹5, 000 preventive-checkup deduction under section 80 D and proposes ₹15, 000. The increase is a recommendation, not a notified entitlement; tax eligibility also depends on the applicable regime/year.
  • Awareness should communicate useful tests, risk factors and follow-up; indiscriminate testing can produce false positives and unnecessary costs.

Implementation and Mains answer

  • Barriers: rural access, affordability, workforce shortages, equipment, funding, interpretation and continuity of care.
  • Public primary-care investment + affordable partnership delivery + community awareness; prioritise equity and cost-effective evidence-based prevention.
  • Track disease control, avoidable complications, out-of-pocket spending and rural coverage alongside screening volume.
  • PDF 'daily Mains' and practice questions are coaching exercises, not UPSC PYQs.

Cancer recall

  • Hodgkin lymphoma: lymphocyte malignancy with characteristic Reed–Sternberg cells; described by Thomas Hodgkin in 1832. Metastasis: spread of malignant cells to distant sites.
  • Mesothelioma: malignancy of mesothelial lining, commonly pleura; asbestos exposure is a major risk. US National Cancer Act, 1971, associated with Nixon's “war on cancer”.
  • Tobacco, alcohol, unhealthy diet, inactivity and obesity are preventable risk factors. Source 22% tobacco-related cancer deaths / another 10% other lifestyle causes are uncited estimates with overlapping risks; do not add them as universal present-day shares.

Cancer: interpret figures carefully

  • GLOBOCAN2022 estimated roughly 1.41 million new cases and 0.917 million deaths in India; these are modelled annual totals. Mortality/incidence ratio is not the proportion of a diagnosed cohort that dies.
  • Ageing, risk exposure, screening and registration affect measured incidence; distinguish crude from age-standardised rates and reported trends from causal evidence.
  • Tobacco control, HPV prevention, accessible early detection and prompt diagnosis/treatment require follow-up and referral capacity; screening alone is insufficient.
  • NP-NCD evolved from NPCDCS; Mission Shakti is a women's empowerment/safety programme, not a dedicated cancer-treatment scheme. IARC India factsheet.

Obesity and the double burden

  • NFHS adults 15–49: women overweight/obesity 20.6%→24%, men 18.9%→22.9%; adult BMI≥25 measure differs from obesity alone. Under-five overweight 2.1%→3.4% uses child-growth standards.
  • BMI=weight(kg)/height(m)²; distribution of body fat and metabolic/functional effects also matter.2025 Lancet clinical/preclinical obesity proposal is not a universally adopted statutory classification.
  • India faces undernutrition alongside excess weight/NCD risk; ICMR diabetes estimate~101 million does not establish India has the world's highest total.
  • Improve affordable nutritious diets, school food, food labelling and safe active spaces.2030 prevalence projections are forecasts, not current counts.

Life-course nutrition

  • NFHS-5 figures: child stunting 35.5%; anaemia among women aged 15–49 about 57%; overweight/obesity around 24% of women and 22.9% of men.
  • Adequate diet among young children is a defined age/feeding indicator, not a statement about every child. High blood-glucose-or-medication prevalence is not equivalent to “everyone counted takes diabetes medicine”.
  • Undernutrition, micronutrient deficiencies and obesity can coexist. Adolescents, men, older adults and urban populations also need nutrition support.
  • Poshan/Anganwadi services and primary-care centres contribute to maternal/child nutrition and life-course screening; eligibility differs between programmes.
  • Combine diverse local diets, counselling, screening, treatment and follow-up. Fortification supports specific deficiencies but does not replace a balanced diet; evaluate outcomes rather than activity counts alone.
