# Road safety, cashless care and hit-and-run compensation

## Treatment ≠ compensation
| Provision | Distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Cashless road-accident treatment | **Up to ₹1.5 lakh per victim, maximum 7 days** at designated hospitals under scheme conditions |
| Hit-and-run compensation | Separate **₹2 lakh for death; ₹50,000 for grievous injury**, 2022 scheme |
- Source January 2025 discussion follows **2024 pilot**. Pan-India **Cashless Treatment of Road Accident Victims Scheme, 2025** notified **5 May 2025**, guidelines **4 June**; do not backdate nationwide formal notification to January. [MoRTH clarification](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2153890&lang=2&reg=48), [2022 compensation notification](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1801656&lang=2&reg=48).
- Statutory basis: Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 **section 162**, inserted through **2019 amendment**. Eligible accidents caused by use of motor vehicles; 'all accidents without conditions' is too broad.
- **NHA IT platform**, police, designated hospitals and state authorities; **eDAR = electronic Detailed Accident Report** supports verification/reporting. Not identical to AB-PM-JAY beneficiary insurance.
## Why and how
- **Golden hour**: first hour after injury, rapid appropriate trauma care can improve survival; emergency transport/referral and blood/trauma capacity matter.
- Good Samaritans need protection, awareness and straightforward assistance procedures.
- Official **2023** report: **1,72,890 fatalities; ages 18–45 = 66.4%**. PDF's **1.8 lakh / 18–34 = 66%** mixes rounding/age groups. [MoRTH report](https://morth.nic.in/sites/default/files/Road-Accident-in-India-2023-Publications.pdf).
- Implementation: rural access, ambulance/hospital capacity, public awareness, prompt reimbursement, coordination, fraud controls and sustainable funding; prevention complements post-crash care.
