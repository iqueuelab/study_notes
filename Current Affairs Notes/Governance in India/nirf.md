# NIRF: rankings and higher-education quality

## India Rankings 2025
- **Tenth edition**, framework launched **2015**, Ministry of Education; **17 categories**. **IIT Madras** first overall for the seventh consecutive year and engineering for tenth; **IISc** university/research, **IIM Ahmedabad** management, **AIIMS Delhi** medical, **NLSIU Bengaluru** law, **Jamia Hamdard** pharmacy, **IIT Roorkee** architecture, **Hindu College** colleges, **IGNOU** open universities, **Jadavpur** state public universities. [Official rankings](https://www.nirfindia.org/Rankings/2025/Ranking.html).
- Overall parameters: **teaching/learning/resources, research/professional practice, graduation outcomes, outreach/inclusivity, perception**; perception weight **10%**, not over 25%.
- Education Minister questioned reputation/peer-perception bias favouring visible institutions; advocated stronger objective data, entrepreneurship/skilling/innovation outputs and an integrated education-data approach. Proposed reform is distinct from an already replaced methodology.
- Ranking can improve comparison and accountability, but self-reported data, disciplinary differences, resources and geographic visibility require scrutiny. Broad access, state-university capacity and research quality matter alongside rank.
