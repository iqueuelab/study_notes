Governance in India

POCSO: child protection, safeguards and timely justice

Editable notes

Framework

  • Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012: gender-neutral protection of persons below 18; penetrative/non-penetrative assault, aggravated offences, sexual harassment and use of children in pornographic material.
  • Under-18 consent is not adult legal consent under POCSO. Age evidence, context and child welfare matter; adolescent relationships require sensitive handling without inventing a statutory consent exception.
  • Special Courts, child-friendly statement recording, identity protection, support and avoiding repeated testimony. Reporting to Special Juvenile Police Unit/local police is mandatory under section 19; section 21 penalises specified failures, with an exemption for children.

  • Section 29 presumptions concern specified offences; section 30 concerns culpable mental state. These do not dispense with foundational facts and fair trial safeguards.
  • Section 35: child's evidence within 30 days of cognizance, recording reasons for delay; trial as far as possible within one year. The source's six-month trial deadline is incorrect. Statute.
  • Implementation: trained investigators, counsellors, forensic capacity, compensation/support and judicial sensitivity; a stayed lower-court interpretation is not settled law.

Implementation priorities: April 2025 revision

  • Underreporting, stigma, victim blaming, intimidation and family pressure can undermine complaints and testimony. Witnesses turning hostile may reflect vulnerability; acquittal alone does not establish a fabricated complaint.
  • Weak evidence collection, forensic delays, overloaded courts and insensitive questioning obstruct timely justice. Strengthen trained police/prosecutors, forensic support, accessible counsellors and support persons, disability-sensitive communication, confidentiality and compensation.
  • Criminalisation of adolescent relationships is a policy concern because the Act treats persons below 18 as children; no general close-in-age or consent exception should be invented. Differentiate coercion/exploitation from relationship context while following the statute and safeguarding the child.
  • Source corrections: mandatory reporting already existed in 2012, not first created in 2019; minimum imprisonment for using a child for pornographic purposes is 5 years, and 7 years on subsequent conviction, not a universal fixed 5–7-year ceiling. Aggravated assault and ordinary assault have different punishments.
  • Section 35 remains 30 days for child evidence after cognizance, with reasons for delay, and trial as far as possible within one year; the PDF's six-month trial deadline is wrong. Investigation deadlines under criminal-procedure law must be identified separately. Sections 29–30 create specified presumptions, not an unconditional presumption in every conceivable POCSO case. Official statute.

January 2026 editorial: measuring justice beyond disposal

  • The source reports 109% case disposal relative to registrations in 2025 in the fast-track system, cumulative ~3.5 lakh disposals by September 2025, and roughly 9.5 cases/court/month versus 3.3 in regular courts. These have different denominators/time windows; 109% can reflect clearing older cases, not disposing every newly filed case or eliminating backlog.
  • It cites conviction rates 35%(2019)→29%(2023) and about 19% in fast-track courts. Treat these as the article's comparisons pending like-for-like cohort/method verification. Neither acquittal nor falling conviction rates alone proves injustice, fabrication or a causal effect of faster trials; the proposed counterfactual 45% is unsupported.
  • The FTSC centrally sponsored scheme began October 2019, including exclusive POCSO courts, with Nirbhaya Fund support. Official December 2024 baseline was 747 functional courts (406 exclusive POCSO), roughly 3 lakh cumulative rape/POCSO disposals; this is distinct from the editorial's later figures. Department of Justice statement.

Child-centred implementation

  • Para-Legal Volunteers(PLVs) connect families with legal services and police/courts; support persons under POCSO rules assist the child through proceedings. They are related but distinct roles. Section 39 provides for guidelines on experts/professionals; do not treat that section as a complete stand-alone appointment procedure.
  • The article reports PLV availability in only 42 of 919 Andhra Pradesh stations and none in 1,577 Tamil Nadu stations, and attributes a universal station-level direction to the Supreme Court in December 2023. Keep these as attributed implementation claims rather than verified nationwide coverage or an exact holding without the order.
  • Priorities: trained investigation, timely forensics, safe and fewer repeated interviews/testimony, psychosocial assistance, protection from intimidation, early interim compensation and support for lost wages/travel/education. Compensation should not depend only on speed of conviction.
  • Marriage offers or compromise cannot replace adjudication and protection in serious POCSO offences; distinguish section 6 aggravated penetrative assault from every POCSO charge. Court performance should include due process, child welfare and evidentiary quality, rather than disposal/conviction targets alone.
