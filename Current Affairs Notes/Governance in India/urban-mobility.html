Urban mobility: public transport and lifecycle choices

Editable notes

June 2025 editorial: access before mode preference

  • Rising urbanisation intensifies congestion, pollution and unequal access to work/services. Source projects over 60% urban by the 2060s; treat as a projection with an unstated methodology. Its 37% convenient public-transport access, 35,000 buses versus two lakh needed are source-context estimates, not universal current fleet totals.
  • Metro systems need high ridership, affordable fares, first/last-mile access and integrated land use. Capital intensity and low farebox recovery do not by themselves establish lack of social value; assess congestion, emissions, access and urban form alongside finances.
  • PM-eBus Sewa (2023) aims for 10,000 e-buses on PPP; Payment Security Mechanism (2024) addresses operator-payment risk and is a distinct scheme. PM E-DRIVE supports electric mobility including buses, three-wheelers, trucks/ambulances and charging. Source's combined 1.1-lakh vehicle figure should not replace category-specific scheme limits. 10,000-bus scheme; scheme distinction.

Compare technologies transparently

ModePlanning question
MetroCorridor capacity, capital/operating costs, station access and integration
TramElectric rail, durable infrastructure; needs street space/right-of-way and sufficient demand
TrolleybusOverhead power; corridor/wire investment versus flexibility
Battery e-busFlexible routing, charging/depot needs, battery replacement and grid emissions
  • Editorial favours trams/trolleybuses and proposes Kochi as a candidate. Its 70-year estimates: +45% tram returns versus 82% e-bus net loss depend on financial assumptions; they are not inherent profitability laws or proof electric buses always lose money.
  • Choose modes using lifecycle costs, passenger demand, energy/emissions, accessibility, service reliability and street constraints; integrate bus/rail/feeder routes, walking/cycling and common interchange hubs. Clear contracts, predictable funding, affordable fares and transparent private participation can reconcile viability with inclusion. An editorial discussion question without a year is not a UPSC PYQ.
