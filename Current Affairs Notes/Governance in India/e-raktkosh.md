# e-RaktKosh and rare blood donor coordination

## Blood safety and rare donors
- **e-RaktKosh**, launched **7 April 2016**, is a centralised blood-centre management platform: donor records/biometric identification, blood grouping, transfusion-transmitted infection screening, antibody screening, stock management and biomedical-waste records. Works within Drugs and Cosmetics law and National Blood Policy standards. [Official functions](https://eraktkosh.mohfw.gov.in/BLDAHIMS/bloodbank/about.cnt).
- June reporting describes planned integration of the **Rare Donor Registry of India** with e-RaktKosh: a national search/coordination system helps locate compatible blood in emergencies. **Bombay phenotype and Rh-null** illustrate rare blood groups; a rare phenotype is not itself a blood disorder.
- Revision: red-cell disorders include anaemias; leucocyte/haematological malignancies include leukaemia, lymphoma and myeloma; haemophilia/von Willebrand disease concern clotting; thrombocytopenia means low platelets. Thalassaemia/sickle-cell disease are inherited red-cell conditions, whose frequency varies substantially by population—do not label them universally rare.
- Real-time availability, confirmatory compatibility testing, donor consent/privacy and reliable transport all matter; a donor match in a database does not by itself complete safe transfusion.

## March 2026: NAT and safe transfusion debate
- The source reports the Supreme Court examining the **feasibility/affordability of compulsory nucleic acid testing (NAT)** in blood banks, with further information sought. The petition invokes **Article 21** and preventable transfusion infections; the hearing is not a final nationwide NAT mandate.
- **NAT amplifies/detects viral nucleic acid (DNA/RNA)** and can shorten the diagnostic window for HIV/HBV/HCV compared with conventional serology. It reduces residual risk, rather than making donated blood infallibly safe. [WHO screening guidance](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/44202/9789241547888_eng.pdf?sequence=1).
- Correct the source's “ELISA detects only antibodies”: immunoassays may detect **antigen, antibody or both**, depending on assay design (HBsAg is an important example). Compare named tests/generations, validated sensitivity and cost rather than treating all ELISAs as identical.
- **Thalassaemia** is inherited impaired globin synthesis; severe transfusion-dependent forms require repeated transfusions, magnifying cumulative exposure. Not every affected person requires regular transfusion. Reports of infected children in MP/Jharkhand highlight screening/quality-control concerns; infection detected after transfusion is not by itself proof of a particular blood unit's causal source.
- Strengthen donor selection, quality-assured screening, traceability, haemovigilance, counselling and equitable access alongside technology assessment. Existing e-RaktKosh digital stock/donor functions do not substitute for laboratory testing. The assertion that India has no national blood policy is wrong: **National Blood Policy 2002** already provides a framework.
