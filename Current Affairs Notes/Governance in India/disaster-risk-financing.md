# Disaster risk financing and preventive resilience

## October 2025: financing prevention alongside response
- Fifteenth Finance Commission's 2021–26 framework expands disaster finance beyond relief: national/state risk-management funds comprise 80% response funds and 20% mitigation funds. State risk-management allocation ₹1,60,153 crore plus national ₹68,463 crore totals ₹2,28,616 crore (rounded ₹2.28 lakh crore).
- Within the state framework, shares of the whole fund: response/relief 40%, recovery/reconstruction 30%, preparedness/capacity 10%, mitigation 20%. Thus 30% is preventive/preparedness and 70% post-disaster; 80% response-fund allocation includes preparedness, not only emergency relief. [MHA annual report](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/AnnualReportEngLish_11102023.pdf), [fund framework](https://ndmindia.mha.gov.in/ndmi/responsefund).
- MHA coordinates central disaster policy; NDMA sets policy/guidelines; NIDM trains/researches; NDRF provides specialist response. The Prime Minister's 2016 ten-point agenda and Sendai Framework 2015–30 support mainstreaming risk into development/public finance.
- Match measures to hazards: wetlands/water bodies and green space for urban floods; vegetation/bioengineering for slope stability; rejuvenate Brahmaputra beels; manage forest fuel/fire lines. GLOF remote sensing/weather stations are monitoring technology, not themselves nature-based restoration.
- Source reports ₹5,000-crore reconstruction across Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Assam and Kerala, ₹5,000 crore for fire services and roughly ₹10,000-crore mitigation projects; these are dated source figures, not all components of one project or automatic state entitlements. Its 2.5-lakh volunteer figure combines Aapda Mitra/Yuva Aapda Mitra discussion and should not imply every planned volunteer already trained.
- CAP multilingual/multimedia warnings, last-mile communications, drills, school safety, Panchayat training, geospatial labs and university networks join finance with local capability. The historical National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project covered eight coastal states; shelters, embankments, evacuation and early warning collectively reduce deaths, rather than a uniform guaranteed seven-day forecast for every hazard. CDRI and regional cooperation share resilience expertise.

## November editorial: fiscal federalism and disaster relief
- Argument: delayed/discretionary assistance and gaps between assessed needs and releases can strain State finances and cooperative federalism. **Wayanad, July 2024** is the case study; the source's **~₹2,200-crore loss estimate versus ~₹260-crore assistance** compares different accounting concepts, not necessarily all public support received. Its roughly 11% comparison is a narrow editorial calculation, not a complete compensation rate.
- Distinguish **allocation, approval, cash release and expenditure**, and separate grants, regular SDRF balances and capital-assistance loans. Later official accounting identifies **₹260.56 crore approved central recovery/reconstruction share**, with **₹78.17 crore first instalment released 17 October 2025**; it is incorrect to call the whole approval an already paid tranche. Source **~₹529-crore loan / ₹780-crore balance** are separate reported financing/balance figures, not interchangeable relief grants. [Official accounting](https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2025-pdfs/RS03122025/401.pdf).
- **Immediate relief** under notified SDRF norms is distinct from the wider **recovery/reconstruction and mitigation windows** already described above. The blanket claim that the framework has no reconstruction provision is incorrect. Relief norms such as **₹4 lakh ex gratia for death** are assistance ceilings, not a valuation of life or full indemnification; the source’s **₹1.2-lakh house-assistance example** is category/terrain-dependent, not one universal reimbursement or full rebuilding cost.
- Additional central assistance follows assessment/approval procedures, not automatic full reimbursement of every State memorandum. Improve transparent criteria and timelines; an accounting balance may include commitments, but that needs evidence rather than assuming every balance is already spent. The Fifteenth Finance Commission methodology already incorporates **past expenditure, exposure and a Disaster Risk Index**—the claim that hazards are wholly ignored is false. [Finance Commission methodology](https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/15th-FC/digitized/15th%20FC%20-%202020-21%20-%20Digitised.pdf).
## Compare mechanisms accurately
| Example | Revision distinction |
|---|---|
| US FEMA | Per-capita damage is an indicator among factors; presidential declaration/assistance is **not automatic** when a threshold is crossed |
| Mexico | FONDEN is a historical model; its trust ceased new commitments from **2021**, with replacement budget arrangements—not an unchanged 2025 automatic fund |
| Parametric insurance | A specified hazard/model trigger may enable rapid payout; **basis risk** means payout can differ from actual losses |
| Philippines / Australia | Source examples illustrate risk indicators and fiscal-sharing rules; they do not prove all aid is immediate, unconditional or free of assessment |
- [FEMA assessment guidance](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_rd_pda-guide_07012025.pdf), [Mexico Finance Ministry](https://www.gob.mx/shcp/prensa/nota-informativa-349793?idiom=es-MX).
- Reform agenda: publish assessments/releases and reasons; update norms transparently; refine hazard/climate/vulnerability measures; combine predictable grants and appropriate risk transfer; strengthen local preparedness, audits and grievance mechanisms. Faster rules should retain accountability, not substitute unchecked post-audit for every safeguard. **Practice mains** is unyearred, so excluded from PYQ banks.
