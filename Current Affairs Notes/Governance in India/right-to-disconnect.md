# Right to disconnect: digital work, rest and labour protection

## December 2025: private member's proposal
- **Supriya Sule's Right to Disconnect Bill 2025**, a private member's Bill, proposes an **Employees' Welfare Authority** and protection against disciplinary action for refusing after-hours contact; **a Bill is not an enacted entitlement**.
- Actual text includes entity-level negotiated charters for employers with **more than ten employees**, employee welfare committees, remote-work policies and counselling/detox centres. **Clause 11 explicitly provides overtime at the normal wage rate**; clause 14 addresses responses where no agreement exists. Therefore the PDF's categorical “no overtime provision” implication is wrong. [Lok Sabha Bill text](https://sansad.in/getFile/BillsTexts/LSBillTexts/Asintroduced/51%20of%202025%20AS125202592416PM.pdf?source=legislation).
- Valid policy questions remain: contractual hours versus mandatory labour standards; employer-controlled availability/on-call time; interaction with statutory overtime/rest rules; emergency exceptions, coverage and enforceable remedies. Digital contact is not automatically identical to all working time.
- **Article 21** links dignity, autonomy and well-being; it does not itself make the proposed detailed statutory scheme operative. France's collective bargaining and European working-time/rest approaches offer comparisons; the blanket assertion every standby period always counts as work oversimplifies the constraints test.
- Four Labour Codes commenced **21 November 2025**, already recorded. Evaluate burnout, unpaid work, productivity and unequal bargaining power alongside implementation.
