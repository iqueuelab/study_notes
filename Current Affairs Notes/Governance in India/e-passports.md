# e-Passports and biometric document security

## May 2025 rollout and design
- An **e-passport remains a physical booklet**, with contactless **RFID chip/antenna**, personal particulars and biometric information, plus digitally signed data. A gold e-passport symbol identifies it. MEA's system uses **public-key infrastructure (PKI)** to verify data integrity and origin. Phased issuance does not invalidate existing ordinary passports before expiry. [Passport Seva FAQ](https://services1.passportindia.gov.in/psp/FaqEPassport).
- The source's “over 120 countries” is an issue-date approximation. ICAO travel-document standards support interoperability, not automatic visa-free access or guaranteed expedited entry.
| Security concept | Function |
|---|---|
| Basic Access Control | Restricts chip access and protects chip–reader communications using document-derived keys |
| Passive Authentication | Checks issuer signature/data integrity; does not alone detect cloning |
| Extended Access Control | Further controls access to sensitive biometrics where implemented |
- Facial image is the interoperable primary biometric; **fingerprints and EAC should not be assumed mandatory in every Indian passport** merely from generic international capabilities. A chip is not by itself tamper-proof; cryptography, inspection and key management work together. [ICAO Doc 9303](https://www.icao.int/publications/pages/publication.aspx?docnum=9303).
- Potential gains: harder forgery, authenticatable identity, compatible e-gates and efficient processing. Benefits depend on destination infrastructure; no system guarantees elimination of identity theft.
- The issue's **“address no longer printed, stored only digitally”** claim is not established by MEA's cited technical definition; avoid presenting this or universal fingerprint storage as verified new Indian policy. Protect privacy through controlled access, secure systems and lawful use, alongside Digital India/service modernisation.
