# Education governance: federalism, access and autonomy

## Reading the “three Cs” critique
- Source editorial argues **centralisation, commercialisation and communalisation** threaten education. These are analytical claims, not a court ruling that NEP 2020 is unconstitutional.
- Education is in the Concurrent List; CABE, Centre–State consultation, school funding and university governance matter. Proposed UGC rules and final operative regulations must be distinguished.
- Samagra Shiksha funding linked to PM SHRI participation raised federal concerns; parliamentary recommendations for release are not themselves binding disbursement orders.
- **RTE Act enacted 2009, commenced 2010**; Article **21A** protects free compulsory education for ages **6–14**. Neighbourhood-school distances arise from rules, not a universal constitutional kilometre limit.
## Assess evidence and reform
- School closures reported since 2014 cannot all be caused by a policy adopted in 2020; distinguish mergers, access impacts and comparable data series.
- HEFA provides capital-financing loans with differing repayment arrangements; one reported student-fee burden cannot describe every financing window.
- NAAC/NTA integrity issues, academic appointments and textbook changes need institution/edition-specific evidence; preserve critical inquiry, diversity and academic autonomy while ensuring accountability.
- Priorities: predictable equitable funding, transparent recruitment/accreditation, State participation, accessible public schools and meaningful outcome monitoring.
