# University Grants Commission and higher-education regulation

## Evolution

**1944 Sargeant Report → 1945 University Grants Committee → 1947 wider university coverage → 1948–49 Radhakrishnan Commission → 1952 grants-referral decision → 28 December 1953 inauguration → November 1956 statutory establishment.**

The initial committee oversaw Aligarh, Banaras and Delhi universities. Maulana Abul Kalam Azad inaugurated the UGC; the **UGC Act, 1956** supplied its statutory basis. Headquarters: **New Delhi**. [UGC genesis](https://www.ugc.gov.in/Aboutus/Genesis)

## Composition and powers

- Section 5 specifies **Chairman, Vice-Chairman and ten other members**, appointed by the Central Government—**not “up to ten”**. The chairman is selected from outside serving Central/State government officers; membership includes government and academic representation.
- Coordinates university education, determines/maintains teaching, examination and research standards, distributes eligible grants and advises governments.
- University establishment and degree-awarding authority depend on law; UGC recognition is not permission for any college to declare itself a university. Under section 3, the **Central Government**, on UGC advice, declares eligible institutions deemed-to-be universities. [UGC Act](https://www.education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/upload_document/ugc_act.pdf)
- **Union List entry 66** already covers coordination/determination of higher-education standards. The **42nd Amendment, 1976** moved general education to **Concurrent List entry 25**; it did not create Union authority over standards for the first time.

## Institutions and reform

| Body | Principal role |
|---|---|
| UGC | University education coordination, standards and eligible grants |
| AICTE | Technical-education planning/standards under its statutory remit |
| NAAC | Higher-education assessment/accreditation |
| NCTE | Teacher-education regulation |
| NCERT | School curriculum, research and educational support |

**NEP 2020 proposal:** Higher Education Commission of India with **NHERC** (regulation), **NAC** (accreditation), **HEGC** (grants), and **GEC** (academic standards/framework). A policy proposal is not proof that UGC has already been abolished.

Digital tools: **SWAYAM** online courses; **National Academic Depository** digital academic awards; **Academic Bank of Credits** credit accumulation/transfer. These support mobility while requiring authentic records, quality standards and institutional interoperability.
## May 2025 editorial critique
- The article argues UGC's grant-making and regulatory roles may conflict and calls for abolition; this is **a reform argument**, not proof of abolition or universally established institutional failure.
- Separate regulator, accreditation, funding and academic-standard functions with clear accountability if pursuing NEP's proposed HECI structure; institutional change requires the applicable law and implementation, not rhetoric alone.
