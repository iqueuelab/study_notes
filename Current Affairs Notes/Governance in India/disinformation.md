# Disinformation: democratic trust and safeguards

## Distinctions and risks
- **Misinformation:** false information without necessarily intending deception. **Disinformation:** deliberately deceptive information. Genuine material used maliciously is sometimes termed malinformation.
- AI-generated text/images/video, coordinated amplification and attention-driven recommendation systems can increase the speed and scale of deception. Deepfake ≠ automatically false or unlawful; context, impersonation and deception matter.
- Consequences: polarisation, communal tension, distorted elections/public debate, fraud, health misinformation, reduced institutional trust and foreign information manipulation/interference (**FIMI**).
- **WEF Global Risks Report 2025:** misinformation/disinformation ranked first over the **two-year** horizon in respondents' risk assessment; armed conflict led the separate immediate-2025 concern. This is a perception survey, not a measured count of every harm. [WEF report](https://www.weforum.org/publications/global-risks-report-2025/).
- The supplied ISB–CyberPeace study summary reports political 46%, general 33.6% and religious 16.8% of its studied disinformation. Treat these as sample-dependent findings, not shares of all Indian online content; missing methodology prevents national extrapolation.
## Response: verify → explain → correct → review
- Check provenance, original date/context and independent evidence before forwarding; teach digital/media literacy in schools and public campaigns.
- Platforms: proportionate risk assessments, transparent moderation rules, researcher access, independent fact-checking and accessible appeals; provenance labels help but are not proof that an unlabelled item is authentic.
- AI developers: safety testing and abuse monitoring; public agencies: timely trustworthy information, cybersecurity capacity and evidence-based corrections.
- Protect journalists/fact-checkers, support cross-border cooperation and investigate coordinated fraud/illegal activity within applicable law.
- **Trade-off:** Article 19(1)(a) expression and legitimate dissent require protection; restrictions must meet Article 19(2) and due process. “False” alone does not create unlimited censorship authority.
- Automated verification can make errors; moderator bias, low trust, linguistic diversity, cross-border attribution and commercial/political incentives complicate implementation.
- A proposed Indian Digital Services Act, national deepfake policy or AI oversight council in the article is a recommendation, not an established institution/law. EU policy cannot be described as already adopted by India.
