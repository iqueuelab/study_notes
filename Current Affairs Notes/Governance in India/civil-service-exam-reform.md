# Civil-service recruitment and examination reform

## 30 May 2025 editorial: two entry routes
- Newspaper-only clipping **“India Needs UPSC 2.0”**, by former RBI Governor **Duvvuri Subbarao**, proposes reducing entry-level age/attempt limits and creating an annual open competitive route into the IAS around **age 40–42**. These are **editorial proposals**, not announced UPSC rules.
- Argument: repeated preparation can create opportunity costs, financial/psychological stress and a **sunk-cost fallacy**; applicants continue because of past investment rather than current expected gains. Critique the incentive structure without assuming unsuccessful candidates lack ability or motivation.
- He recalls his **1972 batch**, approximately **80% first-attempt entrants**, as historical experience. This is not a representative measurement of today's cohort. His suggested **three attempts/age 27** differs from the current rules he discusses.
- **2025 ordinary/general-category framework:** age **21–under 32** on the prescribed reference date and **six attempts**, with category/disability relaxations. The clipping's “21–32” shorthand is not inclusive of attaining 32; eligibility depends on the official notification and category. [UPSC 2025 notice](https://upsc.gov.in/sites/default/files/Notif-CSP-2025-Engl-220125.pdf).
- Selection errors: potentially good administrators excluded and weaker performers selected; a competitive exam cannot perfectly predict decades of administrative performance. More attempts can reward exam technique as well as merit; tighter limits can disadvantage candidates with unequal education/resources or later opportunity.
- Proposed mid-career exam-based **permanent recruitment** would differ from small-number specialist **lateral-entry contractual posts**. Potential benefits: second chance, outside experience, diverse perspectives and reduced all-or-nothing early-career risk.
- Tradeoffs: fairness/reservation, assessable merit, seniority/training, career progression, recruitment capacity and representation. The author also credits young entrants' energy and past scheme innovations; reform should combine youthful talent with experience rather than assuming only one age group can govern well.
