# Workplace sexual harassment: Internal Committees and accountability

## Legal framework and composition
- **Vishaka 1997** guidelines preceded the **Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act,2013**. Bhanwari Devi's case exposed the legal gap; the statute covers public/private workplaces, educational institutions and other covered settings, not government offices alone.
- **Internal Committee (IC, commonly ICC)** at workplaces with **ten or more employees**—not “more than ten”. **Local Committee** handles establishments with fewer than ten and complaints against an employer; domestic workers also have a statutory route. [Act, India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2104?view_type=browse).
- Presiding Officer: **senior woman employee**; at least **two employee members** committed to women's cause/social-work or legal experience; **one external NGO/association member or person familiar with harassment issues**. **At least half women**. External participation does not make the outsider chairperson.
- Inquiry within **90 days**; statutory civil-court powers for summoning/examining and requiring documents. Conciliation only on the aggrieved woman's request; **monetary settlement cannot be its basis**. Confidentiality protects identities and proceedings. Committee recommends action; employer implements lawful recommendations; criminal-law assistance is separate.
## Implementation and July 2025 case study
- Source reports a Balasore student died after self-immolation following a disputed institutional sexual-harassment grievance response. Allegations of committee bias/training failure are allegations, not adjudicated conclusions about every member.
- Failures: committees absent/on paper, poor training, institutional conflicts, power imbalance, retaliation and confidentiality breaches. The Supreme Court's December 2024 compliance concerns underscore enforcement rather than a new statute.
- Improve composition/compliance audits, regular training, safe reporting, anti-retaliation safeguards, external independence and anonymised reporting. Disclosure of aggregate compliance must preserve survivor privacy. Source MCQ is practice, not a dated CSE PYQ.

## March 2026: Railways' SHINE reporting module
- **Sexual Harassment Incident Notification for Empowerment (SHINE)** was launched for women railway employees on **8 March, International Women's Day**. It operates through **HRMS → Employee Self Service**, routing complaints online to the nominated committee and supporting monitoring/confidentiality.
- The Railway Board's **5 March order** also provides for complaints on behalf of **outside visitors, contractual workers or students**, and authorised officials filing on behalf of women employees. This is a reporting channel supporting the existing committee process; a digital submission does not itself decide the complaint. [Board order, reproduced in Southern Railway circular](https://pbtpj.in/circularsuploads/3107.pdf).
- **Vishaka (1997) → workplace-harassment Act (2013)** remains the legal background. Reporting access, trained independent committees, timely inquiry, privacy and protection against retaliation determine practical effectiveness; the app is not a replacement for those duties.
