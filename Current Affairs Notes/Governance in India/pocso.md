# POCSO: child protection, safeguards and timely justice

## Framework
- **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012**: gender-neutral protection of persons **below 18**; penetrative/non-penetrative assault, aggravated offences, sexual harassment and use of children in pornographic material.
- Under-18 consent is not adult legal consent under POCSO. Age evidence, context and child welfare matter; adolescent relationships require sensitive handling without inventing a statutory consent exception.
- Special Courts, child-friendly statement recording, identity protection, support and avoiding repeated testimony. Reporting to Special Juvenile Police Unit/local police is mandatory under section 19; section 21 penalises specified failures, with an exemption for children.
## Penalties after the 2019 amendment
| Offence | Statutory punishment anchor |
|---|---|
| Penetrative sexual assault | Minimum 10 years; may extend to life, plus fine |
| Penetrative assault on child below 16 | Minimum 20 years; may extend to natural-life imprisonment, plus fine |
| Aggravated penetrative assault | Minimum 20 years, natural-life imprisonment or death, plus fine |
| Sexual assault / aggravated sexual assault | 3–5 years / 5–7 years, plus fine |
| Sexual harassment | Up to 3 years, plus fine |
| Using child for pornographic purposes | Minimum 5 years; subsequent conviction minimum 7 years, plus fine |
- Section 29 presumptions concern specified offences; section 30 concerns culpable mental state. These do not dispense with foundational facts and fair trial safeguards.
- **Section 35**: child's evidence within **30 days of cognizance**, recording reasons for delay; trial **as far as possible within one year**. The source's six-month trial deadline is incorrect. [Statute](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/9318/1/sexualoffencea2012-32.pdf).
- Implementation: trained investigators, counsellors, forensic capacity, compensation/support and judicial sensitivity; a stayed lower-court interpretation is not settled law.
