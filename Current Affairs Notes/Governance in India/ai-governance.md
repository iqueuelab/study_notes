# AI governance: inclusion, safety and India’s global role

## July 2025 editorial proposals
- With India then preparing to host the **AI Impact Summit in February 2026**, the editorial proposes using DPI experience (Aadhaar/UPI/CoWIN), technical skills and Global South diplomacy to promote human-centred AI. These are source-era proposals, not agreed summit outcomes.
- **Accountability:** public, measurable pledges by governments/companies/universities, tracked for a year; examples include greener computing, wider training and local-language public services.
- **Inclusion:** proposed **AI for Billions Fund** for cloud credits, fellowships and local-language datasets; development-bank/Gulf support and an underserved-language model challenge are suggestions.
- **Safety:** proposed collaborative sharing of red-team tests, incident logs and model stress tests, plus open tools to assess bias/fairness.
- **Regulation:** proposed voluntary frontier-model code building on Seoul commitments, with timely safety disclosures (editorial suggests 90 days), compute transparency and an accident-reporting channel.
- **Cooperation:** connect existing institutions, broaden developing-country participation and avoid rival blocs fragmenting standards. Balance innovation with safeguards; simplistic US/EU/China labels do not capture every law or policy.
- MyGov consultation is presented as participatory preparation; it does not establish that no previous host consulted its public. The printed mains question is coaching practice, not a UPSC PYQ.

## February 2026: India AI Impact Summit
- At **Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi**, the MeitY/IndiaAI summit programme was scheduled for **16–20 February**. The development agenda links AI access to public-service outcomes and Global South participation; safety, trust and rights remain part of it.
- Three **Sutras: People, Planet, Progress**—human-centred inclusion, environmental sustainability, and inclusive economic/technological progress.
- **Seven Chakras/working groups:** Human Capital; Inclusion for Social Empowerment; Safe and Trusted AI; Science; Resilience, Innovation and Efficiency; Democratizing AI Resources; AI for Economic Development and Social Good. **Health and agriculture are application areas, not the official names of two Chakras** as the PDF suggests. [Official thematic framework, 8 February](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/feb/doc202628782901.pdf).
- Shared compute, datasets and models can lower entry barriers; language representation, privacy, accessible skills, bias testing and accountable deployment determine whether gains reach underserved groups. Public-service applications include health, education, farm advice, fraud detection and disaster warning; each needs domain validation and human oversight.
- The issue's prospective deliverables and Pax Silica discussion were **expectations**, not proof a declaration had been agreed or accession completed by this issue date. AI talent/GPU/startup counts are changing snapshots; a projected GDP contribution is not realised growth.

## India's “third way”: governance argument
- The editorial proposes **context-sensitive, development-oriented AI governance**: encourage adoption and local-language/public-interest applications while retaining safety, privacy, accountability and strategic autonomy. EU/US/China comparisons are broad analytical shorthand; “the US has no regulation” and “India does not regulate AI” are incorrect absolutes.
- **India AI Governance Guidelines, unveiled 5 November 2025**, combine guiding principles, recommendations, an action plan and practical guidance, relying on existing laws where possible. Guidelines are not themselves a comprehensive AI statute. Proposed coordinating/expert/safety institutions must be distinguished from completed institutional establishment. [MeitY announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2186639&lang=2&reg=3).
- The **10 February 2026 IT Rules amendment** addresses realistic **synthetically generated audio/visual/audio-visual information**, with qualifications for routine good-faith editing and specified non-deceptive uses. Its labelling/provenance duties are more specific than “every AI-written text must be labelled”. **Three-hour removal** concerns the specified lawful court/government actual-knowledge route, not every private complaint or every disagreeable post. [Official consolidated rules, especially rules 2(1)(wa), 3(1)(d), 3(3)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/550681ab908f8afb135b0ad42816a1c9.pdf).
- Implementation priorities: sector-specific risk assessment; transparent procurement and audits; accessible grievance/appeal mechanisms; labour-transition support; public digital literacy; shared testing and international cooperation. The article's proposed whistle-blower and accountability reforms are recommendations, not proof they had been enacted.
