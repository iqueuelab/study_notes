Governance in India

Central Consumer Protection Authority

Editable notes

Statutory institution

  • CCPA, under Section 10, Consumer Protection Act 2019, protects consumers as a class against rights violations, unfair trade practices and misleading advertising. Relevant provisions commenced 24 July 2020; Act replaced the 1986 framework. India Code commencement.
  • Chief Commissioner and other Commissioners appointed by Central Government; the Act does not permanently prescribe exactly two Commissioners divided into goods/services. An investigation wing is headed by a Director-General.

Powers and limits

ProvisionFunction
Sections 18–19Investigate on complaint, government direction or own motion; refer matters to relevant regulators; file complaints before consumer commissions/intervene in proceedings
Section 20Recall unsafe goods, withdraw dangerous services, reimburse prices and stop unfair practices
Section 21Direct discontinuance/modification of misleading ads; impose specified penalties and prohibit endorsements
  • Manufacturer/endorser misleading-ad penalty: up to ₹10 lakh, subsequent contravention ₹50 lakh; endorsement prohibition up to one year, subsequent contravention three years, subject to statutory safeguards.
  • CCPA enforcement is distinct from individual compensation adjudication by District/State/National consumer commissions. Licence suspension is not a blanket CCPA administrative power; the Act separately provides court-ordered consequences for specified offences. Consumer Protection Act.

Digital-market relevance

  • E-commerce claims, deceptive advertising and systemic unsafe-product sales make proactive enforcement valuable.
  • Improve investigation capacity, consumer awareness, regulator coordination and platform accountability; digital and cross-border transactions complicate evidence collection and enforcement.

November 2025: dark-pattern declarations

  • 20 November 2025: CCPA announced 26 e-commerce platforms had submitted self/third-party audit declarations of eliminating or not using dark patterns. A declaration is not an independent regulator finding that every interface is compliant. Absence from the published list is not proof of a violation; omit the PDF's speculative company-specific reasons.
  • Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 identify deceptive interface practices impairing consumer autonomy. The later June 2025 advisory called for audits within three months; do not date this exercise solely to 2024. CCPA announcement.
  • Recall all 13 patterns: false urgency; basket sneaking; confirm shaming; forced action; subscription trap; interface interference; bait and switch; drip pricing; disguised advertisement; nagging; trick wording; SaaS billing; rogue malware. Examples: fake scarcity, pre-added purchases, guilt-based refusal buttons, obscured cancellation and charges revealed late.
  • Ethical design uses clear total prices, genuine choice, explicit consent and easy cancellation. Audits need remediation and continuing monitoring; small firms may need design/compliance support. Enforcement powers must be read under the Consumer Protection Act rather than assuming an identical automatic fine for every UI flaw.
