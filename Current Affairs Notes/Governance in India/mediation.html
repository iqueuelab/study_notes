Governance in India

Judicial backlog and mediation

Editable notes

Delay and appropriate alternatives

  • Source 2024 snapshots: SC ~82,000; HCs ~62 lakh; subordinate courts nearly five crore; ~50 lakh cases older than ten years. Totals depend on date/court scope; use NJDG for a comparable series, not these as current counts.
  • Causes: vacancies/staff/infrastructure, scheduling/adjournments, process complexity and avoidable government litigation. Source government 50% /21 judges per million need denominator/year; court use is not inherently frivolous.
  • Mediation: neutral facilitator helps parties reach a voluntary settlement; mediator does not impose a binding adjudicated award as an arbitrator/judge does.

Law and safeguards

  • Mediation Act 2023, already an Act, not a pending “Mediation Bill2023”. Commencement is provision-specific; enacted text does not mean all institutions/procedures were operational in February 2025. Official Act.
  • Final Act's general pre-litigation model is voluntary, not universal mandatory mediation. Some commercial disputes have separate pre-institution requirements; not every crime/public-law issue is suitable for settlement.
  • Confidentiality, trained impartial mediators, informed consent and protection against coercion/unequal bargaining; a settlement can have specified legal effect, but not every failed mediation removes access to court.

Delivery reforms

  • Triage suitable family/civil/commercial cases, competent mediators, legal aid and tracked referrals; settlement statistics need durability/fairness, not only disposal counts.
  • Fill judicial/support vacancies, improve e-court case management and evidence scheduling, reduce routine government appeals and build awareness.
  • ADR complements adjudication; it cannot cure every criminal backlog or guarantee every dispute resolves in a few sessions.
