NFSA and PDS: entitlements, targeting and exclusion safeguards

Editable notes

November 2025: beneficiary-list verification

  • Source reports 2.25 crore people removed over the preceding four–five months following identification of possible ineligibility and State field verification. Treat this as a dated reported beneficiary/person count, not a verified count of ration cards or independently established fiscal savings. Centre's data matching flags cases; States identify/verify beneficiaries under applicable criteria.
  • Records cited: income, vehicle ownership, company directorship and Aadhaar-linked databases. A flag is not conclusive proof: outdated ownership, household changes and mismatches require notice, hearing, accessible correction/appeal and restoration for wrongly excluded households. Add eligible households while reviewing inclusion errors; digital cleaning does not itself resolve coverage ceilings.
  • National Food Security Act 2013: rights-based food/nutrition entitlements; population coverage up to 75% rural and 50% urban, roughly two-thirds using the legal coverage framework. ~81 crore is the programme-era ceiling/coverage scale, not an automatic two-thirds recalculation of every year's estimated population. NFSA overview.
  • AAY: 35 kg per household/month; PHH: 5 kg per person/month. Free foodgrains from January 2023 do not erase this distinction; not every NFSA household receives only a flat 5 kg per person. The rice/wheat mix varies; 20 kg wheat +15 kg rice is not a universal statutory AAY split. Official entitlements.
  • Eldest adult woman 18+ is ration-card household head, with the Act's rule where there is no adult woman. Nutrition provisions cover children six months–six years through Anganwadi services, school-age children through eligible school meals up to class VIII/age fourteen as applicable, and pregnant/lactating women including statutory maternity benefit not less than ₹6,000, subject to legal conditions. Entitlement is distinct from proof of universal delivery.
  • Link ONORC portability, grievance redress and social audits with food quality, nutrition diversity, privacy and offline alternatives. Better targeting may reduce leakage, but exclusion errors can cause hunger and cannot be counted as efficiency gains.
