Governance in India

MPLADS: local assets, fund rules and accountability

Editable notes

Scheme and current rules

  • MPLADS, a Central Sector Scheme announced 23 December 1993, administered by MoSPI since October 1994, lets MPs recommend works addressing local needs, especially durable community assets in drinking water, schools, public health, sanitation and roads. District authorities sanction/implement; money is not paid to MPs personally.
  • Annual entitlement ₹5 crore per MP; funds are non-lapsable. Since 1 April 2023/eSAKSHI, the annual entitlement is authorised in one ₹5-crore instalment at the financial year's beginning/term start; the source's two ₹2.5-crore instalments describe the earlier system. MoSPI clarification, August 2025.
  • Elected Lok Sabha MPs normally recommend within their constituency; elected Rajya Sabha MPs within their State of election. Nominated Rajya Sabha MPs can recommend anywhere in India; the source's nominated-MP restriction to one State is an older formulation.
  • Outside the usual region, elected MPs may recommend up to ₹50 lakh per financial year, subject to guidelines; separate provisions allow up to ₹1 crore/year for rehabilitation/reconstruction after a Centre-declared severe calamity. The source mixes the old ₹25-lakh ordinary ceiling with later ₹50-lakh rules. Official December 2024 parliamentary-reply account.
  • Recommend at least 15% for SC-inhabited areas and 7.5% for ST-inhabited areas, with guideline exceptions where relevant. Eligibility, sanction, procurement, execution and inspection remain distinct responsibilities; MPs may monitor progress without becoming executing contractors.

February 2026: reform versus abolition

  • The editorial discusses allegations about Rajasthan MPs recommending works in Haryana. Cross-State location alone does not establish misuse; assess applicable ceilings, eligibility, sanctions and expenditure. Political allegations and a defence based on rules are competing claims, not an audit finding.
  • Its dated figures: 18th Lok Sabha ₹5,486 crore allocated, ₹1,453.69 crore spent; 17th ₹4,837.87 crore allocated, ₹3,639.53 crore spent (~75.23%), with many works incomplete. Allocation, actual spending and completed assets measure different things, and the 18th term remained ongoing. COVID-related suspension/delays affect comparisons.
  • Case for retention: locally informed small works, visible representation and gap-filling. Criticisms: uneven utilisation, delays, patronage, incomplete works and separation-of-powers concerns. Judicially upheld constitutional validity does not remove the need for audits.
  • Reforms: clear eligibility training, timely district sanctions, geo-tagged completion evidence, public dashboards, independent quality checks and transparent beneficiary/location data. Utilisation alone does not prove quality or absence of corruption.
  • 2020 PYQ: statements 1,2,4 correct (d); unused funds can carry forward. Interpret the question under its exam-era rules, including the district authority's 10% annual inspection statement.
