Governance in India

UDISE+ and school education indicators

Editable notes

System

  • Unified District Information System for Education Plus, Department of School Education and Literacy; online system introduced 2018–19.
  • Covers recognised schools: enrolment, teachers, infrastructure and other education indicators; supports RTE/NEP planning rather than replacing the RTE Act.
  • National student-wise data from 2022–23 differs from earlier school-level aggregate returns. Earlier and later series are not strictly comparable. From 2023–24, reporting also aligns with NEP's 5+3+3+4 stages. Official report.

Source figures: interpret carefully

  • Enrolment: 25.18 crore (2022–23) → 24.80 crore (2023–24): decrease 0.38 crore, about 1.5%, not the PDF practice answer's 6% for these two years.
  • Older graphic: 26.03 crore (2018–19), 26.45 (2019–20), 26.44 (2020–21), 26.52 (2021–22). The roughly 6% comparison uses an older-series baseline and must carry the methodology caveat.
  • Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra cited for large enrolment declines; enrolment change and dropout rate are different indicators.
  • Source says foundational dropout zero and secondary dropout highest. Zero in a reporting metric does not prove universal access/attendance. Demography, migration, deduplication and collection changes require examination before causal claims.
  • Source incorrectly calls Classes 1–5 all of 'elementary': distinguish primary 1–5 from the broader elementary stage.

Definitions and governance

  • GER = all enrolled at a level, regardless of age ÷ population of the official age group × 100; may exceed 100.
  • Retention, dropout, enrolment and learning outcomes are distinct; reported retention gains cannot alone prove a scheme caused better learning.
  • Low student-to-school ratios cited in Assam, Odisha and Karnataka; a low ratio may reflect remoteness/access needs, not simply waste.
  • Responses: accurate student tracking with privacy safeguards, targeted teachers/facilities, transport and retention support, PM POSHAN/Samagra Shiksha, inclusion and learning assessment.

Enrolment and demographic-dividend debate

  • Source compares 2014–15 to 2023–24: total −24.51 million (−9.45%), elementary −18.7 million, secondary −1.43 million, senior secondary +3.63 million; government −19.89 million, aided −4.95 million.
  • These cross-series calculations require the student-wise collection/deduplication caveat above; falling enrolment does not alone prove falling learning, universal dropout or a shrinking current working-age population.
  • Demographic estimates 6–17 population −17.30 million /TFR 2.01 in 2022 are source estimates, not the same survey as school administrative data. A claimed 60.36% correlation is not proof of a single cause.
  • Demographic dividend: higher working-age share can support growth if education, health, jobs and participation improve; lower fertility creates a future ageing transition, not immediate inevitable labour shortage.
  • Strengthen public-school quality, foundational literacy, teachers, accessible transport, inclusion, vocational pathways and women's employment. Consolidating schools needs safeguards for remote children's access.
  • Plan pensions/elder care and productive employment together; public education and digital tools should complement teachers rather than assume technology alone fixes inequality.
