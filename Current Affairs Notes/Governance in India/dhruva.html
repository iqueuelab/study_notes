Governance in India

DHRUVA and DIGIPIN: digital address infrastructure

Editable notes

December 2025: proposed digital-address ecosystem

  • DHRUVA = Digital Hub for Reference and Unique Virtual Address; Department of Posts policy framework for interoperable Address as a Service. Virtual address labels link descriptive/geocoded addresses to authorised services; the draft Post Office Act amendment in this discussion is a proposal, not enacted law.
  • DIGIPIN, the foundation location layer, is a 10-character alphanumeric code, not ten numerical digits. It represents an approximately 4 m × 4 m grid (~16 m²), correcting the source's 12 m². Developed with IIT Hyderabad/NRSC–ISRO; coordinates can be encoded/decoded offline. India Post FAQ.
  • DIGIPIN itself contains no personal data, stays fixed for coordinates and does not distinguish floors/people at one location; it complements existing addresses/PIN codes. DHRUVA's linked personal-address services raise separate consent, access-control and privacy questions.
  • Proposed roles: Address Service Providers create labels; Address Validation Agencies verify; Address Information Agents/consent managers handle sharing permissions; a governance body manages standards/interoperability/grievances. “NPCI-like” is an analogy, not proof NPCI regulates addresses.
  • Potential uses: delivery routing, emergency response, rural location finding, beneficiary onboarding and service discovery. One-time address updates reaching linked services require implemented integration and permission; they are not an automatic universal effect today.
  • Urban-policy critique: person-linked consented records can be incomplete and are different from building/property maps needed for planning. Pair geospatial infrastructure with minimisation, security and lawful consent; do not claim consent makes all urban mapping impossible.
