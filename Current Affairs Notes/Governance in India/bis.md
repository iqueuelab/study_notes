# BIS certification and consumer safety

## Standards and conformity
- National standards body under Department of Consumer Affairs; headquarters New Delhi. BIS succeeded ISI in **1987**, under the earlier 1986 Act; current **BIS Act 2016 commenced in 2017**.
- Formulates Indian Standards, runs conformity certification/testing and supports consumer awareness. Certification is generally voluntary unless an applicable **Quality Control Order** makes compliance compulsory. [BIS](https://www.bis.gov.in/product-certification/product-certification-overivews/?lang=en).
- ISI mark concerns product conformity; hallmarking concerns specified precious-metal purity. Neither means every conceivable product hazard is ruled out.
- Ecomark addresses environmental criteria; **2024 rules provide CPCB implementation in partnership with BIS**, not a wholly unchanged older arrangement. [Environment Ministry](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2061878&lang=2&reg=3).
- E-commerce enforcement targets products violating applicable compulsory standards/marking, not every uncertified product regardless of legal category.
- Use traceable licences, marketplace checks, credible testing and consumer complaints; simplify MSME compliance without weakening safety. Council-size claims require the applicable rules rather than an unexplained fixed 25-member total.

## November 2025: polyester and raw-material QCO withdrawal
- **12 November 2025 notifications** rescinded specified QCOs under **section 16, BIS Act 2016**, with immediate effect, preserving earlier acts/omissions. The package covered polyester staple fibre and specified yarns plus **PTA (purified terephthalic acid)** and **MEG (monoethylene glycol)** among fourteen chemical/polymer notifications. Revocation of these orders does not abolish BIS or every compulsory product standard. [Official Gazette compilation](https://www.bis.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Ethylene-Vinyl-Acetate-EVA-Copolymers-Rescind-Order-2025.pdf).
- **PTA + MEG → polyester → fibre/yarn → fabric/apparel**. Limited qualifying supply and foreign-plant certification delays had raised downstream users' costs; source industry claims of **₹4/kg fibre increases and up to 30% yarn increases** are dated stakeholder estimates, not universal causal measurements.
- Expected benefits: broader sourcing, lower input costs and export competitiveness for spinners, weavers, processors and MSMEs; support MMF/technical-textile uses such as sportswear, upholstery and industrial fabrics. Price relief depends on competition, import conditions and pass-through. PM MITRA/technical-textile policy are complementary programmes, not programmes newly created by rescission.
- **2017 PYQ revision: 1 only (a)**. Mandatory BIS marking for automotive tyres/tubes differs from **AGMARK**, administered by the Indian **Directorate of Marketing and Inspection**, not FAO. Certification requirements depend on notified product categories; all electrical appliances are not automatically covered as one undifferentiated class.
