# Dam safety: institutions, engineering and risks

## Dam Safety Act, 2021
- Surveillance, inspection, operation and maintenance of **specified dams**; prevent failure disasters and strengthen institutional responsibility.
| Level | Institution / role |
| --- | --- |
| National | **NCDS**, policy/protocol/standard-setting; chaired by CWC Chairperson |
| National | **NDSA**, implement/enforce framework and specified dispute-resolution functions |
| State | **State Committee on Dam Safety**, policy oversight |
| State | **State Dam Safety Organisation**, surveillance, monitoring and safety work |
- **Section 5**: NCDS constitution. **Both NCDS and NDSA notified 17 February 2022**; the PDF's January 2025 blanket 'NCDS not constituted' claim conflicts with official records. Distinguish initial constitution, periodic reconstitution and operational gaps. [Jal Shakti release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1807799&lang=2&reg=48).
## Risks and safeguards
- Ageing/structural deterioration; seismic exposure (**Tehri**); inadequate staff/maintenance; extreme inflows; sedimentation/storage loss; interstate operation disputes (**Mullaperiyar, Kerala–Tamil Nadu**); **2019 Tiware breach, Maharashtra**.
- Dams support irrigation, hydropower, water supply and flood moderation, but cannot eliminate every flood.
- Source ranks India **third in large-dam ownership**, gives **over 5,000/~5,700 dams**, **75–80% over 25 years**, **~200/227/234 over 100 years** across text/graphics. Snapshots differ; don't combine into one current inventory. **75% irrigation dependent on dams** is an unverified scope claim.
- **NRLD**, CWC inventory; **DRIP**, rehabilitation with World Bank/AIIB support for relevant phases; **DHARMA**, Dam Health and Rehabilitation Monitoring Application. Digital records are not automatically continuous sensor measurements.
- Source earthquake-safety centre example: seismic assessment/research; do not infer a separate universal enforcement authority from its name.
- Inspect before/after monsoon and distress events; funded rehabilitation, trained staff, instrumentation, emergency action plans, downstream warning/drills, sediment management and climate-sensitive operating rules. Drones/IoT/satellite/AI complement engineering inspections.
## Structure recall from the graphic
| Type | How it resists load |
| --- | --- |
| Embankment | Compacted earth/rockfill, controlled permeability/core and drainage |
| Gravity | Concrete/masonry mass resists water pressure; sound foundation |
| Arch | Curved concrete transfers load to strong canyon abutments |
| Buttress | Sloping slab/face supported by buttresses; transfers force to foundation |
- **Crest** top; **heel** upstream base; **toe** downstream base; **spillway** controlled overflow; **outlet/sluice** release; **gallery** internal inspection/drainage passage; **freeboard** vertical gap above water level to crest. Permeable material allows flow, impervious material restricts it.

## May 2025: specified dams and responsibilities
- **Dam Safety Act 2021** covers dams **over 15 m**, and **10–15 m with specified qualifying features**; owner obligations include inspection, maintenance, records and emergency preparedness. NCDS/NDSA/State committees/organisations are already mapped in this topic; NDSA's specified regulatory/dispute functions do not settle every water-allocation dispute. [Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/17002/1/A202141.pdf).

## Useful answer content
- Large dams alter flows, sediment supply and aquatic ecosystems; reservoirs can submerge forests, settlements and cultural assets. Benefits—water supply, irrigation, hydropower/flood moderation—must be weighed against ecological and social costs.
- Impact assessment, genuine community consultation, rehabilitation/compensation/livelihood restoration, safety studies, early warning and contingency plans; suitable ecological flows/fish passage and catchment management. Afforestation cannot instantly replace lost mature habitats.
- Interlinking may improve supply in particular cases but “surplus” varies seasonally; high finance/energy needs, ecological changes and displacement require basin-level assessment. Tehri illustrates Himalayan seismic/rehabilitation debate; neither every dam is unsafe nor every project automatically sustainable.
