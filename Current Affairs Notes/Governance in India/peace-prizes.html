Gandhi Peace Prize and Indira Gandhi Prize

Editable notes

Two distinct awards

  • Indira Gandhi Prize for Peace, Disarmament and Development: instituted 1986, administered by Indira Gandhi Memorial Trust; recognises peace, development, disarmament and beneficial use of science. 2015 PYQ on 2014 recipient: ISRO (c), for peaceful space applications. The source's past-awardee names/years are not a dependable chronology; for example its “Mandela 1987” is incorrect. Trust’s awardee record.
  • Gandhi Peace Prize: Government of India/Ministry of Culture, instituted 1995; social, economic and political transformation through non-violence/Gandhian methods. Award includes ₹1 crore, citation and plaque; distinguish this from the source's outdated Indira-prize cash figure.
  • Five-member Gandhi jury: Prime Minister (chair), Chief Justice of India, Lok Sabha Leader of Opposition (or leader of its single largest opposition party if no recognised LoP), two eminent persons. Source's “sometimes one” understates the prescribed composition. President is not a jury member; 2025 PYQ: (c), II, III and IV. Parliamentary answer/code.
  • Correct Gandhi award years: Qaboos bin Said Al Said—2019, Sheikh Mujibur Rahman—2020, Gita Press—2021; the PDF incorrectly labels Qaboos as 2022. Prize year and announcement/presentation year can differ. Culture Ministry list.

January 2026: Graça Machel selected for 2025 prize

  • Graça Machel, Mozambican educationist, human-rights advocate and humanitarian, selected for the 2025 Indira Gandhi Prize for Peace, Disarmament and Development. Recognition concerns education, health/nutrition, economic empowerment and humanitarian work amid difficult circumstances; her work also advances women/children's rights and self-determination.
  • Source announcement identifies an international jury chaired by Shivshankar Menon. Prize comprises ₹1 crore, citation and trophy; the compilation's later ₹25-lakh figure is outdated. Trust award record and code.
  • Existing notes retain the distinction from the Government's Gandhi Peace Prize, its five-member jury, correct awardee years and the 2015/2025 PYQs; the repeated malformed Gandhi chronology adds no new fact.
