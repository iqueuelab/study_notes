# Juvenile justice: child-friendly institutions and pendency

## November 2025: frontline capacity study
- **India Justice Report study, released 20 November 2025**: over **55% of 1,00,904 cases across 362 JJBs** pending as at **31 October 2023**. The PDF's “IJR 2024” shorthand should not turn this into a 2024 release or November 2025 caseload. It reports **24% boards incompletely constituted, 30% without legal-services clinics**, and an average **154 pending cases per board** in the studied data.
- Source pendency comparisons **Odisha 83%, Karnataka 35%** concern reporting jurisdictions, not an unconditional ranking of every State. RTI collection exposed fragmented records: **11% rejected, 24% unanswered, 29% transferred, 36% complete** in the source account. Improve comparable data rather than mistake missing returns for zero workload. [Study](https://indiajusticereport.org/files/IJR_JJ%202025_Main%20Report_.pdf).
- NCRB **Crime in India 2023**, cited by the issue: **40,036 juveniles apprehended in 31,365 IPC/Special and Local Law cases**. Persons and cases are different; this is not the JJB pending-case denominator.
## Institutions and safeguards
- **Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015**, in force **15 January 2016**, replaced the 2000 Act. Children are below 18; age at alleged offence matters for a child in conflict with law. **JJB** handles such cases; **Child Welfare Committee** handles children needing care/protection. District units, special juvenile police units, registered child-care institutions and legal aid support the framework.
- JJB: **Principal Magistrate (Metropolitan/Judicial Magistrate first class) + two social workers, at least one woman**. Counselling, rehabilitation, restoration and reintegration underpin child-friendly inquiry.
- **Section 15:** for a child **16 or older** alleged to have committed a heinous offence, assess mental/physical capacity, understanding of consequences and circumstances; it is a preliminary assessment, not a trial or automatic adult prosecution. Heinous = prescribed **minimum imprisonment seven years or more**. Children's Court determines whether adult trial is needed; place of safety until **21**, followed by evaluation rather than automatic prison transfer. Death or life imprisonment without possibility of release is prohibited for a child. [Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2148/1/a2016-2.pdf).
- Fill vacancies, train members, provide legal-aid clinics/counsellors, protect identity and develop interoperable case tracking with child-data safeguards. Delays risk interrupted education, stigma and poorer rehabilitation; these are risks, not proof every delayed case causes future crime.
