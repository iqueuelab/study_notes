# Nursing leadership and advanced-practice reform

## Beyond International Nurses Day
- **12 May**, Florence Nightingale’s birthday: recognition should accompany safe work, fair pay, professional autonomy and policy representation. Source nurses/midwives 47% workforce needs dataset/year before treating as current headcount.
- Advanced-practice **Nurse Practitioners** can improve primary/critical care where trained/licensed; diagnosis/prescription authority differs by jurisdiction and role. Global examples include Australia, US, UK, South Africa, Botswana, Brazil, Thailand, Zambia.
- National Health Policy 2017 supports mid-level providers. **INC NPcritical-care regulations date 2016**, qualifying the source’s 2017-only initiation; primary-care/midwifery initiatives are distinct programmes, not universal independent-prescribing authority. [INC regulations](https://www.indiannursingcouncil.org/uploads/pdf/1625822647112267150560e815b7cf2af.pdf).
- **National Nursingand Midwifery Commission Act 2023** sets reform framework; passage alone does not establish all new bodies/roles fully operational. Concerns: unclear scope/title/licensing and employment pathways, weak training oversight, resistance to expanded roles, hierarchy/gender undervaluation and limited collective voice.
- Stoma care/diabetes education illustrate specialist work; “no national recognition anywhere” is too absolute. International claims of comparable NP/physician outcomes depend on patient group, scope and study design—not identical competence for every procedure.
- Priorities: accredited practical education/faculty, ethics/leadership, enforceable defined scope and accountability, career/pay progression, team-based care and nurse participation in policymaking. Australia illustrates role/title protection, rural-access objectives and nurse-led services; these are lessons to assess, not reforms automatically copied into Indianlaw.
