Governance in India

Municipal finance, fiscal decentralisation and bonds

Editable notes

October 2025: the municipal fiscal architecture

  • Editorial diagnoses vertical fiscal imbalance: cities deliver water, sanitation, waste management, housing and resilience while major tax bases sit with the Union/States. Source estimates cities produce ~65% of GDP yet municipalities control <1% of total tax revenue, with >70% transfer dependence in many states; these have different denominators and require dates/coverage, not a universal audited city account.
  • GST displaced sources such as octroi/entry taxes; the source's ~19% own-revenue loss is an aggregate estimate, not every municipality's loss. Not every local tax/cess was abolished. Statutory GST compensation was principally for States, rather than an automatic separate municipal compensation entitlement.

Constitutional finance and bonds

  • 74th Amendment Act, 1992, Part IX-A constitutionalised urban local government; municipalities existed earlier. 243W permits State laws devolving functions including Twelfth Schedule matters; 243X enables State-law authority over municipal taxation/funds; 243Y extends State Finance Commission review to municipal finances. 280(3)(c) concerns Union Finance Commission recommendations to augment State consolidated funds to supplement municipal resources based on SFC recommendations.
  • Fifteenth Finance Commission ₹4,36,361 crore local-government grants for 2021–26 covers rural and urban bodies, not exclusively municipalities. Devolution depends on applicable law and award conditions; the Constitution does not automatically give cities a fixed direct share of every Union/State tax.
  • Municipal bonds borrow now against future repayment capacity for infrastructure. Transparent accounts, reliable own revenue/transfers, project cash flows, governance and disclosure affect creditworthiness. The editorial criticises insufficient recognition of grants; do not turn this into a verified blanket claim that all credit ratings legally ignore transfers or that all grants are unconditional/permanent.

Reform choices and trade-offs

  • Proposals: predictable adequate untied transfers; timely SFCs; sound property registers/valuation and collection; suitable user fees with affordability protection; skilled municipal accounting; audit transparency and participatory budgeting. Author also proposes using dependable State/GST-related shares as collateral—this needs a lawful structure and is not an existing universal entitlement.
  • Scandinavian local-income-tax examples illustrate alternative fiscal arrangements, not Indian municipal authority. Bonds and PPPs complement fiscal devolution; debt without credible repayment or excessive regressive user charges can worsen exclusion. Ratings should assess the quality and predictability of all lawful revenue, governance and audit compliance.
  • Practice: for the undated 250-word/15-mark bonds question, balance infrastructure financing with repayment, smaller-city capacity, public accountability and equitable service delivery.
