# Prohibitory orders, begging and lawful prosecution

## Distinguish legal stages
- **BNSS163** replaces CrPC144: urgent orders to address nuisance/danger must satisfy statutory conditions and reasons.
- **BNS223** concerns disobedience of a duly promulgated public-servant order; it is different from BNSS223's complaint procedure.
- **BNSS215 /former CrPC195:** court cognizance of specified offences requires the prescribed written complaint by the relevant public servant or authorised superior. Do not convert this into a universal prohibition on every FIR or investigation into independent offences.
- C.Muniappan 2010 and Daulat Ram/Mata Bhikh lines emphasise the complaint requirement; Jeevanandham 2018 is Madras High Court guidance, not a nationwide Supreme Court ruling on every investigation.
## Begging restrictions
- An Indore prohibitory order and editorial criticism are not themselves a final judicial finding of guilt or invalidity.
- Poverty, homelessness and exploitation need rehabilitation and social protection; restrictions require lawful, proportionate enforcement and due process. Donating to a person is not automatically a crime everywhere.
