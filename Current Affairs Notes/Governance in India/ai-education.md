# AI in education and critical thinking

## Learning design
- AI can support feedback, coding and exploration; uncritical reliance can weaken independent reasoning. Effects depend on task, student and how learning is assessed.
- Match permitted use to learning objectives: independent fundamentals, then assisted exploration, verification and explanation of choices.
- Train teachers/students to check errors, bias and sources; require appropriate disclosure and protect personal data/intellectual property.
- Institutional rules, safety audits and accessible training; a blanket ban or blanket adoption cannot replace course-specific decisions.
- Future of Jobs 2025 highlights analytical thinking/adaptability/AI skills; does not establish traditional programming has no value. India's general laws still apply even without a single comprehensive AI Act.

## February 2026: institutional access and independent thought
- OpenAI's education partnerships envisaged **over 100,000 ChatGPT Edu licences** across institutions including **IIM Ahmedabad, AIIMS New Delhi, Manipal Academy of Higher Education, UPES and Pearl Academy**. The PDF's “IIT Ahmedabad” is an error. Partner/EdTech announcements concern intended access and skills; they do not demonstrate measured learning gains. [OpenAI announcement, 18 February](https://openai.com/index/openai-for-india/).
- The creativity editorial treats **writing as a thinking process**, not merely a finished text. **Cognitive outsourcing** means delegating mental work; **cognitive atrophy** describes the concern that repeatedly avoiding that work may weaken independent skills. These are analytical risks, not proof every AI user suffers cognitive decline.
- Distinct risks: fabricated citations and superficial research; academic dishonesty; an illusion that access to a summary equals understanding; persuasive misinformation/deepfakes undermining informed debate. Language, humanities, sustained reading and original argument support judgment and democratic participation.
- Complement existing verification/disclosure rules with **first-pass independent writing, checking original sources, oral explanation of reasoning, process-based assessment and deliberate reflection**. AI can widen access and reduce routine effort while users retain responsibility for judgment. Claims of inevitable generational intellectual regression remain the author's argument, not an established universal causal finding.
