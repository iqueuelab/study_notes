# Press freedom and public perceptions of misinformation

## Pew survey: report released 24 April 2025
- **35-country** study; India's fieldwork belonged to the **2024** survey, while the report appeared in April 2025. India: **65%** regard made-up news as a very big problem; **68%** value press freedom (very/somewhat important); **80%** perceive media as free (completely/somewhat).
- India and Kenya showed a reverse press-freedom gap: a larger share perceived a free press than considered press freedom important. These are **public perceptions**, not an independent measurement of legal/editorial freedom. [Pew report/methodology](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2025/04/24/free-expression-seen-as-important-globally-but-not-everyone-thinks-their-country-has-press-speech-and-internet-freedoms/), [freedom gaps](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2025/04/24/freedom-gaps/).
- The source contrasts India's **159/180 in RSF's 2024 World Press Freedom Index**. An index and a perception survey measure different things; a rank alone does not establish a same-year decline without comparison.
- Editorial hypotheses—social-media focus, trust in state regulation, corporate/political influence and self-censorship—may explain a gap but are **not causal findings proved by these percentages**.
- Mains: misinformation prevention and free journalism are complementary. Fact-checking, transparent corrections, media literacy, journalist safety and plural ownership support accountability; arbitrary censorship can itself weaken reliable information. Assess regulation against Article 19 safeguards.
## May 2025: South Asia Press Freedom Report
- **IFJ**, on behalf of **South Asia Media Solidarity Network**, released its **23rd annual 2024–25 report**; correct title **Frontline Democracy: Media Amid Political Churn**, covering **1 May 2024–30 April 2025**, with country chapters available 3 May. It is distinct from RSF rankings and Pew public-perception surveys. [IFJ launch](https://www.ifj.org/media-centre/newsletters/detail/south-asia-ifj-launches-23rd-annual-south-asia-press-freedom-report/category/press-releases).
- Report's India analysis alleges legal intimidation, government-advertising leverage, raids/financial pressure, concentrated ownership and self-censorship; references defamation, sedition, UAPA/PMLA. These are attributed report assessments, not a judicial finding that every investigation is unlawful. Historical sedition references must not be confused with unchanged IPC 124A in the post-July 2024 criminal-code framework.
- Source cites **Mukesh Chandrakar's Bastar murder**, impunity and journalist safety; regional concerns include violence in Pakistan/Afghanistan and disinformation/hate speech attributed to political networks. “Authoritarian regime” is an attributed democracy-index classification, not this note's independent legal label.
- Shrinking advertising/jobs, unpaid/precarious freelance/gig work, corporate consolidation and AI disruption threaten financial independence/diversity. Digital independent outlets offer alternative investigative/fact-checking voices but face their own financing and safety constraints.
- **WEF Global Risks Report 2024** ranked misinformation/disinformation highly over the short-term horizon; a survey-based global risk assessment does not establish every allegation about a particular political party. Protect journalist safety and due process, plural ownership, sustainable revenue, transparent public advertising and digital/media literacy.
