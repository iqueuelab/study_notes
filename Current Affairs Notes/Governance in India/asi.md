# Archaeological Survey of India

## Institution and mandate
- **ASI established in 1861**, associated with **Alexander Cunningham**, its first Archaeological Surveyor and later Director-General; attached office of the **Ministry of Culture**, headquarters **New Delhi**. [PIB institutional history](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=78804)
- Undertakes exploration/excavation, conservation of centrally protected monuments/sites, epigraphy, archaeological documentation/publication, museum/site management and scientific/underwater research. Regional circles and specialist archaeology, science, epigraphy and horticulture units support these functions.
- **AMASR Act, 1958** governs monuments/sites of national importance, excavations and protection of specified remains; the **Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972** concerns antiquities/art treasures. Not every historic building in India is ASI-protected.
- AMASR's 2010 framework generally provides **100 m prohibited area**, followed by a **further 200 m regulated area**, measured from protected limits; legal permissions and relevant exceptions must be distinguished. “200 m regulated” does not mean merely the first 200 m from the monument.
## Conservation and interpretation
- Remote sensing, material science and radiocarbon dating contribute to investigation; radiocarbon dating requires suitable organic material and does not directly date all stone architecture.
- Renewed Dwarka/Bet Dwarka exploration in 2025 illustrates maritime archaeology; scientific findings and traditions require separate treatment. [ASI underwater initiative](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2104411&lang=2&reg=48)
- HRIDAY was an urban heritage-development programme. **Adopt a Heritage** partnerships support amenities/visitor management; they do not automatically privatise ownership, replace ASI's mandate or fund every restoration.
- Priorities: authentic conservation, records/digitisation, trained conservators, protection from encroachment/pollution and accessible interpretation; visitor revenue should not displace archaeological integrity.

## Permissions: refine the source's NMA shorthand
- **National Monuments Authority**, under the AMASR framework amended in **2010**, makes recommendations on permission applications and heritage bye-laws. [NMA FAQ](https://www.nma.gov.in/show_content.php?lang=1&level=0&lid=9&ls_id=14&nma_type=0). Competent authorities administer the statutory permission process; “NMA itself permits every activity in 100 m/300 m” is too broad.
- The **prohibited area** generally extends **100 m from the protected limits**, followed by the **200 m regulated area**. Distinguish new construction, repair/renovation, public works and notified exceptions rather than treating all works as one category or every century-old building as automatically centrally protected.
- **2004 PYQ: (a), Culture**—ASI's attached-office status. Existing history, wings, Dwarka, digitisation and conservation-versus-amenities distinctions cover the repeated section.
