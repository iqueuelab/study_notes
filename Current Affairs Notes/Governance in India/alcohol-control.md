# Alcohol control: public health and federal policy

## Health burden and determinants
- Alcohol raises cancer, injury, mental-health, suicide and violence risks; dependence affects livelihoods and families. No consumption level can be guaranteed risk-free; risk varies with dose, pattern and context. [WHO alcohol factsheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/alcohol).
- NFHS-5 source figures are rounded: about **23% men and 1% women** report consumption (adult survey population), not 23% of every age group.
- Biological susceptibility and reward pathways interact with stress/anxiety, peer norms and urban lifestyles; commercial drivers include flavoured products, surrogate sponsorship, influencers, price promotions and dense retail access. Genetic susceptibility does not make addiction inevitable.
- Source cites **2.6 million DALYs in 2021**, **₹6.24 trillion economic harm**, **240% growth over 20 years** and **~50% unrecorded use** without sufficient definitions/citations; treat as source estimates pending verification, not comparable established national totals.
## Policy and reform
- Intoxicating liquor is primarily a State subject; excise revenue creates a public-health/revenue tension. Prohibition in Gujarat/Bihar and differing State sales policies produce varied enforcement contexts.
- Relevant frameworks include NAPDDR/Nasha Mukt Bharat, National Health Policy 2017, Mental Health Policy 2014, National Suicide Prevention Strategy 2022 and NCD action planning. NDPS Act 1985 is not the ordinary beverage-alcohol control statute; source conflates NAPDDR and campaign launch dates.
- A coordinated national alcohol-control policy is an **editorial proposal**, requiring Centre–State and health–finance–social-justice cooperation.
- **Seven A's:** affordability (effective taxation plus illicit-market enforcement); allocation (public-health/de-addiction funding); accessibility (outlet density/hours/delivery controls); advertising (surrogate/digital marketing); attractiveness (warnings, packaging and promotion restrictions); awareness (health/family harms); AI (detect misleading online promotion, subject to human oversight).
- Pair prevention with accessible treatment, counselling, surveillance and illicit-supply control; high prices alone can displace demand toward unsafe liquor.
