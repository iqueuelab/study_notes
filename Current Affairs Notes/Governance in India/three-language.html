Governance in India

Three-language policy and foundational learning

Editable notes

NEP2020

  • Three-language approach retains flexibility: choices involve States/regions/students; at least two languages native to India; no language is to be imposed. NEP.
  • Tamil Nadu's two-language position raises federal, identity and implementation debates; Hindi is not India's constitutionally designated national language.
  • Mother-language familiarity can support early learning; multilingual benefits depend on teaching quality, exposure and resources.

Learning priorities

  • Source ASER claim about Class 3 inability to read a Class 2 passage concerns a specific rural reading benchmark, not absence of every literacy skill among all Indian children.
  • Foundational literacy/numeracy, teacher vacancies, materials and training need attention alongside language policy; salary spending shares do not alone measure teaching quality.
  • Flexible sequencing and local consultation can reduce overload. AI translation is a useful aid, not a substitute for language learning or reliable classroom teaching.

Medium versus language subjects

  • NEP recommends home/mother/local/regional medium wherever possible until Grade 5, preferably Grade 8 andbeyond; choice of medium differs from learning three language subjects.
  • NCERT2024 languages initiative included primers in 104 languages/dialects, not necessarily full translated science/maths curricula in 104 languages.
  • Teacher/material shortages, parental Englishpreferences and State linguistic choices affect implementation. Older AISES2002–09 percentages cannot establish a current 2025 trend.
  • Classical-language learning and foreign-language options differ from formalgovernmentclassicalstatus; Sanskrit/Tamil/Telugu/Kannada/Malayalam/Odia/Pali/Prakrit and Persian references must not imply identical notified status.

Maharashtra withdrawal

  • Maharashtra withdrew its 16 April and 17 June 2025 orders concerning the three-language policy in Classes 1–5 after opposition over Hindi and regional-language concerns; a Narendra Jadhav committee was to examine sequencing, the earlier Mashelkar report and public views, with a three-month report period.
  • The issue incorrectly mandates a foreign language: NEP requires at least two of three languages native to India, with flexible State/region/student choice and no imposed language. The source's 20-student arrangement should not be attributed as a universal NEP rule. NEP paragraph 4.13.
  • Education is in the Concurrent List. Mains balance: multilingual learning/national communication alongside linguistic identity, federal choice, teacher availability, materials and foundational learning. Reciprocal opportunities to learn non-Hindi Indian languages and consultation matter; the policy's text alone cannot prove uniform outcomes.
  • Source diagram: 5+3+3+4 structure = foundational (ages 3–8), preparatory (8–11), middle (11–14), secondary (14–18), including three preschool years and twelve school years.
