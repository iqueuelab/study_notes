Governance in India

Indian Statistical Institute: autonomy and the 1959 Act

Editable notes

December 2025: proposed institutional reform

  • The issue reports a Kolkata protest by over 1,500 academics/researchers over the proposed replacement of the Indian Statistical Institute Act, 1959. Academic freedom, representative governance and government control are the policy issues; protest concerns are not enacted legal consequences.
  • ISI, founded in 1931 by P. C. Mahalanobis in Calcutta, is a statistics, mathematics, quantitative economics and computing research/teaching institution under MoSPI. Its work supports sample surveys, statistical capacity and evidence-based economic planning; Mahalanobis distance is associated with its founder.
  • 1959 Act: section 3 declares national importance; 4 authorises examinations/degrees/diplomas in specified statistical and allied fields; 5–6 cover government finance and audit; 7 requires prior approval for specified institutional changes; 8–10 provide review committees and access; 11–12 provide directions and conditional assumption of control. Accountability powers coexist with institutional autonomy. Act text.
  • Correction: the 28 November 2025 revised draft Bill expressly retains Institution of National Importance status and proposes incorporation as a statutory body corporate. It is inaccurate to state that replacement necessarily abolishes that status or leaves ISI without a statute. Debate the proposed governance/appointment structure and safeguards on their terms; a draft is not an operative repeal. MoSPI revised draft.
  • Practice distinction: the source's answer (b), overseas branches, contrasts with the Act's degree, finance and direction provisions; this undated practice MCQ is not a CSE PYQ.
