# Right to repair and the Repairability Index

## May 2025: proposed transparency framework
- **3 May 2025:** DoCA committee submitted Repairability Index framework report; initial category **smartphones and tablets**. **September 2024** committee chaired by **Bharat Khera**; submission/recommendations are not proof of universal mandatory labelling already operating for all electronics.
- Proposed **five-point index**, OEM self-declaration; six parameters: **disassembly depth, repair information, spare-part availability, software updates, tools, fasteners**. Display proposed at physical/e-commerce purchase points and through packaged-product QR codes. [Official report announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2126409).
- **Right to Repair Portal India, 2022:** information in automobiles, mobiles/electronics, consumer durables and farming equipment. Information access is not automatically a comprehensive enforceable right to independent repair.
## Consumer and environmental case
- Affordable spares, manuals/diagnostics, repairable construction and continued software support can extend product life, cut e-waste and build local repair jobs. Planned obsolescence is deliberate shortening of useful life; not every failure or update ending proves it.
- Consumer advocates, including **Pushpa Girimaji** in the source, seek independent/third-party repair choice; manufacturer-only repair can restrict access and increase costs.
- Evaluate safety, cybersecurity, IP and product responsibility alongside affordability and competition. Self-declaration needs clear criteria and credible verification; parts availability and realistic cost matter beyond a score.
- Source contrasts confrontational US campaigns with India's cooperative approach; US legal rules vary by jurisdiction/product, and tone is an editorial comparison. Circular-economy policy should include repair/reuse before material recycling.
