Governance in India

Organ donation, NOTTO and donor leave

Editable notes

Organisation and law

  • National Organ and Tissue Transplant Organisation (NOTTO), under DGHS, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare; apex coordination of donation/transplant networks and donor/recipient registries under the national programme.
  • Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 1994, amended 2011 to incorporate tissues and related regulation; consent, lawful allocation and prevention of commercial exploitation matter.

  • Donation recall: kidneys, liver, heart, lungs, pancreas, intestine; tissues such as cornea, skin, bone, heart valves and blood vessels. Living donation and deceased donation differ; not every organ can be donated by a living person.

Leave: dated rule

  • DoPT order, 25 April 2023: up to 42 days special casual leave for eligible Central Government servant organ donors, subject to medical recommendations and prescribed conditions.
  • January 2025 awareness report does not create a new NOTTO law, automatic recipient entitlement or a rule covering every private employee. Official donor-leave order.
  • Ethics: informed consent, donor welfare, equitable waiting lists, transparent allocation and anti-trafficking enforcement.

Registry compliance and transplantation ethics

  • NOTTO under DGHS/Health Ministry maintains transplant data with the ROTTO/SOTTO network; THOTA 1994, amended to include tissues, and implementing rules regulate transplantation/commercial dealings.
  • March reporting highlighted hospital failure to update data; the directive sought registration/details within 48 hours of transplant and regular reporting. This does not replace pre-transplant authorisation requirements.
  • Reliable donor/recipient/hospital records support allocation, audit and research, but require confidentiality, access control and correction mechanisms.
  • Allocation depends on applicable organ-specific priority/medical criteria; foreigners, living donation and deceased-organ lists have distinct rules. Missing data are a compliance risk, not automatic proof of trafficking.
  • Low deceased-donor rates, public awareness, clinical capacity, transport and equitable access require action alongside enforcement; pledging alone does not guarantee an organ becomes available.
