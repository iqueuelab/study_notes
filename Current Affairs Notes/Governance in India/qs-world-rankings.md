# QS university rankings and higher-education quality

## QS 2026 edition released in 2025
- **54 Indian institutions**, versus 11 in 2015; **IIT Delhi 123** leads, with IIT Bombay, IIT Madras, IIT Kharagpur and IISc among prominent entries. **Ashoka and Shiv Nadar** are among eight first-time Indian entrants. Ranking edition year is not release year. [QS 2026](https://www.topuniversities.com/world-university-rankings/2026).

- Source combines international-faculty/student weights into 10%; separate indicators explain the nine weighted dimensions. Ratios reflect the selected population/institution; source **national 19:1/Ashoka 11.5:1/desired 10–15:1** are contextual, not a universal legal standard.
- Improvement priorities: research quality and integrity, faculty hiring/time, international collaboration/student access, industry-linked employment support and sustainable campuses. Reputation/citation metrics are partial proxies; rank changes do not prove equal improvement in every institution or guarantee graduate jobs.

## QS Asia 2026: released 4 November 2025
- The **regional Asia ranking**, distinct from the global ranking above, includes **1,526 institutions, 557 first-time entries, 25 locations**. **Mainland China 394**, **India 294**; **HKU first**, Peking second, NTU/NUS joint third; **IIT Delhi 59** leads India. [QS release](https://www.qs.com/insights/qs-world-university-rankings-asia).
- Seven Indian institutions appear in the Asian top 100: IIT Delhi, IISc, IIT Madras, IIT Bombay, IIT Kharagpur, IIT Kanpur and Delhi University. The issue reports Delhi **44→59**, IISc **62→64**, Madras **56→70**, DU **81→95**; mainland China has **25** top-100 institutions. These are edition-specific comparisons, not permanent institution quality labels.
- The Asia ranking includes regional indicators such as **staff with PhD and exchange students** and different weights from the global framework. Do not apply the global table above unchanged. Source Asia weights include academic reputation **30%**, employer reputation **20%**, faculty/student **10%**.
- Expansion affects comparative ranks. Improve research quality/integrity, faculty support, international collaboration and student mobility, funding/autonomy and employability; rank decline alone does not prove deterioration in every indicator. Source says 259 new Chinese entries, whereas QS reports **260 debuts**; net additions and debut counts can differ.
