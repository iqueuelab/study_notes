# Skin diseases: WHA priority and equitable care

## WHA78 and equity
- In **May 2025**, WHA78 recognised **skin diseases as a global public-health priority** (WHA78.15). WHO's May 26 report dates committee adoption to May 24; final resolution records the May 27 plenary. [WHO report](https://www.who.int/news/item/26-05-2025-progress-for-ntds-two-resolutions-adopted-at-wha78).
- Nearly **two billion people** are affected in the source estimate; skin disease brings disability, pain, stigma, mental distress and employment/social exclusion, beyond cosmetic concerns.
- Low-resource countries face specialist shortages, underfunding and delayed diagnosis. Darker skin tones are underrepresented in training/research, risking diagnostic inequity.
- Priorities: integrate dermatology into primary care, train workers, improve affordable diagnostics/treatment, invest in inclusive research and public awareness, reduce stigma and monitor access. Resolution-supported priorities are not proof that all countries have already funded implementation.
## ILDS
- **International League of Dermatological Societies**: global nonprofit federation, formally founded **1935**, based in London; source reports over 200 societies across more than 100 countries. [ILDS history](https://www.ilds.org/who-we-are/our-history/).
- Supports WHO collaboration, training/resources, skin-of-colour inclusion and neglected conditions such as leprosy, scabies and fungal disease; organises the **World Congress of Dermatology every four years**.
- Côte d’Ivoire, Nigeria, Togo and Micronesia were among countries supporting the initiative. ILDS advocacy and professional collaboration complement governments' responsibility for equitable services.
