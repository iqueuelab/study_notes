Governance in India

Education governance: federalism, access and autonomy

Editable notes

Reading the “three Cs” critique

  • Source editorial argues centralisation, commercialisation and communalisation threaten education. These are analytical claims, not a court ruling that NEP 2020 is unconstitutional.
  • Education is in the Concurrent List; CABE, Centre–State consultation, school funding and university governance matter. Proposed UGC rules and final operative regulations must be distinguished.
  • Samagra Shiksha funding linked to PM SHRI participation raised federal concerns; parliamentary recommendations for release are not themselves binding disbursement orders.
  • RTE Act enacted 2009, commenced 2010; Article 21A protects free compulsory education for ages 6–14. Neighbourhood-school distances arise from rules, not a universal constitutional kilometre limit.

Assess evidence and reform

  • School closures reported since 2014 cannot all be caused by a policy adopted in 2020; distinguish mergers, access impacts and comparable data series.
  • HEFA provides capital-financing loans with differing repayment arrangements; one reported student-fee burden cannot describe every financing window.
  • NAAC/NTA integrity issues, academic appointments and textbook changes need institution/edition-specific evidence; preserve critical inquiry, diversity and academic autonomy while ensuring accountability.
  • Priorities: predictable equitable funding, transparent recruitment/accreditation, State participation, accessible public schools and meaningful outcome monitoring.

May 14 editorial: education, employability and research outcomes

  • Core argument: enrolment/degrees need disciplinary depth, adaptable skills and meaningful employment, with equity and quality pursued together. Atal Tinkering Labs, coding and inclusion measures cannot alone establish an educational renaissance; NEP implementation needs financing, teachers and evidence of outcomes.
  • Historical context: University Education Commission/Radhakrishnan 1948–49; Education Commission/Kothari 1964–66; NPE 1968, 1986 (modified 1992); NEP 2020. Source's unnamed “Officers' Commission 1985” is not sufficiently identifiable for exam recall.
  • NEP's flexible entry/exit, mother-tongue learning, Indian Knowledge Systems and curricular flexibility raise implementation/quality questions. Claiming they caused low-quality e-commerce jobs requires causal evidence; degrees do not guarantee employment, but employment also depends on labour demand and economic structure.
  • The claim “no industry stakeholders involved” overstates the evidence: Ministry records wide stakeholder/public/state consultation. Assess participation and uptake rather than asserting none. Ministry consultation record.
  • Source's 42.6% employability in 2025 /44.3% in 2023, 11.72% knowledge-intensive employment, 11 QS top-500 universities and CNCI rank 16 among G20 need the original surveys, populations and years. They are not interchangeable with PLFS unemployment or all-graduate job-placement rates. A separate India Skills Report 2025 assessment reports 54.81%, illustrating incompatible measures. Report partner.
  • GII 2024: India 39, Malaysia 33, Türkiye 37; Bengaluru 56th S&T cluster. Rankings differ between country innovation, cluster size and research intensity; one patent assignee's prominence does not prove an absence of domestic innovation. WIPO country results, cluster ranking.
  • Source questions outcomes of CSIR-NMITLI, Aakash tablet and IMPRINT; demand programme-specific delivery/audit evidence rather than claiming no public outcome documentation exists. Vannevar Bush analogy and blanket dismissal of Indian startups are editorial judgments; service ventures and deep-tech ventures coexist.
  • Reform priorities: practical curriculum/apprenticeships, employability and tracer studies, research quality and reproducible outcomes, transparent funding/audits, industry–academic links and academic freedom. Equity does not logically require sacrificing excellence.

Early childhood care and education

  • NEP 2020 foundational stage includes three preschool years 3–6, then grades 1–2; Balvatika I/II/III may serve 3–4, 4–5, 5–6. Provision varies; all government schools have not already implemented every level.
  • Play, stories, art, movement, exploration support holistic development; avoid premature formal drills/ “schoolification”. School readiness must retain care, nutrition and health. KVS.
  • Source case: preschool expansion in Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu drew some 4–6-year-olds from Anganwadis to schools; not a national order to remove all 3–6-year-olds from Anganwadis.
  • Proposed opportunity: release worker capacity for 0–3 children, pregnant/lactating mothers, home visits, while strengthening educational work via Poshan Bhi Padhai Bhi. Anganwadis already had preschool education responsibilities.
  • First 1,000 days=conception to age 2, different from 0–3. Early stimulation, parent support, nutrition and health interact; Perry Preschool/Odisha studies illustrate early intervention, not identical interventions at every age.
  • Implementation: Education–Women and Child Development coordination, Samagra Shiksha budgets, ECCE-trained teachers/workers, recruitment, age-appropriate spaces, home-visit time and quality monitoring. 14 lakh Anganwadis is a rounded infrastructure reference.
