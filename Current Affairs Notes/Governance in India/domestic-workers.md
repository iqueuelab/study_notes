# Domestic workers: labour rights and social protection

## October 2025: case for a comprehensive framework
- Domestic work includes cleaning, cooking, care, driving and other work in private households. Women, marginalised communities and migrants face low bargaining power, irregular pay/hours, abuse, recruitment-agent exploitation and limited access to grievance redressal. Estimates differ widely; the source's **4–90 million** range is not a precise workforce census.
- **Ajay Malik v State of Uttarakhand, 29 January 2025:** the Supreme Court directed specified Union ministries to form an expert committee to **consider recommending a legal framework**, seeking a report preferably within six months. It did **not** order Parliament to enact a particular law within six months. Findings about other accused remained for their own proceedings. [Judgment, directions 55(iv)–(v)](https://lawbeat.in/sites/default/files/2025-01/Ajay%20Malik%20Vs%20State%20of%20Uttarakhand.pdf).
- **ILO Convention 189 (2011)** promotes decent conditions, rest, fair remuneration and social protection for domestic workers. India had not ratified it at the issue date; adoption internationally does not itself make all its provisions directly enforceable Indian law.
- Absence of an umbrella employment statute is not absence of every protection: criminal/anti-trafficking laws, POSH mechanisms and applicable State wage/welfare measures may protect workers. Household privacy and multiple employers complicate inspection, registration and contributions.
- The issue highlights Tamil Nadu's welfare-board approach and a **Karnataka 2025 proposed Bill**, including registration, written terms and a proposed employer welfare contribution. Treat the latter and the **2017 NPDW draft** as proposals, not enacted nationwide entitlements; the quoted hourly wage needs a dated State notification before use as an operative rate.
- Reform priorities: accessible worker/employer/agency registration, enforceable written terms, wages/rest/leave, portable benefits, regulated recruiters, local complaints support and tripartite oversight. POSH Local Committees are district mechanisms; the recommendation for more local access should not be mistaken for an existing Panchayat-level statutory committee everywhere.
