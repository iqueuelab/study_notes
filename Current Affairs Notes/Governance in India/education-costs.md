# School education costs: access, quality and inequality

## December 2025: household schooling costs
- Editorial uses **NSS 80th-round Comprehensive Modular Survey: Education, April–June 2025**, comparing selected measures with **NSS 75th round 2017–18**. **Article 21A**, inserted by **86th Amendment 2002**, protects free/compulsory education **ages 6–14**; NEP 2020's broader **3–18/2030 universalisation goal** is not an identical constitutional entitlement.
- Survey shares: government schools **55.9%**, private aided **11.3%**, private unaided **31.9%**; private-unaided participation is much higher urban (**51.4%**) than rural (**24.3%**), and higher for boys (**34%**) than girls (**29.5%**). These are enrolled-student shares, not all-child attendance/learning rates. [MoSPI release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2160863&lang=2&reg=48).
- Source fee-incidence figures for government institutions (**25.3% rural/34.7% urban**) span schooling stages, including stages outside Article 21A's age coverage; they do not alone prove illegal fees for every elementary pupil. Source annual-fee range examples: government **₹823–₹7,704**, private **₹17,988–₹49,075**, across different residence/stage cells. Do not treat either range as every child's total education cost.
- Private tuition: **25.5% rural/30.7% urban**, increasing sharply at secondary stages. Source annual coaching examples range **₹3,980–₹13,803 rural** and **₹5,815–₹22,394 urban**, from pre-primary to higher secondary. Fees, coaching and other expenses must be separated; a cross-sectional stage pattern does not establish that the same parents switched schools solely because of fees.
- Financial burdens can widen gender/income gaps and intergenerational disadvantage. Perceived quality, aspiration and school supply influence choices; coaching is not proof every private teacher is unqualified or that tuition guarantees better learning. Strengthen public-school staffing, teacher support/accountability, infrastructure, early/secondary access, targeted aid and proportionate fee oversight. MPCE is per-person consumption expenditure, not household income.
