# PM-JAY coverage and fraud control

## Scheme and accountability
- Launched on 23 September 2018; National Health Authority and State agencies administer cashless hospital coverage of **₹5 lakh per family per year** for eligible secondary/tertiary care, with portability.
- Original socio-economic eligibility was expanded in 2024 to people aged **70 and above regardless of income**. It is not universal hospital insurance for every age group.
- Source committee figures on suspensions, de-empanelment and penalties are dated enforcement information; allegations, recommendations, final penalties and criminal convictions are distinct.
- Risks: ghost claims, inflated bills, unnecessary procedures, identity misuse and denial of eligible care. Administrative errors should not automatically be labelled fraud.
- Combine claim analytics with clinical review, beneficiary feedback, medical-necessity checks, due process and recovery/penalties where established.
- Digital trails help audits; blockchain cannot make false input true. Protect patient privacy and preserve accessible appeals and timely legitimate payments.
## June 2025: enforcement figures and audit
- Source repeats a committee account: **600 hospitals recommended for suspension, 1,113 removal, ₹122 crore penalties on 1,504**. March **12, 2025** report and recommendations are distinct from final implementation; do not add categories as necessarily unique hospitals.
- **4 April 2025 parliamentary reply** reported actions: **549 suspended, 1,114 de-empanelled, ₹122 crore penalties on 1,504**, based on States/UTs. Different snapshot/stage explains why the figures differ; de-empanelment is not proof of criminal conviction. [Lok Sabha record](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2990875/1/lsd_18_IV_04-04-2025.pdf).
- **₹5 lakh/family/year** covers eligible secondary/tertiary hospital care; source **12.37 crore families/bottom 40%** describes socio-economic coverage, not universal insurance for all citizens. The separate **70+ income-independent expansion** is already recorded.
- Detect ghost patients, inflated/fake bills, eligibility misuse and medically unnecessary procedures through claim analytics, clinical audits, beneficiary feedback and verification. Higher fraudulent claims threaten public budgets/quality/trust; this does not mechanically mean every beneficiary pays higher premiums.
- Recommendations: coordinated Centre–State anti-fraud units, transparent due-process suspension/de-empanelment/recovery, accessible grievances, provider compliance and timely genuine payments. AI flags need expert review, explanations and privacy; **blockchain cannot make a false diagnosis/input true** or guarantee tamper-proof administration.
- PDF's NHS/US/Australia examples are broad comparisons, not a verified universal protocol or requirement for biometric authentication in all Australian healthcare.
